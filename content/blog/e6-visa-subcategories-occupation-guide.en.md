# Complete Guide to E-6 Visa Subcategories: E-6-1, E-6-2, and E-6-3 by Occupation

The E-6 visa is not a single visa — it splits into three tracks (E-6-1, E-6-2, E-6-3) based on the nature of the activity.

Pure arts work in entertainment, music, fine art, and broadcasting; entertainment performances inside hotels and tourism facilities; and professional athletes are each reviewed under a different track.

This guide covers the differences between the E-6 subcategories, the qualifications for each occupation type, the documents and screening focus, and the problems that arise when the category is misclassified — all in one place.

<h2 id="section-1">E-6 Visa Subcategories: Why Three Tracks?</h2>

The E-6 visa, under Annex 1-2 of the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act, broadly covers arts and entertainment activities. In practice, however, it splits three ways based on where the activity takes place and the nature of the content.

Even when both are called "performances," a classical concert in a concert hall and a live performance on a tourist hotel stage fall into different categories.

The key point is this.

E-6-1 and E-6-2 may look similar on the surface, but the review intensity and required documents are completely different.

### How the Classification Actually Works

The first thing to look at is "where, with what content, and who is hosting the event."

If the activity venue is a performance hall, broadcasting station, or gallery, it falls under E-6-1; a hotel or entertainment establishment puts it under E-6-2; a stadium or sports club puts it under E-6-3.

In actual screening, the registered business type and the business category on the business registration certificate of the inviting organization drive the classification.

### Borderline Cases Where the Line Blurs

Even cases that look simple on the surface — performance venues inside hotels, outdoor stages within tourism complexes, complex resort stages — sit right on the line.

When this part is weak, even if an applicant files under E-6-1, the reviewing officer may reclassify it as E-6-2 and request additional documents.

In a recent comparable case, the classification was changed mid-review, and a new recommendation letter from the Performance Tourism Association had to be obtained.

To pin down which category your activity falls into, you need to look at both the wording of the invitation contract and the business category registered for the performance venue.

<h2 id="section-2">E-6-1 Arts and Entertainment: Pure Arts and Broadcasting</h2>

E-6-1 covers non-profit-driven artistic activities in music, fine art, and literature, along with work in broadcasting, advertising, film, and theater.

Orchestra members, visual artists, classical musicians, on-air talent, fashion models, film actors, and choreographers are typical examples.

### What Actually Carries Weight Under E-6-1

On paper, the core factors are the applicant's track record in the field and the credibility of the inviting organization.

Education or career certifications, awards, broadcast appearance history, and performance portfolios all serve as the basis for the classification decision.

In practice, reviews move faster when the inviting organization is registered with the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism or is a recognized broadcaster or agency.

### Where E-6-1 Applications Typically Get Stuck

A commonly overlooked point is whether the activity matches the wording of the contract.

If the contract says "broadcasting appearance" but the actual schedule includes hotel live performances mixed in, a request for reclassification to E-6-2 will come up during review.

> **Practical tip:** Spell out the activity venue, number of performances, and content type explicitly in the invitation contract. Vague phrasing like "entertainment activities" actually makes classification harder.

E-6-1 often does not require a performance recommendation letter, but whether it is waived depends on the nature of the activity, so this should be confirmed in advance.

<h2 id="section-3">E-6-2 Hotel Entertainment: The Track Where the Recommendation Letter Is Everything</h2>

E-6-2 covers performances such as singing, instrumental music, acrobatics, and magic that take place in hotel facilities under the Tourism Promotion Act, in entertainment establishments, and similar venues.

Live vocalists, dancers, magicians, acrobats, and band members typically fall here, and screening for this category is the strictest of the three.

### Why E-6-2 Screening Is So Demanding

This category has a separate protection process in place, in line with international recommendations on preventing human trafficking and forced labor.

In practice, a performance recommendation letter from the Korea Media Rating Board or the Korea Performing Arts Producers Association functions as an effectively required document.

The recommendation letter is not a mere formality — it is a process that evaluates whether the performance content, the performer's identity, and the contract terms are appropriate.

### Where E-6-2 Documentation Typically Tangles

Even with a full document set, things stall the moment the payment terms, lodging conditions, or working hours in the performance contract are unclear.

In particular, lump-sum cash payment arrangements or structures where the lodging is in the same building as the venue routinely trigger supplementary explanation requests.

> **Caution:** Under E-6-2, changing the activity venue after entry requires advance notification. Performing at a different hotel or entertainment establishment without notification can be treated as out-of-status activity.

Whether your contract terms meet the recommendation letter standards, and how the revised guidelines apply, needs to be reviewed case by case.

<h2 id="section-4">E-6-3 Sports: Professional Athletes and Coaches</h2>

E-6-3 applies to foreign players, head coaches, assistant coaches, and trainers contracted with Korean professional sports organizations such as professional soccer, baseball, basketball, and volleyball.

Workers in newer professional disciplines such as golf, combat sports, and e-sports may also fall under E-6-3, depending on whether the governing body is officially registered.

### What Actually Counts as Qualifying Under E-6-3

The first thing to verify is whether the contracting counterpart is a domestically registered professional club or association.

The player's own career documentation, history of international competition, and verification from previous clubs serve as the core documents.

Coaches and trainers must hold a coaching certification in the relevant sport — or equivalent experience — and a recommendation from the sport's governing body carries significant weight.

### Commonly Overlooked Points Under E-6-3

Accompanying staff who are not the athlete themselves — managers, interpreters, medical staff — must enter on a different visa, not E-6-3.

When this distinction is weak, the visa applications for family and entourage get tangled all at once, delaying the entire schedule.

<h2 id="section-7">What Actually Goes Wrong When You Get the Classification Wrong</h2>

This is where the trouble really starts.

If you file under E-6-1 but the actual nature of the activity is judged to be E-6-2, requests for supplementary documents and a fresh recommendation letter push the whole timeline back by a month or two.

Conversely, routing E-6-2-type activity through an E-6-1 application invites suspicion of misrepresentation, which works against you in any future application.

### The Trap of Changing Activities After Entry

If the activity venue or performance content changes after entry, you can end up in out-of-status activity territory.

In particular, the moment an E-6-2 holder takes on ancillary duties beyond the performance — serving, soliciting customers, hospitality — they become an enforcement target.

In the field, this is the most common finding in joint immigration inspections.

### How Far Subcategory Changes Are Allowed

Changes between E-6 subcategories are possible only when the actual nature of the activity has changed; changes purely for convenience are almost never accepted.

Such cases are processed under the status-of-stay change procedure in Article 24 of the Immigration Act, and a well-supported justification is the key.

The full text of the law is available at the [Korea Law Information Center](https://law.go.kr), and visa issuance guidance can be found at [Hi Korea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

<h2 id="section-8">Application Procedure and Process Flow</h2>

The general framework is similar regardless of subcategory, but the recommendation-letter step and the background-check intensity differ.

1. Selection of inviting organization and contract execution
2. Application for and issuance of a performance recommendation letter (where applicable)
3. Application for a Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance (filed by the Korean-side inviter with the Immigration Office)
4. After issuance of the certificate, visa application at the local Korean diplomatic mission
5. Foreign resident registration within 90 days of entry

Additional procedural guidance and forms are available from the [Ministry of Justice Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr).

<h2 id="section-9">Pre-Application Self-Check for the E-6 Visa</h2>

- Has the contract wording confirmed whether your activity falls under E-6-1, E-6-2, or E-6-3?
- Does the business category on the inviter's business registration match the activity classification?
- Have you confirmed whether a performance recommendation letter is required and which body issues it?
- Does the contract spell out the activity venue, compensation, lodging, and working hours in concrete terms?
- Do you have a separate visa plan for accompanying family members or entourage?
- Have you considered the possibility of changing the activity venue after entry?

> **Caution:** If even one of the items above is ambiguous, it needs to be cleaned up before filing. Once supplementary requests come in after submission, fixing them takes longer than starting over.

<h2 id="section-10">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. If I perform classical music in a performance hall inside a hotel, is it E-6-1 or E-6-2?**

Even if the venue is a hotel, if the performance content is pure art and the host is a cultural organization, it can be classified as E-6-1.

However, if it is a regular live performance hosted by the hotel itself, it is likely to fall under E-6-2.

The host named in the contract and the nature of the event determine the classification.

**Q2. Who issues the E-6-2 performance recommendation letter?**

The Korea Media Rating Board or the Korea Performing Arts Producers Association — depending on the nature of the content — handles issuance.

When applying for the recommendation, the performance contract, the performer's identification, and video/audio materials are all evaluated together.

Which body is the right fit for your case depends on the performance content itself.

**Q3. Can professional e-sports players also enter on E-6-3?**

If there is a contract with an officially registered body such as the Korea e-Sports Association, and the player is recognized as competing in a regular league, an E-6-3 application is possible.

That said, ordinary streamers and content creators are not eligible for E-6-3 and need to be considered under a different visa.

**Q4. After entering on an E-6 visa, can I take on additional performances at another hotel?**

Activity beyond the original contract — without prior change notification — qualifies as out-of-status activity.

E-6-2 holders, in particular, are held to strict advance-notification requirements when changing activity venues.

Violations work against you when extending your stay or applying again later.

**Q5. Is proof of education absolutely required for an E-6 visa application?**

It depends on the subcategory and occupation.

For E-6-3 sports, competition results and career take priority over academic credentials, and for E-6-1 broadcasting and modeling, a portfolio can substitute for educational documentation.

Which type of evidence carries the most weight in your occupation needs to be assessed case by case.

**Q6. Can I change from E-6 to another visa status, such as F-2?**

If you meet the points-based requirements such as period of stay, income, and Korean language ability, a change to F-2 residence status is potentially available.

That said, for E-6-2 holders, the scope of recognized points may be limited due to the nature of the activity, so a separate review is needed.
