# Complete Breakdown of the F-2 Residence Visa Points System and How to Calculate Each Category

The F-2-7 residence visa requires a minimum of 80 points across the core scoring categories to qualify for application.

The score is calculated by adding up seven core categories — education, age, Korean language proficiency, income, length of residence in Korea, and more — along with bonus and deduction items. If applicants don't know their exact score going in, the paperwork can unravel from the very first step.

This article walks through how each category is scored, the points where reviews most often get stuck, and the practical levers that pull your bonus points higher.

<h2 id="section-1">What Is the F-2 Residence Visa Points System?</h2>

The F-2-7 is a pathway that lets non-specialized workers obtain residence status by accumulating points.

It is administered under notices from the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr) and is built around seven core categories plus bonus and deduction items.

### The Broad Eligibility Framework

The baseline requirements are being 18 years or older and having maintained legal residence in Korea for a certain minimum period.

Holders of E-1 through E-7 and D-5 through D-10 visas are eligible, while pure unskilled-labor visas (E-9, H-2) are excluded as a rule.

Scoring 80 or higher on the core categories clears the first hurdle, and any bonus points you've earned can help cover gaps.

### The Basic Structure of the Score

The total is calculated as **seven core categories + bonuses − deductions**.

Very few applicants reach 80 points from core categories alone — most stack up bonus points to push the total across the line.

What separates one applicant from another is how strategically those bonuses are engineered.

<h2 id="section-2">How Education Points Are Scored</h2>

Education is scored based on your highest degree, and domestic degrees are weighted differently than foreign ones.

### Base Points by Degree

| Education | Base Points | Notes |
|------|----------|------|
| Doctorate | 35 pts | Same domestic or foreign |
| Master's | 32 pts | Same domestic or foreign |
| Bachelor's | 25 pts | Same domestic or foreign |
| Associate degree | 18 pts | Recognized Korean institutions |
| High school | 5 pts | Base points only |

Foreign degrees must be verified through the [Korean Council for University Education](https://www.kacis.or.kr) or by apostille / consular authentication to count.

### Bonus Points for Domestic Degrees

Holders of associate degrees or higher from Korean institutions receive additional bonus points.

In particular, a domestic bachelor's degree or higher comes with bonus points stacked on top, making it significantly more advantageous than the base score alone.

In practice, the fastest route to crossing the 80-point threshold is a domestic master's degree combined with bonus points.

> **Practitioner tip:** If foreign academic credential verification is missing, your education score is treated as zero. Verification certificates take time to issue, so start preparing two to three months before you apply.

<h2 id="section-3">Age Points and Korean Language Points</h2>

Younger applicants score higher on age, while Korean proficiency is evaluated by TOPIK level or completion of the Korea Immigration & Integration Program (KIIP).

### Points by Age

| Age Range | Points |
|----------|------|
| 18–24 | 25 pts |
| 25–29 | 23 pts |
| 30–34 | 20 pts |
| 35–39 | 18 pts |
| 40–50 | 15 pts |
| 51 and older | 5 pts |

Age is one category you can't control — the longer you wait to apply, the lower your score drops.

Applicants in their 40s and beyond have to make up the difference in other categories.

### Points for Korean Proficiency

| Korean Level | Points |
|------------|------|
| TOPIK Level 5+ / KIIP Stage 5 completion | 20 pts |
| TOPIK Level 4 / KIIP Stage 4 completion | 15 pts |
| TOPIK Level 3 / KIIP Stage 3 completion | 10 pts |
| TOPIK Level 2 / KIIP Stage 2 completion | 5 pts |

Completing KIIP Stage 5 is the most point-efficient option, because in addition to the Korean language score you also earn social integration bonus points.

The [KIIP program](https://www.socinet.go.kr) is run free of charge, and the testing schedule and stage recognition rules shift slightly year to year.

The quickest path for your specific situation is something best confirmed through consultation.

<h2 id="section-4">Income, Assets, and Length of Residence in Korea</h2>

The income category is calculated against your annual income from the previous year.

Income thresholds are adjusted somewhat each year, so the exact current cutoff needs to be confirmed with your local immigration office.

### How the Income Category Is Calculated

Income points are determined by adding up your wages, business income, and other income filed in your own name.

Hit the threshold and you get 10 points; clear it by a certain margin and additional bonus points are layered on top.

> **Caution:** Income must be documented with tax filings submitted to the National Tax Service ([Hometax](https://www.hometax.go.kr)). Bank statements alone won't be accepted as proof.

### Points for Length of Residence in Korea

| Length of Residence | Points |
|----------|------|
| 5 years or more | 15 pts |
| 4 years | 10 pts |
| 3 years | 7 pts |
| 2 years | 5 pts |
| 1 year | 3 pts |

Only periods of legal residence count, and extended time spent outside Korea will be deducted.

In practice, it's common to see applicants lose residence-length points because short trips abroad have piled up over time.

<h2 id="section-5">Bonus Categories — The Key to Lifting Your Score</h2>

Most applicants can't hit 80 from the core categories alone, which means the bonus items are ultimately what decide who clears the line.

### Major Bonus Categories

The following items qualify for additional bonus points.

- Holding a domestic degree (with tiered points for bachelor's, master's, and doctorate)
- Volunteer service hours (based on cumulative annual hours)
- Completion of KIIP Stage 5 (social integration program)
- Holding officially recognized Korean certifications
- Working in advanced/strategic industries
- Residence in non-metropolitan regions (outside the Seoul capital area)

Volunteer hours are only credited if registered on the [1365 Volunteer Portal](https://www.1365.go.kr); informally drafted confirmation letters won't count.

### A Sample Bonus-Point Strategy

An applicant sitting around 65 points on core categories can typically reach 80 by combining KIIP Stage 5 completion + volunteer work + a Korean certification.

In a recent comparable case, an applicant covered a 12-point shortfall entirely through bonus categories and passed — but figuring out which combination fits your situation always starts with running a score simulation.

<h2 id="section-6">Deduction Categories — Miss These and You're Blocked</h2>

Unlike bonuses, deductions often accumulate without the applicant even realizing it.

### Causes of Deductions

| Reason for Deduction | Points Deducted |
|----------|----------|
| Immigration Control Act violation (fines) | −10 to −50 depending on case |
| Activities outside permitted visa status | −50 or more |
| Tax delinquency | −5 to −10 |
| Criminal record (fines) | −10 to −50 |

Minor offenses like drunk driving, assault, or theft also trigger deductions.

Tax delinquency is something applicants frequently aren't aware of until it surfaces during review.

> **Caution:** Even if you've forgotten a past fine, it will show up exactly as it stands on your [criminal background check](https://www.police.go.kr). Pull your own record first, before you start calculating points.

### When Deductions Are Lifted

Deductions are restored after a set period, and the timing of that restoration determines when you should apply.

For records involving fines, the deduction generally clears after a certain period following completion of the sentence — but the exact restoration date depends on the specifics of your case.

<h2 id="section-7">F-2-7 Application Procedure and Documents</h2>

Once your point calculations are done, the next step is preparing documents and filing the application.

### Application Process

Applications are filed in person at the immigration office having jurisdiction over your residence, after booking online through [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

Processing times vary by office, so finding the fastest one can save you significant time.

### Core Document Checklist

| Category | Documents |
|------|------|
| Identity | Passport, Alien Registration Card, photos |
| Education | Degree certificate, transcripts, credential verification |
| Income | Wage / business income proof, tax payment certificates |
| Korean | TOPIK score report or KIIP completion certificate |
| Bonuses | Volunteer service confirmation, certifications, residence proof |
| Background | Criminal background checks from home country and Korea |

No matter how many documents you submit, if the evidence for each scoring category doesn't line up, supplementary requests will keep coming back.

<h2 id="section-8">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. If I can't reach 80 points on the core categories, is the application automatically denied?**

Even if your core score falls short, you can still apply as long as bonus points bring your total to 80 or higher.

That said, the scope and documentation rules for bonus points are fixed — self-estimated points won't be accepted.

**Q2. Between completing KIIP Stage 5 and earning TOPIK Level 5, which is more advantageous?**

KIIP Stage 5 is more point-efficient.

It earns you both the Korean language points and the social integration bonus on top.

**Q3. Do foreign degrees always require verification?**

To receive education points, you need either credential verification or apostille/consular authentication.

Without verification, your education score is treated as zero, which can push your total below the threshold and lead to rejection.

**Q4. If I have a past drunk-driving fine, is it hard to apply?**

It falls under deductions, and the deduction is only lifted once a certain period has elapsed after the sentence is completed.

Because the restoration timing varies case by case, a preliminary review is essential.

**Q5. Can I apply for permanent residency (F-5) right after being approved for F-2-7?**

After holding F-2-7 for a set period and meeting additional requirements, you can apply to convert to F-5.

F-5 is evaluated under separate review criteria, distinct from the points system.

**Q6. Can I calculate my own score?**

A rough estimate is something you can do on your own, but the recognition rules for bonuses and deductions shift slightly every year.

In particular, the scope of eligible volunteer activities, how residence time is calculated, and the standards for income documentation often differ from how applicants interpret them on their own.
