## What is Korea's Student Visa (D-2)?

The D-2 visa is a study stay qualification established under Appendix 1, Item 8 of the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Control Act. It is granted to foreigners pursuing regular academic programs at Korean educational institutions. It is divided into subcategories: junior college (D-2-1), university (D-2-2), graduate school (D-2-3), university-affiliated language institute (D-2-4), and others.

## Eligibility Requirements

- **Admission letter**: Issued by a Ministry of Justice-approved Korean higher education institution
- **Financial ability**: Proof of at least one year's tuition and living expenses
- **Academic requirements**: Meeting the admitting institution's requirements
- **Health**: Some schools require a medical certificate

## D-2 Visa Subcategories

| Category | Description |
|----------|------------|
| D-2-1 | Junior college regular program |
| D-2-2 | University undergraduate program |
| D-2-3 | Graduate school (master's/doctoral) |
| D-2-4 | University-affiliated language institute |
| D-2-5 | Branch campus of foreign university |
| D-2-6 | KAIST and similar institutions |
| D-2-7 | Government scholarship students |

## Required Documents

1. **Visa application form**
2. **Passport** (valid for 6+ months)
3. **Passport-size photo** (1 copy)
4. **Admission letter** from Korean institution
5. **Financial proof**: Bank statement, scholarship letter, etc.
6. **Academic transcripts** (from most recent school attended)
7. **Graduation or enrollment certificate**
8. **Criminal background check** (required by some countries)
9. **Application fee**: Varies by visa type

## Application Procedure

### Applying from Abroad (First Entry)

1. Apply to Korean institution and receive admission letter
2. Visit nearest Korean embassy or consulate
3. Submit visa application documents
4. Receive D-2 visa (approximately 1–3 weeks)
5. Register as a foreigner within 90 days of arrival (per Article 31 of Immigration Control Act)

### Changing Status from Within Korea

1. Visit local Immigration Office
2. Submit status change application and documents
3. Approval (approximately 2–4 weeks)

## Stay Duration and Extension

- **Duration**: Up to 2 years (within enrollment period)
- **Extension**: Possible upon submission of enrollment certificate
- **Part-time work**: Requires separate part-time work permit (max 20 hours/week)

## Part-Time Work for Students

D-2 visa holders may work part-time under Article 20 of the Immigration Control Act.

- **Conditions**: Enrolled for 6+ months, GPA of 2.0 or above
- **Hours**: Maximum 20 hours per week (unlimited during vacation periods)
- **Application**: At the Immigration Office in the area of your school
- **Restricted industries**: Adult entertainment, massage establishments, etc.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Can I get a D-2 visa just by enrolling in a language school?**
A. Yes, university-affiliated language institutes (D-2-4) are covered under D-2. However, private language academies require a D-4 (general training) visa.

**Q. Can I stay in Korea after graduation to look for a job?**
A. You can change to a D-10 (job-seeking) visa to remain in Korea for up to 1 year after graduation to search for employment.
