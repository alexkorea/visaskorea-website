# E-7 Visa Salary Standards and Minimum Wage Requirements — How Each Job Category Is Actually Calculated

The E-7 visa salary must meet a certain percentage of Korea's per capita GNI (Gross National Income), tied to your specific job category, before issuance moves forward.
This applies to foreign professionals hired into roughly 85 designated occupations, with the applicable salary varying by company size and job tier.
Below is a breakdown of how minimum salaries are calculated by occupation, what documents prove them, and where applicants most often get stuck during review.

<h2 id="section-1">The Starting Point for E-7 Salary Standards</h2>

### How GNI-Linked Standards Work

E-7 is not simply a "clear the minimum wage" visa.
The [Ministry of Justice Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr) publishes an annual minimum salary for each occupation, calculated from the per capita GNI figure released by the Bank of Korea.
In practice, once that year's GNI figure is finalized, the relevant ratio (for example 80% or 100%) is applied to set the floor.
In many cases, this GNI-based salary actually comes out higher than the hourly minimum wage conversion.

### Relationship with the Minimum Wage Act

The employment contract itself follows the minimum wage published by the [Ministry of Employment and Labor](https://www.moel.go.kr).
This is the standard typically reflected as hourly or monthly pay on a foreign worker's contract.
For visa review, however, you must satisfy whichever of the two thresholds is higher.
If your salary is weak on this front, even a fully legal contract will fail at the visa stage.

<h2 id="section-2">How the Minimum Salary Is Applied by Job Category</h2>

### Difference Between General Professionals and Skilled Workers

E-7 is broadly divided into Professional Personnel (E-7-1), Semi-Professional Personnel (E-7-2), General Skilled Workers (E-7-3), and Skilled Technicians (E-7-4).
Each category applies a different GNI ratio.

| Category | Job Type | GNI Standard Applied | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E-7-1 | General professionals | 80% or more of prior-year per capita GNI | Education and experience requirements separate |
| E-7-2 | Semi-professionals | 80% or more of prior-year per capita GNI | Adjustments by occupation |
| E-7-3 | General skilled workers | At or above minimum wage | Subject to intake quota |
| E-7-4 | Skilled technicians | Points system + at or above minimum wage | Residency duration condition |

The exact ratio applied to each occupation changes annually, so you should re-check the standard tied to your specific job code right before filing.

### Exceptions for Advanced Tech and High-Income Roles

Workers in advanced technology fields designated by the [Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy](https://www.motie.go.kr) qualify for relaxed standards compared to the general E-7.
This is precisely where things tangle if the wrong job code is assigned.
In actual review, the most common sticking point is a mismatch between the occupation code the company filed and the actual duties performed.

> **Caution:** The advanced technology category list is revised every year. To confirm exactly which category your role falls into this year, verify through a consultation.

<h2 id="section-3">Calculating the Minimum Salary Tied to the GNI Standard</h2>

### How the Formula Actually Works

The GNI-based salary is calculated as "prior-year per capita GNI × the ratio for the occupation."
The reference figure is the confirmed GNI value released through the [Bank of Korea Economic Statistics System](https://ecos.bok.or.kr).
In practice, applications filed before the new figure is finalized still use the prior standard, while applications filed after the year change reflect the new GNI.

### Items Counted Toward Salary vs. Excluded

Salary includes some items and excludes others.

- Counted: base pay, regular bonuses, job allowances
- Excluded: variable incentives, partial meal allowances, vehicle allowances, business travel costs
- Borderline: performance pay (depends on payment pattern)

A salary that looks high on paper often falls short of the threshold once you tally only the items that actually count.
This is usually where the contract has to be revised.

<h2 id="section-4">Where Salary Calculations Actually Get Stuck</h2>

### Mismatches Between Contract and Actual Payments

Reviewers do not look at the contract salary alone.
The company's four major insurance filings, withholding tax records, and bank deposit records are reviewed alongside it.
If those three diverge, the salary itself becomes suspect.
Even with thick paperwork, a weak narrative connecting these records will trip you up immediately.

### A Salary Disproportionate to Company Revenue

If a newly incorporated company assigns more than half of its revenue to a single foreign hire's salary, even meeting the formal requirements will not save it at review.
Reviewers look at the company's financial statements alongside the foreign employee's salary ratio.
Without a strong explanation here, the application is usually denied.

> **Practical Tip:** The smaller the company's revenue, the more strictly proof of salary source and ability to pay will be scrutinized.

<h2 id="section-5">Documentation Differences by Company Size</h2>

### Large and Mid-Sized Enterprises

Listed companies and corporations above a certain size can demonstrate ability to pay relatively easily, using financial statements and the business registration certificate.
As long as the salary standard is met, these cases generally clear review without major issues.

### Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises and Startups

SMEs and startups face additional documentation requirements.

| Document | Content | Verification Point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VAT taxable base certificate | Revenue for the past 1–2 years | Ability to pay the salary |
| Four-major-insurance subscriber roster | Ratio of Korean hires | Foreign hire quota |
| Corporate registry extract | Capital and officer structure | Company stability |
| Office lease agreement | Whether the workplace exists | Screening for paper companies |
| Statement of reason for foreign hire | Why a Korean was not hired | Specialty of the role |

The first thing to check is whether the company has the revenue and staffing structure to legitimately employ a foreign worker.
If this is weak, no matter how high the listed salary is, it will be questioned at review.

### Mandatory Quota for Foreign Hires

According to [Hi Korea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) guidance, E-7 foreign employment cannot exceed a certain ratio relative to the number of Korean employees.
Violating this ratio blocks issuance even when the salary is met.
The ratio applied to each company depends on the occupation and corporate form, so the exact figure for your company should be confirmed directly with the relevant immigration office.

<h2 id="section-6">Recently Changed Review Points</h2>

### Strengthened Points System

E-7-4 Skilled Technicians is operated under a points system, where salary, Korean language ability, length of stay in Korea, and prior work experience are all scored together.
The weight given to the salary score has recently been adjusted.
Because this scoring table changes each year, this year's score for your case should be confirmed accurately through a consultation.

### Wage Arrears and Tax Delinquency History

If the company has a history of unpaid wages or tax arrears, the foreign hire application itself can be restricted.
A tax payment certificate issued by [Hometax of the National Tax Service](https://www.hometax.go.kr) is required as supporting documentation.
In practice, even a single past delinquency that has since been settled can make review noticeably stricter.

### Comparison with Existing Foreign Employees' Average Salary

If the company already employs foreigners under E-7, the reasonableness of the salary gap between the existing staff and the new hire is also reviewed.
A new hire at the same rank receiving a noticeably lower salary easily draws suspicion, even if the formal requirements are met.
This is where the difference shows up.

<h2 id="section-7">Common Pitfalls in E-7 Salary Calculation</h2>

### Timing of Currency Conversion

For salaries paid by overseas remittance, the amount may clear the threshold at one exchange rate and fall below it at another.
Specifying the salary in Korean won within the employment contract is the safer route.

### Application During the Probation Period

If the contract includes a wage reduction clause for the probation period, the probation-period salary must also exceed the threshold.
"80% during probation" is a common practice, but you have to separately confirm whether that 80% still clears the GNI floor.
This is where applicants slip up most often.

### Conversion for Part-Time or Reduced-Hour Roles

E-7 is, in principle, a full-time, regular-employment visa.
Starting in a position with fewer than 40 hours per week causes the salary to be auto-converted, easily falling short of the standard.
The form of the contract itself needs to be checked first.

<h2 id="section-8">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

### Q1. Does the E-7 salary standard change every year?

Yes. It is updated annually based on the prior-year per capita GNI confirmed by the Bank of Korea.
For the most current standard at the time of your application, check [Hi Korea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) or confirm through a consultation with an administrative agent.

### Q2. Is E-7 possible as long as I clear the minimum wage?

No.
Minimum wage governs the legality of the employment contract itself, while the E-7 visa applies a separate GNI-linked standard.
Depending on the occupation, 80% or more of GNI — sometimes higher — is required.

### Q3. Can performance pay or incentives be included in salary?

If the payment pattern is regular and the amount is specified in the contract, there is a chance it can be counted.
Highly variable incentives or one-time bonuses are difficult to recognize as part of the calculation.
Whether your specific company's pay structure qualifies should be reviewed individually.

### Q4. If my salary falls just slightly below the threshold, is rejection automatic?

It depends on the occupation.
Advanced-technology and high-income preferential roles may have relaxed standards, and points-system occupations may allow other items to compensate.
Confirming your job code and the result of your point calculation comes first.

### Q5. If the company's revenue is small, is it enough to just match a single foreign hire's salary to the threshold?

Even meeting the formal requirements draws suspicion at review.
If the salary paid to a single foreign worker is unrealistic relative to the company's revenue, additional proof of ability to pay will be requested.
Newly incorporated companies tend to be especially vulnerable here.

### Q6. How many points is salary worth in the E-7-4 points system?

Points are allocated by salary band, and the scoring table is revised each year.
The points your current salary would earn this year, and the gap to the passing line, need to be calculated separately.
