# The Complete Guide to E-7 Visa Education and Work Experience Requirements

The education and work experience requirements for the E-7 visa may look straightforward on the surface, but in actual review, outcomes hinge on **the relevance of your degree major to the job**, **how years of experience are counted**, and **the consistency of supporting documents**. The baseline rule is: "Bachelor's degree or higher + job related to major" OR "Associate degree + 1 year of relevant work experience" OR "High school or below + 5+ years of experience." But this is only the minimum threshold — additional weighted requirements apply depending on the occupation.

The place where most applicants get stuck is precisely this "major relevance" issue. Even with a bachelor's degree, if the major is disconnected from the job, the reviewer will ask for documents proving the connection through work experience. In fact, it's not uncommon to see applicants with no degree but solid experience pass more cleanly. The key is whether the three pillars — degree, experience, and job description — connect into a single coherent story.


## <span id="section-1"></span>1. E-7 Education and Experience Requirements at a Glance

The E-7 (Specially Designated Activities) visa is issued when foreign nationals are hired in professional, semi-professional, or skilled-function occupations designated by the Ministry of Justice. Eligibility opens only when **either education or experience clears the threshold**. Put differently, if both are borderline, filing itself becomes difficult.

### The Lens for Evaluating Education and Experience

Even before the document count, what reviewers look at is **"whether this person can actually perform this job."** Education is the starting point for that judgment; experience is supporting evidence. When education is strong, review ends quickly. When it's weaker, experience has to fill the gap.

## <span id="section-2"></span>2. How Far Education Credentials Are Recognized

### The Bachelor's Degree Standard

In Korea, a bachelor's degree means graduation from a 4-year university. For overseas universities, the same applies as long as the degree is recognized as a bachelor's under the home country's education system. That said, **overseas degrees require an apostille or consular verification** for reviewers to accept them as authentic.

### Associate Degrees and 2- or 3-Year Programs

Graduates of 2-year junior colleges are classified as holding an "associate degree," which triggers additional experience weighting. A point many miss in practice is verifying whether the home country's junior college system is at the same level as a Korean associate degree. When systems differ by country — 3-year, 2.5-year, etc. — the relevant authority runs a separate equivalency review.

### Major Relevance

Even before the degree itself, reviewers look at **the link between your major and the job**. For example, if an applicant with a bachelor's in Business Administration applies for an IT engineer role, the reviewer will ask, "Why are you qualified for this job?" If that explanation is weak, no quantity of documents will prevent the case from being held up.

<div>
  <strong>⚠️ Caution:</strong> Submitting just a copy of the degree certificate is not enough. The transcript must show how the courses you took connect to the job. If the transcript is not available in English or Korean, a translation plus notarization is required separately.
</div>

### Preferential Treatment for Master's and Doctoral Degrees

A master's degree or higher waives the experience requirement, but scrutiny of major relevance becomes even stricter. Even a PhD holder moving into a job in a completely different field can end up with a harder explanation than a bachelor's holder with no experience.

## <span id="section-3"></span>3. Work Experience Requirements and How Years Are Calculated

### When Does the Clock Start?

In principle, it starts from regular employment after graduation. Periods of internship, part-time work, or freelancing are excluded by default — and to avoid that exclusion, you need records of social insurance contributions or equivalent official documentation.

### What Must Appear on the Employment Verification Letter

Even with many documents, if the employment verification letter is missing these items, the experience won't be recognized.

<div>
  <strong>✅ Employment Verification Checklist</strong>

    Official name of employer (company) and business registration number (or overseas equivalent)
    Exact start and end dates (YYYY-MM-DD)
    Specific description of position and duties
    Weekly working hours (to determine part-time status)
    Signature of issuer, company seal, and date of issue
    Contact information of the issuing officer (for reviewer verification)

</div>

### Judging Whether Jobs Are the Same or Similar

The most frequent snag in counting years of experience is whether "that experience is in the same field as the applied job." For example, even with 3 years as a server engineer, if the applied position is frontend development, the reviewer will ask for additional evidence of related technology stacks and project history.

### The Overlapping-Experience Problem

Claiming you worked at two companies during the same period doesn't add up — only one will be counted. Typically, the primary income source is treated as the main experience. Overlapping periods to pad length will immediately raise suspicion.

## <span id="section-4"></span>4. Scenarios by Education and Experience Combination

### Scenario 1: Overseas Bachelor's + 1 Year of Experience (IT Engineer)

A Computer Science bachelor's plus 1 year of experience is the cleanest structure. When core courses (data structures, algorithms, databases, etc.) are visible on the transcript, review moves quickly.

### Scenario 2: Bachelor's with Unrelated Major + 3 Years of Experience

A Business bachelor's applying as a marketing manager is usually smooth. On the other hand, a Humanities bachelor's applying as an IT manager has to spell out how the experience contributed to IT responsibilities on a project-by-project basis.

### Scenario 3: Associate Degree + 5 Years of Experience (Skilled Function)

For E-7-4 (skilled function) categories like cooks or welders, experience outweighs education. Typically, 4–5 years or more of specialized experience in the same field combined with a home-country certification is required.

### Scenario 4: High School Graduate + Long-Term Experience

With only a high school diploma, at least 5 years of experience in the same job is required. In this case, **third-party evidence from official institutions** — such as tax certificates issued by the home country's tax authority or national pension (or equivalent) contribution records — becomes decisive.

<div>
  <strong>💡 Practical Tip:</strong> When education is weak, showing the "depth" of experience beats simply extending years. A portfolio document describing one or two projects in concrete detail persuades reviewers more than 10 years' worth of employment letters.
</div>

## <span id="section-5"></span>5. Requirement Differences Across the 85 Occupations

E-7 is not a single set of requirements — each of the 85 occupations has its own detailed criteria. Broadly, it divides into Professional (E-7-1), Semi-Professional (E-7-2), General-Function (E-7-3), and Skilled-Function (E-7-4) personnel.

### E-7-1 Professional Personnel

Includes university professors, researchers, IT specialists, management professionals, and so on. Review is centered on degrees, and those with a master's or higher have a strong chance of experience being waived.

### E-7-2 Semi-Professional Personnel

Duty-free shop sales staff, hotel receptionists, airline cabin crew, and similar roles. **Hands-on experience in the relevant industry and language ability** are evaluated before education.

### E-7-3 General-Function Personnel

Animal keepers, aquaculture technicians, shipbuilding welders, and similar roles. Certifications are decisive here. Foreign certifications go through an equivalency review against domestic counterparts.

### E-7-4 Skilled-Function Personnel

Evaluated through a points-based system, scoring education, experience, income, and Korean language ability. Here the "total points" matter before "meeting requirements."

### Experience-Related Documents

In addition to the employment verification letter, bundling in **pay stubs, certificates of employment, tax payment records, and social insurance contribution history** strengthens credibility. When only an employment verification letter is submitted, reviewers start by questioning "whether the company actually exists."

### Translation and Notarization

It's safer to use a professional translation service or a certified translator. Self-translations almost always end up being redone. Before translation quality, the uniformity of format and terminology is what reviewers check first.

## <span id="section-7"></span>7. Pressure Points in Actual Review

### The Weight of the Job Description

The employer-issued job description must interlock with the applicant's experience and education as three connected pillars. A frequent snag in practice is when the duties written in the job description are too generic. Rather than "performs marketing duties," it should read something like "global B2B SaaS marketing, lead generation in English-speaking markets, monthly OKR management." Only then can the reviewer judge the relevance of education and experience.

### The Salary Benchmark

For E-7-1, a minimum salary is required based on occupation-specific benchmarks or on a Gross National Income (GNI) linkage from the prior year. If salary falls short, even when education and experience are met, the application will be denied. The key is this: **education and experience are for "gaining entry eligibility"; salary is a "condition of maintenance."**

### The Inviting Company's Eligibility

As much as the applicant's education and experience, the inviting company's financial health and foreign-employment quota are scrutinized. If the company is weak on prior-year revenue, national tax payment records, or the ratio of foreign to domestic employees, the case can be rejected regardless of the applicant's own qualifications.

### The Coherent Story Reviewers Look For

In the end, what matters is whether "the degree, experience, and job connect as a single story." Degree as foundation, experience as deepening, and this job as the next step — when this structure is visible, even a lean document set clears review quickly. Conversely, piling up documents without a story actually invites suspicion.

## <span id="section-8"></span>8. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Missing Degree Verification

Submitting only a copy of the original overseas degree without apostille or consular verification leads to immediate rejection. If your home-country school doesn't support this process, a separate authorization is needed — so start preparation **at least 4 weeks in advance**.

### Mistake 2: Inflating Years of Experience

Vague phrasing like "about 5 years" won't fly. Start and end dates must be specified in YYYY-MM-DD format, and overlapping employment in the same period is not summed.

### Mistake 3: Omitting the Transcript

Submitting only the degree certificate and leaving out the transcript weakens proof of major relevance. Especially in mismatched-major cases, missing a transcript weakens this link and immediately triggers additional requests from the reviewer.

### Mistake 4: Vague Job Descriptions

Using only abstract phrasing like "international duties" or "managerial role" makes matching against education and experience impossible. Industry, product, target market, and tools used should all be written out.

### Mistake 5: Name or Date Inconsistencies

When the English name on your passport differs from the names on your education and experience documents, or when date formats differ and create contradictions — these weak points damage the credibility of the entire factual record.

<div>
  <strong>⚠️ Caution:</strong> Falsified employment verification letters can escalate into criminal matters on charges of forgery. Documents submitted in the past remain on record for reviews under other residency statuses, so any conflict with your current application will be exposed immediately.
</div>

## <span id="section-9"></span>9. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. What if I have the degree certificate but the school no longer issues transcripts?

When a home-country university, by regulation, no longer reissues transcripts after a certain period, options that can be discussed include submitting an official school letter (stating the reason for non-issuance) together with the original transcript received at graduation, or a certified copy of the academic record. Each case needs confirmation with the competent immigration office.

### Q2. If my major is completely unrelated to the job, would switching majors in graduate school help?

Yes. A master's degree aligned with the current job's field substantially offsets a mismatched bachelor's major. With a Bachelor's in Business + Master's in Computer Science applying for an IT role, the Business bachelor's can actually be read as "a developer with business understanding."

### Q3. Does freelance experience count as work experience?

Conditionally, yes. If the engagement was contract-based or service-based rather than employment-based, a bundle including tax payment certificates, service contracts, remittance records, and confirmation from the client must be submitted. A plain self-written résumé does not qualify as experience.

### Q4. I graduated from a Korean university — can I go straight to E-7 with no experience?

Holders of a bachelor's degree or higher obtained in Korea have the experience requirement relaxed. However, major-to-job relevance, inviting-company eligibility, and minimum salary benchmarks all still apply identically. The degree alone does not guarantee automatic approval.

### Q5. What if the previous company that used to issue my employment verification has gone out of business?

A common alternative is a combination of a personal employment confirmation letter from the former company's CEO, records of social insurance enrollment at that time, and withholding tax receipts for earned income. For overseas experience, tax authority payment records and social security records from the home country are decisive.
