# E-7 Visa Korea 2026 — Complete Guide for Specific Activities

The E-7 (Specific Activities) visa allows skilled foreign professionals to work legally in Korea across 87 designated occupations.
Whether you're an IT developer, researcher, chef, or trade specialist, the E-7 visa offers a path to employment in Korea.
This guide covers eligibility requirements, required documents, and the application process for 2026.

## What is the E-7 Visa?

The E-7 Specific Activities visa is regulated under Annex 1 of the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act.
It is designed for foreigners with specialized professional skills or technical expertise in designated occupations.

The key feature of E-7 is **occupation-specific eligibility** — you must apply for a specific occupation code from the 87 designated categories, and your education/experience must match that occupation.

## Eligible Occupations (Selected Examples)

- **IT/Software:** Software developers, systems engineers, information security specialists
- **Research/Development:** Natural science and engineering researchers
- **Design:** Product designers, fashion designers, video production specialists
- **Food Service:** Hotel chefs, Korean/Western/Japanese cuisine specialists
- **Trade/Business:** International trade professionals, business consultants
- **Healthcare:** Physicians, nurses (Korean license required)

For the complete occupation code list, visit [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr).

## Eligibility Requirements

**For the Foreign Applicant**

- Relevant academic degree (associate's degree or higher) or equivalent experience in the designated occupation
- Special qualifications required by the occupation (licenses, certifications)
- No disqualifying criminal history

**For the Sponsoring Employer**

- Registered Korean corporation or business
- Minimum revenue/employment scale requirements (varies by occupation)
- Compliance with local/foreign employee ratio rules

Recent E-7 reviews have increasingly scrutinized employer financial health and local hiring ratios. Pre-application screening is strongly recommended.

## Required Documents

**From the Applicant**

- Visa issuance authorization application form
- Passport copy
- Degree certificate with transcripts (apostille or notarized)
- Career certificates (if applicable)
- Occupational license or certification copies

**From the Employer**

- Invitation letter
- Business registration certificate
- Corporate registration document
- Recent financial statements
- Employment contract (with salary specified)
- Local employee status report

## Application Process

1. **Confirm occupation code and eligibility**
2. **Sign employment contract** (salary and job duties must be specified)
3. **Employer applies for Visa Issuance Authorization** at local immigration office
4. **Applicant applies for visa** at Korean embassy/consulate abroad
5. **Register as foreigner** within 90 days of entry at local immigration office

Foreigners already in Korea can apply for a status-of-stay change to E-7 directly.

## Stay Period and Extension

- **Initial stay:** 1 year (up to 3 years depending on occupation and contract)
- **Extension:** Renewable annually while employment continues
- **Path to residency:** After sufficient stay, applicants may qualify for F-2 (residence) or F-5 (permanent residence)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1. Can I get an E-7 visa without a degree?**

Yes, in many occupations 5 or more years of relevant work experience can substitute for an academic degree. Requirements vary by occupation code.

**Q2. Is there a minimum salary requirement?**

Yes. Salaries must generally meet national average wage thresholds. Below-standard contracts are a common cause of rejection.

**Q3. Can I work a part-time job on E-7?**

No. E-7 only authorizes work at the specific employer listed in your visa. Working elsewhere requires a separate permit.

**Q4. How long does processing take?**

Typically 2 to 4 weeks for visa issuance authorization. Processing may take longer during peak seasons.

**Q5. Can my family join me in Korea?**

Yes. Your spouse and dependent children can apply for F-3 (dependent/accompanying) visas.
