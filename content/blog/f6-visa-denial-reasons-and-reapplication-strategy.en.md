# Spouse Visa F-6 Denial Reasons and Reapplication Strategies

When an F-6 marriage visa application is denied, many couples feel frustrated and uncertain about how to proceed. However, denial is not a permanent rejection, and if you accurately identify the reasons for denial and systematically address them, you can successfully obtain the visa through reapplication. According to the Immigration Control Act, the F-6 visa is granted to foreign nationals who maintain a marital relationship with a Korean citizen, and various factors including genuineness of marriage, financial capability, and criminal records are reviewed during the screening process.

The most common reasons for F-6 visa denial include insufficient proof of marriage genuineness (approximately 40%), failure to meet income requirements (approximately 25%), document forgery or provision of false information (approximately 15%), criminal history or immigration law violations (approximately 10%), and other reasons (approximately 10%). Within 90 days from the date of receiving the denial notice, you can file an appeal or proceed with a new application after addressing the reasons for denial. Since all previous application records are reviewed during reapplication, you must prepare specific supplementary evidence rather than simply resubmitting the same documents.

<h2 id="section-1">Immediate Steps to Take After Receiving F-6 Visa Denial Notice</h2>

When you receive an F-6 visa denial notice, the first thing you must do is **verify the exact reasons for denial**. The denial notice issued by the Immigration Office includes specific reasons for denial along with legal grounds. Without accurately understanding this information, effective response is impossible.

The denial notice typically includes the following information:

- Date of denial decision and issuing authority
- Legal basis under the Immigration Control Act
- Specific reasons for denial (listed in 1-3 items)
- Period and method for filing an appeal
- Information regarding reapplication

If the reasons for denial in the notice are abstract or difficult to understand, you can **visit or call the Immigration Office that issued the decision to request a detailed explanation**. It's important to specifically inquire from the responsible official about which documents were missing or which aspects failed to meet the screening criteria.

<div style="background:#fff3cd;border-left:4px solid #ffc107;padding:15px;margin:20px 0">
<strong>⚠️ Important:</strong> You can file an appeal within 90 days from the date you received the denial notice. If this period passes, your right to appeal expires, so you must check and record the date.
</div>

Additionally, receiving a denial notice does not mean that a foreign spouse currently staying in Korea must leave immediately. If there is remaining time on their current visa (tourist visa, short-term visit visa, etc.), they can legally stay during that period while preparing for reapplication. However, if the stay period is approaching expiration, you must separately apply for an extension of stay period.

Checklist to verify after receiving denial notice:

1. Confirm the date of receiving the denial notice and the appeal deadline
2. Verify the legal basis for the denial reasons
3. Check current status of stay and expiration date
4. Review the list of previously submitted documents and copies
5. Obtain contact information for the Immigration Office and the case officer

<h2 id="section-2">Detailed Analysis of Main Denial Reasons</h2>

F-6 visa denial reasons can be broadly classified into five types, each requiring different response strategies.

### Insufficient Proof of Marriage Genuineness

The most frequent reason for denial occurs when the Immigration Office determines that the marriage is a sham marriage solely for visa purposes or not a genuine marital relationship. You may be suspected of lacking genuineness in the following cases:

- Significant age difference between spouses (generally 15 years or more)
- Very short courtship period (less than 3 months from meeting to marriage)
- Inability to communicate (absence of common language)
- Insufficient evidence of the marriage process
- Inability to answer basic information about spouse during interview
- Failure to prove cohabitation after marriage

### Failure to Meet Income Requirements

This applies when the Korean spouse's income (or combined household income) falls below the Ministry of Justice's announced standards. The minimum income requirement for 2024 needs to be confirmed with the relevant Immigration Office, and denial generally occurs in the following situations:

- Korean spouse's income over the past year is below the standard
- Submitted income verification documents are insufficient or lack credibility
- Income sustainability is uncertain due to temporary or daily employment
- No cash income despite having assets such as real estate

### Document Forgery or Provision of False Information

When submitted documents contain forgery, alteration, or false information, this is a very serious reason for denial:

- Forgery of income certificates, employment certificates, etc.
- Forgery of marriage certificates or foreign documents
- False statements during interviews
- Providing false information about past immigration records

<div style="background:#ffebee;border-left:4px solid #f44336;padding:15px;margin:20px 0">
<strong>⚠️ Warning:</strong> If document forgery or provision of false information is detected, it will be very disadvantageous during reapplication and can have permanent effects on future visa applications. In such cases, you must seek professional assistance.
</div>

### Criminal History or Immigration Law Violations

Visa issuance may be denied in the following cases:

- Criminal history in Korea or abroad (especially serious crimes)
- History of illegal stay during previous residence in Korea
- History of receiving deportation orders
- History of Immigration Control Act violations
- Concerns about harming national security or public order

### Other Reasons

- Discovery of serious illness in health examination results (especially infectious diseases)
- Missing required documents
- Ineligibility for application itself (e.g., marriage registration not completed)
- History of repeated denials in previous visa applications

<h2 id="section-3">Differences Between Appeals and Reapplication and Selection Criteria</h2>

There are two main ways to respond to F-6 visa denial: **appeal (administrative adjudication)** and **reapplication**. You must understand the characteristics of each and the appropriate situations for selection.

### Appeal (Administrative Adjudication)

An appeal is a procedure to request cancellation or modification of the decision from a higher authority (Minister of Justice) when you believe the Immigration Office's denial decision is unjust.

**When appeals are appropriate:**
- When submitted documents were sufficient but the examiner's judgment appears unjust
- When there are clear errors in legal interpretation or application of standards
- When qualifications can be sufficiently proven with existing materials alone without additional documents
- When the reasons for denial are factually incorrect or based on misunderstanding

**Appeal procedure:**
1. File within 90 days from the date of receiving denial notice
2. Prepare appeal application and attach supporting materials
3. Submit to the Immigration Office that issued the decision or to the Ministry of Justice
4. Review period: generally 30-60 days (extendable)
5. Results: Acceptance (decision cancelled), Rejection (denial maintained), Dismissal (procedural defect)

**Advantages and disadvantages of appeals:**

Advantages:
- No new application fee required
- Can legally challenge the unjustness of the decision
- Can prepare for reapplication while appeal is in progress

Disadvantages:
- Acceptance rate is not high (approximately 10-15%)
- Takes time to receive results
- Time delay until reapplication if rejected

### Reapplication

This involves making a new F-6 visa application after addressing the reasons for denial.

**When reapplication is appropriate:**
- When reasons for denial are clear and can be addressed
- When requirements can be met through additional documents or evidence
- When objective standards such as income requirements were not met
- When quick visa acquisition is necessary

**Precautions for reapplication:**
- All previous application records will be reviewed
- Simple repeated applications have adverse effects
- Must specifically address reasons for denial
- Recommended to attach an explanatory statement (letter of explanation) for supplementary documents

<h2 id="section-4">Preparing Supplementary Documents to Prove Marriage Genuineness</h2>

When denied due to insufficient proof of marriage genuineness, the most important aspect of reapplication is objectively proving that the marital relationship is genuine. Rather than simply claiming "we are in love," you must systematically prepare specific and verifiable supporting materials.

### Evidence of Courtship Process

Organize the process leading to marriage in chronological order for submission:

**1. Create a timeline from first meeting to marriage**
- Time, place, and circumstances of first meeting
- Dates and locations of major meetings
- Timing and circumstances of proposal
- Date of meeting both families
- Date of marriage registration

**2. Specific supporting materials**
- Photos taken together (20-30 photos by period, with dates indicated)
- Messenger conversation records (excerpts of major conversations, with Korean translation attached)
- Email, letter exchange records
- Video call records (screenshots)
- Travel evidence together (flight tickets, accommodation reservations, travel destination photos)
- Records of exchanging gifts (receipts, delivery records)

### Proof of Communication Ability

Materials demonstrating that the couple can communicate smoothly:

- Common language proficiency certificate (language test scores, completion certificates)
- Korean language learning history (foreign spouse)
- Spouse's native language learning history (Korean spouse)
- Interview records conducted without interpreter
- Diaries or records created together

### Proof of Cohabitation and Living Community

Materials showing actual cohabitation after marriage:

**Housing-related:**
- Joint tenancy contract
- Resident registration certificate (same address)
- Management fees, utility bill payment records
- Furniture, appliance purchase receipts
- Interior photos of home (showing couple living together)

**Joint financial management:**
- Joint bank account
- Records of transfers under spouse's name
- Jointly used credit card
- Joint expenditure records (groceries, dining out, etc.)

**Social relationships:**
- Photos from gatherings with friends and family
- Wedding invitation and venue photos
- Evidence of interaction with both families' parents
- Records of community participation

### Detailed Statement About Spousal Relationship

Prepare a statement containing a genuine story rather than formal content:

**Content to include:**
- Specific circumstances of how you met
- What attracted you to each other
- Memories and experiences shared together
- Process of overcoming cultural differences
- Future plans (children, residence, occupation, etc.)
- Relationship with spouse's family

**Precautions when writing:**
- Both spouses should write separately (content should match)
- Include specific dates, places, events
- Prioritize factual description over emotional expression
- Write to connect with photos or evidence

<div style="background:#e8f5e9;border-left:4px solid #4caf50;padding:15px;margin:20px 0">
<strong>💡 Tip:</strong> While more supporting materials are better, rather than submitting all materials indiscriminately, it's more effective to classify them by period and organize them systematically with a table of contents. It's important to make it easy for examiners to verify.
</div>

### Third-Party Statements

Statements from third parties who can objectively verify the marital relationship:

- Statements from both parents
- Statements from wedding officiant or attendees
- Testimonies from friends
- Statements from neighbors or colleagues

Statements should include the writer's identity (name, contact information), relationship with the couple, circumstances of becoming acquainted with the couple, specific examples to judge the genuineness of the relationship, and require signature and seal.

<h2 id="section-5">Solutions When Income Requirements Are Not Met</h2>

When F-6 visa is denied due to failure to meet income requirements, you can satisfy the requirements through the following methods.

### Understanding Income Standards

The income requirement for F-6 visa is determined by Ministry of Justice announcement and may change periodically, so current standards need to be confirmed with the relevant Immigration Office. Generally, the following principles apply:

- Korean spouse's sole income or combined household income recognized
- Continuous income over the past year required
- Regular income preferred over temporary income
- Employment income, business income, property income, etc. recognized

### Methods to Meet Income Requirements

**1. Increasing Korean spouse's income**

The most direct method, with the following efforts possible:

- Full-time employment
- Extending work hours (part-time → full-time)
- Starting a side job or second job
- For freelancers, increasing income and strengthening documentation

Building an income history of at least 3-6 months before reapplication is recommended.

**2. Combining household member income**

When the Korean spouse's income alone is insufficient, income of cohabiting household members can be combined:

- Parents' income can be combined when living with parents
- Must be registered at the same address on resident registration
- Need to submit household member's income verification documents
- Household member's consent and statement for income provision required

**Required documents:**
- Resident registration certificate (confirming household member relationship)
- Household member's income certificate, employment certificate
- Household member's consent form (notarization recommended)
- Statement from household member expressing intention to support

**3. Asset verification**

Even if cash income is insufficient, sufficient assets can be recognized:

- Financial assets such as deposits, savings
- Real estate (for actual residence)
- Securities such as stocks, bonds

When proving assets, submit balance certificates, real estate registry, appraisals, etc., and generally assets at 2-3 times the income requirement level may be needed (specific standards need to be confirmed with relevant authority).

**4. Comprehensive proof of living stability**

When income or assets alone are insufficient, comprehensively prove living stability through the following methods:

- Stable residential environment (homeownership or long-term lease)
- National health insurance, national pension and other social insurance subscription history
- Credit rating and debt status
- Future income increase plans (scheduled employment, business plans, etc.)

### Income Requirement Exceptions

Income requirements may be waived or relaxed in the following cases (specific application needs to be confirmed with relevant authority):

- When pregnant or having children
- When Korean spouse has difficulty working due to illness or disability
- When foreign spouse has legal employment and income in Korea
- When there are special circumstances requiring humanitarian consideration

When claiming these exceptions, you must submit detailed explanations along with relevant evidence (medical certificates, pregnancy confirmation, employment certificates, etc.).

<h2 id="section-6">Timing and Strategic Preparation Methods for Reapplication</h2>

While F-6 visa reapplication is possible at any time, **hastily reapplying without sufficient preparation is not advisable**. As denial history accumulates, subsequent applications receive stricter scrutiny, so it's important to apply after thorough supplementation.

### Appropriate Reapplication Timing

**1. Cases suitable for immediate reapplication**

- When simple document omission or formal defect is the reason for denial
- Translation errors or document validity period issues, etc.
- When supplementary documents are already prepared

**2. Reapplication recommended after 3-6 months**

- Failure to meet income requirements: Need to accumulate new income history
- Supplementing marriage genuineness proof: Accumulating additional courtship/cohabitation records
- Improving Korean language skills: Completing language courses, etc.

**3. Reapplication recommended after 6 months or more**

- When there were serious defects
- When there is history of multiple denials
- When the situation itself needs significant improvement

### Step-by-Step Guide for Reapplication Preparation

**Step 1: Accurate analysis of denial reasons (1-2 weeks)**

- Thoroughly review denial notice
- Request specific explanation from Immigration Office
- Review all previously submitted documents
- Create list of deficiencies

**Step 2: Establish supplementation plan (1 week)**

- Develop response measures for each denial reason
- Create list of required documents
- Set priorities for time-consuming items
- Decide whether to seek professional consultation

**Step 3: Prepare supplementary documents (1-6 months)**

- Prepare systematically in chronological order
- Secure credibility of each document (notarization, apostille, etc.)
- Verify translation accuracy
- Check consistency between documents

**Step 4: Complete application and final review (1-2 weeks)**

- Clearly state differences from previous application
- Write explanation for supplementation items
- Create complete document list and table of contents
- Final review and check for omissions

**Step 5: Reapplication and follow-up management**

- Emphasize supplementation items at submission
- Keep receipt and submitted document list
- Prepare for quick response to additional document requests
- Prepare for interview request

### How to Write Effective Explanations

The explanation submitted during reapplication is not simply an excuse, but a document that clearly explains what has been improved and how compared to the previous application.

**Content to include in explanation:**

1. **Greeting and background of reapplication**
   - Previous application date and denial notice date
   - Circumstances leading to reapplication

2. **Supplementation content by denial reason**
   - List each denial reason by item
   - Specific supplementation measures for each reason
   - Specify related supporting document list

3. **Additional proof items**
   - Changed circumstances since previous application
   - Newly prepared supporting materials
   - Future plans

4. **Conclusion and request**
   - Reconfirm genuine marital relationship
   - Request visa issuance

**Example structure of explanation:**

```
Title: Explanation Regarding F-6 Visa Reapplication

1. Background of Reapplication
- Applied on [Date], received denial notice on [Date]
- Reasons for denial: Insufficient proof of marriage genuineness, failure to meet income requirements

2. Supplementation Content by Denial Reason

[Denial Reason 1: Insufficient proof of marriage genuineness]
Supplementation measures:
- Continuously cohabiting and building living community for past 6 months
- Weekly family activities and community participation (evidence attached: 30 photos)
- Regular interaction with Korean spouse's family (evidence attached: family gathering records)
- Opened joint bank account and joint financial management (evidence attached: account copy)
Related documents: Attachments 1-10

[Denial Reason 2: Failure to meet income requirements]
Supplementation measures:
- Korean spouse obtained full-time employment ([Date])
- Maintained stable income for recent 6 months (monthly average XXX)
- Living with parents to combine household income (combined income XXX)
Related documents: Attachments 11-15

3. Other Additional Proof Items
- Foreign spouse obtained TOPIK Level 2
- Secured stable housing (signed 2-year lease contract)
- Completed health examination with no abnormalities

4. Conclusion
Our couple genuinely loves each other and wishes to build a family in Korea...
```

<h2 id="section-7">Common Mistakes and Prevention Methods</h2>

There are mistakes that many applicants repeat during the F-6 visa reapplication process. Recognizing and preventing these in advance is the shortcut to success.

### Mistake 1: Simply resubmitting the same documents

**Problem:**
Resubmitting the same documents as before without accurately identifying the reasons for denial. Since examiners review all previous application records, submitting the same documents without new supplementation has a very high probability of being denied again.

**Prevention:**
- Carefully read the denial notice and identify exact reasons for denial
- Call the Immigration Office to specifically confirm what was lacking
- Add at least 30% new evidence compared to previously submitted documents
- Attach explanation specifying supplementation items

### Mistake 2: Hastily manipulating or exaggerating documents

**Problem:**
Manipulating income certificates or writing exaggerated content due to desire to quickly obtain a visa. This is a very serious problem that, if detected, will have permanent adverse effects on all future visa applications.

**Prevention:**
- All documents must contain only the truth
- If income is insufficient, use legal methods such as household member combination or asset verification
- Do not write uncertain content
- Even with professional help, never create false documents

### Mistake 3: Neglecting translation accuracy

**Problem:**
Using only online translators when translating foreign language documents into Korean, or submitting inaccurate translations by asking acquaintances who are not professional translators.

**Prevention:**
- Use certified translators or professional translation companies
- Be especially careful with legal terminology and government document translations
- Reconfirm consistency between translation and original content
- Clearly indicate translation date and translator information

### Mistake 4: Insufficient interview preparation

**Problem:**
Only supplementing documents without interview preparation, unable to answer even basic information about spouse during interview.

**Prevention:**
- Memorize basic information such as spouse's birthday, family composition, occupation, hobbies
- Remember major dates and locations such as first meeting, proposal, wedding
- Coordinate in advance so both parties' statements match
- Create list of expected questions and practice
- Practice answering naturally without nervousness

### Mistake 5: Stay period management mistakes

**Problem:**
Becoming an illegal resident as the current status of stay period expires while preparing for reapplication.

**Prevention:**
- Mark current stay period expiration date on calendar and check frequently
- Apply for stay period extension at least 1 month before expiration
- Can proceed with current visa extension separately from F-6 reapplication
- Consult with Immigration Office if stay period is inevitably approaching

### Mistake 6: Inconsistency in income verification

**Problem:**
Income amounts presented in various documents do not match each other, or there are unexplainable large amount fluctuations.

**Prevention:**
- Verify matching amounts in income certificate, employment certificate, salary statements, etc.
- Verify matching of bank deposit records and salary amounts
- If there are sudden large deposits, prepare materials explaining the source
- Freelancers or self-employed should match sales ledger with tax filing records

### Mistake 7: Underestimating communication ability

**Problem:**
Thinking you can get a visa even when the couple cannot communicate at all.

**Prevention:**
- Acquire minimum common language ability
- Prove Korean language learning efforts (course certificates, transcripts)
- Show usage history of interpretation apps or translation tools
- Prepare to have at least simple conversations directly during interview

<h2 id="section-8">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

### Q1: If F-6 visa is denied, is there a restriction period for reapplication?

**A:** Legally, there is no restriction on when you can reapply. Reapplication is possible from the day after receiving the denial notice. However, if you hastily reapply without sufficiently addressing the reasons for denial, there is a high probability of being denied again, so realistically, reapplying after a supplementation period of at least 1-6 months is recommended. Especially in cases of failing to meet income requirements, 3-6 months of time is needed to build a new income history.

### Q2: Can I proceed with appeal and reapplication simultaneously?

**A:** Yes, it's possible. You can prepare for or submit a reapplication while an appeal is in progress. Many people adopt a strategy of preparing and reapplying while waiting for appeal results. However, if the appeal is accepted (granted), reapplication becomes unnecessary, so you should make strategic decisions based on the situation. Generally, since appeal acceptance rates are not high (approximately 10-15%), preparing for reapplication simultaneously can reduce time waste.

### Q3: If F-6 visa is denied multiple times, does it become permanently unobtainable?

**A:** Simply having many denials does not mean you can never obtain a visa permanently. However, it is true that screening becomes stricter as denial history accumulates. What's important is **whether the reasons for denial have been properly addressed**. In fact, there are many cases of obtaining visas after 3-4 denials through proper supplementation. Especially when it's not a serious reason like document forgery or provision of false information, you can reapply with sufficient supplementation. ### Q4: If income requirements are not met but there are children, is an exception granted?

**A:** Having children may result in relaxed income requirements or recognized exceptions. Especially when there are children with Korean nationality or during pregnancy, humanitarian consideration may be given. However, this doesn't mean income requirements are completely waived, and you must prove minimum livelihood maintenance capability. Having children doesn't automatically result in visa issuance, but screening is conducted considering the child's welfare and the principle of family unity. In this case, you should submit the child's birth certificate, family relationship certificate, childcare plan, etc., and even if income is insufficient, you must supplement livelihood capability through other methods (household member combination, asset verification, etc.). Specific standards need to be confirmed with the Immigration Office.

### Q5: The denial reason only states "suspicion of marriage genuineness," so how can I find out specifically what the problem is?

**A:** When the denial notice reason is abstract, you can confirm specific details through the following methods. This allows you to identify what issues arose during the screening process. Third, **consulting with professionals such as immigration specialists or lawyers** is also a good method. Professionals can judge what aspects were lacking based on experience and suggest specific supplementation directions.
