When a Korean-Chinese person holding an F-4 (Overseas Korean) visa is caught drunk driving, two separate procedures begin simultaneously. One is criminal punishment under the Road Traffic Act. The other is the immigration office's criminal review (sabeom shimsa).

The most common point of confusion: "I paid my fine. Why is my visa at risk?" Criminal fines and immigration administrative penalties are entirely separate matters.

## 1. How Drunk Driving Triggers Criminal Review {#section-1}

Article 46 of the Immigration Act defines the grounds for deportation. Drunk driving can fall under provisions covering foreign nationals who give "reasonable grounds to believe they may engage in conduct harmful to national interests or public safety," or who have violated the law — making it subject to sabeom shimsa.

When police catch someone for drunk driving, there is a system by which police notify the immigration office. Foreign national criminal cases are often flagged to immigration simultaneously when charges are filed.

> **Key point:** Even if the criminal court imposes a fine or suspended sentence, the immigration criminal review proceeds separately.

---

## 2. BAC Levels and Expected Outcomes {#section-2}

Criminal penalties for drunk driving depend on blood alcohol concentration (BAC). Immigration criminal review also references these levels.

| BAC Level | Road Traffic Act Penalty | Review Risk |
|-----------|--------------------------|-------------|
| 0.03%–0.08% | Fine (license suspension) | Low — first offense may end in warning |
| 0.08% or above | Jail or fine (license revocation) | Moderate — may affect visa renewal |
| 0.2% or above, or 3+ offenses | Jail or heavy fine | High — departure order or deportation risk |
| Refusal to take test | Same as 0.2%+ | High |

In practice, a first-offense DUI at under 0.08% with no accident often results in just a written warning at the sabeom shimsa stage. However, 0.1% or above, or a second offense, is likely to result in visa renewal denial or a departure order.

**Factors with the most impact on the review outcome:**
- BAC level
- Whether it is a first offense
- Whether an accident occurred (victim involved)
- Whether the test was refused

## 3. How a Prior DUI Affects Visa Renewal {#section-3}

If you have a prior DUI and attempt to renew your F-4 visa, immigration can flag the record and either deny renewal or grant conditional approval.

**Common scenarios:**

- One minor DUI (0.03%–0.05%, first offense, no accident): Renewal likely possible
- DUI with license revocation or second offense: Extension may be placed on hold or granted for short term only
- Prior refusal to take test: Heightened scrutiny; outcome depends on immigration's discretion
- DUI that caused injury: Renewal likely denied or review initiated

> **In practice:** Criminal records are checked at the time of visa renewal. Even after paying the fine and closing the criminal case, the prior record remains and is factored directly into the renewal decision.

## 4. Can You Keep Your Status After License Revocation? {#section-4}

License revocation and visa cancellation are separate matters. Having your license revoked does not automatically cancel your visa.

However, DUIs that result in license revocation typically involve BAC of 0.08% or above or an accident, and immigration applies stricter standards in these cases.

**Likelihood of maintaining status:**

- Minor DUI (first offense, low BAC): Warning likely, status maintained
- License revocation (0.08%+, first offense): Departure order or short-term conditional stay possible
- Second offense or injury accident: High risk of forced deportation

## 5. Factors That Can Reduce the Penalty {#section-5}

The same drunk driving offense can result in different outcomes. The following factors genuinely help in reducing the penalty.

**Mitigating factors:**
- Long period of lawful stay in Korea (especially 10+ years)
- Korean family members — spouse, children, or dependents
- No accident occurred — simple detection only
- Genuine remorse and completion of preventive education
- If there was a victim, full compensation completed

**Aggravating factors:**
- Repeated DUI offenses (2 or more)
- Refused to take the BAC test
- Injury or death accident
- Simultaneously caught driving without a license
- Hit-and-run allegation

## 7. FAQ {#section-7}

**Q. I paid my fine in full. Do I still face sabeom shimsa?**
A. Yes. Paying the criminal fine closes the criminal process — it has no bearing on the immigration administrative review.

**Q. Can I leave Korea while sabeom shimsa is pending?**
A. You can physically leave, but departing during a pending review can be interpreted unfavorably. There is also a risk of being denied re-entry. Consult a professional before making this decision.

**Q. My visa renewal is coming up and I have a DUI on record. What should I do?**
A. Consult with a professional administrative scrivener before the renewal date. The right strategy depends on the severity of the offense and the specific immigration office handling your case.

**Q. My license was revoked due to drunk driving, but is my visa okay?**
A. They are separate. But DUIs involving license revocation face stricter review standards. Do not wait to be notified of a review — taking proactive steps before a decision is issued is far more effective.

**Q. Is refusing the breathalyzer worse than actually being over the limit?**
A. Yes. Under the Road Traffic Act, refusing the test is treated the same as a BAC of 0.2% or above — and immigration views it even more unfavorably.
