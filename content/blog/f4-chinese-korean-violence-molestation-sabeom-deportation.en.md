# Korean-Chinese F-4 Assault & Sexual Harassment Immigration Review and Deportation Standards: A Practical Guide

When a Korean-Chinese F-4 visa holder receives criminal punishment for assault or sexual harassment, they are immediately subject to immigration violation review (사범심사), and depending on the severity of the punishment, this can lead to visa cancellation, forced deportation, or entry ban.

Almost all cases with fines or above are subject to review, and sexual harassment cases — even those resulting in only a fine — frequently lead directly to an entry ban.

This article organizes the practical standards from the moment a review notice is issued through deportation decisions, entry ban periods, and the key points for drafting a written opinion.

<h2 id="section-1">When F-4 Assault Immigration Review Begins</h2>

### How Criminal Disposition Results Are Reported to Immigration

Immigration review does not start at the police stage — it begins after a prosecutorial or court disposition is finalized and automatically reported to the Immigration and Foreigner Office.

Fines, suspension of indictment, suspended sentences, and actual prison terms are all subject to reporting.

A commonly overlooked situation is when a person, unaware that they have been criminally punished, goes to extend their visa only to be served with a review referral notice on the spot.

Under [Article 46 of the Immigration Act](https://www.law.go.kr/) and its enforcement rules, the reviewing officer first examines whether grounds for deportation apply.

### Response Deadline After Receiving the Notice

The review notice specifies a deadline for submitting a written opinion, and this deadline is typically short.

If you fail to submit a written opinion, statement of remorse, settlement agreement, or other supporting documents within this period, the decision will be made based solely on the documents already on file.

> **Caution:** Departing the country after receiving the notice may be considered as fleeing, which can extend the entry ban period.

<h2 id="section-2">Review Standards by Severity of Punishment in Assault Cases</h2>

### The Difference Between Simple Assault and Bodily Injury

In practice, the weight given to simple assault (a crime not prosecutable against the victim's will) versus bodily injury differs significantly.

| Offense | Settlement Possible | Impact on Review | Notes |
|------|---------------|----------------|------|
| Simple Assault | No prosecution if settled | Low–Medium | Settlement agreement is key |
| Bodily Injury | Punishable even after settlement | Medium–High | Injury duration matters |
| Special Assault | Punishable even after settlement | High | Aggravated by weapon/group |
| Violations of the Punishment of Violences Act | Aggravated punishment | Very High | High deportation risk |

The key point is this.

What matters more than the fine amount is the actual offense name on which the conviction is finalized — this determines the review outcome.

### The Dividing Line Between Suspended Sentence and Actual Imprisonment

If an actual prison sentence is finalized, the likelihood of a deportation decision rises sharply.

Depending on the circumstances, a suspended sentence may result in a shortened stay period or activity restrictions rather than visa cancellation.

In fact, there are quite a few cases where suspended sentence cases end up in deportation because the person failed to properly submit a written opinion or supporting documents.

Since this year, internal immigration review guidelines have been partially adjusted, changing the aggravation weight given to prior similar offenses.

The standards applicable to your specific case must be confirmed through consultation for accuracy.

<h2 id="section-3">Why Sexual Harassment Cases Are Treated More Seriously Than Assault</h2>

### The Impact of the Sex Crimes Special Act

Violations of the Sexual Violence Punishment Act, unlike ordinary criminal cases, carry additional measures such as personal information registration and employment restrictions.

In immigration practice, even a fine for a sex crime is directly treated as grounds for deportation or entry ban.

### Filming with Cameras, Forcible Molestation, and Molestation in Crowded Places

| Type | Punishment Tendency | F-4 Review Outcome Tendency |
|------|----------|----------------------|
| Forcible Molestation | Fine to imprisonment | Mostly visa cancellation/deportation |
| Filming with Cameras | Fine to imprisonment | High deportation rate |
| Molestation in Crowded Places | Mostly fines | Mostly visa cancellation followed by departure order |
| Indecency via Telecommunications | Mostly fines | Stay restriction or departure order |

In particular, cases involving minor victims almost invariably result in deportation.

> **Practical Tip:** Even if a settlement is reached at the criminal stage, sexual harassment cases are evaluated separately in immigration review. Materials demonstrating remorse and prevention of recurrence are needed in addition to the criminal settlement agreement.

<h2 id="section-4">Deportation Decisions and Entry Ban Periods</h2>

### When a Deportation Order Is Issued

A deportation order is issued when grounds under [Article 46 of the Immigration Act](https://www.law.go.kr/) apply.

Once the order is issued, the process proceeds either through protective custody (foreigner detention center) or self-funded departure.

The deadline for filing an objection is extremely short, so you must respond immediately upon receiving the order.

### Calculating the Entry Ban Period

The entry ban period is determined comprehensively based on the nature of the offense, severity of punishment, history of similar offenses, and length of stay.

Simple assault with a fine tends to receive a shorter period, while sex crimes and actual prison sentences tend to receive longer periods.

Family relationships, raising children in Korea, and the degree of financial settlement may act as factors reducing the entry ban period.

However, the precise period standards are governed by internal guidelines applied separately from [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) announcements.

The expected period for your specific case can only be estimated after reviewing the case records.

<h2 id="section-5">Key Points for Drafting an Immigration Review Opinion</h2>

### Accurate Case Structure Beats Length

If the written opinion is weak, no settlement agreement will produce a good result.

Even with abundant documents, if the sequence of facts, motive, victim recovery, and recurrence prevention are not clearly organized, the reviewing officer will lack a basis for judgment.

The first thing to examine is the discrepancy between the facts stated in the judgment or summary order and your own account.

### Composition of Settlement Agreement, Statement of Remorse, and Letters of Appeal

- Settlement agreement with the victim (including a no-punishment statement)
- Personal statement of remorse (acknowledgment and promise to prevent recurrence)
- Letters of appeal from family, acquaintances, and employer
- Evidence of settlement in Korea (family ties, children's schooling, length of residence)
- Records of completed treatment or education (anger management, sexual violence prevention training)

If this section is weak, deportation becomes the likely outcome.

<h2 id="section-6">The Difference Between Voluntary Departure and Departure Order</h2>

### Both Are Lighter Than Deportation, But

Voluntary departure means leaving the country within a deadline at your own initiative, while a departure order is a measure where immigration sets the deadline and orders departure.

Compared to forced deportation, the entry ban period may be set shorter.

However, the timing for re-entry after voluntary departure or a departure order varies depending on the type of case.

### Re-entry and Re-application Strategy

There are cases where, after F-4 cancellation, individuals consider returning on an H-2 or short-term visa.

In such cases, the entry ban must either be lifted or expire first, so timing calculations are critical.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

### Q1. Are assault cases with fines under 1 million won also subject to immigration review?

Regardless of the fine amount, any finalized criminal punishment is subject to reporting.

Even with a small fine, if the offense is bodily injury or special assault, the outcome can be severe.

### Q2. Does the review still proceed even after a settlement?

A criminal settlement affects the prosecutorial and court disposition, but immigration review is conducted separately based on the disposition result.

The settlement agreement is attached to the written opinion as a mitigating factor.

### Q3. Can a fine for sexual harassment also lead to deportation?

Even with a fine, many cases lead to deportation or visa cancellation.

The outcome depends on the victim's age, the nature of the conduct, and prior similar offenses.

### Q4. Can I file an objection to a deportation order?

An objection can be filed under the [Immigration Act](https://www.law.go.kr/), but the deadline is very short.

After the deadline passes, you must move to the administrative litigation stage.

### Q5. Does it matter if my family members are Korean nationals?

The nationality and residency status of your spouse and children can serve as mitigating factors in immigration review.

However, in sex crime cases, family relationships alone are unlikely to change the outcome.

### Q6. Can the entry ban period be shortened?

You can apply to have the entry ban lifted before it expires, but the requirements for approval are strict.

The eligible timing and grounds require case-by-case review.
