The F-5 permanent residency income requirement varies by household size. Here's how to calculate your eligibility based on Korea's 2026 median income standards.

## The Income Standard

Under Korea's Immigration Control Act, F-5 applicants must demonstrate income at or above the **standard median income** for their household size.

2026 approximate monthly thresholds:

| Household Size | Monthly Standard |
|---|---|
| 1 person | ~KRW 2.28M |
| 2 persons | ~KRW 3.81M |
| 3 persons | ~KRW 4.88M |
| 4 persons | ~KRW 5.95M |

## What Counts as Income

**Accepted income types:**
- Employment income (salary, wages)
- Business income (must be filed in comprehensive income tax return)
- Pension income (national pension, retirement pension)
- Rental income (from registered property)

**Not accepted:**
- Government subsidies or support payments
- Unreported income

A common issue in practice: business owners who haven't filed income tax returns cannot prove their business income. At least 1–2 years of filed returns should be in place before applying.

## Supporting Documents

- **Employment income**: Withholding tax receipt, health insurance premium certificate
- **Business income**: Comprehensive income tax assessment, business registration
- **Rental income**: Lease agreements + tax return records

## Combining Spouse Income

If your Korean or legally resident foreign spouse has income, it can be combined to meet the threshold. Spouse income documentation must also be submitted.

## When Income Falls Short

Options to consider:
1. **Choose the best reference year**: Use the year with the highest reported income
2. **Combine household income**: Include spouse and dependents
3. **Check for exemptions**: Some high-contribution foreigners may qualify for reduced income requirements
