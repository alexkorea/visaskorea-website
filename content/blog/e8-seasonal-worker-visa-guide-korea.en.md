# Foreign Seasonal Worker E-8 Visa Guide — Eligibility, Documents, and Practical Points

The E-8 seasonal worker visa is a short-term employment status that allows foreign nationals to be legally hired for up to five months during peak farming and fishing seasons.

Workers can only be brought in through fixed channels — MOUs between Korean local governments and foreign counterparts, family invitations through marriage immigrants, international students, and a few others — and direct recruitment by employers is essentially impossible.

This guide walks through eligibility, permitted industries, required documents, post-arrival procedures, and the practical bottlenecks that trip people up most often.

<h2 id="section-1">What Is the E-8 Visa</h2>

E-8 is a **residency status dedicated to seasonal work**, classified under short-term employment in Annex 1-2 of the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act.

It was launched as a pilot program in 2015 to fill concentrated labor demand for crop cultivation and harvesting, fishery aquaculture, and seafood gathering, and has since been formalized as an official visa category.

It is administered by the Ministry of Justice and the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr), while actual workforce allocation is handled by city, county, and district governments.

### How It Differs From Other Work Visas

Compared to E-9 (Non-Professional Employment) or H-2 (Working Visit), E-8 has a **maximum stay of just five months** and does not, in principle, allow workplace changes.

E-9 runs through the Employment Permit System (EPS), while E-8 operates through local-government MOUs and family invitation channels.

### Permitted Industries

The focus is on agriculture, livestock, and fisheries, with permitted crops and species varying by city, county, and district.

In practice, even within the same province, permitted occupations often differ from one county to the next.

> **Caution:** Assigning foreign workers to tasks outside the permitted crop list can lead to disqualification of the workplace. Employers must check with their local government to confirm which crops are permitted for their site.

<h2 id="section-2">Who Can Apply — Foreign Worker Eligibility</h2>

To obtain an E-8 visa, an applicant must fall into **one of three channels**.

### 1) Local Government MOU Channel

Workers recruited in their home country under an MOU signed between a Korean local government and its foreign counterpart.

This is the largest channel by volume, with agreements signed between specific regional governments in countries like Vietnam, the Philippines, Cambodia, Laos, and Kyrgyzstan.

The sending local government conducts initial selection, and the Korean local government matches workers with employers.

### 2) Marriage Immigrant Family Invitation Channel

A marriage immigrant (F-6) residing in Korea may invite relatives within the fourth degree of kinship from their home country.

The core requirements are family relationship documentation and a guarantee of identity from the marriage immigrant, and this is usually where things stall — typically over authentication requirements for proof of family ties.

### 3) Status Change for Foreigners Already in Korea

A pilot program allows certain foreign nationals holding D-2 (Study) or D-4 (Language Training) status to switch to E-8 during periods that do not conflict with their academic calendar.

That said, not all local governments operate this option.

> **Practical Tip:** Start by figuring out which channel applies to you — the document sets for marriage-immigrant family invitations and MOU channels are completely different, and mixing them up creates a mess.

<h2 id="section-3">Employer Eligibility and Pre-Application Procedures</h2>

For E-8, **employer requirements are scrutinized even more closely than the worker's**.

### Basic Employer Requirements

- Registered agricultural/fisheries business entity or valid fishing permit
- Cultivation area or aquaculture facility above a minimum scale
- No recent administrative penalties for wage arrears, unreported industrial accidents, etc.
- Foreign worker housing that meets minimum standards

The local government's allocation review committee evaluates these items to determine the number of workers approved.

### Housing Is the Most Common Sticking Point

By far the most frequent reason for rejection in the review is **inadequate housing**.

Makeshift structures inside greenhouses, standalone shipping containers, and units without separated toilets and showers are all grounds for rejection.

> **Caution:** Since the recent tightening of housing standards for foreign workers, requirements have shifted slightly every year. You should inspect your farm's housing against current standards in advance.

### When to Apply for Allocation

Local governments accept allocation applications **once or twice a year**, only during designated periods.

If you miss the window, you have to wait for the next round — so you need to work backwards from your peak-season schedule.

<h2 id="section-4">Documents — Worker vs. Employer</h2>

E-8 documents split into two tracks, with additional papers depending on the recruitment channel.

### Documents Submitted by the Foreign Worker (at the Korean Embassy/Consulate)

| Document | Content | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Visa Application Form | Embassy form | Signed in person |
| Passport & Visa Photo | Valid 6+ months | Must meet spec |
| Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance | Issued in Korea, then forwarded | Filed by employer |
| Health Certificate | Tuberculosis and other listed items | Designated medical facility |
| Recommendation Letter from Sending Local Gov | MOU channel only | Issued by sending body |
| Family Relationship Documents | Family invitation only | Apostille or consular authentication |

### Documents Submitted by the Employer (to Local Gov / Immigration)

| Document | Content | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Seasonal Worker Allocation Application | Local government form | During application window only |
| Agricultural/Fisheries Business Registration | Issued by NAQS or fisheries cooperative | Within last 6 months |
| Standard Employment Contract | Ministry of Employment and Labor form | Bilingual (Korean + native language) |
| Housing Photos & Floor Plan | Exterior, interior, bathroom | Must demonstrate compliance |
| Business Registration & Lease Agreement | Proof of land rights | Owned or leased both acceptable |
| Application for Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance | Submitted to immigration | After allocation is finalized |

> **Practical Tip:** Housing photos shouldn't just show the exterior. You need interior shots that capture the bathroom, cooking area, and heating equipment. If this part is weak, the allocation itself will be denied.

<h2 id="section-5">Application Procedure and Processing Time</h2>

The E-8 process has four stages: **local government allocation → Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance → visa issuance at overseas embassy → entry**.

### Stage 1 — Local Government Allocation Application

The employer files an allocation application with their city, county, or district office.

The allocation review committee examines workplace scale, housing, and past employment history to finalize the headcount.

### Stage 2 — Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance (CCVI)

Once allocation is approved, the employer applies for a CCVI at the local [Immigration Office](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

Processing time varies by immigration office, and backlogs build up significantly during peak farming season.

### Stage 3 — Visa Issuance at Overseas Embassy

The worker takes the issued CCVI number to the Korean embassy or consulate in their home country and applies for the visa there.

### Stage 4 — Foreigner Registration After Entry

Anyone staying more than 90 days must complete foreigner registration within 90 days of entry.

Since E-8 stays are typically five months, all workers fall under this requirement.

<h2 id="section-6">Period of Stay, Extensions, and Re-Entry</h2>

E-8 stays are capped at **five months per visit**, and re-entry is not freely permitted.

### Extension of Stay

Extensions are restricted in principle, but where the same workplace needs a worker for an extended crop cycle, **up to three additional months** may be granted under current operating guidelines.

These rules change yearly, so confirm the current standards with your local immigration office.

### Re-Entry the Following Year (Faithful Worker Re-Hiring)

Foreign workers who completed their contract without absconding in a prior season can be brought back by the same employer the next season.

The re-entry process for "faithful workers" is streamlined, but **even a single past absconding incident reduces the employer's allocation for the following year**.

> **Caution:** Absconding directly impacts the employer's next-year allocation. The first one to two weeks after entry are generally considered the highest-risk period.

### Workplace Change

E-8 workers are not permitted to change workplaces in principle.

That said, if the employer's business closes, or if there are documented human rights violations, wage arrears, or other issues not attributable to the worker, reassignment within the same local government's jurisdiction may be considered.

<h2 id="section-7">Common Bottlenecks in Practice</h2>

When the paperwork is complete but the process stalls, it almost always comes down to one of these five issues.

### 1) Inadequate Housing

As noted above, housing is the single biggest reason for rejection.

Containers placed next to greenhouses on the farm, or units with outdoor-only toilets, will receive correction requests.

### 2) Missing Family Relationship Authentication

In the marriage immigrant family invitation channel, this is where applicants forget the **apostille or consular authentication** on family documents from the home country.

Even if the documents themselves are in hand, they'll be sent back immediately if they haven't been properly authenticated.

### 3) Errors in Wage or Working Condition Entries

If the standard employment contract lists wages below the minimum wage, or if the native-language translation is missing, the application will come back for correction.

Costs vary by case, and exact figures are provided during the free consultation.

### 4) Mismatch Between Crops or Species

Cases where the work actually performed differs from the crops or species approved by the local government.

If this is discovered, the employer's allocation for the following year is reduced or revoked.

### 5) Delayed Entry Timing

Cases where the visa was issued but the worker arrives after peak season has already passed.

Delays in the home country's exit procedures are particularly common, so peak-season planning must be calculated backwards from the actual entry date.

> **Practical Tip:** The CCVI must be used within three months of issuance. If you miss that deadline, you're starting over from the beginning.

<h2 id="section-8">FAQ</h2>

**Q1. Can E-8 holders work in fields other than agriculture?**

Only work within the approved crops or fishery species at the assigned workplace is allowed.

Working at a restaurant, factory, or other industry is treated as illegal residence and illegal employment.

**Q2. Can family members (spouse, children) accompany the worker?**

E-8 is a short-term work visa, so dependent family status (F-3) is not granted.

The expectation is that workers enter and work alone within the five-month period.

**Q3. Can E-8 be changed to E-9 or another long-term work visa?**

E-8 is short-term employment status, and changing to another work visa from within Korea is restricted in principle.

The typical path is to leave Korea, go through the EPS process again, or reapply under a different status.

**Q4. What happens if a worker absconds?**

The worker faces deportation and re-entry restrictions, while the employer may see allocations reduced or revoked for the following year.

The incident is also reported to the sending local government, which can create consequences in the worker's home country.

**Q5. Can employers recruit foreign workers directly?**

Not in principle.

Recruitment must go through designated channels — either the local government MOU route or marriage immigrant family invitations.

**Q6. How should wages be paid?**

Wages must meet at least the minimum wage as set out in the standard employment contract, and must be paid into an account in the worker's own name.

Direct cash payments or excessive deductions for room and board can be treated as wage arrears, which affects allocations the following year.
