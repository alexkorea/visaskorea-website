# F-2 Residence Visa Point System: A Clear Breakdown of How Each Category Is Scored

The F-2-7 point system requires a minimum of 80 points to qualify for application, but in actual screening, the distribution of points across categories is what determines approval.

The basic categories are education, income, Korean language ability, and age — four in total — with bonus and deduction points added on top to determine the final score.

This article covers the scoring table for each category, real-world score calculation examples, and the parts where applicants most often get stuck in practice.

<h2 id="section-1">The Basic Structure of the F-2-7 Point System and What 80 Points Really Means</h2>

The F-2-7 residence visa is classified under the point-based residence (skilled foreign talent) category.

It is administered by the Korea Immigration Service under the Ministry of Justice, and the legal basis is specified in Appendix 1 of the [Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act](https://www.law.go.kr).

### How the Scoring Categories Are Structured

The point structure is broadly divided into three areas.

- Basic categories: education, income, Korean language ability, age (maximum score baseline)
- Optional categories: bonus points for volunteer work, tax payment history, completion of the Korea Immigration and Integration Program (KIIP), etc.
- Deduction categories: criminal penalties, immigration law violations, tax arrears, and other negative points

It is uncommon to clear 80 points with the basic categories alone.

Typically, the structure works out to 60–70 basic points plus 10–20 bonus points to reach the 80-point line.

### 80 Points Is Not a Pass Score

This is one of the most common misconceptions.

80 points is the "application eligibility threshold," not a guaranteed pass score.

In actual screening, reviewers also consider the distribution of points, the credibility of bonus items, and any grounds for deductions.

Even with the same 80 points, an applicant who built the score around education and income will see different results from one who barely scraped together bonus points.

> **Note:** The scoring table may have some categories adjusted each year. For the exact point values at the time of your application, check with [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) or your local immigration office.

<h2 id="section-2">How Education Points Are Calculated — Degrees and Major-Related Bonuses</h2>

Education is a category where you can earn up to 35 points.

The point allocation differs based on the type of degree and the country that issued it.

### Base Points by Degree Type

| Degree Type | Point Range | Notes |
|---------|---------|------|
| Doctoral degree | Highest range | Recognized for both domestic and overseas |
| Master's degree | Upper-middle range | Exact degree title must be confirmed |
| Bachelor's degree | Middle range | Based on 4-year programs |
| Associate degree | Lower range | 2–3 year programs |

For overseas degrees, additional degree verification documents are required.

In particular, degrees from China and Southeast Asia require [Apostille certification](https://www.0404.go.kr) or consular verification.

### Bonus Points for Domestic Degrees

Holders of degrees from Korean institutions receive additional bonus points.

- Domestic bachelor's or above: bonus awarded
- Domestic master's or above: higher bonus
- Domestic doctorate: highest bonus

In practice, there are cases where a domestic master's degree results in a higher total score than an overseas doctorate.

Since certain fields of study qualify for separate bonus points, the first step is to check whether your major is among the bonus-eligible fields.

The list of bonus-eligible departments changes each year, so we recommend confirming whether your specific major qualifies through a consultation.

<h2 id="section-3">Korean Language Points — The Difference Between TOPIK and KIIP</h2>

Korean language ability is scored at a maximum of around 20 points.

There are two main ways to provide proof.

### Points by TOPIK Level

Based on the score report issued by [TOPIK at the National Institute for International Education](https://www.topik.go.kr).

| TOPIK Level | Point Range | Validity Period |
|----------|---------|---------|
| Level 6 | Highest | 2 years from the date of result announcement |
| Level 5 | Upper | Same |
| Level 4 | Upper-middle | Same |
| Level 3 | Middle | Same |
| Levels 1–2 | Lower or not recognized | Same |

Score reports past their validity period are not accepted, so checking the expiration date before applying comes first.

### Points by KIIP (Korea Immigration and Integration Program) Stage

You can also earn points based on the KIIP completion stages, operated through the [Social Integration Information Network](https://www.socinet.go.kr).

- Completion of Stage 5 (basic course): Korean language points + bonus
- Passing the Stage 5 evaluation: additional bonus
- Passing the comprehensive evaluation: highest bonus

A frequently overlooked point in practice is whether KIIP and TOPIK scores can be combined.

Since only the higher score within the same Korean language category is recognized, there is no need to prepare both.

It is more point-efficient to focus on whichever option produces a better result for you.

<h2 id="section-4">How Income and Age Points Are Actually Calculated</h2>

Income is worth up to 10 points, and age is worth up to 25 points.

Since these two categories are hard for the applicant to adjust, running a simulation in advance comes first.

### How Income Points Are Calculated

The baseline is your annual income from the previous year.

- Employment income: withholding tax receipt, employment income withholding ledger
- Business income: comprehensive income tax return, VAT return
- Other income: income certificate (issued through [Hometax](https://www.hometax.go.kr))

Income points are divided into brackets based on GNI (Gross National Income).

Points vary by GNI 1x, 2x, 3x, and 4x brackets, and the threshold amounts change each year according to the GNI announced by the Bank of Korea.

The applicable GNI threshold for the current year needs to be confirmed at the time of application.

### Age Point Brackets

Age 35 is the dividing line.

| Age Range | Point Trend |
|---------|---------|
| 18–24 | Lower-middle |
| 25–29 | Middle |
| 30–34 | Upper |
| 35–50 | Highest or upper-middle |
| 51 and above | Decreasing points |

Points tend to peak in the early to mid 30s.

If you are weak in this area, applicants in their late 40s to 50s need to compensate with bonus points to reach the qualifying line.

> **Practical tip:** Scores can differ depending on whether you apply just before or just after turning 35, so timing your application around your birthday can also be a strategy.

<h2 id="section-5">Bonus Categories — Volunteer Work, Tax Payments, Recommendation Letters</h2>

Bonus categories are the decisive cards when your basic score falls short of the 80-point line.

### Volunteer Work Record

Only records registered on the [1365 Volunteer Portal](https://www.1365.go.kr) are recognized.

Volunteer activities done personally or through unregistered organizations do not count toward your score.

- Points are divided into brackets based on accumulated hours
- Records from the past 3 years are primarily recognized
- The original volunteer certificate must be attached

In practice, many applicants have enough volunteer hours but fail to earn points because their activities were not registered with 1365.

### Tax Payment Bonus

A separate bonus is given for income tax payment records.

- Based on accumulated tax paid over the past 3 or 5 years
- Tax payment certificate issued through [Hometax](https://www.hometax.go.kr)
- Any record of tax arrears voids the bonus and triggers deductions

The tax bonus accumulates separately from income points, so even with lower income, a steady tax payment record can keep your score alive.

### Recommendation Letters and Institutional Bonuses

Recommendation letters from certain institutions earn bonus points.

- Recommendation from a central government ministry head
- Recommendation from a metropolitan government head
- Recommendation from institutions certified to endorse outstanding foreign talent

These recommendation bonuses are difficult to obtain, which is why their point value is high.

If you are a potential recommendation candidate at one of these institutions, the first step is to confirm in advance whether you meet the eligibility criteria.

<h2 id="section-6">Deduction Categories and Commonly Overlooked Points</h2>

Deductions often carry a larger point swing than bonuses, so even a single flagged item can knock you below 80 points.

### Major Grounds for Deductions

- Criminal record: fines or heavier penalties trigger deductions (deductions may apply regardless of pardon or restoration of rights)
- Immigration Act violations: overstaying, illegal employment, activities outside permitted status
- Unpaid national or local taxes
- Violations of identity guarantor terms

Deductions do not just shave off points — they affect the credibility of the screening itself.

### Frequently Overlooked Deduction Points

The following are commonly missed in practice.

- Past part-time work done without permission to engage in activities outside your status
- Small fines (such as traffic violations) that went unreported
- Criminal records from abroad (verification through a home-country criminal record certificate is needed)
- Short-term overstays caused by late filing of stay extension applications

In particular, criminal records from abroad often appear in full on a [criminal record certificate](https://www.0404.go.kr), even if the applicant has forgotten about them.

If this is not properly explained, you may be denied regardless of your score.

> **Note:** Deduction grounds most often surface at the time of application from records the applicant was unaware of. Reviewing your own history before applying comes first.

<h2 id="section-7">Score Calculation Simulations by Case</h2>

Looking at how scores actually add up through hypothetical cases makes things easier to understand.

### Case A — Early 30s, Domestic Master's

- Education: domestic master's (base + domestic bonus)
- Korean: TOPIK Level 5
- Income: GNI 2x bracket
- Age: early 30s (upper bracket)
- Bonuses: KIIP Stage 5 completion, 100 hours of volunteer work

Basic points alone come out to the mid-70s, and adding bonuses crosses the 80-point line.

This case represents the most textbook approval pattern.

### Case B — Late 40s, Overseas Doctorate

- Education: overseas doctorate (highest base points)
- Korean: TOPIK Level 4
- Income: GNI 3x bracket (highest)
- Age: late 40s (deductions begin)
- Bonuses: 5-year accumulated tax payment record

Basic points are high, but the points lost in the age category are substantial, so bonuses are needed to compensate.

With income and tax records supporting the score, 80 points can be cleared, but raising the Korean score to Level 5 or above brings the application into the safe zone.

### Case C — Late 20s, Domestic Bachelor's

- Education: domestic bachelor's
- Korean: TOPIK Level 4
- Income: below GNI 1x bracket
- Age: late 20s (middle)
- Bonuses: 200 hours of volunteer work

This is a case where weak education and income make it difficult to hit the 80-point line through bonuses alone.

In this scenario, the strategy is to raise the Korean score to Level 6 or boost bonus points by passing the KIIP comprehensive evaluation.

In a recent similar case, simply raising the Korean level by one notch was enough to reach the qualifying line.

<h2 id="section-8">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. If my F-2-7 score is above 80, is the visa automatically issued?**

80 points is the application eligibility threshold. Actual screening considers the distribution of points, deduction grounds, and the credibility of bonus items as a whole.

Even with the same 80 points, the outcome can differ.

**Q2. If I submit both a TOPIK score and a KIIP completion certificate, are the points combined?**

In the Korean language ability category, only the higher of the two scores is recognized.

That said, KIIP completion can also be counted separately as a social integration bonus, so submitting it still has value.

**Q3. Do overseas degrees receive the same points as domestic degrees?**

The base points for the degree itself are the same, but holders of domestic degrees receive additional bonuses.

Overseas degrees must go through Apostille certification or consular verification to be recognized.

**Q4. I have a past fine on my record — does that affect my score?**

Fines or heavier penalties are subject to deductions, and in some cases the visa may be denied outright.

The record may remain regardless of pardon or restoration of rights, so checking in advance comes first.

**Q5. Does the scoring table change every year?**

Specific point values may be adjusted in part based on policy.

In particular, the GNI threshold amounts and the list of bonus-eligible departments change every year, so confirmation based on the time of application is needed.

**Q6. How long does it take to move from F-2-7 to permanent residency (F-5)?**

After residing for a certain period on F-2-7 status, you can apply for F-5 permanent residency.

The calculation of the qualifying period and the requirements for permanent residency are separate matters, so we recommend confirming a roadmap tailored to your specific case through a consultation.
