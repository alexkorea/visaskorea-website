# Complete Guide to the 4-Month Visa Extension Rule (Essential Checks Before Your Stay Expires)

You can apply to extend your period of stay starting **4 months before** the expiration date, and your application must be filed by the expiration date itself.

Nearly all long-term visas — including D-2, D-8, E-7, F-2, and F-6 — follow the same rule, and even a single day past the expiration date classifies you as an illegal resident.

This article walks through exactly when the 4-month rule applies, the points where people most often get stuck, what to do if you're already late, and how the rules differ by visa type — all based on actual review standards.

<h2 id="section-1">What the 4-Month Visa Extension Rule Actually Means</h2>

### How is "4 months before" calculated?

The Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act allows extension applications to be filed starting **4 months before** the expiration of your period of stay.

For example, if the expiration date on your Alien Registration Card is September 15, 2026, the application window opens on May 15 of the same year.

The calculation is counted backward from the expiration date, and it uses calendar days — not business days.

When you book an appointment through [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) e-Government, the application button itself stays disabled until this 4-month window opens.

This is where the trouble starts.

Many people misread "available starting 4 months before" as "must be done 4 months before," but in reality you can file at any point in that window, as long as the application is submitted by the expiration date.

### Why was a 4-month window set up?

Review typically takes 2 to 6 weeks, and if a single supplementation request comes in, you'll need an additional 2 to 4 weeks.

On top of that, plenty of Immigration Offices push appointment slots back by 1 to 2 months.

According to data from the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr), peak seasons (February–March, August–September) see even longer appointment backlogs.

The takeaway: if you have less than a month left, you're already running late.

<h2 id="section-2">Why the 4-Month Rule Is So Strict</h2>

### Even one day past expiration counts as illegal stay

Your period of stay ends at midnight on the expiration date, and from the very next day you're in illegal residence status.

From that point, it's no longer treated as a simple extension — it gets classified as an **application after overstay**, and a fine is imposed.

Under Articles 25 and 94 of the Immigration Act (available on the [Korean Law Information Center](https://www.law.go.kr)), the fine scales with how many days you overstayed, and the record stays on your file for future visa reviews.

The first thing to check is not the back of your Alien Registration Card, but the expiration date on your [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) My Page.

It's common for the date printed on the physical card to differ from the date in the system — and the system date takes precedence.

### Being late tangles up your future reviews too

In practice, you don't just pay a fine — your "compliance record" takes a hit at your next extension or status change review.

This counts especially heavily under the point-based F-2 and F-5 visas, where it feeds directly into your score.

If you're working toward permanent residency (F-5), a single late extension can undo years of preparation.

| Overstay period | Application type | Practical impact |
|---|---|---|
| 4 months before to day of expiration | Standard extension | Regular review |
| 1–30 days after expiration | Post-overstay filing | Fine + record |
| 31–90 days after expiration | Post-overstay filing | Heavier fine + unfavorable future review |
| 91+ days after expiration | Subject to deportation | Possible exit order / entry ban |

<h2 id="section-3">Key Extension Points by Visa Type</h2>

### D-2, D-4 Student / Trainee Visas

Enrollment certificates, grades, and attendance are the core factors.

More important than the paperwork is the **continuity of enrollment**.

Extensions can be refused while you're on leave, and many people miss the 4-month window during the transition from language training to a degree program.

### D-8, D-9 Investment / Trade Visas

Your corporation's revenue, employment maintenance, and capital status are all checked.

As discussed in [Points to Watch When Applying for a D-8 Visa Extension](/blog/d8-visa-extension-application-cautions), a weak explanation of cash flow can derail things faster than the bank balance itself.

The most dangerous moment is when the corporate fiscal close-out overlaps with your extension filing.

### E-7 Specially Designated Activities Visa

Employment contract renewal, continued enrollment in the four major insurances, and income requirements are all evaluated together.

The [E-7 Visa Extension Review Standards](/blog/e7-visa-extension-korea-2026) get partially revised every year — confirm the exact income requirement for your specific occupation through a consultation.

### F-2, F-6 Residence / Marriage Visas

The F-2-7 point system and F-6 marriage authenticity review are re-examined every cycle.

F-6 in particular reviews cohabitation, Korean language ability, and capacity to support family all together.

If this part is poorly documented, the extension itself may go through, but the next step (F-5) can trip you up.

> **Note:** The points above describe the general flow, and review criteria have recently tightened for some visa categories. Whether the specifics apply to your situation requires expert confirmation.

<h2 id="section-4">Practical Application Process Aligned With the 4-Month Rule</h2>

### Step 1 — Confirm the expiration date (D-120)

Starting 120 days before expiration, log into [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) and check your period of stay.

The key here is the **date registered in the system**, not the date printed on your card.

### Step 2 — Pre-check your documents (D-90)

By 90 days before expiration, you should have the basic documents plus the visa-specific additional documents lined up.

| Category | Common documents | Visa-specific additions |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | Passport, Alien Registration Card, photo | Documents proving visa eligibility |
| Residence | Lease agreement or proof of residence | Family relationship proof (F-series) |
| Income | Employment / income verification | Business registration, financial statements (D-series) |
| Studies | N/A | Enrollment, grades, attendance (D-2/D-4) |

### Step 3 — Book your e-Government appointment (D-60)

By 60 days before expiration, it's safest to lock in either an [HiKorea e-Government](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) appointment or a walk-in slot.

Processing times vary by Immigration Office, and we help you identify the fastest one available.

### Step 4 — File and respond to supplementation requests (within D-30)

If a supplementation request arrives after filing, you typically have 14 days to respond.

A late response can result in the application being closed as denied.

<h2 id="section-5">Common Sticking Points and How to Handle Them</h2>

### When you can't get an appointment

The main Seoul Immigration Office routinely has appointments backed up more than a month during peak season.

In practice, the workaround is not to change jurisdiction but to **use a branch or field office** to buy yourself time.

### When one document arrives late

Employment certificates and income verification certificates being delayed at issuance is one of the most frustrating problems.

The key is to **get a receipt number** even if your submission is incomplete.

As long as the filing is logged before the expiration date, additional documents submitted within the supplementation window are still accepted as a valid application.

### When you missed the 4-month window and have overstayed

In a recent similar case, we resolved things with only a fine by combining a voluntary report with a well-organized written explanation.

That said, outcomes depend on the sincerity of the reason, the number of overstay days, and any prior violation history.

This is the stage where handling it on your own can stack up additional penalties.

> **Practical tip:** If your expiration date falls on a weekend or holiday, it does NOT automatically extend. Plan to file a week earlier based on business days.

<h2 id="section-6">Reasons for Denial and Reapplication Prospects</h2>

### Common reasons for denial

- Income or revenue below the visa threshold
- No enrollment in the four major insurances, or interrupted coverage
- Criminal penalties or Immigration Act violations during your stay
- Unreported changes in family or employment relationship
- Discrepancies between submitted documents and actual circumstances

### If denial is decided

The rule is **departure within 30 days** of the decision date.

However, before departure there is still room for additional review through a [change of resident status](/blog/korea-visa-status-change-guide) or a formal objection.

This is where things diverge.

An objection contests the facts, while a status change shifts you to a different visa — and selecting the right route for your situation requires expert confirmation.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

### Q1. Can I apply earlier than 4 months in advance?

No.

The [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) system only opens applications once you're within 4 months of your expiration date.

Before that, the appointment booking itself is rejected.

### Q2. Can I file on the actual expiration date?

If your application is completed within business hours that day, it counts as a valid filing.

But if even a single document is missing, it gets flagged as overstayed on the spot.

### Q3. Can I still apply for an extension after my visa has expired?

It will be processed as a post-overstay application.

A fine is imposed, and the outcome depends on how many days you overstayed and your reason.

If you exceed 91 days, you may become subject to an exit order — so move as fast as possible.

### Q4. The expiration date on my Alien Registration Card differs from the system. Which one matters?

The system registration date is what counts.

Even if the date printed on the physical card looks longer, going past the system date classifies you as illegal stay.

### Q5. If I'm denied an extension once, can I apply again?

It depends on the reason.

If the denial was simply due to insufficient documentation, you can re-file after supplementing. If you fall short of the qualifications themselves, look at a status change first.

### Q6. Can I travel abroad while my extension is being reviewed?

The application receipt alone does not guarantee re-entry.

Check the separate **re-entry permit** or the [re-entry permit exemption system](/blog/reentry-permit-exemption-guide) before you depart.
