# How Foreigners Can Get a Korean Tax Payment Certificate: A Practical Guide to Avoiding Roadblocks in Visa Extensions and Residency Reviews

Foreigners can obtain a Korean tax payment certificate through one of three channels — Hometax, Government24, or an in-person visit to a tax office — depending on which method best fits the individual's situation.

The eligible recipients are foreigners who have earned income in Korea or registered a business, as well as those asked by the Immigration Office to submit one when applying for a visa extension, change, or permanent residency.

This guide covers the types of tax payment certificates, the documents required for each application channel, the parts that frequently get tangled during real reviews, and the reasons certificates are denied — all organized from a practitioner's perspective.

<h2 id="section-1">What Exactly Is a Tax Payment Certificate and Why Do You Need One</h2>

### Definition and Types of Tax Payment Certificates

A tax payment certificate is **a document that proves there are no unpaid national taxes as of the application date**.

It is divided into the national tax certificate issued by the National Tax Service and the local tax certificate issued by local governments.

The Immigration Office typically requires both for visa reviews.

Submitting only one of them triggers a request for supplementation, which prolongs the processing period.

### Common Situations Where Foreigners Are Asked for One

The most common cases in practice include:

- Extension of work or investment visas such as E-7, D-8, and D-10
- F-2 residence visa point-system application
- F-5 permanent residency application
- F-6 marriage migration visa extension
- Application for activities outside residency status after business registration

In particular, for F-5 permanent residency reviews, **tax payment history itself is directly tied to the score**.

A history of delinquency reduces points or becomes grounds for rejection.

### What's the Difference Between National and Local Taxes?

| Category | Issuing Authority | Main Tax Items | Application Channel |
|------|----------|----------|----------|
| National Tax Certificate | National Tax Service (Tax Office) | Income tax, corporate tax, VAT | Hometax, Tax Office |
| Local Tax Certificate | City/County/District Office | Resident tax, automobile tax, property tax | Government24, Wetax, Community Service Center |

> **Note:** In most cases, both types are required for submission to Immigration. Bringing only one will result in a supplementation notice.

<h2 id="section-2">3 Ways for Foreigners to Get a Tax Payment Certificate</h2>

### 1. Online Issuance via Hometax (National Tax)

This is the fastest method and is free.

Access the National Tax Service [Hometax](https://www.hometax.go.kr) and apply after identity verification.

To apply on your own as a foreigner, you must have one of the following:

- A joint certificate (formerly the public certificate) based on your foreigner registration number
- A financial certificate
- Simple authentication (KakaoTalk, Naver, etc.) — partially supported

The part that most often gets stuck in practice is the **identity verification step**.

If you've only recently received your foreigner registration card, the certificate itself may not be issuable, or identity verification may fail.

When things get tangled at this stage, you'll end up needing to visit the tax office in person anyway.

### 2. Online Issuance via Government24 / Wetax (Local Tax)

Local tax payment certificates are issued through [Government24](https://www.gov.kr) or Wetax.

As with Hometax, identity verification is required.

The issuance system interface differs slightly depending on the city/county/district that has jurisdiction over your residence.

### 3. In-Person Issuance at a Tax Office or Community Service Center

This is the most reliable method when the online route hits a snag.

The required items are:

- Original foreigner registration card
- Power of attorney and the agent's ID, if applying by proxy
- Fee (cash or card)

The advantage of in-person issuance is **immediate, on-the-spot issuance**.

The downside is that issuance may be refused if you visit a tax office outside your jurisdiction.

> **Practical Tip:** Certificates submitted to Immigration have a short validity period. Typically, only those issued within the last 30 days are accepted. If you obtain it too early, you'll need to get it reissued.

<h2 id="section-3">The Tax Payment Certificate Required Varies by Visa Type</h2>

### For Work Visa (E-series) Extensions

For extensions of work visas such as E-7 and E-9, the national tax certificate takes priority because income has been generated under the applicant's name.

The local tax certificate is additionally required if the applicant owns a vehicle or property.

In practice, even when **the company has only withheld tax at source and the individual has not separately filed**, it is necessary to confirm that the tax payment certificate comes out clean.

If the company has failed to remit withholding tax, traces of it can sometimes appear on the individual's tax payment certificate.

### For Investment Visa (D-8) Extensions

Since the core of a D-8 visa is corporate operation, **the corporate tax payment certificate** and **the representative's personal tax payment certificate** are both required simultaneously.

If the corporation has unpaid VAT or is on an installment plan, the application gets flagged immediately.

A weakness here works against you in the extension review.

### For F-2 and F-5 Applications

For the F-2 point system and F-5 permanent residency, **the tax payment history itself is reflected in the score**.

Beyond simply having no delinquency, a tax payment record of a certain length is required.

| Visa | Required Documents | Review Focus |
|------|----------|-------------|
| E-7 Extension | Personal national and local tax certificates | Delinquency status |
| D-8 Extension | Corporate + representative tax certificates | VAT filing and payment record |
| F-2 Application | Personal tax certificate, income amount certificate | Tax payment record score |
| F-5 Application | Tax payment history for the past several years | Tax compliance |
| F-6 Extension | Personal or spouse's tax certificate | Proof of livelihood capacity |

The tax payment record standards vary by visa type and application timing.

The exact standards applicable this year require separate confirmation based on the individual's circumstances.

<h2 id="section-4">Real Cases of Tax Payment Certificate Issuance Being Denied or Tangled</h2>

### When Delinquency History Is Discovered

This is the most common reason for denial.

It often surfaces at the time of issuance even though the applicant was unaware.

Frequently discovered delinquency items include:

- Unfiled VAT after business registration
- Penalties for unfiled comprehensive income tax
- Small-amount local taxes such as automobile tax and resident tax
- Taxes linked to overdue 4 major insurances at past business locations

Even small delinquencies prevent the issuance of a tax payment certificate.

### During Installment or Payment Deferral

Even if installment approval has been granted, the tax payment certificate may show **"delinquency exists"**.

In this case, Immigration will require additional explanation during the review.

You'll need to submit the installment plan and payment receipts together.

### Right After a Foreigner Registration Number Change or Renewal

Immediately after your foreigner registration number is renewed due to a change in residency status, system integration can lag, sometimes blocking issuance.

It's usually resolved within a few days, but the timeline can get tangled if your visa expiration is imminent.

<h2 id="section-5">Documents Frequently Required Alongside the Tax Payment Certificate</h2>

### Difference from the Income Amount Certificate

The tax payment certificate proves "there is no delinquency," while the income amount certificate proves "how much was earned."

For F-2 point system and F-5 permanent residency reviews, **both are required**.

The income amount certificate is only issued for years in which comprehensive income tax filing has been completed.

If you did not file for the previous year, issuance itself is not possible.

### Business Registration Certificate

For business-related visas such as D-8 and D-9, a business registration certificate is additionally required.

If there is a history of suspension or closure, Immigration will request additional explanation.

### 4 Major Insurances Enrollment Certificate

For work visa extensions, the 4 major insurances enrollment certificate issued by organizations such as the [National Pension Service](https://www.nps.or.kr) and the National Health Insurance Service is also required.

If the tax payment flow and insurance enrollment flow don't align, the review may raise suspicions.

| Document | Issuing Authority | Use in Visa Review |
|------|----------|----------------|
| Tax Payment Certificate | National Tax Service, Local Government | Confirms delinquency status |
| Income Amount Certificate | National Tax Service | Income level / score |
| Business Registration Certificate | National Tax Service | Verifies business existence |
| 4 Major Insurances Enrollment Certificate | Each agency | Confirms regular employment |

<h2 id="section-6">The Practical Order for Resolving Delinquency Issues</h2>

### First, Check the Delinquency Items

Look up your own delinquency records on Hometax or Wetax.

Small-amount local taxes are often unknown even to the applicant.

### Reissue Immediately After Payment

The tax payment certificate is reissued either on the day of payment or the next business day.

If visa expiration is imminent, an approach where you **first submit the payment receipt to Immigration and supplement it afterward** is also used.

Whether this approach is possible depends on how each individual immigration office operates.

### How to Handle Cases During Installment Payments

If you submit the installment approval document and payment plan together, the reviewer may acknowledge your intent to pay normally.

However, whether it is accepted varies case by case.

> **Note:** If delinquency is discovered while visa expiration is within 30 days, the timeline is highly likely to get tangled. Quick judgment is necessary.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

### Q1. Can I get a tax payment certificate without a foreigner registration card?

It depends on your residency status.

Short-term residents who have earned income and been issued a tax identification number can obtain one by visiting the tax office in person.

Whether this applies to your specific case requires separate confirmation.

### Q2. Can a proxy obtain it if I am not in Korea?

Yes, it's possible.

A power of attorney, a copy of your ID, and the proxy's ID are required.

However, since the power of attorney format and authentication method vary by tax office, it must be confirmed in advance.

### Q3. Can the tax payment certificate be issued in English?

The National Tax Service issues English-language tax payment certificates.

These can be used for submission overseas.

English versions of local tax certificates are only issued by some local governments.

### Q4. How long is the issued tax payment certificate valid?

In principle, it is valid for 30 days from the date of issuance.

For Immigration submission, the most common requirement is that the certificate be issued within 30 days of the application date.

### Q5. I have a delinquency and my visa extension is imminent. What should I do?

The principle is to first pay the delinquent amount immediately and have the certificate reissued.

If payment is difficult, there are procedures such as installment application and advance explanation to Immigration, but these are handled differently case by case.
