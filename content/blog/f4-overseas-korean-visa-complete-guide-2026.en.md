# F-4 Overseas Korean Visa: Complete Guide to Eligibility and Application

The F-4 visa allows foreign nationals of Korean heritage to maintain long-term residence in Korea that is, in practical terms, close to permanent residency.

It is available to those who previously held Korean citizenship and their lineal descendants who have since acquired foreign citizenship.

This guide walks through the points where applicants most often get stuck in practice — eligibility assessment, required documents, restrictions on unskilled labor, and revocation triggered by criminal misconduct.

<h2 id="section-1">Legal Basis of the F-4 Overseas Korean Visa and Who Qualifies</h2>

The F-4 visa is grounded in the Act on the Immigration and Legal Status of Overseas Koreans (commonly called the Overseas Koreans Act).

The Act broadly guarantees the residence and activities of overseas Korean nationals within Korea, but it clearly restricts engagement in unskilled labor.

You can find the full text of the law at the [Korean Law Information Center](https://law.go.kr).

### Definition of Overseas Korean Nationals

The core point is this.

Eligibility extends to those who previously held Korean citizenship — including those who emigrated abroad before the establishment of the Korean government — as well as their lineal descendants.

In practice, you must objectively prove that you, or one of your parents or grandparents, once held Korean citizenship.

Family registers, removed family registers (jejeok-deungbon), and family relation certificates are the strongest forms of evidence.

### Situations Where Issuance May Be Restricted

Even if you qualify as a Korean descendant on paper, issuance may be denied in the following situations:

- Individuals deemed likely to harm the safety or public order of Korea
- Men under the age of 41 who acquired foreign citizenship to evade military service
- Those with a prior deportation record
- Those with a record of criminal misconduct

The military service evasion issue, in particular, has become one of the most frequent sticking points in recent reviews.

If there is any chance this applies to you, pre-screening before applying is essential.

<h2 id="section-2">F-4 Eligibility Criteria — Education, Work Experience, and Certification</h2>

Overseas Koreans aged 18 or older must meet certain requirements to be granted F-4 status.

These requirements can be satisfied through education, work experience, or a nationally recognized certification.

### Education-Based Criteria

- Graduation from a 4-year university or higher
- Graduation from a 2- or 3-year junior college plus a set period of work experience
- A degree recognized by the Korean government

### Experience and Certification-Based Criteria

If your educational background is insufficient, you may still qualify through one of the following:

- Permanent residency in an OECD member country
- Being aged 60 or older
- Holding a national technical certification
- Service as a representative director or registered executive of a corporation
- Tax payment record above a certain threshold in the prior year

> **Practical tip:** The detailed criteria for education and experience are adjusted each year. To check which category you fall under, refer to the announcements on [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr), and confirm exact applicability through a consultation.

### Switching from H-2 to F-4

Holders of the Working Visit (H-2) visa may convert to F-4 if they meet certain qualifications.

Acceptable conditions include obtaining a craftsman-level or higher certification, or working for a set period in agriculture, livestock, fisheries, manufacturing, or at a company located outside the metropolitan area.

Since conversion requirements vary by industry classification and region, your individual case should be checked first.

<h2 id="section-3">F-4 Application Documents and Procedure</h2>

The documents required vary depending on the application type (status change inside Korea or application from abroad).

The basic framework is the same, but documents proving your overseas Korean status are the heart of the application.

### Common Required Documents

| Category | Document | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Application | Integrated application form | One photograph attached |
| Identity | Passport original and copy | Valid for 6+ months |
| Korean descent | Family relation certificate | Issued in home country or Korean removed family register |
| Korean descent | Birth certificate | Proof of parent's Korean nationality |
| Eligibility | Education/experience evidence | Varies by category |
| Residence | Proof of address | Lease agreement, etc. |

Documents issued overseas must be apostilled or authenticated by a consulate.

Translations must be notarized before submission.

### Application Steps

1. Identify which eligibility category applies to you
2. Obtain home-country documents and apostille them
3. Verify Korean family relation documents
4. Complete the application form and prepare a photograph
5. Visit the competent immigration office or apply online through [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr)
6. Submit additional materials if requested
7. Issuance or status change is completed

The point that most often causes hold-ups in practice is a mismatch in name or date of birth between home-country documents and the Korean removed family register.

When this evidence is weak, supplementary requests keep coming and processing times stretch out.

### Processing Time

Processing times vary by immigration office.

When the application proceeds without needing supplementary documents, it can move relatively quickly, but if the proof of Korean descent raises any doubt, additional requests for home-country records can extend the timeline by several months.

> **Caution:** Because home-country administrative systems differ from Korea's, proving that the same person appears in both can be tricky. For Chinese-Korean applicants in particular, the household register (hukou) and personnel files (dang'an) may also need to be reviewed.

<h2 id="section-4">Restrictions on Unskilled Labor During F-4 Residency</h2>

The biggest difference between F-4 and other long-term residency visas is the restriction on engaging in unskilled labor.

It is not uncommon for someone to overlook this and end up working in a way that leads to status revocation.

### Occupations Classified as Unskilled Labor

Unskilled labor occupations are designated by Ministry of Justice notice.

- Day labor at construction sites
- Kitchen assistance or waiting tables at restaurants
- Cleaning and sanitation work
- Simple tasks in agriculture, livestock, or fisheries
- Simple assembly or packaging in manufacturing

Engaging in this kind of work is not a lawful activity under F-4 status.

### Permitted Activities

- Office work, managerial roles
- Professional and technical occupations
- Running a business (sole proprietor or corporate representative)
- Occupations that make use of a nationally recognized certification

What matters most is the actual content of your work.

Even if your job title is "office worker," if you are in fact doing unskilled labor, you are subject to enforcement.

### Penalties for Violations

When an unskilled labor violation is identified, the following steps follow:

| Stage | Action Taken | Impact |
|-------|--------------|--------|
| First offense | Corrective order/fine | Status retained |
| Repeated or serious | Revocation of status | Departure order |
| Employer | Penalty for employer | Employer also held responsible |

If a violation has occurred, it is safer to have it fully reviewed — without omission — at your next extension or change application.

Since the detailed classification of unskilled occupations is partially adjusted each year, check whether your specific job falls under the rule through a consultation.

<h2 id="section-5">F-4 Status Revocation — Criminal Misconduct and Penalties</h2>

Receiving F-4 status once does not mean the matter is settled forever.

If a criminal incident occurs during your stay, your status may be revoked.

### Grounds for Revocation

- A sentence of imprisonment or heavier
- Habitual drunk driving
- Assault, injury, or sex offenses
- Gambling- or drug-related crimes
- Repeated engagement in unskilled labor

In practice, drunk driving and assault are the most common reasons for revocation.

For drunk driving in particular, the severity of the disposition depends on the measured blood alcohol level.

### Misconduct Review Procedure

If you become subject to action for criminal misconduct, you will undergo a separate review at the misconduct review office of the immigration office.

This stage does not end with simply submitting documents.

Your personal statement, written apology, plan for preventing recurrence, and family relationships are all reviewed together.

> **Caution:** Once a deportation order is issued through the misconduct review, an entry ban can be imposed alongside it. How you respond immediately after the incident shapes the outcome.

You can review the basic outline of the misconduct review process at the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr) website, though how it applies to your specific case will differ from one matter to the next.

<h2 id="section-6">Comparing F-4 and F-5 (Permanent Residency) Visas</h2>

Many overseas Koreans set F-5 permanent residency as their long-term goal.

Knowing the differences between the two statuses in advance makes strategy easier to plan.

| Item | F-4 | F-5 |
|------|-----|-----|
| Period of stay | Up to 3 years, renewable | Permanent (no time limit) |
| Unskilled labor | Restricted | No restriction |
| Re-entry permit | Limited short-term exemption | Broader exemption |
| Effect of misconduct | Status revocation | Permanent residency revocation |
| Application requirements | Korean descent + education/experience | Period as F-4 + additional requirements |

The most common path is to reside under F-4 for a certain period, accumulate the necessary qualifications, and then transition to F-5.

A history of unskilled labor during that time can count against you during the F-5 review.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

### Q1. My parents never held Korean nationality, but my grandparents did. Can I still qualify for F-4?

Yes, it's possible. If you can prove that even one of your lineal ascendants held Korean citizenship, you are within the eligible group. That said, establishing the family chain from grandparent to parent to yourself can be intricate, so a pre-review is advisable.

### Q2. I'm a Chinese-Korean and have no Korean family register. How do I prove my status?

You will need to secure both home-country-issued family records and the Korean removed family register together. Differences in the Chinese characters used for names or in dates of birth often block the proof of identity, so additional supplementary documents may be needed.

### Q3. Can I run a restaurant on an F-4 visa?

Yes — operating the business as the owner is permitted. However, if you yourself perform unskilled labor such as kitchen assistance or waiting tables, that may constitute a violation. The actual content of your work is the standard.

### Q4. Can a single DUI lead to F-4 revocation?

It depends on the blood alcohol level, whether an accident occurred, and your prior record. Even a one-time offense can trigger misconduct review if the level exceeds a certain threshold, and the likelihood of revocation rises with repeated incidents. How you respond immediately after the incident makes a critical difference.

### Q5. Can I bring family members in under F-4 status?

A spouse and minor children may be invited under the F-1 accompanying-family status. The stability of the principal applicant's residency and the housing situation will be reviewed alongside the application.

### Q6. My F-4 expired and I forgot to renew. What should I do?

Once your stay period passes, you are in irregular-stay status and subject to fines and a departure order. Renewal before expiration is the rule, but if you've already passed it, voluntarily report to immigration right away and follow the subsequent procedure.
