# E-7-1 Visa Application Requirements: Complete Guide to Specified Activity Visa Qualifications, Documents, and Procedures

The E-7-1 visa is issued only for 86 professional occupations designated by the Ministry of Justice, and you can apply only when your education, experience, employer, and salary all align simultaneously.

It targets holders of a bachelor's degree or higher, or those with 5+ years of experience in the same field, and the Korean employer must meet specific corporate requirements.

Below, we walk through E-7-1 visa application requirements, occupation categories, document composition, key review points, rejection reasons, and the bottlenecks that trip people up most often in practice.

<h2 id="section-1">First, Let's Get Clear on What the E-7-1 Visa Actually Is</h2>

The E-7-1 falls under the "Specified Activity" visa category and specifically covers **professional personnel**.

Simple office work or general sales positions don't qualify — your role must precisely match one of the occupation codes designated by the Ministry of Justice.

### Subcategories of the E-7 Visa

The E-7 visa splits into three branches.

- E-7-1: Professional personnel (managers, professionals, some clerical workers)
- E-7-2: Semi-professional personnel (clerical workers, some service workers)
- E-7-3: General skilled workers (some skilled technicians)

Even within the E-7 family, application requirements and salary thresholds differ.

In practice, it's common for applicants to misidentify which category they belong to before even starting their application.

### Representative E-7-1 Occupations

Among the occupations announced by the Ministry of Justice, the following are representative fields under E-7-1.

- Management, planning, and finance managers
- HR, education, and public relations managers
- Computer systems designers and analysts
- Aviation, shipbuilding, and mechanical engineering technicians
- University lecturers and foreign school teachers
- Designers, interpreters/translators, and advertising specialists

For your application to pass, the occupation code must connect in a single clean line with your academic background and experience.

You can find the detailed occupation list in the foreign resident information notices at [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

<h2 id="section-2">E-7-1 Visa Application Requirements — Education and Experience</h2>

Education and experience are the very first hurdles for the E-7-1.

It may look simple at a glance, but the actual review hinges on whether your **major and job duties align**.

### Education Requirements

The baseline is one of the following.

- **Bachelor's degree or higher** in the relevant field + 1+ year of experience
- **Master's degree or higher** in the relevant field (experience may be waived)
- **5+ years of experience** in the relevant field (qualifies even without a degree)

The most common sticking point here is how "relevant field" gets interpreted.

If your major is business administration but your job is IT systems design, you'll be flagged for major-job mismatch and need supplementary documentation.

### Experience Requirements

Applying based on experience makes documentation tricky.

- National insurance enrollment history or employment certificates
- Pay stubs or tax payment records
- Detailed employment certificates specifying job duties

If these descriptions are weak, only your raw "employment period" will be recognized, and job relevance may be denied.

In actual reviews, your documentation needs to show "what you did, how deeply, and for how long."

| Category | Basic Requirement | Practical Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|
| Bachelor's | Degree + 1 year experience | Major-job alignment |
| Master's | Degree only | Thesis/research field matched to job |
| Experience | 5+ years | Verified via national insurance or tax records |
| Certifications | Nationally recognized | Confirm occupation-specific requirements |

<h2 id="section-3">Employer Requirements — Often the Company-Side Conditions Matter More</h2>

The most common scenario is preparing thoroughly based on your own qualifications, only to get blocked on the employer side.

For the E-7-1, **whether the employer qualifies** is checked before the foreign applicant themselves.

### Baseline Requirements for the Employer

- A corporation or sole proprietorship in operation for a certain period after business registration
- A minimum number of Korean employees
- Compliance with the cap on the foreign employee ratio
- No tax arrears or labor law violations

The "1 foreign employee per 5 Koreans" principle is one example — ratio rules apply in many cases.

These ratio calculations vary by occupation, so the exact standard for your company's situation should be verified through a consultation.

### Company Size and Revenue

Revenue scale and number of regular employees must meet certain thresholds for foreign hiring eligibility to be recognized.

Startups or one-person corporations are unlikely to be recognized without supplementary documentation.

In practice, if the company has small revenue or is newly established, you'll need separate documentation proving the business plan and hiring necessity to have a chance at approval.

> **Caution:** If the employer's qualifications are weak, even an outstanding applicant can be rejected.

<h2 id="section-4">Salary Requirements — The Biggest Bottleneck in Actual Reviews</h2>

Salary requirements are the most clear-cut deciding factor for the E-7-1.

Minimum salary thresholds are set per occupation, and falling short means your application gets returned outright.

### Core of the Salary Standard

- Based on the previous year's per-capita Gross National Income (GNI)
- Required at a certain percentage of GNI depending on occupation
- Separate thresholds apply for some high-tech occupations

GNI figures are updated annually, so you'll need to reconfirm the current year's exact threshold at the time of application.

We'll walk you through the precise minimum salary threshold for your specific occupation during consultation.

### Commonly Overlooked Points in Salary Calculation

What counts toward your annual salary beyond base pay is where things often get tangled.

- Included: base salary, regular bonuses, fixed allowances
- Excluded: variable bonuses, partial meal/transportation allowances, business travel expenses

If your employment contract only states "performance bonuses separate," that amount is unlikely to be recognized as part of your annual salary.

If this is weak, you may think you've met the salary threshold but be judged as falling short in practice.

<h2 id="section-5">Application Procedure — Overseas Applications vs. Domestic Status Change</h2>

There are two main routes for E-7-1 applications.

The procedure differs depending on which country you're currently in and what visa you hold.

### Route 1: Overseas Visa Issuance Application

This applies when an overseas resident is obtaining the E-7-1 for the first time.

1. The Korean employer applies for a Certificate for Confirmation of Visa Issuance
2. Review at the Immigration and Foreign Affairs Office
3. Confirmation number is issued
4. Receive the visa sticker at the Korean consulate in your country of nationality
5. Register as a foreign resident within 90 days of entry

### Route 2: Status Change While in Korea

This applies when switching from D-10 (job seeking), D-2 (student), or similar statuses to E-7-1.

1. Complete the status change application form
2. Submit in person at the jurisdictional Immigration and Foreign Affairs Office
3. Review (typically 2–4 weeks)
4. Updated Alien Registration Card is issued

For status changes, the Immigration Office reviews directly, so any missing documents will trigger immediate requests for supplementation.

Processing times vary significantly by office, and finding the fastest jurisdictional office to process through is a common practical tactic.

| Procedure | Overseas Visa Issuance | Domestic Status Change |
|---|---|---|
| Applicant | Employer (in Korea) | Applicant (in Korea) |
| Processing time | 2–4 weeks | 2–4 weeks |
| Entry process | Receive visa at consulate, then enter | Effective immediately after change |
| Foreign resident registration | Within 90 days of entry | Updated immediately upon change |

<h2 id="section-6">Required Documents — Split Between Applicant Documents and Company Documents</h2>

E-7-1 documentation is divided between the applicant's side and the employer's side.

If either side is weak, you'll get an immediate supplementation request.

### Applicant's Documents

- Visa issuance confirmation application or status change application
- Passport copy and standard-format photo
- Diploma and academic transcripts (apostilled or consular-verified)
- Employment certificate (with specific job duties stated)
- Certification copy (for the relevant occupation)
- Criminal record certificate (required for some occupations)

Your diploma and employment certificate need apostille or consular verification from your home country.

This step is the most common source of schedule delays.

### Employer's Documents

- Employment contract (specifying salary and job duties)
- Copy of business registration certificate
- Corporate register copy (for corporations)
- Company brochure and organizational chart
- Statement of reason for hiring a foreign national
- Recent revenue and tax payment certificates
- List of Korean employees (for national insurance enrollment verification)

The statement of reason must show "why this foreign national specifically needs to be hired."

Rather than writing at length, specifically describing how the job and the applicant's expertise interlock raises your approval odds.

> **Practical tip:** The company brochure should be built not as boilerplate PR material, but as documentation that **substantiates the need to hire a foreign national**.

<h2 id="section-7">Key Review Points and Rejection Reasons</h2>

The E-7-1 review isn't just a document checklist — it examines "whether this foreign national is genuinely needed in this position."

No matter how many documents you submit, a weak underlying narrative makes approval unlikely.

### Most Common Rejection Reasons

- Major and job duties don't clearly align
- Salary falls short of the GNI threshold
- Insufficient company revenue or size
- Determined that a Korean worker could fill the role
- Employer's history of prior visa violations
- Inadequate documentation of experience

In particular, "potential replacement by a Korean worker" isn't explicitly stated, but carries significant weight in actual reviews.

### Keys to Increasing Approval Odds

- Match the occupation code precisely with your actual job duties
- Build a single coherent narrative connecting your degree, experience, and duties
- Clearly itemize salary components
- Specifically articulate the employer's hiring necessity

If this is weak, you can be rejected even when every other requirement is met.

The legal basis can be found in [Annex 1-2 of the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act](https://www.law.go.kr).

<h2 id="section-8">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

**Q1. Can I apply for the E-7-1 with only work experience, without a bachelor's degree?**

Yes.

If you can document 5+ years of experience in the relevant field, you can apply without a degree.

However, a simple employment certificate alone isn't sufficient — you'll also need objective records such as national insurance enrollment history or tax payment records.

**Q2. Can I change my status from D-10 to E-7-1?**

Yes.

You can sign an employment contract while in Korea and apply for a status change.

That said, if the genuineness of your job-seeking activity during D-10 stay is questioned, or if the employer's qualifications fall short, you may be rejected.

**Q3. How long is the stay period for the initial application?**

Typically issued in 1- to 2-year increments, varying based on occupation and company situation.

You must apply for renewal before expiration, and renewal requirements are often slightly more relaxed than initial issuance.

We'll provide accurate guidance on the expected stay period for your case through consultation.

**Q4. Is failing to meet the salary threshold an automatic rejection?**

In most cases, yes.

However, separate standards may apply depending on occupation, and relaxed standards may apply for high-tech industry positions.

You'll need to confirm in advance which standard applies to your specific occupation.

**Q5. My company is a brand-new startup — can we still hire someone on an E-7-1?**

It's not easy, but it's not impossible.

Newly established companies with small revenue or few employees must provide separate documentation substantiating both hiring necessity and business sustainability.

Many cases get rejected at this point, so prior review is recommended.

**Q6. After a rejection, when can I reapply?**

You can reapply immediately, but submitting the same documents will produce the same result.

You need to accurately understand the rejection reason and reapply with supplemented documents to get a different outcome.

The rejection reason is stated in the disposition notice, but interpreting its actual meaning is often difficult.
