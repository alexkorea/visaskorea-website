# F-6 Visa Denial Reasons and How to Appeal — What to Do After Receiving a Rejection

A denial on your F-6 visa isn't the end of the road.
Once you understand exactly why you were rejected, you can address the gaps in your documents and supporting materials, then either reapply or file a formal appeal to reverse the outcome.
Below, we walk through the most common denial reasons, the appeal process, and the strategies that actually work.

<h2 id="section-1">Common Reasons for F-6 Visa Denial</h2>

### Insufficient Evidence of a Genuine Marriage

The most frequent stumbling block in real-world reviews isn't missing paperwork.
What matters most is whether the relationship is authentic.
Even applicants with a complete document set get denied if the story of how they met, how they communicated, and the context of their relationship doesn't hold up.

What reviewers actually look at:

- How and when the couple first met
- Proof of ongoing contact during the relationship (messages, photos, visit records)
- Travel history showing mutual visits before marriage (entry/exit records)
- Whether the couple has lived together after marriage, or plans to

> **Practical note:** Scrutiny increases when the relationship is short, conducted primarily online, or involves a significant age gap. If any of these apply to you, building a clear record of the relationship's authenticity is the first priority.

### Sponsoring Spouse's Income Falls Below the Threshold

If the Korean spouse's income doesn't meet the required level, the application will be denied.
Reviewers look not only at the income amount but also at its consistency and stability.
Freelancers, self-employed individuals, and day laborers may be assessed differently than salaried employees, even at equivalent income levels.

> **Important:** Income thresholds are updated annually. Before reapplying, always verify the current year's requirements — using last year's figures as a benchmark will likely produce the same result.

### External Factors That Raise Doubts About the Marriage's Legitimacy

When country of origin, age difference, and the circumstances of the marriage combine in ways that raise red flags, reviewers may suspect a marriage of convenience.
In these cases, embassies often request additional documents — invitation letters, wedding photos, statements from people who know the couple — and the consular interview tends to be more intensive.
If this is the reason for your denial, adding more documents alone won't be enough.
A written explanation that lays out the full context of the relationship in concrete detail is essential.

<h2 id="section-2">What to Do Immediately After Receiving a Denial</h2>

### Start by Reading the Denial Notice Carefully

The denial notice issued by the embassy or immigration office includes the **reason for rejection, either as a code or a written description**.
If you don't interpret that reason correctly, a reapplication will likely produce the same result.
Whether the cause is insufficient documentation, failure to meet income requirements, or doubts about the relationship — the answer shapes every step that follows, so get the diagnosis right first.

### Decide Whether to Reapply or File an Appeal

| | Reapplication | Formal Appeal (Review) |
|---|---|---|
| When it applies | When you can address the gaps and start fresh | When you're contesting the denial decision itself |
| When to file | Any time after resolving the issues | Within the statutory deadline after denial |
| Reviewing authority | Same office or a different one | Ministry of Justice, Immigration and Foreign Policy Bureau |
| Processing time | Generally faster | Takes longer |
| Cost | Government filing fee + administrative handling | Confirm separately |

> **Practical note:** A formal appeal is effective when there is a procedural or legal flaw in the denial, or when the wrong standard was applied. If the denial was simply due to missing documents, reapplying is usually the more practical path.

<h2 id="section-3">How to File a Formal Appeal</h2>

### Deadlines and Submission Methods

An appeal must be submitted within the **statutory deadline** from the date of the denial notice.
Under Article 76 of the Immigration Control Act, you can file a written appeal at your local immigration office or submit it through the [Hi Korea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) online system.

Required content in your appeal:

- A description of the denial decision being challenged
- The grounds for the appeal (factual disputes or interpretation of law)
- A list of supplementary documents attached

### The Most Common Stumbling Block When Writing an Appeal

The core of a successful appeal is **a logical explanation of why the denial was unwarranted**.
Emotional appeals and simple clarifications don't carry weight in formal review.
You need to cite the relevant provisions of the law tied to the denial code and demonstrate, with supporting evidence, that your situation meets the required criteria.

> **Important:** A poorly written appeal wastes your one formal review opportunity. Before filing, make sure you fully understand what the denial code actually means.

<h2 id="section-4">Document Strategy for Reapplication</h2>

### A Personal Statement Needs Specifics, Not Length

The personal statement is the element most often neglected in reapplications.
A narrative with concrete dates, locations, and situations is far more compelling to a reviewer than pages of vague writing.
Phrases like "we love each other deeply" carry no evidentiary weight from a reviewer's perspective.

What your personal statement must include:

- The date and circumstances of your first meeting (app, mutual friend, etc.)
- Key moments during the relationship (visits, trips, meeting each other's families)
- What led to the decision to get married
- Your plans for life in Korea after entry

### Supplementary Document Checklist by Denial Reason

The documents you need to add depend on why you were denied.
Use the table below to check whether anything is missing before you reapply.

| Denial Reason | Examples of Supplementary Documents |
|---|---|
| Doubts about relationship authenticity | Photos from the relationship, chat/message history, flight records showing mutual visits, photos with each other's families |
| Failure to meet income threshold | Last 3 months of pay stubs, employment certificate, income tax payment confirmation |
| Doubts about marriage legitimacy | Wedding photos, invitation, marriage registration confirmation, written consent from both families |
| Unsatisfactory interview responses | Request for re-interview + prepared answers for expected questions |
| Document formatting errors | Reissued documents, re-confirmed notarization or translations |

> **Practical note:** In recent cases, applicants who reapplied with additional documents but no personal statement were denied again. Without a direct written response to the denial reason, more paperwork alone won't change the outcome.

<h2 id="section-5">Key Strategies for Succeeding on Review</h2>

### What Reviewers Actually Look For

In an F-6 visa review, the first thing a reviewer asks is: **what has changed since the denial?**
Resubmitting the same documents produces the same result.
The outcome only changes when supplementary materials directly and precisely address the stated reason for denial.

Review preparation checklist:

- Have you fully interpreted the denial reason code?
- Do you have supplementary materials that specifically address that reason?
- Does your personal statement include concrete dates, locations, and circumstances?
- Have you updated the sponsoring spouse's income documents to the most recent versions?

### Preparing for a Consular Interview

A consular interview may be added to the reapplication or appeal process.
Inconsistent answers between the two applicants during an interview is grounds for immediate denial.
The following points must be aligned between both parties before the interview:

- The date and location of your first meeting
- When and how the proposal happened
- Each other's family members' names and occupations
- The wedding date and venue
- Your planned address in Korea

Because interview patterns vary by immigration office, prepare a targeted list of expected questions and coordinated answers based on the specifics of your case.

<h2 id="section-6">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

### Q. Can I reapply immediately after an F-6 visa denial?

You can reapply at any time.
That said, reapplying without resolving the reason for denial will produce the same outcome.
Repeated denials stack up in your review history and can make future applications harder to approve.

### Q. Can I file an appeal and reapply at the same time?

Filing both simultaneously is generally not advisable.
Submitting a reapplication while an appeal is pending can result in the appeal being automatically cancelled.
Which route makes more sense depends on the nature of the denial and how much time remains within the statutory deadline.

### Q. What if the embassy won't tell me the specific reason for the denial?

Embassies are not always required to disclose detailed denial reasons.
In that case, use the denial code or the language in the notice to infer the likely cause.
You can also submit a request for additional information through the [Immigration and Foreign Policy Bureau](https://www.immigration.go.kr) civil affairs window or via an administrative information disclosure request.

### Q. Can I resubmit the same documents when reapplying?

Core required documents must be resubmitted, and any that have passed their validity period will need to be reissued.
The critical point is not just resubmitting the same package — it's **adding supplementary documents and a personal statement that directly address the denial reason**.
Resubmitting identical documents without addressing the underlying issue will not change the result.

### Q. What happens if I miss the appeal deadline?

Once the appeal window has closed, that route is no longer available.
You will need to go through the full reapplication process from the beginning.
Keep in mind that your prior denial history will factor into the new review, so addressing every gap in your application with extra care is essential.

### Q. What affects the chances of success on review?

Denials caused by document errors or formatting issues are relatively easier to overcome with corrections.
Cases where the authenticity of the relationship itself is in question require significantly more evidence and careful preparation.
Review standards also vary by immigration office, so confirming which office has jurisdiction over your case matters.
