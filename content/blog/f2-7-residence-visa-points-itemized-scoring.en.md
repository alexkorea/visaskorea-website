# F-2 Residence Visa Points System: Complete Guide to Score Calculation by Category

The F-2 residence visa points system requires a minimum of 80 points out of 120, and where you accumulate those points often determines whether you pass the threshold.
The program targets professionals, former international students, and long-term workers on E-7 visas who have resided in Korea for five years or more, with Korean language proficiency and income forming the two main pillars.
Below, we break down every scoring category—age, education, Korean language, income, length of stay, and more—based on the actual review criteria used in practice.

<h2 id="section-1">Basic Structure and Passing Threshold of the F-2 Points System</h2>

### Total Score and Passing Criteria

F-2-7 is a points-based system operated under the official notification of the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr).
Out of 120 total points, applicants need at least 80 to be eligible, and anyone scoring below 50 is automatically disqualified.
In practice, applicants who comfortably clear 90 points tend to fare better with reviewing officers' discretion than those who just barely cross the 80-point line.

The first thing to assess is which categories you're strong in and which are coming up short.
The typical strategy is to lay a foundation with education and Korean language, then build up with income and length of stay.

### Basic vs. Bonus Categories

Scores are broadly divided into **basic categories** and **bonus/deduction categories**.

| Type | Sub-Categories | Maximum Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | Age, education, Korean proficiency, income, length of stay | 100 points |
| Bonus | Community service, volunteering, KIIP completion, distinguished talent | 20 points |
| Deductions | Immigration law violations, tax delinquency, criminal penalties | Up to –20 points |

Bonus categories can lift your score in a single stroke, making them a critical lever for those falling short of 80 points.
In particular, completing the [Korea Immigration and Integration Program (KIIP)](https://www.socinet.go.kr) translates directly into points.

<h2 id="section-2">How Age and Education Points Are Calculated</h2>

### Age Category Scoring

Age is calculated based on your age at the time of application, with applicants in their early 30s receiving the highest scores.

| Age Range | Score |
| --- | --- |
| 18–24 | Relatively low |
| 25–29 | Mid-range |
| 30–34 | Highest bracket |
| 35–39 | Upper-mid range |
| 40–50 | Gradually decreasing |
| 51 and above | Lowest |

In actual reviews, applicants in their early 30s tend to rack up the most points once age combines with education and income.
Since detailed point allocations are adjusted year to year, please consult us directly to confirm the exact figure for your age bracket this year.

### Education Category Scoring

Education is evaluated based on your highest degree earned, with a distinction made between degrees obtained in Korea and abroad.

- Doctorate holders: Highest score
- Master's degree holders: Upper tier
- Bachelor's degree holders: Mid tier
- Associate degree: Base score
- High school or below: No points

A separate bonus applies for degrees earned domestically.
**Earning your degree at a Korean university adds extra points**, which is a major weapon for former international students.
In fact, it's common for a master's degree from Korea to outscore a foreign doctorate in the total tally.

<h2 id="section-3">How Korean Language Proficiency Points Are Calculated</h2>

### TOPIK and KIIP Recognition Standards

Korean language proficiency is recognized through either [TOPIK](https://www.topik.go.kr) levels or KIIP stages.
The higher of the two scores applies, but the two cannot be combined.

| Standard | Equivalent Level | Score Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TOPIK Level 6 / KIIP Stage 5 completed | Highest | Maximum points |
| TOPIK Level 5 / KIIP Stage 5 not completed | Upper | Upper-tier points |
| TOPIK Level 4 / KIIP Stage 4 | Upper-mid | Upper-mid points |
| TOPIK Level 3 / KIIP Stage 3 | Mid | Base points |
| TOPIK Level 2 or below | Lower | Minimum points |

A common stumbling block in practice is whether KIIP Stage 5 has actually been completed.
Stopping at Stage 4 places your Korean language score one tier lower, resulting in a 5–10 point swing in your total.

### The Practical Value of Completing KIIP

Completing KIIP Stage 5 boosts both the Korean language score and the bonus category simultaneously.
Because a single completion lifts your score in two places, it should be the first card to play for any applicant short of 80 points.
That said, the wait time between applying for KIIP and being assigned a slot is long, so timing management is critical.

> **Practical tip:** In some cases, a provisional completion certificate may be accepted before the official KIIP Stage 5 certificate is issued. Whether this works for you depends on your specific situation.

<h2 id="section-4">How Income and Asset Points Are Calculated</h2>

### Annual Income Criteria

Income is calculated by adding up earned and business income from the 12 months preceding your application.

| Income Bracket | Score |
| --- | --- |
| 4× or more of prior-year GNI | Maximum points |
| 3× or more of prior-year GNI | Upper tier |
| 2× or more of prior-year GNI | Upper-mid tier |
| 1× or more of prior-year GNI | Base score |
| Below prior-year GNI | No points |

The GNI figure is updated annually based on releases from the [Bank of Korea](https://www.bok.or.kr), so we'll walk you through how this year's GNI maps to your income during the consultation.
In actual reviews, withholding tax receipts and income certificates are cross-checked against each other.

### Assets Do Not Carry Separate Points

A frequently overlooked point is that **assets are not directly converted into points**.
No matter how much real estate or financial assets you hold, if they don't translate into reported income, they won't show up on your score.
In fact, reported employment income carries far more weight than asset holdings.

<h2 id="section-5">How Length of Stay, Bonuses, and Deductions Are Calculated</h2>

### Length of Stay Points

The longer your legal stay in Korea, the higher your score.

| Length of Stay | Score |
| --- | --- |
| 5+ years | Baseline requirement met |
| 7+ years | Bonus added |
| 10+ years | Additional bonus |

Length of stay is calculated based on the actual number of days physically spent in Korea, per immigration records.
If you've taken frequent short trips abroad, your cumulative days will be reduced, so check your immigration history first via [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

### Bonus and Deduction Categories

Bonus categories can be decisive for applicants who fall below 80 points.

- Community service exceeding a set number of hours
- Distinguished talent recommendation (from universities or institutions)
- Korean government commendations
- Holding nationally recognized certifications

Deductions, on the other hand, can sink your score in a single blow.

- History of immigration law violations
- Tax delinquency
- Criminal penalties of fines or higher
- Engaging in activities outside your visa status

**A single deduction dropping an applicant from 80 to the low 70s is one of the most common rejection patterns we see in practice.**

> **Note:** If you have a past immigration law violation, you should verify any amnesty or your record before applying through the points system. The size of the deduction depends on the type of violation.

<h2 id="section-6">Strategies for Boosting Insufficient Scores</h2>

### Categories You Can Improve Quickly

When you're falling short of 80 points, there's a clear pecking order for what you can boost fastest.

1. Complete KIIP Stage 5 (Korean language score + bonus)
2. Take the TOPIK exam (moving up one level lifts your score)
3. Log volunteer hours
4. Strengthen income documentation (consolidate any unreported income)

Education takes too long to improve in the short term, and age is simply not adjustable.
Realistically, volunteer work and KIIP are your most practical options.

### Handling Deduction Factors

Deduction factors only heal with time.
Tax delinquency can be cleared immediately by paying what's owed, but criminal penalties only reset after a specified waiting period.
If you're weak here, even a strong overall score can stall in review.

<h2 id="section-7">F-2 Residence Visa Points System FAQ</h2>

**Q1. If I clear 80 points, am I guaranteed to pass?**
80 points is the eligibility threshold, not a passing line.
In actual reviews, applicants in the low 80s and those above 90 see meaningfully different outcomes.

**Q2. Can I apply without completing KIIP Stage 5?**
Yes, TOPIK scores can substitute for the Korean language requirement.
However, you won't qualify for the separate bonus points reserved for KIIP Stage 5 completers.

**Q3. Can spouses or children come along?**
Once the F-2-7 principal applicant is approved, their spouse and minor children can stay together on an F-3 dependent visa.
Eligibility is assessed alongside the principal applicant's income and housing stability.

**Q4. Do past minor traffic fines count as deductions?**
Minor traffic fines don't qualify as criminal penalties and therefore aren't subject to deductions.
However, criminal penalties of fines or higher do trigger deductions.

**Q5. Are there cases where I can apply without 5 years of stay?**
Priority categories such as advanced industries and specialized professions sometimes have the length-of-stay requirement relaxed.
Since this depends on your specific occupation and certifications held, a preliminary review is advised.

**Q6. How long does the points-based review take?**
Processing times vary by jurisdiction, and supplementary document requests can extend the timeline.
