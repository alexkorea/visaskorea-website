# Chinese-Korean F-4 Assault & Sexual Harassment Violation Review and Deportation Standards Explained

When a Chinese-Korean F-4 visa holder is booked on assault or sexual harassment charges, an immigration violation review (사범심사) is conducted separately from the criminal proceedings, and depending on the outcome, it can lead to status cancellation or even forced deportation.

This applies to ethnic Korean nationals of Chinese citizenship residing in Korea under the Overseas Korean (F-4) status. Whether the criminal outcome is a fine, suspended sentence, or actual prison term, an immigration violation review notice may follow.

This article walks through the differences in review standards between assault and sexual harassment cases, the factors behind status cancellation decisions, the dividing line between a departure order and forced deportation, and the points where applicants most often get stuck in practice.

<h2 id="section-1">When Does the F-4 Assault Violation Review Begin?</h2>

### Immigration Is Notified From the Criminal Booking Stage

Once you are booked by the police, you become subject to a violation review by that fact alone.

In practice, immigration is typically notified automatically once the case is referred to the prosecutor's office.

Even a summary indictment resulting in only a fine does not exempt you from notification.

People often relax thinking the criminal process is over, only to receive a separate immigration notice in the mail.

### Notification Timing and Appearance Request

After the criminal disposition is finalized, an appearance request from the immigration office is usually sent within 1 to 3 months.

The scheduled appearance date varies by jurisdiction, so you'll need to confirm the exact schedule with your local immigration office.

> **Caution:** Ignoring the appearance request or leaving the country on your own can directly trigger an entry ban.

### Which Offenses Are Subject to Violation Review?

- Simple assault, bodily injury
- Joint assault, special (aggravated) assault
- Indecent act by force, quasi-indecent act by force
- Public indecency
- Filming with cameras and similar devices

In particular, indecent act by force is classified as an offense with a high likelihood of status cancellation, even when penalized with only a fine.

<h2 id="section-2">F-4 Violation Review Standards for Assault Cases</h2>

### Where Simple Assault and Bodily Injury Diverge

Assault and bodily injury offenses lead to different levels of disposition.

In the actual review, reviewers first look at whether there was a settlement with the victim, prior criminal record, and whether alcohol was involved.

Even if you've reached a settlement with the victim, the violation review will not automatically close.

| Category | Criminal Disposition | Violation Review Tendency | Notes |
|------|----------|--------------|------|
| Simple assault (settled) | No right to prosecute | Warning or shortened stay period | When no prior record |
| Simple assault (fined) | Fine | Shortened stay to departure order | Case-by-case |
| Bodily injury (prison) | Imprisonment | Status cancellation, deportation possible | Suspended sentences reviewed too |
| Special assault | Aggravated punishment | High likelihood of status cancellation | When a weapon is involved |

### Joint Assault and Group Incidents

Joint assault carries a disposition one level higher than simple assault.

In practice, the key factors are the degree of participation, whether you led the act, and the extent of the victim's harm.

If a victim's medical certificate is attached, status cancellation review becomes more aggressive.

### Alcohol Involvement Aggravates the Case

A very high percentage of assault cases occur under the influence of alcohol.

When alcohol is involved, your written statement of reflection needs a separate, sincere explanation of your alcohol management.

If this explanation is weak, the case can move straight to a departure order.

<h2 id="section-3">F-4 Violation Review Standards for Sexual Harassment Cases</h2>

### Indecent Act by Force Is Dangerous Even With Just a Fine

Indecent act by force is treated as one of the most serious offenses under criminal law.

In practice, even a summary indictment with only a fine puts you on the list for status cancellation review.

Even when a settlement with the victim exists, the immigration decision moves on its own track, separate from the criminal disposition.

### Camera Filming, Public Indecency, and Other Sex Offenses

For violations of the Sexual Violence Punishment Act, reviewers also check whether you are subject to personal information registration.

If you become subject to such registration, the likelihood of status cancellation rises sharply.

| Offense | Relevant Law | Default Violation Review Direction |
|------|----------|-------------------|
| Indecent act by force | Criminal Act Article 298 | Status cancellation review prioritized |
| Quasi-indecent act by force | Criminal Act Article 299 | Status cancellation review prioritized |
| Public indecency | Criminal Act Article 245 | Mostly departure order |
| Camera filming | Sexual Violence Punishment Act Article 14 | High likelihood of status cancellation |
| Obscene act using communication media | Sexual Violence Punishment Act Article 13 | Departure order to status cancellation |

Exact statutory text can be checked at the [National Law Information Center](https://law.go.kr).

### What a Victim Settlement Actually Means

A victim settlement has a major impact on criminal sentencing.

In the violation review, however, the weight shifts more toward the nature of the offense and the risk of recidivism than the existence of a settlement.

If you only submit a settlement letter and your other supporting documents are weak, it's hard to block status cancellation.

> **Practice tip:** A settlement letter has real impact only when combined with documents proving family relationships, your established life in Korea, and efforts to prevent recidivism.

<h2 id="section-5">The Dividing Line Between Status Cancellation and Forced Deportation</h2>

### Grounds for Status Cancellation Under the Immigration Act

Articles 89 and 46 of the Immigration Act set out the grounds for forced deportation and status cancellation.

Assault and sexual harassment cases are generally reviewed under the categories of "acts inconsistent with one's status of stay" and "violation of public order."

The first thing to look at is whether your disposition is at the level of imprisonment or only a fine.

### Practical Differences Between Departure Order and Forced Deportation

| Disposition | Meaning | Impact on Re-entry | Visa Options |
|------|------|-------------|---------------------|
| Shortened stay period | Reduces remaining period | Minimal impact | Reapplication possible |
| Departure order | Order to leave voluntarily | Entry ban for a set period | Case-by-case |
| Forced deportation | Forced return | Entry ban 5+ years | Long-term restrictions |
| Status cancellation | F-4 status revoked | Difficult to regain same status | Must look at other statuses |

Entry ban periods can be checked via [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) or the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr), but actual application to your case must be confirmed with the relevant authority.

### If Status Cancellation Is Decided

F-4 status cancellation does not deny your ethnic Korean identity itself; it strips you of your right to reside.

Re-entry under H-2 (Work and Visit) may be possible afterward, but it depends on the case.

If this point is weak, you may end up separated from family for an extended period, which is why resolving the matter at the violation review stage is the most important thing.

<h2 id="section-6">Common Stumbling Blocks in Violation Review Response</h2>

### Written Statements Often Decide the Outcome

Your personal written statement is one of the most decisive documents in a violation review.

Rather than writing at length, focus first on concretely laying out the circumstances of the incident, your reflection, and your plan to prevent recurrence.

A frequently overlooked area is the explanation of your family relationships and livelihood in Korea.

### Composition of Supporting Documents

- Family relations certificate, proof that family members live in Korea
- Employment contract, certificate of employment, business registration
- Lease agreement, resident registration
- Victim settlement letter and receipt for settlement payment
- Volunteer work, donations, completed education programs, and other evidence of social contribution
- Psychiatric treatment or counseling records (related to alcohol or anger management)

Even with a lot of documents, the impact is weak if explanations connecting them to your specific situation are missing.

### Whether to Bring a Representative

The principle is that you appear in person and give your statement directly, but prior consultation with a representative such as a licensed administrative agent (행정사) often makes the difference in the outcome.

In particular, if you're not confident in your Korean for the statement, the facts can easily get tangled, so preparation in advance is essential.

> **Caution:** After you appear for the violation review, opportunities to submit additional supplementary statements are limited.

<h2 id="section-7">Why You Should Prepare From the Criminal Stage</h2>

### The Prosecutor's Disposition Determines the Violation Review

Suspended indictment and a fine lead to very different violation review outcomes.

When suspended indictment is granted, cases proceeding all the way to status cancellation become much less common.

At the prosecutor stage, submitting a thorough statement of reflection, settlement letter, and victim recovery documents makes the subsequent violation review much lighter.

### Running Criminal and Immigration Matters in Parallel

When the criminal defense lawyer and the immigration administrative agent work separately, the documents can fall out of alignment.

In practice, building the immigration response strategy together starting just before the criminal disposition has the biggest impact on the result.

### When You Have Prior Convictions

If you have prior convictions such as drunk driving or assault, the current case is judged more severely.

In a recent similar case, a single prior fine led all the way to status cancellation, so you should accurately confirm your prior record and then build the strategy.

You can check your criminal record by requesting a criminal record certificate through the [Korean National Police Agency on Government24](https://www.gov.kr).

<h2 id="section-8">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

### Q1. I was fined 500,000 KRW for assault — am I still subject to violation review?

Even a small fine doesn't exclude a booked case from violation review notification.

However, whether the case proceeds all the way to status cancellation depends on the facts and on whether a settlement was reached.

You need a quick assessment of how your case is classified as soon as you receive the notification.

### Q2. I received suspended indictment for indecent act by force — does my F-4 status remain?

Suspended indictment is on the lighter side of criminal dispositions, but the violation review proceeds separately.

Indecent act by force is classified as serious by its nature, and many such cases enter status cancellation review.

A close look at the specific facts of your case is the first step.

### Q3. If I settle with the victim, does the violation review close?

A settlement has a major impact on the criminal disposition, but it doesn't automatically close the violation review itself.

The settlement letter is submitted as one piece of supporting evidence, and the result depends on how the rest of your documents are organized.

### Q4. What happens if I leave the country before receiving the violation review notice?

Leaving on your own doesn't close the violation review — it can instead make you subject to an entry ban.

You may be turned back at the airport on your next entry attempt, so departure should be reviewed beforehand.

### Q5. If F-4 is cancelled, can I switch to another visa?

In some cases you can transition to another status such as H-2, but this depends on whether an entry ban applies.

The answer becomes clear only when your disposition and any entry ban period are considered together.

### Q6. Do I really have to appear at the violation review in person?

In principle, in-person appearance is required.

That said, submitting a prior written opinion through a representative or bringing an interpreter are options commonly used in practice.
