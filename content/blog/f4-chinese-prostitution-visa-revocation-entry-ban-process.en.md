# Korean-Chinese F-4 Holders Caught for Prostitution: Visa Cancellation, Entry Ban, and Immigration Review Practical Guide

When a Korean-Chinese F-4 visa holder is caught for prostitution, the matter does not end with a simple fine — it leads directly to overseas Korean status cancellation, deportation, and an entry ban.

A violation of the Act on the Punishment of Arrangement of Commercial Sex Acts is one of the most heavily weighted grounds in immigration office reviews, and administrative measures proceed independently of any criminal disposition.

This document covers the post-incident flow, immigration review criteria, the linkage between status cancellation, deportation, and entry bans, and key practical response points.

<h2 id="section-1">Core Flow of Korean-Chinese F-4 Prostitution Immigration Review</h2>

### Criminal and Administrative Dispositions Run Separately

A frequently overlooked point is that **criminal disposition and immigration administrative disposition proceed as entirely separate tracks**.

Even if the prosecutor's office issues a suspended indictment or a fine, immigration will not treat the matter as lightly.

In practice, it is common for the immigration review notice to arrive before the prosecutor's decision is finalized.

In fact, the lighter the criminal disposition, the more strongly immigration may play the status cancellation card.

### The First 72 Hours After Being Caught Are Critical

On the ground, the sequence after being caught is rapid: police investigation → immigration notification → summons for immigration review.

If your statements get tangled at this stage, no amount of material submitted later will easily reverse the situation.

> **Caution:** Your first statement is permanently recorded in the immigration review file. Even claims of "just tagging along" or "running an errand" can be reclassified as arrangement or habitual offense if they are inconsistent.

<h2 id="section-2">Grounds and Legal Basis for F-4 Overseas Korean Status Cancellation</h2>

### Simultaneous Application of the Overseas Korean Act and Immigration Control Act

F-4 status is granted under Article 5 of the Act on the Immigration and Legal Status of Overseas Koreans, but the moment grounds for status cancellation under Article 89 of the [Immigration Control Act](https://law.go.kr) arise, the status is immediately suspended or revoked.

Prostitution cases are also connected to the deportation grounds under Article 46 of the same Act.

### Typical Categories Leading to Status Cancellation

| Category | Immigration Review Assessment | Likely Outcome |
|------|---------------------|-------------|
| Simple prostitution (buyer) | First offense, amount, frequency reviewed | Possible status cancellation |
| Arranging prostitution / providing premises | Treated as aggravating factor | Status cancellation / deportation |
| Working at a prostitution establishment | Habitual offense presumed | Status cancellation / long-term entry ban |
| Fleeing or false statements when caught | Decisive blow to credibility | Extended entry ban period |

Although these all look like "prostitution" on the surface, **simple buying versus arrangement or working at an establishment produces very different outcomes.**

In particular, if evidence of working at an establishment emerges, restoring F-4 status becomes nearly impossible.

<h2 id="section-3">Criteria for Deportation and Entry Ban Duration</h2>

### Entry Bans Are Not Uniform

According to [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) notices and internal immigration processing standards, entry ban periods in prostitution cases are applied differentially case by case.

The first things examined are your written statement, the report on circumstances of arrest, the number of people caught together, the amount involved, and whether you attempted to flee.

In actual reviews, even for the same simple buying offense, the entry ban duration varies significantly depending on the attitude shown in your statement.

> **Practical tip:** Entry ban duration is determined more by **whether habitual offending is recognized** than by the number of times caught. Repeat visits to the same location, mobile phone messages, and bank transfer records become decisive evidence.

### Difference Between Exit Order and Deportation

| Category | Exit Order | Deportation |
|------|----------|----------|
| Voluntary departure | Voluntary departure format | Forced repatriation |
| Entry ban record | Relatively shorter | Long-term imposed |
| Possibility of re-entry | Possible upon amnesty/lifting | Highly restricted |
| Use in immigration review | Statement / remorse can be negotiated | Limited effect of materials |

In practice, **the first task is determining whether the case can be wrapped up with an exit order.**

If this angle is weak, the case moves straight to deportation.

<h2 id="section-4">Common Pitfalls and Real Case Patterns</h2>

### Reversing Your Statement Is the Most Fatal Mistake

In practice, it is common to say "I just went along without knowing" in the first police interview, only to switch to "I knew it was an establishment" at the immigration review.

This single reversal weighs the most heavily in the credibility assessment.

### When Chat Logs and Transfer Records Are Also Seized, It Is Not Treated as a Simple Purchase

In recent similar cases, defendants claimed a one-off purchase, but **were classified as habitual offenders** because KakaoTalk and Chinese messenger conversations along with repeated transfer records were secured alongside.

This is where outcomes diverge.

Even for a single arrest, when digital evidence is attached, the disposition outcome changes completely.

### Additional Scrutiny of Overseas Korean Status

Unlike H-2, F-4 is residence based on overseas Korean status, so when status is cancelled, the inquiry sometimes expands into doubts about the overseas Korean status itself.

In particular, the pattern of family relationship documents and past entry/exit records being re-examined together is frequently seen in actual reviews.

<h2 id="section-5">What Practitioners Look at in Immigration Review Response</h2>

### Organizing the Facts Matters More Than Documents

Rather than writing at length, a statement organized line by line as **circumstances of the incident → your level of awareness → actions right after being caught → remorse materials** is more effective.

Even with many documents, if the factual flow is weak, contradictions will surface within them.

### Where Letters of Remorse, Settlements, and Donation Records Fit In

In prostitution immigration reviews, unlike criminal cases, settlements themselves are difficult.

Instead, the following materials are frequently used as mitigating evidence in actual reviews.

- A sincere letter of remorse
- Family relationship certificates and proof of dependents
- Records of settlement and employment history in Korea
- A pledge against re-offending and proof of completed education
- Medical and psychological counseling records (if applicable)

> **Caution:** Submitting a templated letter of remorse as is actually loses you points on sincerity. It must be written based on the facts of your own case.

### Items You Must Check Before Appearing at the Immigration Review

| Check Item | Verification Point |
|-----------|-------------|
| Police statement record | Consistency with first statement |
| Communication / transfer records | Whether any can be interpreted as habitual conduct |
| Residency history | Past violations or warnings |
| Family / livelihood proof | Whether dependents reside in Korea |
| Availability for appearance | Non-appearance immediately works against you |

Non-appearance is effectively the fastest route to the harshest disposition.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. If the case ends with just a fine, can F-4 be maintained?**

No.

Even with a fine, immigration determines status cancellation separately.

The criminal outcome is only a reference; the immigration review conclusion determines your residence status.

**Q2. If I leave voluntarily, can I re-enter without an entry ban?**

Voluntary departure itself does not exempt you from an entry ban.

However, if the matter is settled in the form of an exit order, the entry ban period may be shorter compared to deportation.

This varies by case, so checking the notice comes first.

**Q3. Can my status still be cancelled even if my family is in Korea?**

Yes, it is possible.

Family ties are considered as a mitigating factor, but where there are signs of arrangement or working at an establishment, status cannot easily be maintained on family grounds alone.

**Q4. Does self-reporting after being caught help my case?**

It depends on the situation, but self-reporting after police charges have already been filed has limited effect.

Conversely, organizing materials before charges are filed and converting to voluntary departure can change the outcome in some cases.

This judgment is hard to make alone.

**Q5. Is an entry ban permanent?**

It is not permanent in principle.

However, if classified as arrangement or habitual conduct, it is imposed long-term, and while a lifting application is possible after a certain period, it is not automatic.

**Q6. If F-4 is cancelled, can I switch to another visa?**

Where status is cancelled on prostitution grounds, switching to another residence status such as H-2 at the same time is effectively impossible.

Departure, a waiting period, and lifting of the entry ban come first.
