# E-7 Visa Education and Work Experience Requirements: Complete Guide — Key Points That Decide Approval

The E-7 visa technically allows you to apply by meeting either the education or work experience requirement, but in actual review, what really determines approval is how closely both align with the job duties.

This visa targets foreign professionals, and out of 85 occupational categories designated by the Ministry of Justice, your education and experience must directly connect to the work you'll be performing.

This guide covers what counts as qualifying education for the E-7, how work experience is calculated, the points where applications commonly stall in practice, and strategies to strengthen weak spots — all in one place.

<h2 id="section-1">How Education Requirements Are Actually Applied for the E-7 Visa</h2>

### What Counts: Bachelor's, Master's, and Doctoral Degrees

For the E-7 visa, your degree must be **in the same field as the job duties you'll be performing** to be recognized.

Bachelor's degree holders typically also need at least one year of relevant field experience, while those with a master's degree or higher can sometimes apply without any work experience.

This is where complications begin.

You can't judge eligibility by the degree title alone — in practice, reviewers also look at **which courses you completed on your transcript**.

For instance, even with a business degree, applying for an IT role often gets flagged as a major mismatch.

### Degree Field and Job Relevance

The most commonly missed point is that degree field codes and occupational codes don't always align.

Each occupational category designated by the Ministry of Justice and [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) has its own list of recognized majors.

If your major doesn't match the job duties, your bachelor's degree alone won't satisfy the education requirement.

When this area is weak, you'll need to compensate with work experience. Since these supplementary criteria are partially adjusted each year, confirm this year's standards through a consultation.

### Recognition Procedures for Overseas Degrees

Degrees obtained abroad must go through a degree verification process.

Chinese degrees require CHSI (China Higher-education Student Information) verification, while degrees from other countries need procedures through [Government24](https://www.gov.kr) or the relevant country's notarial authorities.

Even with extensive paperwork, a single missed step in the verification stage can derail the entire application.

<h2 id="section-2">How Work Experience Is Calculated for the E-7 Visa</h2>

### Counting Recognized Experience Periods

In principle, only **full-time work experience accumulated after obtaining your degree** is recognized.

The standard baselines are: bachelor's degree plus 1 year, or 5+ years of experience in the same field with no degree.

| Education Level | Required Experience | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Doctorate | None required (if job-aligned) | Major must match |
| Master's | None required, or 1 year | Job field must match |
| Bachelor's | 1+ year in same field | Job matching essential |
| No degree | 5+ years in same field | Limited by occupation |

Internships, part-time work, and experience in unrelated fields are generally not counted.

### What Must Be Included on Your Employment Certificate

If your employment certificate is incomplete, your work experience itself can be rejected.

Missing any of the following typically causes problems at this stage.

- Company name and business registration number (or equivalent identifier)
- Exact start and end dates of employment
- Job title and specific duties performed
- Clear indication of full-time status
- Issuer's official seal and contact information

Rather than writing lengthy descriptions, what matters most is **clearly showing how your duties align with the occupational category you're applying under**.

### Handling Employment Gaps and Non-Standard Experience

When there are gaps in your work history or your record includes freelance work or entrepreneurship, reviewers tend to scrutinize more closely.

Insufficient explanation here often results in experience not being recognized as full-time.

The better approach is to back up the gaps with objective documentation (tax filings, contracts, etc.) — this measurably improves approval rates.

<h2 id="section-3">Strategies to Strengthen Weak Education or Work Experience</h2>

### Compensating for Education Gaps

When your education doesn't align with the job, you can supplement with **additional certifications, internal training records, or relevant project experience**.

That said, the weight given to certifications varies by type, and some occupations only count nationally accredited certifications as bonus criteria.

You'll need to check the occupation-specific guidelines to determine whether your certifications qualify as bonus factors.

### Workarounds for Insufficient Experience

If your experience falls short by a few months of the one-year mark, examine whether internship and full-time periods at the same company can be combined.

Conditional recognition is possible in some cases, and there have been recent approvals in similar situations.

Whether this applies to your specific situation, however, can only be determined by reviewing your actual documents.

> **Practical tip:** When your education is weak, prepare thorough work experience documentation; when your experience is weak, build out education and certification documentation. There's a real tendency for evaluators to be more lenient on one side when the other side is rock solid.

<h2 id="section-4">How Education and Experience Requirements Differ by Occupation</h2>

### General Professionals vs. Skilled Workers

Even within the E-7 category, general professionals (E-7-1) and skilled workers (E-7-4) have different education and experience requirements.

| Category | Education | Experience | Special Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-7-1 General Professional | Bachelor's recommended | 1+ year in field | Limited to 85 occupations |
| E-7-3 General Skilled | High school + certification | 5 years in same field | Occupation-restricted |
| E-7-4 Skilled Worker | Separate point system | E-9 etc. residence history | Cumulative points |

E-7-4 operates on a points system where **domestic residence history and Korean language ability** carry more weight than education.

### Verifying Your Occupational Code

The first thing to check is which occupational code your job duties fall under.

The [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr) publishes the education and experience standards for each occupation, and these are partially revised each year.

If your occupational code is misclassified, you can be denied even with sufficient education and experience.

<h2 id="section-5">Common Sticking Points in Actual Reviews</h2>

While the process may look straightforward on the surface, education and experience documents frequently get caught at these stages.

- Mismatch between degree field and occupational code
- Vague descriptions of job duties on employment certificates
- Missing apostille or consular authentication for overseas education
- Failure to specify full-time status
- Inconsistent employment dates across submitted documents

Even with extensive paperwork, weakness in any one of these areas triggers a request for supplementary documents — and slow responses to those requests can result in denial.

<h2 id="section-6">E-7 Visa Education and Experience Document Checklist</h2>

Before applying, check whether you have the following ready.

- Original degree certificate + apostille (or consular authentication)
- Transcript + notarized English translation
- Degree verification certificate (for overseas degrees)
- Employment certificate (with full-time status and duties specified)
- 4 major insurances enrollment history or equivalent records
- Copies of certifications (where applicable)

Here's the core point.

You need to be able to explain in one sentence how each document connects to the job duties.

Without that clear connection, even a thick stack of documents won't carry weight in evaluation.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

### Q1. Can I apply for an E-7 if my major doesn't match the job?

It's possible, but you'll typically need to compensate with additional work experience or certifications. Some occupations strictly require a major match, so case-by-case review comes first.

### Q2. Do graduates from Korean universities also need work experience?

Master's degree holders and above can often apply without experience, while bachelor's degree holders may need 1+ year of experience depending on the occupation. Even with a domestic degree, job alignment is reviewed separately.

### Q3. Is freelance experience recognized?

In principle, only experience based on full-time employment contracts is recognized. However, when activities equivalent to full-time work can be proven through tax filings, contracts, and similar evidence, partial recognition has occurred in some cases.

### Q4. Where do I get my degree verified?

Overseas degrees require an apostille from the issuing country or Korean consular authentication. Chinese degrees typically require additional CHSI verification.

### Q5. What if my employment certificate isn't in English?

It needs to go through notarized translation. Simple translations are usually not accepted — only with a notarized translation included will full-time status and job duties be recognized.

### Q6. Can I be denied even with sufficient education and experience?

Yes — denials can occur for other reasons such as occupational code matching, wage requirements, or the company's eligibility as an employer. Education and experience are only the starting point; the employer's qualifications are reviewed alongside.
