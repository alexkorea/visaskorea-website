# Complete Guide to E-7-1 Visa Requirements and Application Process (2026)

The E-7-1 visa only passes when three pillars — education, work experience, and employment contract — line up cleanly with each other.

If your major doesn't match the job, your career documentation is thin, or the hiring company's requirements are weak, the application gets blocked at that exact point.

This article walks through the real screening criteria for E-7-1 visa requirements, occupation matching, document preparation, the application procedure, and the common bottlenecks — all from a practitioner's perspective.

<h2 id="section-1">What Is the E-7-1 Visa and Who Is It For?</h2>

The E-7-1 is a Specially Designated Activities visa that permits foreign nationals to be employed in roughly 85 specialized occupations designated by the Minister of Justice.

It targets professionals in technology, management, administration, and research. General office work — basic clerical tasks or sales support — does not qualify.

The legal basis can be found in [Attached Table 1 of the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act](https://www.law.go.kr) and the guidance published on [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

### Categories of E-7-1 Eligible Occupations

E-7-1 occupations are broadly divided into managers, professionals and related workers, and clerical workers, and each occupation carries its own education and experience requirements.

For example, IT developers (2620) and designers (2854) are both in the professional group, yet the scope of recognized work experience differs between them.

The first thing to check is exactly which occupation code your specific role maps to.

### Differences Between General E-7, E-7-1, E-7-3, and E-7-4

Within E-7, sub-categories 1, 3, and 4 are all different, with different eligibility criteria and different rules on whether a points-based system applies.

E-7-1 covers professional personnel, E-7-3 covers semi-skilled workers (points-based), and E-7-4 is a separate points-based track for skilled workers.

In practice, the first thing that trips applicants up is misidentifying which track they actually belong to.

<h2 id="section-2">The Three Core Requirements of the E-7-1 Visa</h2>

E-7-1 visa requirements break down along three axes: education, work experience, and the inviting company.

If any of these three is out of alignment with the others, no amount of paperwork will keep the case from being put on hold.

### Education Requirement

The default standard is a bachelor's degree or higher in a field directly related to the occupation.

A master's degree or higher may open the door to a waiver of the experience requirement, but a bachelor's degree typically still needs to be paired with at least one year of relevant work experience.

If your major differs from the job, education alone isn't enough — you have to make up the gap with experience, and this is where outcomes start to diverge.

### Work Experience Requirement

If you have at least five years of work experience in the same or a closely related field, part of the education requirement can be substituted.

This is where the problems start. If your certificate of employment doesn't spell out the specific duties, employment period, and type of employment, it's hard to get it accredited.

In particular, employment certificates from your home country company often require an apostille or consular authentication.

### Inviting Company Requirements

The number of Korean nationals employed, revenue size, years in business, and whether the business is being operated normally are all part of the review.

On the surface it looks like just having a business registration is enough, but in practice cases get hung up on weak revenue, Korean employees who aren't enrolled in the four mandatory insurances, and exceeding the cap on foreign employees within the same industry.

If this side is weak, it doesn't matter how strong your personal credentials are — the company side will block the application.

<h2 id="section-3">Occupation Code Matching in Practice</h2>

Occupation code matching is not a matter of "close enough."

### Where the Job Description Makes or Breaks the Case

What matters with the job description isn't length — it's how precisely it overlaps with the standard job description for the occupation code.

If anything, writing something vague like "general marketing duties" can get the role classified as sales support and rejected.

### Major and Department Matching

If your department name differs from the occupation, you'll need to show your coursework with a transcript, not just a graduation certificate.

The most commonly missed step is failing to attach a separate supplementary explanation when the weight of your major coursework doesn't align with the occupation.

This year's occupation-specific criteria are updated annually in the Ministry of Justice's [Foreign Residence Guide](https://www.immigration.go.kr) manual, so confirm the exact criteria applicable to your occupation through a consultation.

<h2 id="section-4">Required Documents and Commonly Missed Items</h2>

| Category | Documents | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| Applicant | Visa application form, passport copy, photo | Standard format |
| Education | Degree certificate, transcript | Apostille or consular authentication |
| Experience | Certificate of employment | Specify duties, period, employment type |
| Employment | Employment contract | Wages, duties, working conditions |
| Company | Business registration, tax clearance, list of four-insurance enrollees | Establish company credibility |
| Job | Job description, organizational chart | Must match the occupation code |

> **Practical tip:** A certificate of employment must include the home country company's official seal, signature, and contact information — a one-line "this person was employed here" is not enough to get the experience recognized.

> **Caution:** Documents issued in your home country require either an apostille or consular authentication at the Korean diplomatic mission. The procedure differs depending on whether your country is a treaty member, so check with the relevant authority.

<h2 id="section-5">E-7-1 Visa Application Procedure</h2>

| Step | Content | Handled By |
|------|------|----------|
| Step 1 | Apply for Certificate of Visa Issuance (COA) | Domestic Immigration Office |
| Step 2 | COA forwarded to Korean mission in home country | Immigration Office → Mission |
| Step 3 | Apply for visa in home country | Applicant |
| Step 4 | Foreigner registration within 90 days of entry | Local Immigration Office |

### The Certificate of Visa Issuance (COA) Stage

This is the stage where most applications get stuck.

Because the company files as the applicant and both the candidate's qualifications and the company's requirements are screened at the same time, submitting with only one side prepared leads to repeated requests for additional documents.

Processing times vary by immigration office, and filing with the office that can move fastest is one way to shorten the timeline.

### Visa Issuance and Post-Entry Procedures

Once the COA is issued, you receive the visa at the Korean mission in your home country and must complete foreigner registration within 90 days of entering Korea.

From that point on, whether you're enrolled in the four mandatory insurances and whether your first payslip matches what was reported becomes a verification point again during later extension or status-change reviews.

<h2 id="section-6">Common Reasons for Denial and How to Respond</h2>

| Reason for Denial | Common Scenario | Core Response |
|----------|------------------|----------|
| Job mismatch | Role doesn't match major or experience | Strengthen job description and training records |
| Company requirements not met | Insufficient revenue or Korean hires | Provide business plan and revenue trend data |
| Insufficient proof of experience | Only a basic employment certificate submitted | Detailed proof of duties and period |
| Wages below threshold | Contract below minimum standard | Recalculate against occupation-specific wage guide |

### Wage Standards Will Put a Case on Hold Fast

E-7-1 applies separate minimum wage standards by occupation, and they're updated every year.

The specific threshold amounts change annually, so check the exact figures applicable this year through a consultation.

### When the Company Side Is Weak

For newly formed entities or companies with low revenue, business registration alone is not enough.

If the explanation here is thin, the application gets held up or denied on company-side grounds. In a recent similar case, reinforcing the business plan and showing the flow of transactions with clients was enough to get the application through on supplementary review.

<h2 id="section-7">E-7-1 Visa and Family Accompaniment</h2>

E-7-1 holders can invite their spouse and minor children on the F-3 dependent visa.

### F-3 Dependent Visa Requirements

Your E-7-1 status must be valid, and you'll need family relationship documents plus apostilles on the home-country-issued documents.

The most commonly missed step is failing to get certified Korean translations of the home country marriage and birth certificates.

### Scope of Activities Allowed Under Dependent Status

As a rule, F-3 holders are restricted from working and need separate permission for activities outside their status of stay.

This piece depends on the E-7-1 holder's own residence status, so confirm in advance.

<h2 id="section-9">FAQ</h2>

**Q1. Can I apply for the E-7-1 visa without a bachelor's degree?**

Without a bachelor's degree, you'll need at least five years of recognized experience in the same field, and the supporting documentation must clearly demonstrate your duties, the period of employment, and the type of employment.

**Q2. Can I apply if my major and job duties are different?**

It's possible, but you have to make up for the gap through experience outside your major that demonstrates relevance to the role. The job description and training records are what carry the case.

**Q3. Can a newly formed company hire an E-7-1 foreign national?**

If the company is new, you'll need to reinforce documentation around revenue, business plan, and Korean employment. Business registration alone won't be enough.

**Q4. Is it possible to switch from E-7-1 to E-7-4?**

It is, if you meet the points-based criteria. However, length of residence, income, education, and Korean language ability are all evaluated together, so you should run a preliminary points calculation.

**Q5. After a denial, when can I reapply?**

As soon as you've prepared documents that address the reasons for denial. That said, refiling with the same documentation will get blocked for the same reason.

**Q6. Does my home-country employment certificate have to be in English?**

Documents issued in your native language need a Korean translation, notarization of the translation, and either an apostille or consular authentication.
