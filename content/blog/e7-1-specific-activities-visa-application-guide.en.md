# E-7-1 Visa Application Requirements and Process: Complete Guide (Specially Designated Activities Visa)

The E-7-1 is a professional-workforce visa that gets approved when your education, work history, and job duties line up cleanly in a single chain.

You first need to pinpoint exactly which of the 86 eligible occupations your intended role falls under, and unlike other work visas, the reviewers also look at the size of the hiring company and the salary level together.

This guide walks you through the classification of the 86 occupations, the education and experience requirements, the company-side requirements, the application process, and the points where applications most often get stuck.

<h2 id="section-1">What Is the E-7-1 Visa?</h2>

The E-7-1 corresponds to 86 professional-workforce occupations under the Specially Designated Activities (E-7) status defined in [Attached Table 1-2] of the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act.

What distinguishes it from E-7-2 (semi-professional), E-7-3 (general skilled labor), and E-7-4 (skilled labor) is that it requires **a bachelor's degree or higher, or equivalent work experience**.

The 86 occupation codes (2030–2891) announced by the Ministry of Justice through [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) are directly tied to the Korean Standard Classification of Occupations.

### How It Differs From Other E-7 Sub-Categories

| Category | Target Occupations | Education Requirement | Salary Requirement |
|------|----------|----------|----------|
| E-7-1 | 86 professional occupations | Bachelor's or higher, or 5+ years experience | 80%+ of prior-year per-capita GNI |
| E-7-2 | Semi-professional workforce | Associate degree or experience | Separate criteria |
| E-7-3 | General skilled labor | Certification/experience-based | Separate criteria |
| E-7-4 | Skilled labor (points-based) | Meeting required point threshold | Separate criteria |

This is where the real difference shows up.

For the E-7-1, the **alignment** between your degree field and your job duties is the very first thing reviewed.

### Broad Categories Within the 86 Occupations

At a high level, they break down into the following five groups:

- Managers (executives, business support, marketing, advertising, PR, etc.)
- Professionals (engineering, IT, medical, education, design, etc.)
- Clerical workers (specialized-language translation/interpretation, overseas sales, etc.)
- Selected service workers
- Selected sales, culture, arts, and sports roles

The education and experience requirements for each occupation are detailed in our [E-7-1 Occupation-by-Occupation Education and Experience Requirements Guide](/blog/e7-visa-occupation-academic-career-requirements).

In practice, the part applicants most often miss is choosing the right occupation code.

The job the company wants you to do has to line up neatly with the description of the occupation code, and if you pick the wrong code, you'll get blocked immediately — even with sufficient education and experience.

<h2 id="section-2">E-7-1 Visa Application Requirements (Applicant-Side)</h2>

The first thing to look at is the applicant's own qualifications.

### Education and Experience Requirements

The basic rule is one of the following two:

- **A bachelor's degree or higher in a field related to the target occupation, plus 1+ years of relevant experience**
- **5+ years of experience in a field related to the target occupation**

The key word here is "related."

In actual review, immigration looks at how directly your degree's stated major connects to the occupation description.

For example, a business administration graduate applying for an overseas sales role will usually have relevance recognized easily, but the same graduate applying for an IT system developer role will run into trouble immediately unless they can show separate IT experience.

> **Note:** For master's degree holders, the 1-year experience requirement is waived for some occupations and required for others. Check the table in our [E-7 Occupation-Specific Education and Experience Requirements](/blog/e7-visa-academic-experience) article to see which group your occupation falls into.

### Salary Requirement

The baseline rule is **80% or more** of Korea's prior-year per-capita Gross National Income (GNI).

That said, some occupation codes require 100% or more, and the threshold can also vary based on the applicant's age and experience.

The exact salary line that applies this year changes annually, so we'll confirm the current figure for your specific occupation during your free consultation.

### License and Certification Requirements

Occupations that legally require a Korean license to practice — doctors, lawyers, professional engineers, and so on — require that license as a precondition.

Applications submitted without the required license get rejected at the document review stage.

<h2 id="section-3">E-7-1 Visa Application Requirements (Company-Side)</h2>

E-7 applications get blocked more often at the **company-qualification stage** than on the applicant's own qualifications.

### Company Size Requirement

There's a cap on the ratio of foreign hires relative to domestic employees.

As a rule, you can hire E-7 foreign workers up to **20% of the Korean-citizen workforce**.

For workplaces with 5 or fewer Korean employees, the cap is effectively 1 foreign worker.

### Company Revenue and Financial Requirements

Immigration assesses whether the company can actually pay the foreign worker's salary.

They review revenue, operating profit, the number of people enrolled in the four major insurance programs, and whether the business location actually exists.

In practice, newly established corporations or companies with minimal revenue often get rejected even when the foreign applicant has identical education and experience to a successful case.

### Occupations Requiring an Employment Recommendation Letter

Some of the 86 occupations require an employment recommendation letter from the relevant ministry.

| Occupation Group | Recommending Agency |
|--------|----------|
| IT, electronics, ICT | [Ministry of Science and ICT](https://www.msit.go.kr) |
| Industry and technology (general) | [Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy](https://www.motie.go.kr) |
| Design | Korea Institute of Design Promotion |
| Aviation and shipping | Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport / Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries |

> **Practical tip:** You can still apply without a recommendation letter, but having one acts as a positive factor during review. Start by checking whether your company's industry and your job role fall within the scope eligible for a recommendation.

### Recently Changed Items

The company size requirements and salary thresholds are partially adjusted each year.

In particular, IT and R&D occupations sometimes get temporary easing, so check with the relevant authority to confirm the latest rules that apply to your specific company and occupation.

<h2 id="section-4">E-7-1 Visa Application Process</h2>

There are two main paths.

One is entering from abroad with a Certificate of Visa Issuance, and the other is changing status from another visa type to E-7 from within Korea.

### Path 1: Certificate of Visa Issuance (CoA) → Visa Issuance → Entry

| Step | Description | Party Responsible |
|------|------|----------|
| Step 1 | Company applies for the Certificate of Visa Issuance at the local immigration office | Korean company |
| Step 2 | Certificate issued (typically 2–4 weeks) | Immigration Office |
| Step 3 | Visa issued by the Korean embassy in the applicant's home country using the certificate number | Foreign applicant |
| Step 4 | Alien registration within 90 days of entry | Foreign applicant |

### Path 2: Status Change Within Korea (from D-10, D-2, etc. to E-7-1)

| Step | Description |
|------|------|
| Step 1 | Sign an employment contract with the company |
| Step 2 | Obtain an employment recommendation letter (if applicable to the occupation) |
| Step 3 | File a status-change permit application with the local immigration office |
| Step 4 | Review and notification of result |

If you're converting from D-10 to E-7-1, we've broken down each step in [D-10 to E-7 Conversion Process](/blog/d10-to-e7-conversion).

Processing times vary by immigration office, and we'll route your case to the fastest one.

### Required Documents (Foreign Applicant)

- Passport copy and photo
- Degree certificate and graduation certificate (apostilled or consular-certified)
- Employment certificate (issued by prior employer, with job duties specified)
- Copies of relevant licenses or certifications (where applicable)
- Certificate of Visa Issuance application or status-change application form

### Required Documents (Company)

- Business registration certificate
- Corporate registry extract
- Employment contract
- Tax payment certificate, four-major-insurance subscriber list
- Company introduction, statement of reasons for hiring a foreign worker
- Employment recommendation letter (if applicable)

> **Note:** An employment certificate that simply says "worked here" is not enough. It must specify the position held, the duties performed, and the duration, and these need to line up cleanly with the occupation code description. If this explanation is weak, even a bachelor's degree won't save the application.

<h2 id="section-5">Where E-7-1 Applications Most Often Get Stuck in Review</h2>

Even with a thick stack of documents, what decides approval lies elsewhere.

### Mismatch Between Duties and Education

This is when the bachelor's-degree major and the job the company wants the applicant to do don't connect.

For example, the major is English literature but the company wants to hire the applicant as an IT developer.

If you can't establish job-duty alignment through separate experience or certifications, the application gets rejected outright.

### Insufficient Company Scale

This covers cases where there are too few Korean employees, or the revenue base looks too thin to support the foreign worker's salary.

When a newly established corporation tries to hire its first foreign worker through E-7-1, this is typically where it gets stuck.

### Salary Below Threshold

If the contract salary falls below the GNI baseline, the application is rejected even when every other requirement is met.

Whether you can include allowances and benefits in the salary calculation depends on the specific case.

### Wrong Occupation Code Selected

This is when the work the applicant intends to do doesn't actually fit cleanly into any of the 86 occupations and gets force-fit into a code anyway.

If the reviewer judges the occupation to be a poor fit, the applicant has to refile under a different code.

For strategies on refiling after a rejection, see [E-7 Visa Refusal Grounds and Refiling Strategy](/blog/e7-visa-refusal-grounds-and-retry-plan).

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. Can I apply for the E-7-1 in a field different from my major?**

A. It depends on the case. Even if the field is unrelated to your major, you can apply if you can document 5+ years of experience in that field. That said, the employment certificate needs to lay out your work duties in a way that connects to the occupation code description.

**Q2. Is the E-7-1 difficult if the company is too small?**

A. The size of the company itself matters less than the **ratio of foreign to domestic employees** and the **ability to pay the salary**. If there are fewer than 5 Korean employees, the cap is 1 foreign worker. Newly established corporations with low revenue typically get stuck at this step.

**Q3. If I have a master's degree, do I need any experience?**

A. It depends on the occupation. Some occupations waive the experience requirement for master's holders and above, but not all do. You need to confirm how the rule applies to your specific occupation code.

**Q4. Can I enter Korea on a D-10 visa and then switch to the E-7-1?**

A. Yes. If you find a company while on the D-10 job-seeker visa, you can move to E-7-1 through the status-change permit process. The company and the job duties do need to match your education and experience.

**Q5. What happens if my salary is just slightly below the 80% GNI threshold?**

A. In principle, it's grounds for rejection. That said, depending on the occupation, you can sometimes include allowances, incentives, or housing support in the salary calculation. Whether that approach is available in your case is something we'll confirm during consultation.

**Q6. If I get the E-7-1, can I bring my family with me?**

A. Spouses and minor children can reside in Korea on the F-3 accompanying visa. For the F-3 application process, see our [F-3 Accompanying Person Visa Guide](/blog/f3-accompanying-person-visa-korea-guide).
