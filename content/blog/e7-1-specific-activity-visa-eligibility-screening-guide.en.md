# E-7-1 Visa Requirements Complete Guide — Core Review Criteria for Specially Designated Activities

The E-7-1 visa requires three things to line up at the same time: **occupation code, education and experience, and employer qualifications**.

It targets foreign nationals seeking to work in one of roughly 85 specialized occupations designated by the Ministry of Justice, and applicants must meet one of the following: a bachelor's degree plus 1 year of experience, a master's degree or higher, or 5+ years of experience in the same field.

This guide walks through the points where applications most often stall in real reviews — occupation code matching, employer eligibility, and the document workflow — from a practitioner's perspective.

<h2 id="section-1">What Is the E-7-1 Visa and Who Is It For?</h2>

E-7-1 is a residence status created under the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act for the purpose of **attracting skilled professionals**.

It does not cover private academy instructors or basic administrative support; the work must fall within an occupation explicitly listed in the classification table published by the Ministry of Justice.

The key point is this.

The work you intend to do must fit a specific occupation code, and you must precisely satisfy the education and experience requirements that code calls for.

### How E-7-1 Differs from the Other E-7 Categories

E-7 is divided into 1, 3, and 4.

| Category | Target | Education / Experience Standard |
|------|------|----------------|
| E-7-1 | Specialized personnel (managers / professionals) | Bachelor's + 1 yr / Master's or higher / 5 yrs experience |
| E-7-3 | Skilled technical personnel | Separate standards per occupation |
| E-7-4 | Points-based skilled workers | Must meet the points table |

In practice, it's often unclear whether a given role belongs under E-7-1 or E-7-3.

If your case is weak on this point, it will be rejected right at the application stage.

### Who Typically Applies?

The most common cases are IT developers, designers, trade/commerce staff, overseas sales, and planning or marketing roles.

The main applicants tend to be foreign nationals hired by Korean companies — both new hires and experienced workers — who hold a bachelor's degree or higher.

If the occupation is ambiguous, your first step should be to check the occupation classification table at [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

<h2 id="section-2">E-7-1 Visa Requirements — Matching Occupation Code with Education and Experience</h2>

The first thing to look at is the occupation code.

You need to pinpoint exactly which of the roughly 85 occupations your work falls under.

### How Education and Experience Requirements Are Actually Applied

The Ministry of Justice sets three baseline tracks.

- Bachelor's degree in a related field + 1+ year of experience in the relevant role
- Master's degree or higher in a related field (experience may be waived)
- 5+ years of experience in the same occupation (in some cases acceptable even without a degree)

The phrase **"related field"** is the critical part.

For example, if you intend to do IT development work but your degree is in a humanities subject, you'll need to compensate through experience or prove your qualifications through certifications.

This is where outcomes really diverge in actual reviews.

When the degree and the job don't line up and the supporting logic is weak, this is usually where applications get stuck.

### Employer (Sponsoring Company) Requirements

The company also has to meet eligibility standards.

| Item | Standard |
|------|------|
| Korean employment ratio | Principle of 5 Korean nationals per 1 foreign hire |
| Revenue / capital | Separate standards per occupation |
| Wage level | Must meet prior-year Gross National Income (GNI) standard |
| Business status | Actively operating, no overdue taxes |

> **Note:** The GNI-based wage standard changes every year. Confirm this year's exact threshold during a consultation.

Small, newly established corporations are often rejected because they don't have enough Korean employees.

There are alternative ways to demonstrate eligibility in such cases, but whether they apply depends on the company's industry and scale.

<h2 id="section-3">E-7-1 Visa Application Process Step by Step</h2>

It looks simple on the surface, but in practice the document preparation stage is where most applications stall.

| Step | Activity | Average Duration |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | Preliminary review of occupation and qualifications | 1–2 days |
| 2 | Apply for Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance (CoA) | 3–4 weeks |
| 3 | Visa issuance at Korean embassy in home country | 1–2 weeks |
| 4 | Alien registration after entry | Within 90 days of entry |

### Why the Certificate of Confirmation (CoA) Stage Is the Most Important

E-7-1 applications are almost always processed via the **Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance** route.

The company submits the application to Korean Immigration first, and once the certificate is issued, the applicant collects the visa sticker at the Korean embassy in their home country.

The review of this certificate is effectively the real review.

If you clear this stage, the embassy step is closer to a formality.

### What You Need to Do After Entering Korea

Within 90 days of entry, you must complete alien registration at the immigration office with jurisdiction over your area.

At this point, you'll need to submit the company's business registration certificate, employment contract, and proof of residence.

The period of stay is usually granted in increments of 1 to 3 years, with 1 year being the most common for first-time applications.

<h2 id="section-4">What Actually Decides E-7-1 Reviews</h2>

Even with a thick stack of documents, what reviewers look at first — before deciding pass or fail — is the **explanation of job-role fit**.

It's more important that the narrative across job, education, and the company's role description stays consistent — even in a single sentence — than that you write at length.

### Commonly Overlooked Points of Evidence

The points most often missed in practice are:

- Job descriptions in the employment contract that are too vague
- Insufficient explanation linking the degree major to actual work duties
- A mismatch between the industry on the company's business registration and the occupation code
- Experience certificates that describe past roles only as generic office work

When this kind of explanation is missing, your case can unravel even with a lot of documents.

### The Wage Requirement Trap

Even if you nominally meet the GNI standard, if reviewers find the actual payment structure suspicious, they'll request additional documentation.

There's a growing tendency to scrutinize probation periods, the proportion of incentives in total pay, and the timing of enrollment in the four major insurance programs.

> **Practical tip:** The way wages are evidenced has changed recently. Whether and how the new approach applies to your specific company structure needs to be verified by a professional.

<h2 id="section-5">E-7-1 Visa Required Documents</h2>

Documents are split between the applicant and the company.

### Documents the Applicant (Foreign National) Prepares

- Passport copy
- Photo (meeting the required format)
- Diploma, academic transcript (apostille or consular verification)
- Experience certificate (specifying role and duration)
- Certifications / portfolio (if applicable)
- Criminal background certificate (for some occupations)

### Documents the Company (Sponsoring Employer) Prepares

- Business registration certificate
- Employment contract
- National and local tax payment certificates
- Roster of enrollees in the four major insurance programs
- Company introduction, financial statements
- Job description (carries the most weight)

> **Note:** Getting your diploma apostilled or consular-verified is a process that takes place in your home country and takes time. When the entry timeline is tight, this is the step that most commonly causes delays.

For additional documents required for specific occupations, see the official notices from the [Ministry of Justice Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr).

The full statutory text is searchable at the [Korea Law Information Center](https://www.law.go.kr) under the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act.

<h2 id="section-6">E-7-1 Visa Denial Reasons and Reapplication Strategy</h2>

Denial reasons follow an almost fixed set of patterns.

The most common reasons are insufficient job-role fit, an undersized company, and falling short of the wage standard.

### What to Watch for When Reapplying After a Denial

Once denied, there's no point in resubmitting the same paperwork.

You need to redraft the job description and reinforce the company-side materials.

In particular, building a fresh logic that connects the role to the degree is the critical piece.

> **Practical tip:** In a recent similar case, the degree and experience were sufficient, but a weak one-page job description led to a denial. When reapplying, the job description section is the first thing to strengthen.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. Can I apply for an E-7-1 visa with experience alone, without a degree?**

There are occupations where this is possible.

You'll need to document 5+ years of experience in the same field, and your experience records must clearly show the company seal, the period, and the role.

**Q2. Can a small company sponsor an E-7-1 foreign worker?**

The 5-Korean-employee rule is the standard, but exceptions can apply depending on the industry, revenue, and the demonstrated need to attract technical talent.

That said, the evidentiary burden grows, so a preliminary review is advisable.

**Q3. What should I do if the occupation code is ambiguous?**

The safest approach is to spell out your duties in a job description, choose the closest matching occupation code, and then have it reviewed in advance.

Applying under the wrong code creates a much heavier burden when you have to reapply after rejection.

**Q4. Can I switch from E-7-1 to F-2-7?**

Yes.

Your E-7 length of stay, Korean language ability, income, education, and other factors are scored on a point system, and if you exceed a certain threshold, you can transition to F-2-7 residence status.

**Q5. How long does the Certificate of Confirmation review take?**

Three to four weeks on average, varying by immigration office, time of year, and whether additional documents are requested.

It tends to run longer around year-end and just before the new school term.

**Q6. Will an application be denied if the company can't meet the GNI wage standard?**

In principle, yes.

That said, occupation-specific exceptions sometimes apply, and there are cases where calculations that include incentives and benefits are accepted.

Whether your specific company's wage structure meets the threshold needs a preliminary review.
