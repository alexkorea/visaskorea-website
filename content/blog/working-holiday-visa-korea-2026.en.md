## What is Korea's Working Holiday Visa (H-1)?

The Working Holiday Visa (H-1) is a stay qualification established under Appendix 1, Item 29 of the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Control Act. It allows nationals of countries with bilateral working holiday agreements to work, travel, and study Korean in Korea simultaneously. As of 2026, Korea has working holiday agreements with over 30 countries.

## Eligibility Requirements

To apply for the H-1 visa, you must meet the following requirements:

- **Age**: Between 18 and 30 years old (25 or under for some countries)
- **Nationality of an agreement country**
- **No criminal record in your home country or Korea**
- **Round-trip airfare or funds to purchase it**
- **Financial means to cover living expenses**
- **Traveling alone (no accompanying family members)**

## Countries with Working Holiday Agreements with Korea

Australia, Canada, New Zealand, Japan, France, Germany, Ireland, Sweden, Denmark, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Czech Republic, Austria, Hungary, Portugal, Netherlands, Italy, Belgium, Spain, Argentina, Chile, and more (verify current list at Korea Ministry of Foreign Affairs website).

## Required Documents

1. **Visa application form** (from Korean embassy/consulate)
2. **Valid passport** (6+ months validity, at least 1 blank visa page)
3. **Passport-size photo**
4. **Proof of financial means**: Bank statement (typically USD 3,000–5,000 equivalent)
5. **Round-trip ticket or proof of funds**
6. **Criminal background check** (varies by country)
7. **Health insurance certificate** (required by some countries)

## Application Procedure

1. Apply at a Korean embassy or consulate in your home country
2. Document review and interview (required for some nationalities)
3. Visa issuance (typically 1–2 weeks)
4. Register as a foreigner within 90 days of arrival in Korea (per Article 31 of Immigration Control Act)
5. Work is permitted after receiving your Alien Registration Card (ARC)

## Stay Duration and Work Conditions

- **Duration**: Up to 1 year (some countries allow 1 extension for up to 2 years total)
- **Permitted work**: Most industries including agriculture, manufacturing, and services
- **Restricted work**: Some industries (e.g., adult entertainment) are prohibited
- **Work duration**: Maximum 6 months with the same employer (varies by agreement)

## Living Costs and Tips

Monthly living costs in Seoul range from approximately KRW 1,000,000–1,500,000. To reduce accommodation costs, consider goshiwon (single rooms), share houses, or working holiday accommodation platforms. Korean language skills significantly expand employment opportunities.

## Common Rejection Reasons

- Insufficient financial proof
- Not a national of an agreement country
- Exceeding age limit
- Previous use of Korean working holiday visa (one visa per person rule)
- Criminal record

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Can I attend a language school on a Working Holiday Visa?**
A. Yes, language school attendance is permitted on H-1. However, a D-2 (student) visa is required for degree programs at universities.

**Q. Can I start working immediately upon arrival?**
A. You must obtain your Alien Registration Card first. Working before registration violates the Immigration Control Act.
