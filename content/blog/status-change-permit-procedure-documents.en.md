# Complete Guide to Status of Stay Change Permission Procedures and Documents

For a status of stay change permission, the **reason for the change and whether you meet the eligibility requirements** are reviewed before the number of documents you submit.

This is the procedure foreigners already legally residing in Korea go through when they want to engage in activities different from their current visa allows, letting them switch status domestically without leaving and re-entering the country.

This guide covers who can apply for a status of stay change, the procedural flow, the document structure, and the pain points that come up in practice.

<h2 id="section-1">What Is Status of Stay Change Permission?</h2>

### Legal Basis for Change Permission

Status of stay change permission is grounded in Article 24 of the [Immigration Act](https://www.law.go.kr).

A foreigner who has finished the activities permitted under their current status and wishes to engage in activities under a different status must obtain permission from the Minister of Justice in advance.

In practice, applications are submitted to the local Immigration Office, and review proceeds through the [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) electronic system.

### The Difference Between Change and Extension

A common point of confusion is the difference between a change and an extension.

An extension means staying on the same status with the period extended; a change means switching the status itself.

| Category | Status Change | Period Extension |
|----------|---------------|------------------|
| Legal basis | Immigration Act Article 24 | Immigration Act Article 25 |
| Reason for application | Change of status (activity) itself | Continuing same status, extending duration only |
| Review intensity | Full review of new status requirements | Confirmation that current activity continues |
| Impact if denied | Departure or reapplication review | Departure or switch to short-term status |

### When Do You Need a Change Permission?

The most common cases include the following.

- D-2 student to D-10 job-seeking
- D-10 job-seeking to E-7 specific activities
- E-9 non-professional employment to E-7-4 skilled worker
- F-6 marriage immigrant to F-5 permanent residence
- C-3 short-term visit to D-8 investment (limited)

In particular, switching from C-3 short-term visit to a long-term status is generally not allowed, and even those who entered under a visa waiver agreement face strong restrictions.

This is where things differ. Some status-to-status changes cannot be filed domestically and require departure followed by re-entry.

<h2 id="section-2">Eligibility and Conditions for Status of Stay Change</h2>

### Basic Eligibility Requirements

You must hold a valid status of stay at the time of application.

Your stay period should not be on the verge of expiring; typically, you can apply starting four months before expiration.

> **Caution:** If your stay period has already expired, the change application will be denied, and you may instead be subject to a fine.

### Meeting the Requirements of the New Status

You must fully meet all the requirements of the new status you are applying for.

For example, to change from D-10 to E-7, you must meet E-7's education, career, and income requirements, and you'll also need an invitation from an employer.

In practice, no matter how many documents you submit, if this part is weak, the application will not pass.

### Absence of Disqualifying Factors

The following factors weigh negatively in a change application.

- History of immigration law violations (overstay, sham marriage, etc.)
- Criminal record (fines and above)
- Tax delinquencies
- Long-term unpaid health insurance premiums

Screening on delinquencies has been tightened recently, so whether these apply accurately to your specific situation should be confirmed with a professional.

<h2 id="section-3">Status of Stay Change Permission — Procedure Flow</h2>

### Step 1 — Preliminary Review

The first thing to check is whether the change between your current and target status is institutionally possible.

| Step | Contents | Average Duration |
|------|----------|-------------------|
| Preliminary review | Confirm whether change is possible and requirements are met | 1–3 days |
| Document prep | Personal documents, invitation documents, activity evidence | 1–3 weeks |
| Filing | Visit the local Immigration Office or apply online via [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) | 1 day |
| Review | Includes supplementary requests | 2 weeks–3 months |
| Notification of result | Approval, denial, or conditional approval | Immediately–few days |

### Step 2 — Document Preparation

Documents are divided into personal documents, inviter's documents, and activity evidence.

Even with a large stack of documents, if the explanation of the reason for change is weak, supplementary requests will keep coming.

### Step 3 — Filing and Review

Filing is handled at the local Immigration Office, and some statuses can be filed online through [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

Processing times vary by office, and additional supplementary periods may be added depending on the case.

### Step 4 — Handling the Result

If approved, you'll have your alien registration card reissued and begin activities under the new status.

If denied, review the denial reasons and consider reapplication or filing an objection.

<h2 id="section-4">Status of Stay Change Permission Documents — Common and Status-Specific</h2>

### Common Documents

These documents are required regardless of the status.

- Integrated application form (Form No. 34)
- Original passport and copy
- Original alien registration card and copy
- Color photo (3.5cm × 4.5cm)
- Fees (government-set fees + administrative processing costs)
- Proof of residence (lease agreement, accommodation confirmation, etc.)

### Status-Specific Additional Documents (Examples)

| Change Type | Key Additional Documents |
|-------------|--------------------------|
| D-2 → D-10 | Diploma, transcript, job search plan |
| D-10 → E-7 | Employment contract, business registration, degree/career certificates |
| E-9 → E-7-4 | Skilled worker point score, Korean language proficiency proof, length-of-service proof |
| F-6 → F-5 | Marriage certificate, income proof, residence period proof |

### What Matters More Than Documents

In practice, the **explanation of the reason for change** and **consistency across documents** determine the outcome more than the document list itself.

If the job duties on the employment contract don't align with your degree major, you'll receive a supplementary request.

If there are gaps between the marriage date on the marriage certificate and your residence history, additional clarification will be required.

> **Practical Tip:** A change-of-status statement is easier for the reviewer to read when the facts and timeline are summarized concisely, rather than written at length.

<h2 id="section-5">Common Sticking Points in Status of Stay Change Permission</h2>

### Vague Explanations of the Reason

The most common ground for denial is **lack of persuasiveness in the reason for change**.

Generic phrases like "I want to keep working in Korea" or "I want to live with my family" carry little weight in the review.

You need to explain with concrete facts (a job offer, your child's school, the stage of a business in progress).

### Inconsistent Dates Across Documents

If graduation date, hiring date, residence period, and other dates don't line up with each other, you'll attract suspicion.

In particular, if there are signs you were already engaged in employment activities during the D-10 job-search period, this can lead to a violation of activities outside your status.

### Insufficient Proof of Income and Funds

This is a common issue across E-7, F-5, D-8, and other statuses.

Even with a bank balance, if the explanation of the source of funds is weak, things can quickly unravel.

Income standards change every year, so confirm this year's exact thresholds through consultation.

### Misjudging the Timing of Application

You should apply as soon as the reason for change arises; delay can result in being treated as a violation of activities outside your status.

Timing management is especially tricky in the transition from finishing studies to D-10 or E-7.

<h2 id="section-6">What to Do If Your Change Permission Is Denied</h2>

### Check the Reason for Denial

The denial notice will include a reason code.

You need to determine first whether the issue was failing to meet requirements, insufficient evidence, or a disqualifying factor — your reapplication strategy depends on this.

### Reapplication and Objection

If the requirements were not met, supplementing the missing parts and reapplying is the standard route.

If there is room to dispute the judgment itself, you can consider filing an objection or administrative appeal through the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr).

In a recent similar case, depending on the denial reason, an indirect change to a different status proved effective. The right strategy for your situation requires a case-by-case review.

### Departure and Re-entry Strategy

When domestic change is blocked, there's a route of leaving the country and obtaining a new visa at an overseas Korean mission.

This route has different review standards and a different processing authority, so judging which route is more advantageous in advance is critical.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. How long does the status of stay change review take?**

Usually between 2 weeks and 3 months. It varies by status type, immigration office, and whether supplementary requests are made. For an accurate timeline, check with the relevant office.

**Q2. How many days before the stay period expires do I need to apply?**

You can apply up to the expiration date, but considering the supplementary period, it's safer to apply at least 1–2 months in advance. Applications after expiration are not accepted and become grounds for departure.

**Q3. Can I change from short-term visit (C-3) to long-term stay?**

In principle, this is restricted. There are some exceptions (humanitarian reasons, special cases), but they are very narrow, and in practice, departure and re-entry is the usual path.

**Q4. Can I leave the country during the change application process?**

It's better not to leave if you can avoid it. Departure during the application process can complicate the procedure, and depending on the case, the application itself may be terminated.

**Q5. When I apply for a change, are my family members' statuses also changed?**

Not automatically. Family statuses (F-1, F-3, etc.) also require separate change applications, and these are usually filed after your own status is finalized.

**Q6. If my change permission is approved, how long is the new stay period?**

It depends on the standards for the new status. Even within the same status, the granted period varies by the applicant's circumstances, and the exact period depends on the review outcome.
