# Permanent Resident Re-Entry Permit Requirements: What to Check Before Leaving Korea

If you hold an F-5 permanent resident visa and plan to be away from Korea for more than one year, you must obtain a re-entry permit before departure.

This applies to all F-5 permanent residents, while short-term departures within one year are automatically exempt without a separate application.

Below is a practical breakdown of the types of re-entry permits, when to apply, how permanent residency can be forfeited if you skip the process, and how to extend the permit while abroad.

<h2 id="section-1">Re-Entry Permit Requirement for Permanent Residents — Why It Must Be Secured Before Departure</h2>

### Legal Basis for the Re-Entry Permit

Article 30 of the Immigration Act requires registered foreign nationals to obtain re-entry permission from the Minister of Justice before leaving Korea and returning.

F-5 permanent residents are not exempt from this obligation.

In fact, because permanent residency is the most stable residence status, holders often underestimate the departure procedure — and that is precisely where most cases go wrong.

You can review the full legal text — Article 30 of the Immigration Act and Article 39 of its Enforcement Decree — on the [National Law Information Center](https://www.law.go.kr).

### What Forfeiture of Permanent Residency Actually Means

If you stay abroad for more than one year without a re-entry permit, your permanent residency itself is forfeited.

That is where the real problem begins.

Once permanent residency is lost, returning to Korea does not automatically restore your F-5 status. You will need to go through a fresh visa process or re-qualify for permanent residency from scratch.

It is far from rare for a permanent residency held for more than ten years to disappear over just a few days' difference.

<h2 id="section-2">Re-Entry Permit Exemption Conditions — Automatic Exemption Within One Year of Departure</h2>

### Who Qualifies for Automatic Exemption

For F-5 permanent residents who plan to re-enter Korea within two years of their departure date, automatic exemption applies — no separate re-entry permit application is required.

That said, the calculation is based on the actual elapsed time between departure and re-entry, so simply memorizing "one year" can be risky.

| Category | Automatic Exemption Period | Separate Application Required |
|---|---|---|
| General registered foreign nationals | Within 1 year of departure | Required if exceeding 1 year |
| F-5 permanent residents | Within 2 years of departure | Required if exceeding 2 years |
| F-4 overseas Koreans | Within 2 years of departure | Required if exceeding 2 years |

Automatic exemption periods can change with legal amendments, so it is safer to check the announcements at [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) before your actual departure.

### Cases Where Automatic Exemption Does Not Apply

There are situations where exemption is denied even if you return within the automatic exemption window.

- Departure while subject to a departure ban or an ongoing investigation
- Grounds for cancellation or suspension of permanent residency have arisen
- Grounds for entry refusal apply at the time of re-entry

In particular, those who depart right after a departure ban is lifted frequently run into trouble at this stage.

If you are unsure whether your situation qualifies for automatic exemption, confirm with your local Immigration Office before leaving.

<h2 id="section-3">Permanent Resident Re-Entry Permit Validity — Single vs. Multiple</h2>

### Single Re-Entry Permit

A single re-entry permit can be used for one departure and one return only.

If you re-enter Korea even once and leave again while abroad, you need to obtain a new re-entry permit.

For a short overseas business trip or a one-time family visit, the single permit is appropriate.

### Multiple Re-Entry Permit

A multiple re-entry permit allows unlimited entries and exits during its validity period.

For permanent residents who travel frequently between Korea and abroad, the multiple permit is far more practical in real terms.

| Category | Single | Multiple |
|---|---|---|
| Number of uses | 1 time | Unlimited within validity |
| Maximum validity | Up to 1 year | Generally up to 2 years |
| Best suited for | One-time travelers | Frequent travelers |
| Government fee | Per government schedule | Per government schedule |

The maximum validity of a multiple re-entry permit depends on the applicant's residence situation and the reason for departure.

The exact validity you can secure in your case is best confirmed through a consultation.

<h2 id="section-4">Re-Entry Permit Application Documents and Procedure</h2>

### Standard Documents

The basic documents required are as follows.

- Re-entry permit application form
- Passport (original and copy)
- Alien Registration Card
- Documents proving the reason for departure (overseas employment contract, school admission letter, medical diagnosis, etc.)
- Documents proving overseas residence

The paperwork itself looks straightforward.

In practice, however, the persuasiveness of the documents proving your reason for departure is what determines the outcome of the review.

Abstract reasons such as "running an overseas business" or "living with family" often result in a single permit only — or even rejection — if the supporting documents are weak.

### Where to Apply and Processing Times

Applications can be filed at the [Immigration Office](https://www.immigration.go.kr) with jurisdiction over your residence, or through the HiKorea e-Government portal.

Processing times vary significantly between offices.

Some offices in Seoul process applications on the spot, while certain regional offices can take several days.

If your departure schedule is tight, we can guide you to the fastest available filing route.

> **Practical tip:** Applying about one month before departure gives you breathing room to handle single/multiple decisions and any supplementary document requests.

<h2 id="section-5">Forfeiture of Permanent Residency Due to Missed Re-Entry Permit — Common Pitfalls on the Ground</h2>

### What Happens If You Exceed the One-Year Threshold

If you enter Korea after exceeding the automatic exemption period, the issue surfaces immediately at airport immigration.

Forfeiture of permanent residency is processed on the spot, and your entry is converted to a regular visa procedure.

Even permanent residents who have lived in Korea for over ten years face the same outcome if they exceed the automatic exemption period by even a single day.

### When There Are Unavoidable Circumstances

If you were unable to return due to circumstances beyond your control — illness, accidents, war, natural disasters abroad — you may apply at a Korean overseas mission for a re-entry permit or for restoration of permanent residency, accompanied by documents proving the cause.

In practice, the scope of recognized "unavoidable circumstances" is extremely narrow.

Reasons such as "work took longer than expected" or "family matters came up" are commonly rejected.

Whether your specific circumstances fall within the recognized scope needs to be reviewed on a case-by-case basis.

<h2 id="section-6">Re-Entry Permit Extension — Handling Expiration While Abroad</h2>

### Filing an Extension at an Overseas Mission

Even if you already hold a re-entry permit, if your stay abroad runs long and the permit risks expiring, you can apply for an extension at the local [Korean overseas mission](https://www.0404.go.kr) (embassy or consulate general).

Here is the critical point.

Once the permit has expired, extension is no longer possible.

The rule is to apply before expiration, and applying right at the deadline is the riskiest move you can make.

### When to File for an Extension

It is safest to file your extension application at least one month before expiration.

Each overseas mission has its own processing speed and document requirements, and some require in-person visits by the applicant.

It is wise to confirm the procedures of the mission in the country where you are staying before you leave Korea.

<h2 id="section-8">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. Do F-5 permanent residents really need to obtain a re-entry permit?**
Re-entry within two years of departure qualifies for automatic exemption, but if your absence will exceed two years, you must obtain a re-entry permit.

Failure to apply results in forfeiture of permanent residency.

**Q2. Can automatic exemption be denied even for trips under one year?**
Yes — automatic exemption may not be recognized if you depart immediately after a departure ban is lifted, if grounds for suspension or cancellation of permanent residency have arisen, or if grounds for entry refusal apply, among other situations.

Your individual case needs to be verified.

**Q3. I exceeded the automatic exemption period without obtaining a re-entry permit. Can my permanent residency be restored?**
If unavoidable circumstances can be proven, a restoration procedure can be attempted through an overseas mission, but the recognized scope is very narrow.

The outcome depends heavily on the strength of the supporting evidence.

**Q4. How long can a multiple re-entry permit be valid for?**
The validity is generally decided within two years, though it varies depending on the applicant's reason for departure and residence situation.

The maximum period available in your case needs to be confirmed at the application stage.

**Q5. My re-entry permit is about to expire while I am abroad. What should I do?**
You need to file an extension application at the local Korean overseas mission before expiration.

Once it has expired, extension is no longer possible, so it is safest to act at least one month before the expiration date.

**Q6. How many days before departure should I apply?**
Legally, any time before departure works, but accounting for supplementary document requests and review queues, leaving at least one to two weeks of lead time is safer.
