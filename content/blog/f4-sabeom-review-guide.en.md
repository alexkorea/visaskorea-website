When a Korean-Chinese (Joseonjok) person holding an F-4 (Overseas Korean) visa becomes involved in a criminal investigation or receives a criminal penalty, Korean immigration authorities separately conduct what is called a **sabeom shimsa** (criminal review). This review is entirely separate from criminal court proceedings. Even if you receive a not-guilty verdict or suspended prosecution in criminal court, immigration can independently revoke your status or order deportation.

The sabeom shimsa can undermine your residency status faster than criminal punishment. This is where problems begin in practice.

## 1. What Is Sabeom Shimsa? {#section-1}

Sabeom shimsa is an administrative procedure conducted by Korean immigration authorities under the Immigration Act, in which immigration reviews the residency status of a foreign national who has engaged in criminal conduct.

**Criminal prosecution and sabeom shimsa are entirely separate.**

| | Criminal Court | Sabeom Shimsa |
|--|--|--|
| Authority | Court | Immigration office |
| Purpose | Determine criminal punishment | Determine whether to maintain residency status |
| Effect of not-guilty verdict | No punishment | Review can still proceed independently |
| Outcomes | Fine, imprisonment | Warning, departure order, deportation |

Even if criminal proceedings result in suspended prosecution or acquittal, immigration can independently decide to deport you.

---

## 2. Who Does It Apply To? {#section-2}

Sabeom shimsa applies when a case falls under the deportation grounds in Article 46 of the Immigration Act. The most common situations for F-4 holders in practice include:

**Frequently occurring categories:**

- **Drunk driving or refusal to take a breathalyzer** — Road Traffic Act violation
- **Assault or aggravated assault** — Criminal Act Articles 260 and 261
- **Sexual harassment or indecent assault** — Criminal Act Article 298
- **Prostitution** — Act on Punishment of Acts Related to Prostitution
- **Unauthorized employment (simple labor)** — Working outside permitted F-4 occupations
- **Unauthorized absence** — Violating residency reporting obligations
- **Narcotics** — Narcotics Control Act violation

> **Note:** F-4 visa holders are prohibited from working in simple labor occupations. Employment outside permitted categories is also grounds for review.

## 3. How the Process Works {#section-3}

Sabeom shimsa typically proceeds as follows:

**Step 1 — Notification**
The police or prosecutor's office notifies immigration of the criminal case involving a foreign national. In many cases, immigration learns of the situation automatically once a case is opened.

**Step 2 — Interview and Investigation**
Immigration summons the foreign national for questioning and investigates the facts. Your statements and any evidence submitted at this stage are critical.

**Step 3 — Internal Review**
Immigration internally evaluates the severity of the offense, length of stay, family situation in Korea, and ties to Korean society.

**Step 4 — Decision Notification**
The outcome is communicated in writing.

> **In practice:** Your attitude and preparation at the interview stage affect the severity of the outcome. Being uncooperative or dismissive can count against you.

## 4. Types of Penalties {#section-4}

| Penalty | Description | Typical Situation |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Warning | Status maintained, written warning only | First offense, minor violation |
| Departure order | Required to leave voluntarily by a set deadline | Moderate violations |
| Deferred departure | Departure order temporarily suspended | Pending litigation, financial dependents |
| Forced deportation | Compulsory removal | Serious offense; likely accompanied by entry ban |

**Entry ban:** Deportation orders typically result in an entry ban lasting several years to decades. During an entry ban, you cannot enter Korea under any visa category.

## 5. Factors That Affect the Outcome {#section-5}

The same offense can produce different outcomes. This is where results diverge.

**Mitigating factors:**
- First offense
- Long period of lawful stay in Korea
- Korean family members (spouse or children)
- Victim compensation agreement
- Demonstrated remorse and preventive measures

**Aggravating factors:**
- Repeated same-type offenses
- Serious harm or large-scale damage
- Serious offenses — sexual crimes, narcotics
- Uncooperative attitude during investigation
- Simultaneous unauthorized employment violation

## 6. How to Appeal {#section-6}

If you wish to contest the decision, the following options are available:

**Administrative appeal**
You can file an administrative appeal with the Ministry of Justice, Immigration and Foreign Policy Bureau within 90 days of receiving the penalty notice. In some cases, you may simultaneously apply to suspend enforcement while the appeal is pending.

**Administrative litigation**
After completing the administrative appeal, or alongside it, you may file an administrative lawsuit. However, litigation requires significant time and cost.

> **Key point:** If you miss the 90-day appeal deadline, the penalty becomes final. As soon as you receive a decision, consult with a professional administrative scrivener or attorney.

## 7. FAQ {#section-7}

**Q. I received a suspended prosecution in criminal court. Will I still face sabeom shimsa?**
A. Yes. Suspended prosecution is a decision by the prosecutor not to indict — it is separate from immigration proceedings. Immigration reviews the underlying conduct itself.

**Q. Can I apply for a visa extension while sabeom shimsa is pending?**
A. Extensions are likely to be placed on hold during a pending review. In most cases, the extension decision is made after the review concludes.

**Q. If I receive a deportation order, will I also be banned from entry?**
A. Not necessarily, but deportation orders typically come with entry bans. The ban period ranges from 1 year to permanent exclusion, depending on the severity of the case.

**Q. Am I subject to sabeom shimsa even though I have Korean heritage?**
A. F-4 visa holders without Korean citizenship are treated as foreign residents and are fully subject to sabeom shimsa.

**Q. Should I go to the investigation interview alone?**
A. This is not recommended. Your statements can be used against you. Consulting a professional administrative scrivener before attending to prepare a response strategy is strongly advisable.
