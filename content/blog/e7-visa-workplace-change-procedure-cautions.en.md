# E-7 Visa Job Change (Workplace Transfer) Procedures and Key Considerations: Where the Real Bottlenecks Are

An E-7 visa job change isn't just about switching companies — it's a separate review process that requires prior authorization from Korea Immigration.

This applies to foreign nationals already residing in Korea on an E-7 visa who want to move to a new company, or whose job duties have changed significantly within the same company.

This guide covers when to file for workplace change authorization, how occupation-code matching is judged, document preparation, common stumbling blocks, and how to respond to a denial.

<h2 id="section-1">E-7 Job Change Is "Prior Authorization," Not Notification</h2>

Many people think of a job change as simply a matter of "letting immigration know."

In reality, you must first receive **workplace change authorization** from the Immigration Office before you can legally start working at the new company.

The legal basis is Article 21 of the [Immigration Control Act](https://www.law.go.kr), and violations can result in administrative penalties or even revocation of residence status.

### Prior Authorization vs. After-the-Fact Notification

E-7 status, in principle, requires **prior authorization**.

A few exceptions — such as a simple internal department move or a change in business address only — can be handled with after-the-fact notification. But when the company itself changes, prior authorization is almost always required.

In practice, the safest flow is **not** "last day at old job = filing date," but rather **obtaining authorization in advance, while still employed at the current company**.

### The Real Risk of Working Without Authorization

It may look harmless on the surface, but starting work at a new company without authorization is frequently exposed through the timing of national insurance registration or withholding tax filings.

When these timestamps don't line up, they accumulate and come back to haunt you at your next extension or F-5 (permanent residence) application.

> **Caution:** Receiving wages from the new company before workplace change authorization is issued may be classified as unauthorized employment.

<h2 id="section-2">Occupation-Code Matching: The Biggest Sticking Point in E-7 Job Changes</h2>

This is where most cases run into trouble.

E-7 visa holders may only work within the 85 designated occupation codes published by [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

If the new role falls under a different occupation code than your current E-7, or if the actual duties don't match the code even when it appears to be the same, things get tangled fast.

### Moving Within the Same Occupation Code

Example: If you currently hold "S-3-7-3110 Management Specialist" and the new role at your new company falls under the same code, the process is relatively straightforward.

That said, the **employer qualification review** — revenue, Korean national employment, business status — starts fresh.

The fact that the previous company qualified does not automatically carry over.

### Moving Across Occupation Codes

This is where it gets difficult.

For example, moving from IT development (Computer Systems Specialist) to marketing (Advertising Specialist) triggers a **full re-review of your educational and career qualifications from scratch**.

If you don't meet the educational or experience requirements for the new occupation, the code change itself can be denied.

When this part is weak, the application should be prepared with the same rigor as a brand-new E-7 filing — not a simple transfer.

> **Practical tip:** The "Job Description" written into your offer letter from the new company becomes the primary document used to determine the occupation code. Vague wording leads to vague outcomes.

<h2 id="section-3">Workplace Change Authorization: Application Procedure</h2>

The first two things to check are your remaining period of stay and the business status of the new company.

Both need to be stable before filing is even meaningful.

### When to File

The recommended flow is:

1. Sign an employment contract with the new company *before* leaving the current job
2. Submit the workplace change authorization application
3. Begin work at the new company *after* receiving the authorization notice
4. Transfer national insurance and withholding tax registration to the new company

In practice, many cases run with no gap between resignation and start date — but if a gap stretches out, immigration will request a separate explanation.

### How to File

Applications can be submitted online via [Hikorea e-Government](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) or in person at the local Immigration Office.

That said, if an occupation code change is involved or if proving employer qualifications is complex, in-person filing is safer.

Online submissions can suffer from slower turnaround when supplementary documents are requested.

### Processing Time

Processing time varies by immigration office, and is heavily affected by whether the occupation code is changing, the size of the company, and the number of supplementary document requests.

<h2 id="section-4">What the New Company Must Qualify For</h2>

More important than your paperwork is **the employer's own eligibility**.

If the new company isn't in a position to hire E-7 foreign nationals, no amount of perfect personal documentation will save the application.

### Basic Employer Requirements

| Item | What's Checked | Notes |
|------|----------------|-------|
| Business status | Actively operating, not suspended/closed | Verified via NTS business status lookup |
| Korean employees | Regular Korean staff retained over a defined period | Occupation-specific foreign-to-Korean ratio caps |
| Revenue/financials | Sufficient revenue and ability to pay wages | VAT returns, financial statements |
| Wage level | Meets the occupation-specific minimum | Updated annually; check with jurisdiction |
| Tax delinquency | No overdue national insurance or national taxes | Delinquency is disqualifying |

### Additional Requirements by Occupation

Some occupations look at additional factors: revenue scale, number of R&D staff, certifications held, and so on.

The "high-growth occupation (talent) track" in particular applies stricter employer criteria.

You need to confirm whether the new company falls under this track before any personal eligibility review is meaningful.

> **Practical tip:** Obtaining the new company's business registration certificate, national insurance roster, and most recent VAT return *before* joining significantly accelerates the review.

<h2 id="section-5">Required Documents</h2>

Even a thick stack of documents won't help if you miss the core items — and supplementary requests will keep piling up.

The table below shows the typical composition. Additional materials may apply depending on the occupation.

| Category | Document | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Applicant | Application form, passport, Alien Registration Card | Photo attached |
| Applicant | Degree certificate, certificates of employment | Re-submission required when occupation code changes |
| New employer | Copy of business registration | Most recent version |
| New employer | Employment contract | Must specify duties and wages |
| New employer | Recommendation letter (where required) | Issued by relevant ministry |
| New employer | Employee roster, national insurance enrollment records | Verifies Korean employment ratio |
| New employer | Tax payment certificate, VAT return | Financial verification |
| Previous employer | Resignation letter or certificate of separation | Confirms departure |
| Previous employer | Withholding tax receipt | Confirms work history |

### Frequently Overlooked Documents

- Documents from the previous employer confirming end of employment
- Job description for the new role (English/Korean)
- Reissued certificates of employment tailored to the new occupation (when the code changes)
- Proof of wage payment capacity (financial statements, bank balance certificates, etc.)

When the previous employer is uncooperative, delays in obtaining separation documents commonly push the entire timeline back.

### Why the Job Description Matters

Reviewers don't just look at the single "duties" line in the employment contract.

They examine a separate job description to confirm whether the actual work aligns with the occupation code's definition.

Rather than writing long descriptions, applications get approved more often when keywords from the occupation code definition map directly onto specific job duties.

<h2 id="section-6">Common Denial Reasons and How to Respond</h2>

In practice, the patterns of denial and supplementary requests are fairly consistent.

### 1. Occupation Code Mismatch

Happens when the new role doesn't align with your education and experience.

Response: Redefine the duties, add supporting career documentation, or reselect the occupation code itself.

### 2. Insufficient Korean Employees at the New Company

E-7 status imposes a foreign-to-Korean employee ratio cap that varies by occupation.

Response: The employer must either hire additional Korean staff or adjust the filing timing.

### 3. Wages Below the Minimum Threshold

Occupation-specific minimum wage thresholds change every year.

Re-verify just before filing that your contract wage meets this year's threshold.

### 4. Disqualifying Issues from the Previous Employment Period

Tax delinquency, immigration violations, or criminal records during your stay will surface during the job-change review.

These need to be resolved before filing.

### 5. Insufficient Explanation of Employment Gaps

When the gap between resignation and new employment is long, you'll need to explain what you did during that period.

> **Caution:** After a denial notice, supplementing your evidence and refiling is often faster than starting over from scratch — but only **after analyzing the denial reasoning**.

<h2 id="section-7">Post-Transfer Follow-Up — It's Not Over Yet</h2>

Receiving workplace change authorization doesn't mean every step is done.

Skipping post-employment follow-up will cause problems at your next extension.

### Alien Registration Change Report

You must file an alien registration change report **within 14 days** of receiving the workplace change authorization.

Missing this filing results in an administrative fine.

### National Insurance Cleanup

The previous employer's loss-of-coverage filing and the new employer's enrollment filing must align in timing.

Misalignment can complicate the calculation of "normal employment periods" later when applying for permanent residence or citizenship.

### Managing the Next Extension

The period of stay granted immediately after a job change can be relatively short.

Reviewing the timing of your extension, the wage threshold compliance, and the stability of the new company all together makes the next step much smoother.

<h2 id="section-8">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. Can I apply for workplace change authorization after resigning?**

Technically yes, but it's not recommended.

If you fail to find a new job within a certain period after resignation, your residence status may become invalid, and you'll be asked to provide additional justification for the gap period.

Filing while still employed remains the safest approach.

**Q2. If I'm moving within the same occupation code, can I skip the education and experience review?**

Your baseline education and experience are already on file, but the new employer's qualifications, wage level, and job-fit are re-examined from scratch.

In fact, more applications get stuck on employer qualifications than on personal qualifications.

**Q3. The new company is a small startup. Is it still possible?**

Company size alone is not disqualifying.

That said, if revenue, Korean staff employment, and proof of wage payment capacity are weak, denial is likely.

For startups, supplementary materials — investment documentation, revenue projections — often determine the outcome.

**Q4. If the occupation code changes entirely, is this still a "job change" or a brand-new application?**

Formally it's still a workplace change, but the review intensity is essentially that of a new application.

If you don't meet the new occupation's education and experience requirements, the application will be denied — in which case a detour through D-10 (job-seeking) status before re-entering on a new E-7 may be worth considering.

Which path is fastest for your situation requires pre-filing review.

**Q5. Can I start work at the new company while the authorization is being processed?**

Working for wages before authorization is issued may be classified as unauthorized employment.

Even under the label of "training" or "onboarding," it carries risk.

The rule is: your first day of work should be the day you receive the authorization notice.

**Q6. How much does a job change cost?**

Costs vary case by case, and we'll walk through the details during your free consultation.

In addition to standard government fees (the published filing fee plus administrative processing), the total depends on whether the occupation code changes and whether supplementary requests are triggered.
