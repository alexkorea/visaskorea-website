# Complete Guide to the E-10 Visa Application Process for Foreign Seafarers (2026)

The E-10 visa application process for foreign seafarers is handled as a single package that combines the employer's (shipping or fishing company) pre-invitation procedures with the applicant's visa issuance steps at an overseas Korean mission.

It applies to foreign seafarers who will work aboard coastal vessels of 5 tons or more, deep-sea fishing vessels, coastal fishing vessels, cruise passenger ships, and similar vessels. Under the Seafarers Act, the seafarer's pocket-book and the employment contract function as the core documents.

Below, we walk through the eligibility requirements, required documents, the practical flow from issuance of the Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance through post-arrival alien registration, and the points where applicants most often get stuck.

<h2 id="section-1">What Is the E-10 Visa and Who Can Receive It?</h2>

The E-10 is an employment-based status of stay granted exclusively to vessel crew members under Annex 1-2 of the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act.

Unlike tourist visas or general employment visas, the holder must be **registered as a seafarer under the Seafarers Act**, and the vessel itself must be Korean-flagged or operated by a Korean company.

### The Three E-10 Subcategories

The E-10 is divided into three categories based on the scope of activity:

- E-10-1 (Coastal Vessel Crew): Crew member aboard coastal vessels of 5 tons or more
- E-10-2 (Fishing Vessel Crew): Crew member aboard fishing vessels of 20 tons or more
- E-10-3 (Cruise Liner Crew): Crew member aboard cruise passenger ships of 20,000 tons or more

In practice, fishing vessel crew (E-10-2) cases are the most common, followed by coastal vessel crew.

### Why It Is Often Confused with E-9 and E-7

One area applicants frequently get tripped up on is the line between E-10 and E-9 (Non-professional Employment).

E-9 mainly covers land-based manufacturing, agriculture and livestock, and fisheries (vessels under 20 tons), while **boarding a fishing vessel of 20 tons or more falls under E-10**.

Because vessel tonnage and registration type determine which visa track applies, checking the shipping company's registration and the tonnage entered in the fishing vessel registry should be your first step.

You can verify the legal basis in the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act on the [Korean Law Information Center](https://www.law.go.kr).

<h2 id="section-2">E-10 Visa Eligibility Requirements</h2>

The first thing examined during the eligibility review is the **employer's qualification**.

The shipping or fishing company must be authorized to employ foreign seafarers, and the vessel must fall under the scope of the Korean Seafarers Act.

### Requirements for the Foreign Applicant

- At least 18 years old
- Seafarer experience or a related certification from the home country (depending on position)
- Pass the medical examination (fit-for-boarding determination)
- No grounds for disqualification (serious criminal record, prior deportation, etc.)

### Requirements for the Employer (Shipping or Fishing Company)

- Owns a vessel registered in the Korean ship registry
- Eligible to enter into a seafarer employment contract under the Seafarers Act
- Enrolled in the four major insurances with no history of wage arrears
- Has remaining quota allocation for foreign seafarer recruitment

The actual review also looks at the company's financial position and its track record managing foreign seafarers in the past.

> **Note:** Even for fishing vessels, if the tonnage is under 20 tons, the case goes through E-9 rather than E-10. Because a single-digit difference in tonnage changes the visa category entirely, checking the fishing vessel registry should always come first.

<h2 id="section-3">Preparing Documents for the E-10 Visa Application</h2>

When it comes to documents, **consistency matters more than volume**.

When the seafarer's pocket-book, employment contract, and vessel details don't line up, the case typically drags out during supplementary document requests.

### Common Required Documents

| Category | Document | Notes |
|------|--------|------|
| Application | Visa Application Form (Form 17) | Applicant's signature |
| Identity | Passport copy, standard-size photo | Valid for at least 6 months |
| Employment | Seafarer Employment Contract | Korean Seafarers Act form |
| Qualification | Seafarer's pocket-book or confirmation of pending issuance | Issued in home country or Korea |
| Health | Boarding medical examination report | Designated hospital |
| Company | Business registration, certificate of vessel nationality | Copies |

### Documents Submitted Separately by the Company

- Application for recruitment of foreign seafarers
- Certified copy of vessel registry (for tonnage and use verification)
- Crew list and explanation of vacancy
- Proof of wage payment capacity (revenue, financial statements)
- Housing provision plan (if applicable)

### Additional Documents by Position

| Position | Additional Document | Notes |
|------|-----------|------|
| Deck Crew | Career certificate | Issued in home country |
| Engine Crew | Engine-related certification | English-language notarization |
| Galley Crew | Sanitation training certificate | Required by some shipping companies |

Even when the document set is otherwise complete, a hold-up at the seafarer's pocket-book stage pushes the entire timeline back.

To save time, it is actually safer to align the home-country issuance schedule with the planned arrival date in Korea from the outset.

<h2 id="section-4">E-10 Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance Application Process</h2>

Most E-10 cases are processed through the **Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance (CCVI)** route.

Rather than the applicant applying directly from their home country, the employer in Korea first obtains the certificate from immigration and sends it to the applicant.

### Process Flow

1. Shipping company develops the foreign seafarer recruitment plan
2. Files the CCVI application at the competent Immigration Office
3. Review (typically 2–4 weeks, varies by office)
4. Certificate issued and sent to the applicant
5. Applicant applies for the visa at the Korean consulate in their home country
6. Visa issued, applicant enters Korea
7. Alien registration within 90 days of entry

### Which Immigration Office You File With Makes a Difference

One thing applicants often overlook is the **choice of competent immigration office**.

The filing must go to the immigration office with jurisdiction over the vessel's home port — usually port-area offices in Busan, Incheon, Yeosu, Mokpo, and similar locations.

Processing speed and the scope of supplementary requests vary by office, so processing time differs from one immigration office to another, and we identify and proceed with the fastest available option.

You can find some of the office-by-office guidance on [Hi Korea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr), but for seafarer cases the general information often doesn't cover the full picture.

<h2 id="section-5">Alien Registration and Boarding Report After Entry</h2>

Arrival isn't the finish line — the full set runs **alien registration → seafarer's pocket-book issuance → boarding report**.

If this final stage is weak, departure schedules end up disrupted.

### Alien Registration Within 90 Days of Entry

- Visit the Immigration Office with jurisdiction over the place of residence
- Alien registration application, passport, photo, fee
- Documents confirming the residence address (housing or dormitory lease, etc.)

### Seafarer's Pocket-Book Issuance (Korean Version)

Even if you hold a home-country seafarer's pocket-book, there are cases where a separate procedure at the **Regional Office of Oceans and Fisheries** under the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries is required in order to board in Korea.

You can find detailed guidance on the [Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries](https://www.mof.go.kr) website.

### Boarding and Disembarking Reports

The shipping company must report each foreign seafarer's boarding and disembarking to immigration.

If these reports are missed, the issue surfaces immediately at the next renewal or re-hire stage.

> **Practical Tip:** If the vessel's departure is scheduled before the alien registration card is issued, there are cases where a temporary identification slip or fact confirmation document can be used instead. That said, not every office handles this the same way, so you need to confirm with the competent office in advance.

<h2 id="section-6">Common Sticking Points in E-10 Visa Applications</h2>

More often than the documents themselves, cases get stuck on **workplace qualifications** and **vessel registration status**.

### 1) Mismatch in Vessel Tonnage and Use

When the tonnage on the vessel registry doesn't meet the threshold, or the vessel is registered for general cargo rather than fishing, the E-10 is denied.

### 2) Weak Explanation of Vacancy by the Shipping Company

If the explanation for "why the position could not be filled with Korean seafarers" is weak, supplementary requests repeat at the certificate stage.

Rather than writing long narratives, attaching objective evidence such as recruitment ad histories and Korean Seafarers' Union search results produces a higher approval rate.

### 3) Missing Apostille or Consular Authentication on Home-Country Documents

Seafarer career certificates, qualifications, and similar documents require home-country notarization plus apostille or Korean consular authentication.

When this is missed, there are cases where documents have to be sent back to the home country right before visa issuance.

### 4) Issues with the Medical Examination Facility

Boarding medical examinations are only valid when performed at a designated facility or a certified medical institution.

It is common for applicants to submit results from a general health checkup only to have them rejected.

In a recent similar case, an issue with the medical examination facility pushed an applicant's arrival back by more than a month.

If you want to check in advance which scenario your case might fall under, we recommend reviewing the notices on the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr) site alongside direct verification.

<h2 id="section-7">E-10 Processing Time and Costs</h2>

Processing time varies significantly depending on the office and the time of year.

| Stage | Typical Duration | Notes |
|------|-------------|------|
| CCVI review | 2–4 weeks | Varies by office |
| Visa issuance in home country | 3–7 days | Varies by consulate |
| Alien registration after entry | 1–3 weeks | At residence-area office |

During peak season (just before the fishing season opens), CCVI review can take longer.

Costs vary case by case, so we provide accurate figures during the free consultation.

<h2 id="section-8">Extending the E-10 and Length of Stay</h2>

The base length of stay under the E-10 varies by position and contract, and the maximum stay per grant is applied separately from the total cumulative stay limit.

### Key Points to Check at Renewal

- Whether a new contract is being signed with the same shipping company
- Confirmation of no wage arrears
- Continued enrollment in the four major insurances
- Track record of fulfilling immigration reporting obligations

If the company side hasn't fulfilled its obligations at the renewal point, the renewal can be denied regardless of the applicant's own circumstances.

When this is weak, the outcome can diverge from yours no matter how diligently you have worked.

> **Note:** The quota for foreign seafarer recruitment and the operating criteria for each occupation are adjusted somewhat each year. To confirm whether the current year's criteria apply to your case, you'll need to check with the competent authority.

<h2 id="section-9">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. Can I change from E-9 to E-10?**

Moving from land-based fisheries (E-9) to boarding a fishing vessel of 20 tons or more (E-10) generally requires a status change application, and both the vessel registration and the shipping company requirements must be satisfied.

Unlike a simple change of employer, the track itself is different, so a prior review needs to come first.

**Q2. Can I board with only my home-country seafarer's pocket-book?**

The recognition of home-country seafarer's pocket-books varies by country and by treaty.

Certain positions additionally require the Korean-issued version, so it is safer to proceed after confirming the specifics of your case.

**Q3. Can family members accompany me on an F-3?**

The E-10 is among the statuses where dependent-family visas (F-3) are generally restricted.

You should be aware in advance that bringing a spouse or children may not be feasible.

**Q4. Will a delay in issuing the seafarer's pocket-book delay my entry to Korea?**

Yes — without either the pocket-book itself or a confirmation that issuance is pending, the CCVI review will often be put on hold.

It is safer to schedule home-country issuance well ahead of your planned arrival date.

**Q5. Can a small fishing company still obtain an E-10 CCVI?**

When the financial position is weaker, you'll need to prepare more substantial supporting materials on wage payment capacity.

Submitting items such as revenue trends, catch volumes, and transaction records together improves the odds.

**Q6. Can I move to another shipping company after arrival?**

Workplace changes are subject to reporting and approval, and both the reason for the change and the qualifications of the new shipping company must be satisfied.

Unauthorized changes can lead to a violation of the status of stay, so the proper procedure must come first.
