Korea's D-10 Job Seeker visa allows skilled foreign professionals to stay in Korea while searching for employment. Under the **Immigration Control Act Enforcement Decree Schedule 1**, the D-10 status is granted to foreigners with specialized knowledge, skills, or abilities who seek employment in Korea.

This guide covers D-10 visa eligibility, required documents, application procedures, and how to transition to an E-7 work visa after securing employment — updated for 2026.

## What is the D-10 Job Seeker Visa?

The D-10 visa is a temporary stay permit designed for qualified foreign professionals actively seeking employment in Korea.

Key features:
- **Duration:** Initially 6 months, extendable up to 1 year total
- **Permitted activities:** Job interviews, recruitment processes, networking
- **Not permitted:** Regular paid employment (salary), business operation
- **Purpose:** Transition to E-7, E-1 through E-6, or other work visas upon job offer

The D-10 is not a work visa itself — it's a bridge visa that allows you to legally stay in Korea while pursuing employment. Once you receive a job offer, you must change your status to the appropriate work visa immediately.

## D-10 Visa Eligibility Requirements

Eligibility falls into three main categories:

**Category 1: Highly Educated Professionals**
- Bachelor's degree or higher from a domestic or overseas university
- Fields include engineering, IT, humanities, business, and more

**Category 2: Experienced Professionals**
- 5+ years of relevant work experience
- Holders of professional licenses or certifications

**Category 3: Special Programs**
- Participants in Ministry of Justice-designated talent programs
- Global startup visa (D-10 specialized track) applicants

## Required Documents for D-10 Visa Application

Documents needed when applying at an immigration office or Korean consulate:

- Visa application form
- Passport (valid for at least 6 months)
- Passport-size photo
- Highest academic degree certificate or graduation certificate
- Academic transcripts
- Work experience certificates (for experience-based applicants)
- Job search activity plan (intended industry, activities)
- Proof of financial ability (bank statement)
- Health insurance enrollment or plan

Documents issued abroad require apostille certification or notarization.

## D-10 Application Procedure

**Changing status from within Korea:**
1. Make an appointment at [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr)
2. Submit status change application and documents
3. Await review (typically 2–4 weeks)
4. Receive D-10 status

**Applying from abroad (new visa):**
1. Apply at nearest Korean consulate
2. Submit documents and await review
3. Enter Korea with D-10 visa

After entering Korea, register as a foreign resident within 90 days.

## Permitted and Prohibited Activities Under D-10

**Permitted:**
- Attending job interviews and recruitment processes
- Internships (unpaid or stipend-based)
- Networking and career exploration activities
- Language study (secondary purpose)

**Prohibited:**
- Regular salaried employment
- Business operations
- Any economic activity beyond D-10 scope

If you start receiving a salary under a D-10 visa, you are engaging in unauthorized employment, which can result in visa cancellation and deportation.

## Transitioning from D-10 to E-7 Work Visa

Once you secure employment, follow these steps to change to E-7:

1. **Obtain employment contract** from your employer
2. **Collect employer documents** (business registration certificate, corporate registry)
3. **Visit immigration office:** File status change application
4. **Submit required documents:** Employment contract, degree certificate, experience certificate, employer documents
5. **Await processing** (typically 2–4 weeks)

E-7 visas are categorized by occupation — make sure to identify the correct E-7 subcategory for your position.

## D-10 Visa Extension

If you haven't found employment within 6 months, you may apply for an extension.

- **File extension** before your current stay expires at the immigration office
- **Show evidence** of active job search (interview records, application history)
- **Maximum stay:** Up to 1 year total including extension

Keep records of all job applications and interviews — these are critical for extension approval.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1. Can I do part-time work on a D-10 visa?**

No, regular paid work including part-time jobs is not permitted on a D-10 visa. Unpaid internships with Ministry of Justice approval may be possible.

**Q2. Can I apply for D-10 immediately after graduating from a Korean university?**

Yes, recent graduates from Korean universities can change status to D-10 for job searching purposes.

**Q3. How long does it take to change from D-10 to E-7?**

With complete documentation, processing typically takes 2–4 weeks. Additional reviews may extend this timeline.

**Q4. Can I start a business on a D-10 visa?**

No. Business operations require a D-8 (Corporate Investment) visa.

**Q5. Can D-10 stay history count toward F-2-7 points?**

D-10 stay history may contribute to F-2-7 point calculations. Consult a specialist to assess your total score.
