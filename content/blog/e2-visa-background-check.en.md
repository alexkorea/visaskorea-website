# E-2 Visa Education Requirements and Criminal Background Check: The Decisive Points in Actual Review

The E-2 conversation instruction visa is determined almost entirely by two documents: the original bachelor's degree certificate and the criminal background check.

Applicants must be nationals of one of seven designated countries (United States, Canada, United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa) and hold a four-year bachelor's degree or higher from that same country.

This guide covers the actual scope of accepted educational credentials, criminal background check issuance and apostille processing, the academic verification procedure, and the points where applicants most often get stuck in practice.

<h2 id="section-1">The Actual Scope of E-2 Visa Education Requirements</h2>

### Which Degrees Are Recognized

Under [Attached Table 1-2 of the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Control Act](https://www.law.go.kr), the E-2 (conversation instruction) visa is issued only to nationals of countries where the relevant foreign language is the native language, who have graduated from a university or higher in that country and hold at least a bachelor's degree.

The key point is that **nationality and the country of degree acquisition must match**.

For example, a U.S. national who received a bachelor's degree in the United Kingdom would, in principle, satisfy the E-2 requirements.

However, a U.K. national who earned a bachelor's degree in the Philippines would be blocked, since the degree was not obtained in a designated country.

In practice, if this element is weak, the application is rejected at the document intake stage.

### Online Degrees and Double Majors

One commonly overlooked area is online degrees.

Degrees earned through purely online programs are not, as a rule, recognized; a certain proportion of in-person attendance must be included.

Double majors or minors do not in themselves count as extra credit, but the major must be clearly stated on the degree certificate.

Proof of degree cannot consist of just a copy of the diploma — **an official degree certificate issued directly by the university (including an official transcript)** is required.

If the school name, major, or graduation date is missing, a request for additional documentation will follow.

> **Caution:** A two-year Associate Degree does not satisfy E-2 requirements. Even three-year bachelor's degrees from some countries may or may not be recognized, so prior review is necessary.

<h2 id="section-2">Criminal Background Check Issuance and Apostille Processing</h2>

### Differences in Issuing Authorities by Country

The criminal background check must be issued by a federal or national-level agency of the applicant's country of nationality.

Documents issued by state-level or local police departments are generally not accepted.

| Country | Issuing Authority | Document Name |
|---------|-------------------|---------------|
| United States | FBI | FBI Identity History Summary |
| Canada | RCMP | Criminal Record Check |
| United Kingdom | ACRO | Police Certificate |
| Australia | AFP | National Police Check |
| New Zealand | Ministry of Justice | Criminal Conviction History |
| Ireland | Garda Síochána | Police Certificate |
| South Africa | SAPS | Police Clearance Certificate |

Only documents issued **within the past six months** are valid.

The issuance process alone takes 4 to 12 weeks on average, so to keep your visa timeline on track, this is the very first document you should start on.

### The Difference Between Apostille and Consular Confirmation

The criminal background check cannot be submitted as issued.

If the country is a **member of the Hague Apostille Convention**, an Apostille must be obtained; if it is not a member, consular confirmation from a Korean consulate is required.

Among the seven designated countries, Canada joined the Apostille Convention relatively recently, so the processing method has changed.

The apostille procedure for Canadian-issued documents varies slightly by province, so it is necessary to confirm the latest steps.

> **Practical Tip:** Using a Channeler for the FBI criminal background check speeds up issuance, but the apostille must be obtained separately from the U.S. Department of State. If this process gets tangled, delays of over a month are common.

<h2 id="section-3">Academic Verification and Social Security Number Authentication</h2>

### Methods of Authenticating the Degree Certificate

The degree certificate, like the criminal background check, also requires an apostille or consular confirmation.

For U.S. degrees, the standard flow is to obtain a Notary stamp from the issuing university and then receive an apostille from the relevant state government.

In the U.K., the [Legalisation Office](https://www.gov.uk/get-document-legalised) handles apostille processing in one consolidated step.

For Canada and Australia, the school itself often handles the authentication procedure directly.

If a degree is forged or contains false information, academic credential forgery constitutes the crime of forging private documents under [Article 231 of the Criminal Act](https://www.law.go.kr), and may also result in a permanent entry ban going forward.

### Matching English Names with the Passport

A frequently overlooked area is ensuring the English name on the degree certificate, criminal background check, and passport all match.

If the middle name appears in full on the degree but only as an initial on the passport, additional documents proving identity may be required.

If this is weak, the review is put on hold, and the entire entry schedule is pushed back during the supplemental documentation period.

<h2 id="section-4">Eligibility of Hiring Academies / Schools and Review of Employment Contracts</h2>

### Eligibility of the Hiring Institution

For E-2 visas, the **qualifications of the hiring institution** are reviewed just as closely as those of the applicant.

Private academies must be officially registered under the [Act on the Establishment and Operation of Private Teaching Institutes and Extracurricular Lessons](https://www.law.go.kr), and academies with a history of closure or administrative sanctions face restrictions on their ability to invite foreign instructors.

Native-speaking assistant teachers in elementary, middle, and high schools are typically recruited through official programs such as EPIK or GEPIK.

For university-affiliated language institutes, it must be clear whether the institute is under the school's umbrella and what the instructor's status is.

### Common Sticking Points in Employment Contracts

The employment contract must clearly specify **weekly teaching hours, salary, contract duration, and work location**.

For academies operating multiple branches, if the teaching location is not clearly stated in the contract, it can become a problem as activity exceeding the authorized scope.

In actual review, examiners verify whether the work location stated in the contract matches the address on the academy's registration certificate.

If this explanation is insufficient, the application is rejected.

<h2 id="section-5">Application Procedure and Processing Time</h2>

### Flow of the Certificate for Confirmation of Visa Issuance Application

The E-2 visa issuance process proceeds in the following order.

| Step | Responsible Party | Processing Time (Average) |
|------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| Hiring confirmation and contract execution | Hiring institution | 1–2 weeks |
| Preparation of criminal background check and degree certificate | Applicant | 4–12 weeks |
| Apostille processing | Applicant (country of nationality) | 1–3 weeks |
| Certificate for Confirmation of Visa Issuance application | Hiring institution (jurisdictional immigration office) | 2–4 weeks |
| Visa issuance (overseas mission) | Applicant | 1–2 weeks |
| Alien registration after entry | Applicant | Within 90 days of entry |

Processing times vary by immigration office, and we identify the fastest available option for you.

### Procedures After Entry

After entry, alien registration must be completed at the jurisdictional Immigration & Foreigner Office within 90 days.

Visit by prior reservation through [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr); a tuberculosis screening certificate is required at registration.

For E-2 visas, **drug testing for resident foreigners** is conducted as part of the quarantine procedure, and a positive result constitutes immediate grounds for revocation of status.

<h2 id="section-6">Reasons for Rejection or Return and Reapplication Strategy</h2>

### The Most Common Reasons for Rejection

The rejection reasons most frequently seen in practice are as follows.

- Minor criminal history on the background check (DUI, assault, etc.)
- Mismatch between the issuing university on the degree certificate and the registry
- Missing apostille or formatting errors
- History of administrative sanctions against the hiring institution
- Insufficient proof of identity due to inconsistent English name notation

DUI history in particular, even if it appears minor, is treated strictly for the conversation instruction visa.

In recent similar cases, even a single DUI record over five years old has sometimes passed after submitting additional explanatory documents, but outcomes vary case by case.

Whether this applies accurately to your situation requires expert confirmation.

### What to Check When Reapplying

Once rejected, reapplying on the same grounds will produce the same result.

The first thing to examine is the specific points raised in the rejection notice, and you must supplement these with objective evidence.

Clear evidence addressing the core reason is stronger than writing at length.

> **Caution:** A rejection history affects all future visa applications. Before repeatedly reapplying based on your own judgment, start with an analysis of the reasons.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

**Q1. I am a U.S. citizen but received my bachelor's degree from a Korean university. Can I apply for an E-2?**

In principle, the E-2 requires a degree obtained in the applicant's country of nationality.

However, cases such as receiving a bachelor's degree in Korea before acquiring U.S. citizenship involve separate considerations, so the timing of degree acquisition and the timing of citizenship acquisition should be reviewed together.

**Q2. My criminal background check shows an old minor offense. Does this mean automatic rejection?**

It depends on the type of record, when it occurred, and the number of incidents.

A single minor offense with a sufficient period elapsed may be eligible for explanation, but records involving violence, sex offenses, or drugs are practically very difficult to pass.

You should first confirm your record accurately before making a judgment.

**Q3. Can I submit only the original English document without an apostille?**

No.

Without an apostille or consular confirmation, the document itself is not recognized and will be returned at the intake stage.

This is often where schedules slip by more than a month.

**Q4. Where do I get the tuberculosis screening?**

It must be done at a medical institution designated by the Ministry of Justice.

The list of designated hospitals can be found in the announcements on [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) and is submitted together with the alien registration.

**Q5. Can I teach somewhere other than the contracted academy on an E-2 visa?**

Activities outside the contracted hiring institution require **permission for activities outside one's status of stay**.

Teaching at another academy without permission constitutes unauthorized activity and is grounds for deportation.

**Q6. Can I bring my family (spouse, children)?**

The spouse and minor children of an E-2 holder may accompany them on an F-3 (dependent) visa.

When applying for the F-3, an apostille on the family relationship certificate is also separately required.
