# Chinese-Korean F-4 Prostitution Case Review: Visa Cancellation and Entry Ban Practice

When F-4 Chinese-Korean residents are caught for prostitution, the criminal penalty is only half of the picture — a separate case review by the Korea Immigration Service follows, leading to status cancellation and an entry ban.

This applies to anyone holding Overseas Korean (F-4) status who has been reported at any stage — investigation, indictment, summary order, or suspension of indictment — for violating the Act on the Punishment of Arrangement of Commercial Sex Acts.

Below, we walk through the disposition flow, what immigration reviewers actually look at, typical entry ban periods, the likelihood of re-entry, and the explanation points that most often trip people up.

<h2 id="section-1">Disposition Flow After Being Caught for Prostitution as F-4</h2>

### Criminal proceedings and immigration proceedings run on separate tracks

This is where most people misunderstand the process.

A suspension of indictment or a fine from the prosecutor does NOT mean the immigration side is over.

The criminal outcome flows into immigration as referral material, and the Korea Immigration Service then runs its own administrative disposition on that basis.

In practice, the most common pattern is paying the fine, relaxing, and then receiving a status cancellation notice about a month later.

> **Caution:** Closing the criminal case is not the same as closing the immigration case review. The exact timing of when referring authorities forward case files to immigration varies, and you should check the status of your own case through [Hi Korea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) or your local immigration office.

### Mandatory referral → Case review → Disposition notice

The typical sequence runs like this.

1. Police investigation and booking
2. Transfer to prosecution and disposition (fine, suspension of indictment, summary, formal indictment)
3. Mandatory referral to immigration under Article 84 of the Immigration Act
4. Case review opened by the competent Korea Immigration Service office
5. Decision among status cancellation, departure order, or forced deportation
6. Registration of the entry ban

Stage 4 — the case review — is where outcomes diverge the most.

<h2 id="section-2">Standards for F-4 Status Cancellation and Forced Deportation</h2>

### Article 89 of the Immigration Act — grounds for status cancellation

Unlike permanent residency (F-5), F-4 status is subject to a relatively broad set of cancellation grounds.

Prostitution falls under Article 46(1)(13) of the Immigration Act (anyone who harms public order or good morals) and (3) (sentenced to imprisonment or a certain level of fine).

The key point for F-4 is this.

Even for Overseas Koreans, prostitution, gambling, and drug cases will almost always shake the underlying status during case review.

| Disposition Type | Applicable Situation | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Status cancellation + departure order | First offense, simple purchase, suspension of indictment or fine | Voluntary departure followed by entry ban |
| Forced deportation | Arrangement, repeat offense, multiple incidents | Detention then forced repatriation with long entry ban |
| Status cancellation + shortened stay period | Minor case, well-prepared explanation | Short-term departure with re-application reviewed later |

The specific application depends on how the incident was uncovered, the content of statements made, and the suspect's role (buyer, seller, arranger). Exact classification should be confirmed with the competent authority.

### The difference between buyers and arrangers

Even within the same category of prostitution, simple purchase and arrangement are classified completely differently in case review.

Buyers fall under Article 21 of the Anti-Prostitution Act, while arrangers fall under Article 19 — and arrangement, employment, and provision of premises carry far heavier sentences and far longer entry bans.

In particular, when F-4 holders are caught as sellers in venues like karaoke rooms or massage parlors, statements made at the scene of investigation flow directly into the case review file, so the weight of that first statement is enormous.

<h2 id="section-3">Entry Ban Period and Registration Standards</h2>

### Typical entry ban periods

The length of the entry ban for prostitution cases is registered differently depending on severity.

The ranges most commonly seen in practice are as follows.

| Case | Entry Ban Period (Observed in Practice) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| First-time buyer, suspension of indictment | Around 1 year | On voluntary departure |
| Buyer with fine | 1–3 years | Depending on sentence and statements |
| Caught as seller | 3–5 years | Applies even with Overseas Korean status |
| Arrangement, employment, premises | 5–10 years or permanent | Combined with forced deportation |

Exact periods depend on the classification in the schedule to Article 17-2 of the Enforcement Rules of the Immigration Act and the individual case, so check your own situation against materials from the [Ministry of Justice](https://www.moj.go.kr) or the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr).

### When the entry ban gets registered

One thing many people miss is the exact moment the entry ban is registered.

The usual pattern is that the entry ban locks into the system the moment you voluntarily depart after status cancellation, but in some cases registration has already happened before departure — meaning it can affect your next ticket booking immediately.

The most common failure pattern in practice is someone trying to re-enter within a few days of departure and being stopped at the airport.

> **Practice Tip:** As soon as you receive a status cancellation notice, do not lock in a departure date right away. Check whether the entry ban has been registered and for how long, then design the voluntary departure schedule around that information.

<h2 id="section-4">What Case Reviewers Actually Look At</h2>

### Organizing your statement matters more than paperwork

A case review is not a criminal trial.

Because the disposition is decided by an immigration reviewer rather than a judge, a tight, accurate statement framing the facts changes outcomes far more than a lengthy letter of apology.

Reviewers focus mainly on these items.

- Consistency between statements made during investigation and the prosecutor's final disposition
- Number of incidents, duration, and amounts involved
- Whether the role was buyer, seller, or arranger
- Length of stay in Korea and family relationships (for F-4, impact on family members beyond the principal)
- Intent and timing of voluntary departure
- Prior record of similar offenses

If this part is weak, the same factual situation can move up one full level in severity.

### Explanation points that often get tangled

The point that gets most tangled in real reviews — even when the case looks simple — is changing your statement.

Admitting the facts at the police stage and then denying them at the immigration stage creates a serious credibility problem.

Acknowledging the facts while precisely framing the circumstances, remorse, and prevention plan tends to work much more favorably toward a reduced disposition.

In a recent similar case, a first-time buyer ended up with a disposition one level heavier because of statement reversal. The specific explanation strategy for your case can be advised after a case review.

<h2 id="section-5">Re-entry and the Possibility of Reacquiring F-4</h2>

### Petition to lift the entry ban

Even before the entry ban period expires, a petition to lift the entry ban may be possible depending on the case.

The grounds are Articles 11 and 12 of the Immigration Act, along with internal immigration guidelines.

That said, prostitution cases are among the harder ones to have lifted, and they are reviewed case by case when there are grounds such as family reunification, serious circumstances, or humanitarian reasons.

The three pivots of a lifting petition are the timing, the reasoning, and the supporting evidence — and the right timing for any individual case needs separate review.

### Re-applying for F-4 after the entry ban is lifted

Even if the petition is granted, F-4 status is not automatically restored.

You have to go through the F-4 re-application process at an overseas mission, and at that stage your prior case record comes back up as review material.

| Stage | Review Points | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lifting the entry ban | Grounds, timing, evidence | Acceptance varies by case |
| Re-applying for visa (F-4) | Case history, time elapsed | Reviewed by overseas mission |
| Post-entry registration | Possibility of conditional terms | Conditional approval possible |

If conditional approval is attached, any repeat offense of the same type during the specified period can trigger immediate cancellation, so the application strategy needs to account for these conditions from the start.

### Switching to other statuses such as H-2

It is common for Overseas Koreans who have lost F-4 to attempt re-entry under a different status such as H-2 (Working Visit).

The key point is this.

While the entry ban is active, entry itself is blocked regardless of the visa category.

After the ban is lifted or expires, designing re-entry under a status that fits the nature of your case is the practical path forward — and which status is realistic depends on your personal history and family situation.

<h2 id="section-6">FAQ</h2>

**Q1. Can I just pay the fine and skip leaving the country?**
Fine payment and immigration disposition are separate. Once a status cancellation notice is issued, the case ends in either voluntary departure within the set period or forced deportation.

**Q2. Will F-4 still be cancelled even with a suspension of indictment?**
Suspension of indictment is also subject to mandatory referral. Even when the criminal disposition is light, status cancellation in the case review is the typical result.

**Q3. Is it better to change my statement from the investigation?**
In practice, it works against you. Building consistent statements and then concretely laying out the circumstances, remorse, and prevention plan helps more in reducing the disposition.

**Q4. Does voluntary departure shorten the entry ban period?**
Voluntary departure is generally evaluated more favorably than forced deportation. The size of the reduction, however, varies case by case.

**Q5. Will this affect my family (spouse, children)?**
The direct impact is on the principal's F-4 only, and accompanying family members are reviewed separately by status. If the family's status is dependent on the principal, a chain effect can occur.

**Q6. Is entry to Korea really 100% blocked during the entry ban period?**
It is blocked at the system level. Exceptional cases such as humanitarian grounds require separate procedures, and actual feasibility depends on case-by-case review.
