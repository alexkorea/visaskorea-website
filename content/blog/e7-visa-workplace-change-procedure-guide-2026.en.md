# Complete Guide to E-7 Visa Job Changes (Workplace Change) Procedures and Key Considerations

Changing jobs on an E-7 visa is not a simple notification — it requires **prior approval from the Immigration Office**.

This applies to every E-7 holder leaving their current employer to work for a new one, and a separate procedure is required even when the occupational code stays the same.

This guide walks you through the conditions, documents, the points where applicants most often get stuck in practice, and the timing of resignation and application — all in one place.

<h2 id="section-1">E-7 Job Changes Require Approval, Not Just Notification</h2>

This is the single most common misconception among foreign workers.

Some visas like E-9 or H-2 can be handled with after-the-fact notification, but **E-7 is different**.

### Legal Basis Under the Immigration Control Act

Under Article 21 of the [Immigration Control Act](https://www.law.go.kr), any foreign national who wishes to change a workplace within the scope of their status of stay must obtain prior approval from the Minister of Justice.

In other words, approval must come through **before** you start at the new company in order to work legally.

In practice, the most common violation is doing this in the reverse order and getting caught.

### Prior Approval vs. After-the-Fact Notification

| Category | Prior Approval | After-the-Fact Notification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal basis | Article 21(1) | Exceptions under the Enforcement Decree |
| Timing | Before starting at the new company | Within 15 days of the change |
| E-7 application | Prior approval as a rule | Limited exceptions for select specialists |
| If violated | Possible revocation of status, departure order | Administrative fine |

> **Note:** Whether you fall under prior approval or after-the-fact notification depends on your occupational code and stay history. You should confirm your exact classification through [Hi Korea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) or with a qualified professional.

<h2 id="section-2">Conditions for Workplace Change Approval</h2>

No matter how many documents you gather, the application is meaningless if these conditions aren't met.

The first things to check are whether the new company meets E-7 requirements and whether your academic background and work experience align with the position.

### Requirements for the New Company

E-7 is not for ordinary jobs — it must be **specialized work matching the occupational code**.

Core requirements the new company must meet:

- Compliance with the Korean Employment Protection screening standards (ratio of Korean nationals employed)
- Revenue and capital thresholds
- Enrollment in the four major social insurances
- Job duties consistent with the E-7 occupational code

If this side is weak, approval is difficult no matter how strong your own credentials are.

### Requirements for the Applicant

Required education and experience vary by occupational code.

Typically, you need a bachelor's degree plus one year of experience, or a master's degree or higher; some occupations also accept five or more years of practical experience.

The key point is **whether the new occupational code connects with your major and career history**.

If your duties at the old company are too distant from the new role, approval can be blocked even within the same E-7 category.

### Salary Requirements

Each E-7 occupation has a recommended salary threshold, adjusted annually in line with Gross National Income (GNI).

The exact threshold that applies this year varies case by case, so it's safest to confirm during a consultation.

<h2 id="section-3">E-7 Workplace Change Application Procedure</h2>

If you get the sequence wrong, you often have to start over from scratch.

### Step-by-Step Flow

1. Receive a formal job offer (employment contract) from the new company.
2. Confirm that the new company meets E-7 employment requirements.
3. Complete resignation procedures with the current company and the loss-of-coverage filing for the four major insurances.
4. Prepare and submit the workplace change approval application via [Hi Korea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).
5. Wait for the review and decision from the competent Immigration Office.
6. Once approved, start work at the new company and update your Alien Registration Card details.

### Processing Time

Processing times differ by Immigration Office, and additional review may be triggered depending on the occupational code and company size.

The fastest cases can be wrapped up within two weeks, while slower ones may take more than a month.

Identifying the fastest competent office and submitting strategically is an area where practical experience really matters.

### Required Documents

| Document | Issuing Source | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Integrated application form | [Hi Korea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) | Form available for download |
| Passport and Alien Registration Card copies | Applicant | Check validity dates |
| Employment contract with new company | New employer | Duties and salary specified |
| Business registration certificate | New company | Most recent version |
| Certificate of employment / career certificate | Current company | Including resignation date |
| Diploma / graduation certificate | School | With apostille or consular confirmation |
| Certificate of tax payment / income | Tax office | From current company |

> **Practical Tip:** Even with a full document set, the **job description in the new employment contract** is the most decisive piece. If the description is vague or reads like simple labor, you'll get a request for supplementation no matter how complete the rest of your documents are.

<h2 id="section-4">Where Applicants Get Stuck in Practice</h2>

The patterns behind supplementation requests after submission are fairly predictable.

### The Gap Between Resignation and Application

If you leave the current company and then drag your feet on the new application, you can end up with prolonged unauthorized activity or unemployment.

E-7 is fundamentally a visa that requires you to have an employer.

If you can't secure workplace change approval within a certain period after resignation, you may need to change your status of stay or depart the country.

### Occupational Code Mismatch

If you were in marketing at your previous company but the new company has you doing engineering work, this is effectively treated as a different occupation — even within the same E-7 category.

In this case, it's not a simple workplace change; an **occupational change review** is added.

Without the academic background or experience required for the new occupation, the chance of rejection is high.

### New Company Falling Short

Companies with low revenue, an insufficient number of Korean employees, or that aren't enrolled in the four major insurances are not qualified to hire on E-7.

In reality, applications are very often blocked by the company's side of the equation, no matter how strong the candidate is.

Reviewing the new company's qualifications before applying saves a lot of wasted time.

<h2 id="section-5">Timing: Resignation and Application</h2>

This is the area that gets tangled most often in practice.

### When You Can Apply Before Resignation

In theory, you can receive a new employment contract while still employed at your current company and submit the change approval application.

However, if Immigration asks for confirmation of your resignation date from the previous employer, the supplementation process can drag on.

The safer approach is generally to submit **once your resignation date is finalized and your new start date is clearly set**.

### The Gap Period After Resignation

Even if your E-7 visa still has remaining validity, a prolonged period without an employer raises flags with Immigration.

In practice, it's standard to complete the workplace change approval within one to two months of resignation.

If that window isn't realistic, switching temporarily to D-10 (job-seeker visa) is one option.

Whether D-10 conversion fits your situation will be reviewed together during a consultation.

### Working at the New Company Before Approval

This is the most dangerous pattern.

Starting work at the new company before workplace change approval comes through can be caught as **activity outside your authorized status**.

If caught, the consequences can range from fines to revocation of status and future restrictions on visa issuance — something to avoid at all costs.

> **Caution:** The mindset of "a few days early at the new company should be fine" is the most dangerous one. Immigration tracks the actual start date through your four-major-insurance enrollment date and salary payment records.

<h2 id="section-6">Extending Stay After an E-7 Job Change and Future Pathways</h2>

Getting workplace change approval isn't the end of the story.

When it's time to extend your stay, you'll be re-evaluated based on the new company.

### Review Points at the Next Extension

- Actual duties performed at the new company
- Salary payment records (bank transactions)
- Continued enrollment in the four major insurances
- Diligence in tax payments

Weakness in any of these can cause problems at the next extension.

### Connections to F-2-7 Points System and F-5 Permanent Residency

E-7 work experience is a core scoring item in the [F-2-7 Resident Visa points system](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) and F-5 permanent residency applications.

When changing workplaces, **whether continuous career credit is recognized** can swing one way or the other.

A job change within the same occupational code preserves career continuity, but introducing an occupational change can reset the calculation.

If you're planning for permanent residency, even the job change itself needs to be approached strategically.

### Impact on Accompanying Family Stay

If your family is in Korea on F-3 (dependent) visas, any instability with your E-7 ripples through to their stay as well.

Delays or rejections of your workplace change approval affect your family's visas too.

If this side is shaky, family stay schedules may also need to be reworked.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

### Q1. When changing jobs on an E-7 visa, must I get approval before starting at the new company?

Yes — as a rule, prior approval is required.

Under Article 21 of the [Immigration Control Act](https://www.law.go.kr), approval must be obtained before changing workplaces, and working before approval can be caught as activity outside your authorized status.

### Q2. Is the procedure simpler if I move within the same occupational code?

It's relatively simpler, but not exempt.

Even with the same occupation, the new company's qualifications and your salary and contract terms are reviewed afresh.

That said, supplementation requests tend to be fewer than when an occupational change is also involved.

### Q3. How long can I leave a gap after resigning?

There's no legally defined number of days, but in practice it's safest to **secure your next workplace within one to two months**.

If it stretches longer, you may need to consider switching to something like D-10.

The safe gap period for your specific case is best confirmed during a consultation.

### Q4. Can I still make an E-7 job change if the new company is small?

Even small companies can qualify if they meet the core requirements on revenue, ratio of Korean employees, and the four major insurances.

That said, newly established entities or companies with minimal revenue tend to face tougher scrutiny.

If the new company's qualifications are borderline, pre-screening before applying is essential.

### Q5. What happens if the workplace change approval is rejected?

It depends on the reason for rejection.

If the issue is with the company's qualifications, you can reapply when switching to a different employer; if it's with the suitability of your occupation, the occupational code itself needs to be reconsidered.

There's a response deadline starting from the date you receive the rejection notice, so you need to move quickly.

### Q6. If my salary drops right after the job change, does it affect my visa?

E-7 has recommended salary thresholds by occupation, and falling below that line can create problems at your next extension.

This year's applicable threshold and the recommended salary for your specific occupation vary case by case and should be confirmed during a consultation.
