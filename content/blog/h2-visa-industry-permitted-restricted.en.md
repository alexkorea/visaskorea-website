# H-2 Working Visit Visa: Permitted and Restricted Industries — Complete Guide (2026 Practitioner's Handbook)

The H-2 Working Visit visa allows overseas Koreans with foreign nationality to work only within designated permitted industries.

It is available to overseas Koreans aged 18 or older from China, Russia, and the CIS region. Even within the unskilled labor category, work is permitted only within authorized industry codes.

This article covers permitted and restricted industries, common pain points when changing workplaces, and the reporting obligations you can't afford to overlook.

<h2 id="section-1">The Basic Structure of H-2 Permitted Industries</h2>

The H-2 visa is not a blanket authorization that opens every industry.

Under Annex 1-2 of the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act and Ministry of Justice notifications, permitted industries are separately designated. Working outside of those industries immediately constitutes out-of-status activity.

### Broad Categories of Permitted Industries

Here's the key point.

H-2 permitted industries are broadly divided into eligible unskilled labor sectors and certain service and production sectors.

- Manufacturing (mainly workplaces with fewer than 300 regular employees or capital under KRW 8 billion)
- Agriculture, livestock, and fisheries
- Construction (subject to the Construction Industry Employment Registration System)
- Selected service industries (restaurants, lodging, domestic care and nursing, etc.)

What trips people up most in practice is starting a job without first confirming whether the workplace actually falls under a permitted industry code.

### Industry Classification Follows the Korean Standard Industrial Classification (KSIC)

The first thing to check is the workplace's KSIC code.

Two businesses may share a similar name, but if their KSIC codes differ, so does their eligibility.

This is especially common with cafés, bars, and karaoke venues — they may look alike on the surface, but a different code can mean H-2 work isn't permitted.

> **Caution:** Permitted-industry notifications are partially revised each year. Always confirm the latest version through the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr) or [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) announcements.

<h2 id="section-2">Common Pitfalls in Manufacturing and Construction</h2>

Manufacturing and construction are where most H-2 overseas Koreans end up working.

But even within the same H-2 visa, the registration procedures for these two sectors differ.

### Manufacturing Workplace Requirements

Not every manufacturing job qualifies in practice.

- Number of regular employees
- Capital or revenue thresholds
- Recognition as a workplace eligible to hire foreign workers

If even one of these three falls short, H-2 employment is blocked — even in manufacturing.

We often see overseas Koreans who joined small fabrication shops only to discover later, at the reporting stage, that the workplace was never eligible to hire foreign workers in the first place.

### Construction Industry Employment Registration System

Working in construction takes more than just getting hired.

You'll first need a Construction Industry Employment Registration Card, issued through the [Construction Workers Mutual Aid Association](https://www.cw.or.kr) or an employment center.

Working at a construction site without this registration will be treated as out-of-status activity if discovered.

In actual reviews, whether you hold the registration card is one of the core verification points.

> **Practitioner's Tip:** Construction registration requires completing prior training first. Keep in mind that immediate on-site deployment right after entry is generally not feasible.

<h2 id="section-3">Industries Where H-2 Work Is Absolutely Prohibited</h2>

The point isn't simply "unskilled labor is fine."

Even within unskilled labor, certain areas are explicitly off-limits to H-2 holders.

### Representative Restricted Industries

| Restricted Sector | Reason | Typical Enforcement Patterns |
|---|---|---|
| Entertainment bars and hostess clubs | Restrictions on adult-entertainment businesses | Caught after joining a place believed to be a café or restaurant |
| Gambling and speculative businesses | Public-interest concerns | Currency-exchange assistants at gaming arcades, etc. |
| Certain transportation | Conflict with license/qualification requirements | Cargo driving, designated driver services |
| Large-scale manufacturing | Workplace size exceeds threshold | Joining workplaces with 300+ employees |
| Office and administrative roles | Outside the H-2 scope of activity | Hires labeled as "office assistants" |

It may look simple on the surface, but an accurate determination requires reviewing both the KSIC code and the actual nature of the business.

### The Trap of "Simple Assistant Work That Looks Like an Office Job"

This is usually where people get caught.

Because the H-2 visa centers on unskilled labor, any work that looks administrative, managerial, or technical is easily reclassified as out-of-status.

If it's unclear whether your work qualifies as unskilled labor or office assistance, the classification criteria shift slightly each year — getting an accurate read on your specific situation this year really requires a consultation.

<h2 id="section-4">Reporting Obligations When Changing Workplaces</h2>

Unlike the E-9, the H-2 visa allows relatively free workplace changes.

But "relatively free" does not mean "no reporting required."

### Workplace Change Reporting Procedure

| Step | Content | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Step 1 | Resign from current workplace | Immediately upon resignation |
| Step 2 | Decide on new workplace | No fixed deadline |
| Step 3 | Submit workplace change report | Within 15 days of the triggering event |
| Step 4 | Update Alien Registration Card information | Processed concurrently with the change report |

In actual review practice, this 15-day window is enforced very strictly.

A delay of just a few days can result in a fine, and repeat lapses can weigh against you in future stay extension reviews.

### What Happens If You Miss the Change Report

Here's what people most commonly overlook.

- Missed report → administrative fine
- Move to a workplace outside permitted industries → out-of-status activity
- Repeated lapses → deductions in future extension and re-entry reviews

More important than the paperwork is the habit of handling it yourself the moment a change occurs.

<h2 id="section-5">Common Out-of-Status Activity Cases During H-2 Stay</h2>

Out-of-status activity isn't just an administrative slip — it can affect future F-4 conversion and overseas Korean status reviews down the line.

### Frequently Seen Violation Patterns

The same patterns show up repeatedly in the field.

- Working at a workplace outside permitted industries
- Working at a location whose actual operations differ from its business registration
- Taking on side work in a different industry alongside the main job
- Running a business under your own name (H-2 holders generally cannot register as business owners)

Particularly risky: if the workplace is registered as a restaurant but actually operates entertainment services on the side, you could be exposed to out-of-status activity findings even if you had no idea.

### Severity of Penalties

A weak record here unravels every visa procedure that follows.

- First offense: notification disposition, administrative fine
- Repeat offenses: forced deportation or departure order
- F-4 conversion application: grounds for denial

The legal basis can be found at the [Korean Law Information Center](https://www.law.go.kr) under Articles 17 and 18 of the Immigration Act.

<h2 id="section-6">When Considering Conversion from H-2 to F-4 or Overseas Korean Status</h2>

For many H-2 holders, F-4 conversion is the ultimate goal.

In that context, your industry history is far more than a record — it's a core piece of evidence.

### Why Industry History Matters in F-4 Conversion

Which industries you worked in during your H-2 stay directly affects whether you meet F-4 eligibility.

- A sufficient track record of lawful employment
- No history of out-of-status activity
- Enrollment history in the four major insurances

All three are evaluated together.

In a recent similar case, an overseas Korean who hopped between short-term gigs and missed several change reports had their F-4 conversion deferred due to insufficient documentation.

Whether your own track record is strong enough for F-4 conversion depends heavily on individual employment history, so a separate review is necessary.

### What Kind of Industry Experience Helps Most

Rather than dragging this out — here's the essence.

- Long-term employment at a stable workplace
- Enrollment history in the four major insurances
- Continuity within the same industry

In general, "how you worked" carries more weight than "how long you worked."

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions About H-2 Industries (FAQ)</h2>

### Q1. Can I work part-time at a café on an H-2 visa?

Cafés are typically classified under restaurant services in the KSIC, but if the actual operation has bar-like characteristics, the location may fall outside permitted industries.

Even for a café, checking the workplace's industry code comes first.

### Q2. Can an H-2 holder register their own business?

In principle, the H-2 is a work visa — not an investment or startup visa.

Registering a business in your own name is not consistent with this status, and even using a family member's name as a workaround can create problems if you are the de facto operator.

### Q3. Does changing workplaces often hurt my F-4 conversion chances?

The moves themselves aren't a direct deduction, but accumulated missed change reports definitely work against you.

The key habit is filing the change report within 15 days every single time you move.

### Q4. Do I need separate registration to work at construction sites?

Yes — you must be registered under the Construction Industry Employment Registration System and complete the prior training first.

Working on-site without registration will be classified as out-of-status activity.

### Q5. What happens if my workplace shuts down while I'm working in a permitted industry?

The visa itself isn't immediately cancelled, but you'll need to move to another permitted-industry workplace and file a change report within 15 days.

Extended periods of unemployment can weigh against you in extension reviews.

### Q6. I've received a prior disposition related to my industry of work — is F-4 conversion still possible?

The outcome depends on the type and timing of the disposition and your subsequent lawful employment history.

An accurate read on your specific case requires reviewing both the disposition details and your overall stay history together.
