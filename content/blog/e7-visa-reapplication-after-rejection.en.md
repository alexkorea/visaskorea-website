# E-7 Visa Rejection Reasons and Reapplication Strategies — The 7 Most Common Roadblocks in Practice

With E-7 visa rejections, the bigger problem isn't the rejection itself — it's **not knowing exactly why it was rejected**.
Whether the issue is a missing document, an occupation classification problem, or an employer eligibility shortfall, the reapplication strategy changes entirely depending on the cause.
This post covers the 7 rejection reasons that come up repeatedly in real cases, along with the items you must review before reapplying.

<h2 id="section-1">Why E-7 Visa Rejections Happen More Often Than You'd Expect</h2>

### The review criteria are not fixed

The E-7 visa is assessed by the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr) based on occupation codes, but in practice, individual officers exercise considerable discretion.
Even with the same occupation and the same set of documents, outcomes can vary by immigration office and by timing.
This is especially true for occupations that have been newly added or recently revised among the 85 permitted categories — the review standards for these haven't fully settled yet.

> **Practical tip:** You can check whether your occupation falls within the 85 permitted categories via the [Ministry of Employment and Labor](https://www.moel.go.kr), but determining whether your actual job duties match the listed occupation title is difficult by comparing lists alone.
> Even a slight mismatch in occupation code can be a grounds for rejection on its own.

### Employer eligibility is reviewed at the same time

One thing many applicants overlook is the **eligibility requirements for the hiring company**.
No matter how qualified the individual applicant is, if the employer doesn't meet the requirements, that alone is grounds for rejection.
Business registration status, industry classification, headcount, and tax compliance all factor in together.

<h2 id="section-2">The 7 E-7 Visa Rejection Reasons That Recur in Practice</h2>

Most cases get caught at one of these points.
If you've received a rejection notice, first identify which of the following applies to your situation.

| # | Rejection Reason | Common Scenario |
|---|-----------------|-----------------|
| 1 | Occupation mismatch | Actual duties appear inconsistent with the applied occupation code |
| 2 | Education/experience mismatch | Degree major has weak relevance to the applied occupation |
| 3 | Salary below minimum threshold | Salary falls below the official standard, or the calculation method is unclear |
| 4 | Employer eligibility not met | Company size or industry doesn't meet the required criteria |
| 5 | Document authenticity concerns | Notarization or apostille missing for overseas-issued documents |
| 6 | Residency history issues | Prior unauthorized work or visa violations on record |
| 7 | Insufficient employment necessity justification | Weak explanation for why a foreign national must be hired |

In practice, rejection is rarely caused by just one issue.
Even when two or three problems are involved, the rejection notice often lists only one representative reason.
This is precisely what makes reapplication preparation so difficult.

<h2 id="section-3">Occupation and Education Mismatch — The Most Common Sticking Point</h2>

### If the connection between your major and your job duties isn't explained clearly, you'll be rejected

E-7 visa screening doesn't simply check whether you have a degree.
It looks substantively at **how closely your major relates to the occupation you're applying for**.
If your major isn't a clear match, you need to compensate with relevant work experience — and if that explanation is weak, the application falls apart quickly.

> **Note:** When substituting work experience for an academic degree, errors often arise in how the experience period is calculated.
> The conversion criteria differ by occupation and are subject to change, so you must verify the standards applicable at the time of your application through the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr).

### Overseas degrees require additional verification steps

Unlike domestic degrees, foreign degrees cannot be verified without notarization or an apostille.
Certain countries' university credentials are more likely to prompt officers to request additional supporting materials.
No matter how thorough your document package is, a missing notarization on a single document can bring the entire review to a halt.

<h2 id="section-4">Salary Standards and Employer Eligibility — Causes More Problems Than Documents</h2>

### The salary requirement goes beyond a simple number

The E-7 visa salary threshold must meet or exceed the standard set by the [Ministry of Employment and Labor](https://www.moel.go.kr), but simply clearing that number doesn't guarantee approval.
Officers also review the **salary structure, payment form, and the ratio of fixed to variable compensation**.
If performance bonuses make up a large portion of total pay, or if the stated salary appears to differ from the actual pay, it will be flagged.

Because the applicable salary standards change each year, confirm the current year's threshold with the relevant authority before submitting.

### Employer eligibility checklist

The hiring company must also meet the following requirements:

- Valid business registration in active standing
- Operations in an industry where foreign national employment is permitted
- Minimum headcount requirement met (varies by occupation)
- Revenue or asset threshold for the past one to two years satisfied
- No outstanding national or local tax arrears

If any one of these is not met, the application will be rejected regardless of the individual applicant's qualifications.

<h2 id="section-5">E-7 Visa Reapplication Strategy — A Step-by-Step Approach</h2>

### Step 1: Identify the exact reason for rejection

When you receive a rejection notice, the first thing to look at isn't the fact that you were rejected — it's **which specific items were flagged**.
The stated reason on the notice may not reflect the full picture.

In a recent case, the notice listed only "occupation mismatch," but the underlying issues also included problems with how the salary was structured.
A pattern of repeated rejections emerges when applicants fix only the stated reason and resubmit everything else unchanged.

### Step 2: Assess what can actually be corrected

The path forward and the feasibility of reapplication depend on the nature of the rejection.

| Rejection Type | Correctable? | Reapplication Direction |
|---------------|-------------|------------------------|
| Missing or erroneous documents | Yes | Supplement and reapply (relatively quick) |
| Occupation mismatch | Conditionally yes | Redefine job duties or review occupation code change |
| Salary below threshold | Requires employer negotiation | Revise contract and reapply |
| Employer eligibility not met | Limited | Change employer or reapply once requirements are met |
| Residency history issues | Difficult | Depends on Ministry of Justice review outcome |
| Insufficient education/experience | Long-term preparation needed | Reapply after building additional qualifying experience |

### Step 3: Decide on the right reapplication timing

Reapplying immediately after a rejection is not always the best move.
If employer eligibility or education and experience are the root cause, rushing to reapply without adequate remediation can actually work against you.
Repeated rejections on the same grounds make subsequent reviews even more stringent.

The right timing varies case by case depending on the nature of the rejection, so this is something that genuinely requires assessment based on individual circumstances.

<h2 id="section-6">Reapplication Documents — They Must Look Different from Your First Submission</h2>

### Submitting nearly the same package will not work

The most common mistake when reapplying is **resubmitting a document set that is essentially the same as the first attempt**.
Review history is on record, so officers can directly compare your previous submission.
Rather than simply adding documents, you need to restructure the entire package so that it directly addresses the cause of rejection.

### The employment certificate and necessity justification are the critical documents

In practice, these two documents are where outcomes diverge:

- **Employment certificate**: Explicitly states how the applied occupation code aligns with the applicant's actual job duties
- **Employment necessity justification**: Clearly explains why a foreign national must be hired rather than a local candidate
- **Salary basis documentation**: Provides written evidence of the salary standard and payment structure

If these explanations are weak, even an otherwise complete document set will be flagged.

> **Practical tip:** For the employment necessity justification, clearly presenting the key arguments is far more effective in actual reviews than writing at length.
> Recent review trends have shifted toward prioritizing logical consistency over document volume.

<h2 id="section-7">FAQ</h2>

**Q. Can I reapply for an E-7 visa immediately after a rejection?**

A. There is no legal waiting period — you can reapply right away.
However, if the reasons for rejection haven't been resolved, there's a high likelihood of the same outcome.
It's more realistic to first identify the cause and allow time for proper remediation.

**Q. My rejection notice lists only one reason. Is fixing just that enough?**

A. The stated reason may not be the full picture.
In practice, multiple issues are often involved even when only one representative reason appears on the notice.
It's safer to review the entire document package for any other weaknesses beyond the flagged item.

**Q. How do I verify whether my occupation falls within the 85 permitted E-7 categories?**

A. You can check the permitted occupation list through the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr) or [Ministry of Employment and Labor](https://www.moel.go.kr) official notices.
However, confirming that your actual job duties match the listed occupation title is difficult by simply comparing the list.
An incorrect occupation code match is itself a grounds for rejection.

**Q. Can I reapply for an E-7 visa if I have a problematic residency history?**

A. Prior unauthorized employment, visa violations, or a deportation history will result in a more rigorous review.
Depending on the circumstances, an entry ban period may be in effect, which can restrict your ability to reapply at all.
In these cases, unlike standard reapplication procedures, it's necessary to first review the relevant regulations through the [Korea Law Information Center](https://www.law.go.kr) and seek a preliminary review from the Ministry of Justice.

**Q. Is it harder to apply for an E-7 visa if the employer is a small company?**

A. Employer requirements vary depending on the occupation.
Some occupations have minimum headcount or revenue thresholds that small businesses may struggle to meet.
Determining whether requirements are satisfied requires reviewing both the occupation code and the company's current status together.
