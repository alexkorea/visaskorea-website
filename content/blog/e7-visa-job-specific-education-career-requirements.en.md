# E-7 Visa Education Requirements: Key Standards That Vary by Occupation

The E-7 visa isn't a one-size-fits-all category where a bachelor's degree clears every hurdle — education and career requirements are set separately for each occupation code.

It targets foreign nationals hired by Korean companies as specialized professionals, and your major must connect to the job. If it doesn't, you'll need work experience to make up the gap.

This article walks through how the education and experience requirements are structured by occupation, where applicants commonly get stuck during review, and how to strengthen weak spots.

<h2 id="section-1">Basic Structure of E-7 Visa Education Requirements</h2>

E-7 education requirements are applied differently by occupation code under the [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr) notice "Introduction Standards for Specialized Occupations."

On the surface, the general rule sounds simple — "a bachelor's degree plus one year of experience, or a master's degree with no experience required" — but in practice, there are many exceptions across occupations.

### How Education and Experience Substitute for Each Other

The first thing to check is your specific occupation code.

- Bachelor's degree + 1 year or more of experience in the relevant field
- Master's degree or higher (some occupations require no experience)
- 5 years or more of experience in a related field (as a substitute when education falls short)

In practice, even with a bachelor's degree, the experience bar is applied more strictly when your major doesn't match the job.

### Judging Major-Job Alignment

The first thing a reviewing officer checks is whether the major listed on your diploma aligns with the duties of the position you've been hired for.

When the major doesn't connect directly to the job, you'll need to fill the gap with course transcripts and detailed work history.

If this part is weak, supplementary document requests will follow no matter how thick your file is.

> **Note:** Whether a major-job mismatch is acceptable depends on the detailed standards for each occupation. Confirm how the rules apply to your specific case through a consultation.

<h2 id="section-2">Education Requirements for Managerial and Professional Occupations (Codes 1100–2000)</h2>

The managerial and professional occupations — the core of the E-7 category — are subject to the strictest education standards.

### Business and Planning Occupations

Overseas sales representatives, management consultants, and marketing officers generally require a bachelor's degree plus 1 year of experience, or a master's degree or higher.

| Occupation | Code | Education Standard | Experience Substitute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Management Consultant | 2715 | Bachelor's or higher | Bachelor's + 1 year, or 5 years experience |
| Overseas Sales Representative | 2742 | Bachelor's or higher | Bachelor's + 1 year, or 5 years experience |
| Product Planning Specialist | 2731 | Bachelor's or higher | Bachelor's + 1 year, or 5 years experience |

Overseas sales positions, in particular, are reviewed alongside your native language ability, Korean or English proficiency, and your connection to the relevant trading country.

### IT and Technical Occupations

Computer systems designers, analysts, and systems software developers typically require a bachelor's degree plus 1 year of experience.

If your major is computer science, information and communications, or electronic engineering, a bachelor's degree alone is usually enough. Applicants from unrelated fields need to add 5 years of experience or OPIc scores and relevant certifications to bridge the gap.

In practice, additional proof is frequently requested when the major name is ambiguous — for instance, departments like "Digital Media Studies" or "Information Culture."

<h2 id="section-3">Education Requirements for Skilled and Technical Occupations (Codes 6000–7000)</h2>

For occupations represented by the Skilled Workforce category (E-7-4), work experience matters more than education.

### Skilled Workforce (E-7-4)

Many applicants convert from E-9 status. The core requirements are 4 or more years of legal residence in Korea plus the points threshold for that occupation.

Education functions as a bonus-point factor — conversion is possible even without a bachelor's degree.

- Korean language ability
- Annual income
- Certifications
- Education
- Age

The point thresholds for each category are adjusted every year, so confirm your score for the current year through [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) announcements and a consultation.

### General Technical Occupations

For some technical roles such as welders, painters, and shipbuilding welders, education isn't a factor — 5 or more years of related experience or a national technical certification is enough.

Issues typically arise around the country of issuance of the experience certificate, the issuing institution, and whether the described duties actually match the occupation.

<h2 id="section-4">How to Use Experience to Make Up for Insufficient Education</h2>

When you don't have a bachelor's degree or your major doesn't align, the biggest sticking point is whether experience can substitute.

The decisive factor is the format and content of your experience certificate.

### What Must Be Included in an Experience Certificate

- Company name, business registration number, address, contact info
- Employment period (start and end dates)
- Position title and a detailed description of duties
- Issue date, issuer's signature, and company seal
- Notarization in the home country or an Apostille

If the duty description is just one line, the reviewer can't reasonably determine whether the role matches the target occupation.

Rather than length, what matters is showing concrete actions performed in the role.

> **Practical tip:** The experience certificate should explicitly include keywords that map to the hiring position (for example, "system design" or "customer data analysis"). A vague label like "office worker" is weak.

### When Both Education and Experience Fall Short

If your annual income meets a certain level, special provisions can relax parts of the education and experience requirements.

The income threshold changes each year, and applying it correctly requires a case-by-case review of the current year's rules.

<h2 id="section-5">Common Sticking Points During Review</h2>

Even when education and experience look sufficient on paper, the review can break down somewhere else entirely.

### Degree Verification and Apostille

Degrees from overseas universities require either an [Apostille](https://www.0404.go.kr) or consular authentication.

Chinese degrees need CHSI (学信网) verification, while degrees from Vietnam, the Philippines, and similar countries must go through home-country foreign ministry authentication.

This step typically adds another 2 to 6 weeks to your timeline.

### Interpreting Major-Job Relevance

When the reviewer can't determine job alignment from the major's name alone, they'll request a detailed course transcript.

Supplementary requests are especially common in these situations.

- The department name doesn't map directly to the job
- Graduates of interdisciplinary or combined-major programs
- Overseas department names that don't fit Korea's classification framework

### Eligibility of the Hiring Company

Even if your education and experience are in order, the visa can be denied when the hiring company's revenue, ratio of Korean employees, or wage level falls below the required standard.

For this, you also need to cross-check the schedule attached to the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Control Act, published on the [Korea Law Information Center](https://www.law.go.kr).

Company requirements have recently been tightened for some occupations, so the precise standard that applies to your employer should be confirmed with a specialist.

<h2 id="section-7">FAQ</h2>

**Q1. Can I apply for an E-7 visa without a bachelor's degree?**

It's possible.

For some occupations, 5 or more years of documented experience in the relevant field can substitute for the education requirement, and the Skilled Workforce category (E-7-4) has no education requirement at all.

That said, the rules differ by occupation, so confirming your occupation code is the first step.

**Q2. Will I automatically be rejected if my major doesn't match the job?**

No, rejection isn't automatic.

You can supplement your application with course transcripts, relevant certifications, and additional proof of work experience.

But when the major-to-job alignment is low, reviews take longer and supplementary requests become much more likely.

**Q3. How do I get my overseas degree authenticated?**

For countries that have joined the Apostille Convention, you obtain an Apostille from your home country's foreign ministry. For non-member countries, you go through consular authentication at the Korean consulate.

China requires CHSI (Xuexin) verification, and Vietnam requires a separate foreign ministry authentication process.

**Q4. Can the experience certificate be issued in English?**

Yes, either English or a Korean translation works.

The translation must be submitted with the translator's signature, and in some cases notarization of the original document is also required.

**Q5. Do the education requirements change every year?**

The introduction standards for each occupation are revised annually — or whenever needed — through Ministry of Justice notices.

IT, overseas sales, and root-industry occupations in particular have seen frequent changes recently, so it's important to check the standards in effect at the time of application.

**Q6. What if my education qualifies but the company is small?**

Even if your education is sufficient, an application can be denied when the company falls below the thresholds for size, revenue, or ratio of Korean employees.

Company eligibility is something to check up front, before preparing the application.
