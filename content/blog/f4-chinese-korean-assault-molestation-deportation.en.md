# Complete Guide to F-4 Korean-Chinese Assault & Sexual Harassment Immigration Review and Deportation Criteria

When an F-4 visa holder of Korean-Chinese ethnicity is booked for assault or sexual harassment, an immigration review (sabeom simsa) proceeds separately from the criminal case, and the outcome can result in cancellation of stay status or a deportation order.

The affected individuals are Chinese nationals of Korean ethnicity residing in Korea on an F-4 (overseas Korean) visa, and the range of incidents is wide — from simple verbal disputes to bodily injury, indecent assault, and illegal filming offenses.

This article covers the flow of the immigration review for assault and sexual harassment cases, the criteria used to determine deportation, the specific points where outcomes diverge, and the practical essentials to focus on when responding.

<h2 id="section-1">Basic Structure of the F-4 Korean-Chinese Assault Immigration Review</h2>

The F-4 visa is a long-term residence status based on overseas Korean identity, but once a criminal incident occurs, the Immigration Office initiates a separate immigration review.

The key point is this: the criminal proceedings and the immigration review move on entirely separate tracks.

### Why Criminal Disposition and Immigration Review Differ

Even if the prosecution stage results in a suspended indictment, the Immigration Office conducts its own independent assessment.

Even with the same outcome — say, a KRW 1 million fine — the disposition can split entirely depending on whether the incident is classified as assault or indecent assault.

In practice, the most common scenario is people feeling reassured by the criminal outcome alone, only to receive a departure order or deportation notice later.

### When the Immigration Review Begins

Typically, the case enters review status the moment the police notify the Immigration Office of the incident.

Particularly for assault and sex offenses, the standard flow is for the police to notify Immigration immediately after confirming the foreign national's identity.

In other words, the immigration review file is already opened before the case itself is concluded.

> **Caution:** Even before the criminal case is closed, a departure order or fact-finding notice may arrive first. If you receive such a notice, immediate preparation for response is essential.

<h2 id="section-2">Deportation Criteria in F-4 Assault Cases</h2>

In F-4 assault cases, whether deportation is ordered is not determined simply by the fine amount.

The first factors to examine are the severity of the victim's injuries, whether a weapon was used, whether a settlement was reached, and any prior record of similar offenses.

### Key Variables That Determine the Outcome

| Variable | Lighter Disposition Likely | Deportation Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Severity of Harm | Simple assault, minor injury | Serious injury, weapon used |
| Settlement | Amicable settlement reached | No settlement, victim insists on punishment |
| Prior Record | First offense | Repeat similar offense, habitual offender |
| Circumstances | Spur-of-the-moment dispute | Premeditated or group violence |

A weakness in even one of the items above can significantly shift the immigration review outcome.

### Where Cases Frequently Get Stuck in Practice

In practice, many people assume "we settled, so it's fine" — and that's exactly where they get stuck.

Immigration actually examines the timing of the settlement document, the flow of the settlement payment, and even the manner in which the victim expressed their wish not to prosecute, all as a whole.

Submitting a single settlement document is not enough — you have to explain the entire flow showing that the settlement was reached in good faith.

### Statutory Basis Under the Immigration Control Act

Article 46 of the [Immigration Control Act](https://www.law.go.kr) defines persons subject to deportation, while Article 89 sets out grounds for cancellation of stay status.

In particular, receiving a sentence of imprisonment or heavier directly triggers grounds for deportation, so defense arguments at the sentencing stage must already take the immigration review into account.

Detailed legal provisions can be verified directly at the [Korean Law Information Center](https://www.law.go.kr).

<h2 id="section-3">F-4 Sexual Harassment Immigration Review — How It Differs from Assault</h2>

The immigration review standards for sexual harassment cases are far stricter than for assault.

Even when only a fine is imposed, the rate at which these cases lead to deportation or a departure order is generally high.

### Differences Between Indecent Assault and Illegal Filming

| Offense | Example Criminal Outcome | Immigration Review Tendency |
|---|---|---|
| Simple harassment (minor) | Fine, suspended indictment | Possible departure order |
| Indecent assault | Fine to imprisonment | Very high deportation risk |
| Illegal filming (camera offense) | Fine to imprisonment | Deportation is effectively the default |
| Targeting minors | Aggravated punishment | Deportation plus long-term reentry ban |

The table above shows general tendencies — the actual disposition depends on the specifics of each case.

### When You Become Subject to Sex Offender Registration

If you become subject to personal information registration under the Sexual Violence Punishment Act, the immigration review will effectively trend toward deportation.

When this factor is in play, it becomes extremely difficult to flip the outcome no matter how strongly you emphasize other mitigating circumstances.

The very act of applying for exemption from sex offender registration can itself become a critical variable in the immigration review.

### When a Settlement Actually Carries Weight

In sexual harassment cases, settlement is an absolutely decisive variable.

But what matters more than the mere fact that settlement money was paid is whether the victim's wish not to prosecute was clearly reflected during the investigation and trial stages.

If the settlement comes late, it may still be reflected in sentencing, but it will rarely earn extra credit in the immigration review.

> **Practical Tip:** A settlement is not just for the criminal outcome — for the immigration review that follows, it must also be properly preserved in a formal written document.

<h2 id="section-4">Immigration Review Procedure and Stage-by-Stage Response</h2>

The immigration review procedure for F-4 assault and sexual harassment cases generally proceeds in the following order.

| Stage | Content | Key Point |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Receipt of case notification | Confirm timing of police-to-Immigration notification |
| Stage 2 | Fact-finding investigation | Submit written statements and supporting materials |
| Stage 3 | Referral to immigration review | Review by the Office's review committee |
| Stage 4 | Decision on disposition | Departure order, deportation, or cancellation of stay status |
| Stage 5 | Objection or administrative litigation | Must be filed within the deadline after notice |

At each stage, the scope and impact of the materials you can submit differ.

### The Written Statement Is Half the Immigration Review

Rather than writing at length, the key is to clearly organize the circumstances of the incident, expressions of remorse, efforts to compensate the victim, and the foundation of your life in Korea.

Even if you submit a large volume of documents, the result is unlikely to be favorable if the statement is weak.

In particular, the clearer your established foundation in Korea as an overseas Korean (family, employment, residence), the more room there is for mitigating consideration.

### A Race Against the Clock After Disposition Notice

Once a departure order or deportation disposition is issued, you must file an objection or administrative appeal within a short period from the date of notice.

Miss this deadline and the disposition becomes final as-is.

The exact deadline and the appeal procedure suited to your case need to be confirmed by a specialist, separately from the general guidance available at [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

<h2 id="section-5">The Unique Nature of F-4 Overseas Korean Status and the Immigration Review</h2>

Unlike ordinary foreign visas, the F-4 is a status based on overseas Korean identity.

This factor cuts both ways in the immigration review.

### When Overseas Korean Status Works in Your Favor

If you have settled in Korea as a family unit and have a long, stable record of residence, there is room for this to be recognized as a mitigating factor.

If you have direct family members who are Korean nationals or permanent residents, the family-unity aspect can be emphasized.

### When Overseas Korean Status Works Against You

If, despite long residence in Korea, you have accumulated repeat offenses of the same type, this easily leads to a finding that "willingness to integrate into Korean society is weak."

In particular, repeated alcohol-related assaults weigh very heavily against you in the immigration review.

[[f4-chinese-korean-dui-sabeom-process]] It is also common for a DUI immigration review and an assault immigration review to proceed simultaneously.

### Relationship with the Overseas Koreans Act

Article 5 of the [Act on the Immigration and Legal Status of Overseas Koreans](https://www.law.go.kr) allows for restrictions on the grant of F-4 status in certain cases.

When reapplying after cancellation of stay status, the same grounds often come back to obstruct the application.

<h2 id="section-6">Possibility of Reentry After Deportation</h2>

Once a deportation order becomes final, reentry is restricted for a certain period.

The length varies depending on the severity of the case, and separate procedures exist for amnesty or for lifting an entry ban.

### Reentry Restriction Periods

| Case Type | General Restriction Trend |
|---|---|
| Minor assault, settlement completed | Relatively short |
| Indecent assault, sex offender registration | Long or close to permanent |
| Repeat DUI + assault | Long |
| Group or premeditated crime | Long or permanent |

The periods above reflect general tendencies — the exact period for your case must be confirmed against the official disposition document.

### Lifting an Entry Ban and Reapplying

An application to lift an entry ban is evaluated comprehensively based on the grounds for the original disposition, the time elapsed, evidence of remorse, family relationships, and similar factors.

Even if the ban is lifted, F-4 status is not automatically re-granted.

A separate qualification review is conducted again upon reapplication, meaning you effectively have to clear two gates.

> **Caution:** The end of the reentry restriction period does not mean automatic entry is allowed. A separate procedure and supporting explanation are always required.

<h2 id="section-7">What Actually Changes the Outcome in an Immigration Review</h2>

The variables that determine the immigration review outcome are less about the documents themselves and more about the narrative those documents convey.

### The Five Essentials

- Genuineness of the settlement with the victim
- Consistency of statements about the circumstances of the incident
- Proof of your foundation of life in Korea
- Whether you have prior similar offenses, and the time between them
- Concrete demonstration of remorse and intent to prevent recurrence

It may look simple on the surface, but actually organizing these five points to match the real standards used in the immigration review is not easy.

### Commonly Overlooked Items

Many people fail to separately organize life-foundation materials such as proof of family relationships, certificates of employment, and tax payment records at the immigration review stage.

When these materials are missing, all you're left with is the claim that "I've lived in Korea for a long time" — which lacks persuasive force.

In fact, materials containing concrete numbers and durations often work more powerfully than the written statement itself.

<h2 id="section-8">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. Can I still be deported even if I only received a fine?**

Yes, it's possible. The weight of the criminal disposition and the immigration review outcome are separate matters — particularly for sex offenses and group assaults, even a fine can lead to a departure order or deportation.

**Q2. I received a suspended indictment — will the immigration review still proceed?**

A suspended indictment is also subject to immigration review. What matters for the review standards is not whether you were indicted, but the fact that the incident itself occurred.

**Q3. I reached a settlement — why was I still issued a departure order?**

The evaluation depends on the timing of the settlement, its content, and how the victim's wish not to prosecute was expressed. A single settlement document is often not enough on its own.

**Q4. Can I challenge the immigration review outcome?**

Objections, administrative appeals, and administrative lawsuits are all available. However, since the deadline from the date of notice is short, you need to begin the process the moment you receive the notice.

**Q5. My whole family is on F-4 — what happens if only I receive a disposition?**

Only your own status is cancelled; your family's status is not directly affected. That said, the family-unity aspect can still serve as mitigating material in your own case.

**Q6. If I leave Korea immediately after the incident, can I avoid the immigration review?**

Voluntary departure can shorten some procedures, but the entry ban itself can still be imposed separately. Voluntary departure is not always the better choice.
