# Complete Guide to Re-entry Permit Exemption Requirements and Application

Registered foreign residents who return to Korea within one year of departure are automatically exempt from the re-entry permit requirement.

Permanent residents (F-5) receive the same exemption when returning within two years, and the rule applies to those who have completed foreign resident registration.

This guide walks through the exemption requirements, the exceptions where it does not apply, and the application procedure when your absence exceeds one year — all from a practical standpoint.

<h2 id="section-1">Core of the Re-entry Permit Exemption System</h2>

A re-entry permit is the procedure required when a registered foreign resident temporarily departs Korea and returns under the same status of stay.

In the past, you had to obtain either a single or multiple re-entry permit before leaving the country.

Now, under Article 30 of the Immigration Act and Article 44-2 of its Enforcement Rules, you can depart and re-enter without a separate permit as long as certain conditions are met.

### Legal Basis for the Exemption

The legal basis for the exemption is set out in Article 44-2 of the [Enforcement Rules of the Immigration Act](https://www.law.go.kr).

The Ministry of Justice's [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) portal also outlines who qualifies and what the departure procedure looks like.

In practice, you can depart without any separate application as long as you carry your alien registration card and passport.

### Difference Between a Simple Departure and an Exemption

This is where confusion most often arises.

"Exemption" means "no permit is required" — it does not mean "you are guaranteed to be able to return after leaving."

If you exceed the time limit or your status of stay changes, the exemption falls away and you have to go through the visa process all over again.

<h2 id="section-2">Exemption Requirements — Who Qualifies</h2>

There are two key conditions.

You must have completed foreign resident registration, and you must return within the prescribed period.

| Category | Exemption Period | Notes |
|------|----------|------|
| General registered residents | Within 1 year of departure date | Valid only within the remaining period of stay |
| Permanent residents (F-5) | Within 2 years of departure date | Applies without any separate notification |
| Unregistered short-term visitors | Not eligible | Visa reissuance required for re-entry |

### When Your Remaining Stay Period Is Shorter Than the Exemption Period

Even if the exemption period is one year, the expiration date of your own period of stay takes precedence if it comes sooner.

For example, if your stay expires in six months, you must re-enter within those six months for the exemption to hold.

This is the point where most people get tripped up in practice.

### The Two-Year Exemption for F-5 Permanent Residents

Even permanent residents can lose their F-5 status itself if they stay outside Korea for more than two years.

Because this ties into the grounds for cancellation of permanent residency under Article 46 of the Immigration Act, calculating your remaining time before departure is essential.

If your planned absence is approaching one year, it pays to verify the details in advance.

<h2 id="section-3">Exceptions Where the Exemption Does Not Apply</h2>

The exemption is automatic, but there are cases that fall outside its scope.

Overlooking this can mean being refused departure at the airport or being blocked at re-entry.

### Departure Bans and Unpaid Penalties

According to [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr) materials, the exemption simply does not apply if a departure ban has been imposed on you.

The same is true for unpaid administrative fines, individuals in deportation proceedings, and those involved in ongoing criminal cases.

In these situations, departure itself is blocked, so checking your standing in advance comes first.

### Holders of Specific Statuses of Stay

Certain short-term statuses such as Culture and Arts (D-1) and Short-Term Visit (C-3) are not eligible for the exemption.

Because the scope varies by status, the first step is to confirm whether your particular status qualifies.

Operational guidelines for some statuses have changed recently, so accurate confirmation for your specific situation may require professional advice.

### Absences Exceeding One Year

If illness, study, family matters, or similar reasons mean your absence will run beyond one year, you must apply for a re-entry permit before leaving.

If you leave without applying and stay away for more than a year, your status of stay is forfeited.

> **Caution:** If you exceed one year by even a single day, the exemption falls away and you have to go through a new visa procedure. Calculating dates before departure is the very first thing to look at.

<h2 id="section-4">How to Apply for a Re-entry Permit for Absences Over One Year</h2>

If the reason for your departure means you expect to be away for more than a year, you need to obtain a re-entry permit before you go.

Skipping this step puts you in the position of having to obtain a brand-new visa to return.

### When and Where to Apply

Applications are submitted before departure at your local Immigration Office or branch office.

You can also book an appointment online in advance through [HiKorea Electronic Civil Services](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

Processing times vary by office, and we can help you identify the fastest available location.

|------|------|------|

| Passport | At least 6 months' validity remaining | 1 copy |
| Alien registration card | Original | Carry on departure |
| Proof of reason for departure | Medical, study, family, etc. | Varies by reason |

### Single vs. Multiple Re-entry Permits

A single permit allows one departure and re-entry, while a multiple permit allows several within the validity period.

For business or research purposes involving frequent travel, a multiple permit is usually the better choice.

If, however, only one departure is clearly planned, a single permit is faster procedurally.

<h2 id="section-5">Things to Verify Before Departure</h2>

More important than the paperwork is the final check right before you leave.

On the ground, small omissions snowball into major problems.

### Verify the Days Remaining on Your Stay

Start by checking the expiration date of stay shown on your alien registration card.

Even if you return within the exemption period, the exemption is meaningless if your stay has already expired.

### Carry Your Alien Registration Card

You must have your alien registration card on you when you depart.

Leaving without it can tangle the procedure when you try to re-enter.

If you have lost it, getting a replacement before departure comes first.

### Departure Reporting and Immigration Inspection

There is no separate report required at departure, but users of automated immigration gates can speed things up by presenting the alien registration card alongside the passport.

> **Practical tip:** When departing under the exemption, you do not need to tell the officer at the departure inspection counter that you are "leaving under the re-entry exemption." It is handled automatically in the system.

<h2 id="section-6">Common Sticking Points in Practice</h2>

The exemption system looks simple, but in actual application there are many points where things diverge.

### Conflict Between Status Changes and the Exemption

If your status-change review is underway right before departure, the exemption frequently gets tangled.

Leaving Korea before the review result is issued can cause the change application to be automatically canceled.

In that case, you have to file a new application after re-entering.

### Calculating the Two Years for Permanent Residents

The biggest snags occur with permanent residents who try to come back having used almost all of their two years.

The calculation between departure date and entry date can hinge on a single day, so advance verification is essential.

The window between roughly 1 year 9 months and 2 years after departure especially calls for a careful review.

### Reporting Obligations After Re-entry

There is no separate reporting requirement after re-entering under the exemption.

That said, if any change has occurred in your status of stay, a change report applies within 14 days.

In recent cases, missing the change report has resulted in administrative fines.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. Does the exemption apply if I depart without my alien registration card?**
The exemption is premised on being a registered foreign resident.

If you have no registration card or are unregistered, you do not qualify.

**Q2. I'm a few days past the one-year mark — can I still enter?**
Even a single day over one year and the exemption disappears.

In that case, unless you obtained a re-entry permit in advance, you will have to get a new visa from your home country.

**Q3. What happens if an F-5 permanent resident exceeds two years?**
Your permanent residency may be canceled.

If there are unavoidable circumstances, applying for a re-entry permit before the two-year mark is the first priority.

**Q4. Which is better — single or multiple?**
For one departure, single is usually the better choice; for several, multiple. 

Since it depends on your individual travel pattern, checking in advance comes first.

**Q5. What if my period of stay expires while I'm abroad?**
Even within the exemption period, entry can be refused if your period of stay has expired.

Before departure, you need to verify whether an extension of stay or a re-entry permit procedure is in order.

**Q6. What about traveling abroad together as a family?**
Exemption requirements differ depending on each person's status of stay.

You need to check each family member's period of stay and status separately.
