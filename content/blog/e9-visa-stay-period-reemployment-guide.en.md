# E-9 Non-Professional Employment Visa: Complete Guide to Stay Period and Re-employment

The standard stay period for the E-9 Non-Professional Employment Visa is 3 years from the date of entry, and if both employer and worker agree, a re-employment extension of 1 year and 10 months is possible.

This visa applies to simple-labor workers from 16 sending countries who entered Korea under the Employment Permit System pursuant to the "Act on the Employment, Etc. of Foreign Workers," and is limited to selected industries including manufacturing, construction, agriculture and livestock, fisheries, and parts of the service sector.

This article covers the practical pain points workers most often run into: the re-employment application process after the 3-year term ends, how to calculate the 4-year-10-month cap, the special re-entry provision for diligent workers, and how all of this interacts with workplace changes.

<h2 id="section-1">Basic Structure of the E-9 Visa Stay Period</h2>

The E-9 is designed around a short-term rotation principle.

The first thing to understand is that the stay period is not simply "3 years."

### 3 Years After Entry + 1 Year 10 Months of Re-employment

Under Article 18 of the "Act on the Employment, Etc. of Foreign Workers," a foreign worker's employment activity period is 3 years from the date of entry into Korea.

Before the 3-year term expires, if the employer obtains re-employment approval from the Minister of Employment and Labor, the worker may be employed for an additional period within 1 year and 10 months.

Combined, this yields a maximum of 4 years and 10 months.

> **Note:** The 4-year-10-month cap is engineered to stay "under 5 years of continuous residence." Under the Immigration Act, 5 years or more of continuous stay triggers permanent residency eligibility, so this upper limit is set by policy design.

### The Difference Between Stay Period and Employment Activity Period

This is where confusion most often arises.

The "stay period" is the duration stamped on the visa sticker, while the "employment activity period" is the total working period allowed by law.

In practice, even if a stay period extension has been granted, you cannot work once you exceed the employment activity period cap.

| Category | Period | Legal Basis |
|------|------|------|
| Basic employment activity period | 3 years from entry | Foreign Workers Employment Act, Art. 18 |
| Re-employment extension | Within 1 year 10 months | Foreign Workers Employment Act, Art. 18-2 |
| Total cap | 4 years 10 months | Policy ceiling |
| Diligent worker re-entry | Additional 4 years 10 months | Foreign Workers Employment Act, Art. 18-4 |

<h2 id="section-2">Re-employment Application Timing and Procedure</h2>

Many people assume re-employment "can be handled right before the term ends," but in reality the timing is much tighter.

### Application Window

The employer must apply for re-employment approval at the competent Employment Center no later than 7 days before the employment activity period expires.

The worker is not the applicant — the employer is the party that must file.

If this step is weak, cases of falling out of status past the expiration date do actually occur.

### Status of Stay Change After Re-employment Approval

Once the Ministry of Employment and Labor grants re-employment approval, the next step is filing for a stay period extension at the Immigration Office.

If the order is reversed, you have to start over.

> **Practical tip:** The sequence is Employment Center approval → Immigration stay period extension → Alien Registration Card renewal. If any single step is skipped, the next step is blocked.

### Grounds for Re-employment Denial

Re-employment is not automatic.

- The employer received administrative sanctions for unpaid wages
- A history of concealing industrial accidents or violating labor laws
- The employer's foreign worker hiring permit was previously revoked
- The worker has a record of frequent workplace changes

Any one of these can block re-employment at this stage.

In particular, the review standards regarding the number of workplace changes have recently been tightened.

Whether your specific situation falls under any of these requires a case-by-case review, so confirm the exact application through consultation.

<h2 id="section-3">Special Re-entry Program for Diligent Workers</h2>

The end of 4 years and 10 months is not necessarily the end.

In fact, this is where paths diverge.

### Requirements for Diligent Worker Recognition

Under Article 18-4 of the "Act on the Employment, Etc. of Foreign Workers," re-entry within a short period after departure is possible if all of the following requirements are met.

- Worked at the same workplace for 4 years and 10 months (exceptions for employer-caused factors such as closure or business suspension)
- The employer has filed a hiring application for the worker after re-entry
- The work falls within designated industries such as agriculture, livestock, fisheries, or manufacturing with 50 or fewer employees
- Departed from Korea after completing re-employment as a Non-Professional Employee (E-9)

The core point is: "same workplace for 4 years 10 months + employer's intent to rehire."

### Re-entry Waiting Period After Departure

Previously, re-entry was only possible after a longer waiting period following departure, but for diligent workers this has been shortened to allow re-entry after a much shorter interval.

That said, the exact number of waiting days and the eligible industries change every year through Ministry of Employment and Labor notifications.

For the standards that apply to your industry this year, check the [Ministry of Employment and Labor's Foreign Workforce Management System (EPS)](https://www.eps.go.kr) or seek consultation.

### Additional Stay After Re-entry

If you re-enter as a diligent worker, you can again engage in employment activities for up to 4 years and 10 months.

In other words, in theory you can work in Korea under E-9 status for up to 9 years and 8 months total.

> **Note:** In a recent similar case, a worker with a record of workplace changes was blocked from re-entry over how the "same workplace" requirement was interpreted. The deciding factor was whether the change was attributable to the employer or the worker.

<h2 id="section-4">How Workplace Changes Relate to Stay Period</h2>

A workplace change by an E-9 worker does not affect the calculation of the stay period itself.

However, the number of changes and the reasons behind them play a decisive role in the re-employment review.

### Allowed Number of Workplace Changes

Under Article 25 of the "Act on the Employment, Etc. of Foreign Workers," as a rule, the worker may not exceed 3 workplace changes during the initial 3-year period.

During the 1-year-10-month re-employment extension, up to 2 additional changes are permitted.

However, changes due to employer-caused reasons (business closure, suspension, unpaid wages, unfair treatment, etc.) do not count toward this limit.

| Category | Change Limit | Notes |
|------|----------|------|
| Initial 3 years | Up to 3 | Worker-attributable basis |
| Re-employment 1 yr 10 mo | Up to 2 | Worker-attributable basis |
| Employer-caused changes | Not counted | Proof required |

### Proving the Reason for Change Is Key

In actual reviews, the supporting evidence for "why you changed workplaces" is the turning point.

Wage-arrears complaints, Labor Relations Commission rulings, employer confirmation statements, and copies of payroll ledgers are the core documents.

Even with stacks of paperwork, a weak explanation of the reason will fail to qualify as employer-caused.

### Job-Search Period Cap

If you fail to find new employment within 3 months from the date of filing for a workplace change, you become subject to departure.

Extensions are possible for special reasons such as illness, pregnancy, or injury, but you must have proper supporting documents.

If you get stuck at this stage, you must leave the country even if stay period remains, so timing management is critical.

<h2 id="section-5">Transitioning from E-9 to E-7-4 (Skilled Workers)</h2>

Don't just focus on extending your stay — review transitioning to a higher-tier visa at the same time.

### When You Can Apply for E-7-4 Transition

Workers with 4 or more years of experience in the same field under E-9 or E-10 status may apply to switch to E-7-4 (Skilled Worker) through the points-based system.

Once converted to E-7-4, you exit the short-term rotation framework, opening paths to long-term residency, family accompaniment, and F-2/F-5 status.

This is the biggest game-changer for E-9 workers.

### Points System Evaluation Categories

- Age
- Education
- Korean language ability (TOPIK, KIIP, etc.)
- Income
- Certifications
- Work experience
- Settlement base in Korea

The points allocated to each category are adjusted annually through Ministry of Justice notifications, and the passing score and category weightings change frequently.

For this year's applicable standards, check [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) announcements or seek case-specific consultation.

### Common Sticking Points in the Transition

- Proving same-field experience (mismatch between business registration codes and the industry codes on the labor contract)
- Korean language proof (whether you meet the required KIIP level)
- Income standard (how prior-year annual income is calculated)

In particular, workers who have changed workplaces multiple times often run into split decisions on whether the experience counts as the "same field."

Whether your career counts as same-field work needs to be reviewed in advance through your labor contract, payroll ledger, and employment insurance records.

<h2 id="section-6">Common Issues During E-9 Stay</h2>

These are the consultation types we encounter most often in practice.

### Overstay

This happens when the employer forgets to file for re-employment, or when the Immigration stay period extension is missed.

Even a single day past the deadline classifies you as an illegal stayer under the Immigration Act, and you face fines, forced deportation, and re-entry restrictions.

> **Note:** Even if the overstay results from the employer's filing error, legal liability still falls on the worker as well. Check your expiration date yourself.

### Failure to Renew Alien Registration Card

Even if your stay period has been extended, you still need to file separately to renew your Alien Registration Card.

Applications can be filed starting 60 days before the stay period expires, and filing after expiration incurs a fine.

### Unauthorized Workplace Abandonment

If you leave your workplace during the contract period without the employer's consent, the hiring permit is revoked and you become ineligible to file for a workplace change.

In that case, you have almost no option other than departure.

<h2 id="section-7">FAQ</h2>

**Q1. What is the maximum number of years I can work in Korea on an E-9 visa?**

The base is 3 years + 1 year 10 months of re-employment = 4 years 10 months. If you qualify for diligent worker re-entry, an additional 4 years 10 months becomes possible, for a maximum of 9 years 8 months. However, the diligent worker requirements are strict and do not apply to every worker.

**Q2. Does the worker file the re-employment application themselves?**

No. The applicant for re-employment approval is the employer. The worker should communicate their intent to be re-employed to the employer in advance and verify that the application is actually being filed.

**Q3. Does an employer closing the business count toward my workplace change limit?**

It does not. Changes due to employer-caused reasons (closure, suspension, unpaid wages, unfair treatment, etc.) are not counted. However, you need documentation to prove this, and confirmation from the labor authorities is required.

**Q4. What happens if I miss the re-employment application window?**

The employer must apply to the Employment Center at least 7 days before the employment activity period expires. If this window is missed, employment terminates at the same time as expiration, status of stay is lost, and you become subject to departure. After-the-fact remedies are very limited.

**Q5. Can I sponsor family members if I transition from E-9 to E-7-4?**

Once converted to E-7-4 (Skilled Worker), you can sponsor a spouse and minor children on an F-3 dependent visa. Under E-9 status, family accompaniment is, in principle, not permitted.

**Q6. For diligent worker re-entry, do I have to work at the same workplace?**

In principle, you need a re-employment application from the same employer you worked for during the 4 years and 10 months before departure. However, exceptions such as the workplace closing or suspending operations may allow matching with a different employer.
