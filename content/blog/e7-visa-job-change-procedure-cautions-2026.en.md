# Complete Guide to E-7 Visa Job Changes (Workplace Change) — Procedures and Key Considerations

Changing jobs on an E-7 visa is not a simple notification — it requires **prior approval**.
Before reporting to your new employer, you must first obtain workplace change permission from the Immigration Office. Moving without authorization can put your entire residence status at risk.
Below, we walk through the full E-7 job change procedure, required documents, common practical roadblocks, and the most frequent reasons for denial.

<h2 id="section-1">Changing Jobs on an E-7 Visa: It's Approval, Not Notification</h2>

Many people mistakenly assume the E-7 works like the E-9 — that you can switch employers first and notify the authorities afterward.
**Here's the key point.** Under the Immigration Control Act Enforcement Decree, E-7 holders must obtain **prior approval** for any workplace change.

### The Difference Between Notification and Approval

Notification is an after-the-fact report; approval requires advance authorization.
If you start working at your new company before approval is granted, you may be treated as engaging in unauthorized employment from that moment forward.
In actual reviews, immigration officers also check your national insurance enrollment date and the date your employment contract was signed.

### Governing Legislation

Under Article 21 of the [Immigration Control Act](https://www.law.go.kr), any activity outside your residence status, as well as workplace changes or additions, is subject to approval by the Minister of Justice.
Detailed criteria follow the "Standards for Visa and Residence Permit Changes by Foreigner Residence Status" published on [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

> **Note:** Some occupational categories (including certain specialized roles) may only require prior notification, but whether this applies depends on your specific occupation code and the most recent regulatory updates. Confirm through a consultation whether your case falls under the notification or approval requirement.

<h2 id="section-2">Check Whether the Job Change Is Even Possible</h2>

Before you start gathering documents, the first thing to examine is **occupational consistency**.

### Are You Staying Within the Same Occupation Code?

The E-7 is subdivided into 85 occupational categories, each with its own qualification requirements.
Generally, moves within the same occupation code have a much higher approval rate.
If the occupation changes, you're essentially undergoing a brand-new E-7 qualification review.

### Re-examining Education and Career Requirements

The duties you'll perform at the new company must align with your degree, major, and prior career.
If this link is weak, your application can be denied even within the same E-7 category.
In practice, the outcome often depends on how clearly you can connect your career history to the new company's job description.

### The Company Must Meet Requirements Too

Whether the new employer is qualified to hire E-7 foreign nationals is just as important.

| Checkpoint | What to Verify | Notes |
|---------|---------|------|
| Domestic Hiring Ratio | Ratio of foreign to Korean employees | Caps vary by occupation |
| Revenue & Capital | Meets minimum thresholds for the industry | Newly established companies reviewed separately |
| National Insurance | Proof that the business operates normally | Non-enrollment may lead to denial |
| Wage Level | Meets the prior year's per-capita GNI standard | Updated annually |

> **Practical Tip:** Wage standards change each year, and the applied ratio varies even within the same occupation code. To confirm the exact threshold for your occupation this year, please reach out for a consultation.

<h2 id="section-3">The E-7 Workplace Change Approval Process</h2>

The procedure looks simple on paper, but in practice the most common stumbling block is the timing of the application.

### Step-by-Step Flow

| Step | Action | Responsible Party |
|-----|-----|---------|
| Step 1 | Resign from current company and file employment-status change | Previous employer |
| Step 2 | Sign employment contract with new employer | Applicant & new employer |
| Step 3 | Prepare documents and book a HiKorea appointment | Applicant |
| Step 4 | Visit the competent Immigration Office to file the application | Applicant |
| Step 5 | Review and notification of approval | Immigration |
| Step 6 | Update Alien Registration Card information | Applicant |

### Why Timing Matters

As a rule, the application should be filed during the **gap period** between leaving your old company and starting your new one.
This is where most cases get stuck.
Leaving a 14-day-plus gap after resignation can jeopardize your residence status — but applying before resignation risks complications with your former employer's cooperation.

### Processing Time

Processing time varies by Immigration Office.
Typically it takes 2–4 weeks, but high-volume offices like Seoul, Suwon, and Incheon can take longer.
We can advise on the fastest-processing jurisdictions and application routing on a case-by-case basis.

<h2 id="section-4">Required Documents and Frequently Overlooked Items</h2>

Even one or two missing items can trigger a correction request and push your timeline back by a month.

### Documents the Applicant Submits

- Integrated Application Form (Form 34)
- Original passport and Alien Registration Card
- Standard-format photograph
- Certificate of resignation or release letter from previous employer
- Copy of previous employer's business registration certificate
- National Insurance Enrollment/Termination Certificate

### Documents the New Employer Submits

- Employment contract (specifying position, duties, and salary)
- Copy of business registration certificate
- Corporate registry extract
- List of currently employed foreign workers and Korean hiring status
- Revenue documentation (VAT tax base certificate, etc.)
- Occupation-specific supporting documents (varies by industry)

### Items Most Commonly Missed

The new employer's documents are where things go wrong most often.
It's common to submit only the business registration certificate while omitting revenue documentation, or to write a job description so brief it fails to demonstrate the connection to the occupation code.
**More important than the documents themselves** is making sure the job description clearly articulates the link between your education, career history, and the new role.

> **Practical Tip:** Rather than writing a lengthy job description, your best bet for approval is to directly mirror the core duties listed in the occupation code definition itself.

<h2 id="section-5">Common Reasons for Denial</h2>

It may look straightforward on the surface, but E-7 workplace changes are denied more often than people expect.

### Occupational Mismatch

This is the most common reason for denial.
Even if your previous role and new role fall under the same E-7 code, the application can be rejected if the actual duties appear materially different.
For example, if you entered as an IT developer (S133) but the new company assigns you to sales or administrative duties, the application is immediately compromised.

### Insufficient Salary

If your annual salary falls below the prior year's per-capita GNI standard, your application will be denied.
This is assessed based on base salary and fixed allowances, not total compensation.
Incentives and performance bonuses are excluded, so the contract structure must be reviewed at the drafting stage.

### New Employer Falls Short of Requirements

Newly established companies or those with low revenue may have their very ability to hire foreign workers questioned.
The same applies to companies that fail to meet the Korean hiring ratio.

### Personal Disqualifying Factors

If you have a history of Immigration Control Act violations during your stay (fines, late filings, etc.), this affects your scoring positively or negatively.
A weakness here can leave your case on hold even if every other document is flawless.

> **Note:** Denial criteria have recently been tightened for certain occupational categories. Whether your case falls under these stricter standards depends on the occupation code and company size, so please verify through a consultation.

<h2 id="section-6">Post-Approval Steps You Must Not Skip</h2>

Approval isn't the finish line.
In practice, missing post-approval steps frequently causes problems at the next extension.

### Reporting Changes to Alien Registration Details

You must report changes to your Alien Registration details within **15 days** of the workplace change approval date.
This can also be done online through [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

### Sorting Out National Insurance

Loss of coverage at the previous company and enrollment at the new one must transition seamlessly.
A prolonged gap will cause the next extension review to question whether you actually worked.

### Tax Filing

When changing jobs mid-year, your new employer can handle the consolidated year-end tax settlement, or you must obtain a withholding tax receipt from your previous employer.
This documentation resurfaces as proof of income at your next visa extension.

### Preparing for the Next Extension

The first extension review after a job change is noticeably stricter.
Officers verify whether you are actually performing the role at the new company and whether your salary is paid as reported.
**This is where outcomes diverge.** Keeping pay stubs, tax invoices, and work deliverables from the moment you start the new job makes the next extension far easier.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

### Q1. If I change companies on an E-7 visa, do I need to get a new visa?

You don't get a new visa — instead, you obtain workplace change approval under your existing E-7 status.
That said, if the occupation changes, the review effectively becomes as thorough as a brand-new application.

### Q2. How many days after resignation do I need to apply?

The exact statutory timeframe varies by occupation and circumstance, but in practice, you should apply as quickly as possible.
A long gap can raise doubts about your residence status, so the safest window is typically right after resignation or just before starting at the new company.

### Q3. What if my previous employer won't cooperate?

Even without a release letter from your previous employer, you can substitute documents like a National Insurance Termination Certificate or a notice of employment contract termination to prove that you resigned.
This is a frequent pain point in practice and often requires case-specific handling.

### Q4. Can I start work at the new company before approval?

No.
Starting work before approval is considered unauthorized employment, and if detected, both you and the new employer can be fined.

### Q5. What happens if the occupation changes entirely?

Even within the E-7 category, switching occupation codes means you must satisfy the education and career requirements all over again.
If you don't meet them, one workaround is to first convert to a [D-10 job-seeker visa](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) and then secure new employment.

### Q6. Is there a limit on how many times I can change jobs?

There's no statutory cap on the number of job changes, but switching too frequently in a short span will raise doubts about your stability and sincerity at the next review.
In practice, frequent job changes themselves can act as a negative factor in the scoring.
