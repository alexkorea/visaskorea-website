# Complete Guide to F-4 Korean-Chinese Immigration Review Process for DUI and Visa Impact

When a Korean-Chinese national holding F-4 status is caught driving under the influence, an immigration review proceeds separately from criminal punishment. If the blood alcohol concentration is 0.08% or higher, or if caught twice or more, it can lead to cancellation of residence status.

This applies to Chinese nationals of Korean descent residing in Korea on an F-4 (Overseas Korean) visa. Refusal of a breathalyzer test, accidents causing injury, and fleeing the scene all fall within the scope of immigration review.

This article covers the review flow, disposition standards by BAC level, practical response points, visa impact, and the possibility of re-entry from a hands-on perspective.

<h2 id="section-1">When the F-4 Korean-Chinese DUI Immigration Review Begins</h2>

A point many people miss is that immigration is notified before the criminal case is even concluded.

When police catch a DUI offense, they are required to automatically forward the report to the local Immigration Office.

From this moment, you are registered as a subject of immigration review, and a record is left on your residence file even without your knowledge.

### Separation of Criminal Proceedings and Immigration Review

The immigration review does not wait until the prosecutor's fine or the court's suspended sentence is finalized.

In practice, while criminal outcomes are referenced, immigration determines the severity of the disposition based on its own separate standards.

In other words, a fine of 2 million KRW does not automatically lead immigration to issue an equally lenient disposition.

### Summons After Notification

Typically, immigration sends a summons within 1–3 months of the incident.

On the day of appearance, a statement is recorded, the facts are confirmed, and disposition opinions are heard.

> **Caution:** Ignoring a summons is not treated as simple non-attendance but is applied as grounds for an aggravated disposition.

<h2 id="section-2">Disposition Standards by BAC Level and F-4 Visa Impact</h2>

Here is the key point.

Even for the same DUI offense, the disposition varies completely depending on BAC level and number of offenses.

Under [Immigration Act](https://law.go.kr) Article 46 and the internal disposition criteria of the [Korea Immigration Service](https://immigration.go.kr), cases are divided by stage.

| Category | Blood Alcohol Concentration | General Disposition | Notes |
|------|---------------|----------------|------|
| 1st offense, simple DUI | 0.03–under 0.08% | Warning, residence status may be maintained | Mitigating circumstances must be submitted |
| 1st offense | 0.08–0.2% | Cancellation of residence under review | Departure order possible |
| 1st offense | 0.2% or higher | Deportation review zone | Entry ban may also apply |
| 2 or more offenses | Regardless of BAC | Deportation, entry ban | High likelihood of F-4 revocation |
| Breathalyzer refusal | - | Treated equivalent to 0.2% or higher | Refusal itself is grounds for aggravation |
| Accompanied by injury accident | - | Deportation, prolonged entry ban | Reflects whether victim settlement was reached |

### The 0.08% Threshold Where Outcomes Diverge

Though it may seem simple, the results differ dramatically just above and just below 0.08%.

This is because the license revocation standard under the Road Traffic Act is directly linked to immigration disposition.

If this point is weak, even a lenient criminal disposition will still be blocked on the visa side.

### Second-Time Offenders Are Most Often Caught Out

In practice, it is common for people who became complacent after a first disposition to face direct deportation on the second offense.

Once F-4 status is revoked, regaining the same qualification is practically very difficult.

The cumulative criteria applied this year are interpreted differently case by case, so whether they apply to your situation must be confirmed through consultation.

<h2 id="section-3">The Full Flow of the Immigration Review Process</h2>

First, you need to understand the step-by-step schedule from notification to disposition.

| Stage | Content | Duration | Key Check |
|------|------|----------|----------|
| Stage 1 | Police arrest, immigration notification | Immediate | Statement consistency |
| Stage 2 | Receipt of summons | 1–3 months | Schedule can be adjusted |
| Stage 3 | Immigration appearance, written statement | 1 day | Bring mitigating materials |
| Stage 4 | Disposition Review Committee | 2–6 weeks | Opinion letter can be submitted |
| Stage 5 | Disposition notification | 1–2 months | Objection possible |
| Stage 6 | Execution of disposition | Immediately after notice | Departure deadline specified |

### Preparation Before Appearance Decides the Outcome

Even with abundant documentation, wavering testimony can derail everything at once.

In practice, statement organization, mitigating materials, and proof of family ties and domestic livelihood are all prepared in advance before appearance.

If this explanation is insufficient, the same BAC level can result in a heavier disposition.

### The Opinion Letter Is the Last Chance

The opinion letter submitted at the Disposition Review Committee stage determines half of the outcome.

Here, family relationships, degree of settlement in Korea, circumstances of the incident, and efforts to prevent recurrence must be presented in a multidimensional way.

Clearly organizing 3–4 core reasons is more effective than writing at length.

<h2 id="section-4">The Real Difference Between Residence Cancellation and Departure Order</h2>

The point many people actually confuse is this.

Although departure order and deportation sound similar, they differ completely in terms of future re-entry possibility.

| Type of Disposition | Meaning | Entry Ban Period | Re-entry Possibility |
|----------|------|--------------|-------------|
| Warning, corrective order | Residence maintained | None | Immediate continuation |
| Cancellation of residence + departure order | Voluntary departure | Usually within 1 year | Possible if reasons are demonstrated |
| Deportation | Forced repatriation | Usually 5–10 years | Very limited |
| Entry ban registration | Blacklist | Case-by-case | Separate release procedure required |

### Voluntary Departure Is the Key to a Departure Order

If you receive a departure order, leaving voluntarily within the specified deadline is decisive for future re-entry.

If you miss the deadline, it converts to deportation, and the result becomes much heavier for the same DUI offense.

### F-4 Reacquisition for Deportees

If you receive a deportation order, it constitutes a disqualifying ground under the [Overseas Korean (F-4) Status Granting Guidelines](https://hikorea.go.kr), effectively blocking reacquisition.

However, there have been cases of reapplication based on amnesty, passage of time, etc., after the entry ban period ends.

Determining what path is open in your case must start from analyzing the disposition notice.

<h2 id="section-5">Mitigating Circumstances That Actually Work in Immigration Review</h2>

More important than documents is a sincere explanation of the facts.

| Mitigating Factor | Example Supporting Documents | Weight |
|-------------|--------------|--------|
| Supporting family in Korea | Family relation certificate, child's enrollment certificate | High |
| Long-term legal residence | Alien registration card, residence history | High |
| First-time offender | Criminal record certificate | High |
| Settlement with victim | Settlement agreement, deposit records | Medium |
| Completion of DUI education | Road Traffic Authority certificate | Medium |
| Pledge to prevent recurrence | Handwritten pledge | Low |

### Proof of Family Ties Is the Strongest Factor

Especially when there is a Korean national spouse or child, the severity of the disposition changes significantly.

If this element is missing, the case is handled the same as any simple foreign DUI offender.

### Why Settlement Changes the Outcome

In DUI cases involving injury accidents, settlement with the victim is the decisive variable that can downgrade deportation to a departure order.

The timing of submitting the settlement agreement is also important, and getting it in before the Disposition Review Committee is best.

<h2 id="section-6">Post-Disposition Objections and Administrative Litigation</h2>

Receiving a disposition notice does not mean everything is over.

There remain stages of objection, administrative appeal, and administrative litigation under [Immigration Act](https://law.go.kr) Article 60.

### The 30-Day Objection Window Is Critical

You must file an objection within 30 days of receiving the disposition notice. Missing this deadline practically eliminates room to contest.

Many people lose this deadline while waiting for criminal results.

### Standards for Going to Administrative Litigation

If the objection is dismissed, you can proceed to administrative appeal or administrative litigation.

However, DUI immigration review is an area where wide disposition discretion is recognized, so the bar for winning is strict.

Whether your case is worth contesting must be judged by directly examining the disposition reasoning.

<h2 id="section-7">FAQ</h2>

**Q1. I was caught for F-4 DUI for the first time. Do I have to leave the country immediately?**

If your BAC was under 0.08% and there was no accident, you may be able to maintain residence.

However, if you skip the in-person statement and fail to submit mitigating materials, there are cases where the same BAC level results in a departure order.

**Q2. If I just pay the fine and the criminal case is closed, does the immigration review also end?**

No.

Separate from criminal proceedings, the immigration review proceeds independently, and it is common for a departure order to be issued after the fine has been paid.

**Q3. Is refusing a breathalyzer test handled the same way?**

Refusal is generally handled as equivalent to 0.2% or higher.

The refusal itself counts as an aggravating factor, so the outcome can be heavier than for a simple DUI.

**Q4. If my F-4 is revoked, can I change to another visa?**

Changes to other statuses like F-2 or H-2 are restricted by disqualification provisions.

However, if you have Korean national family members, separate pathways such as F-6 may be reviewed.

**Q5. I received a departure order but have ongoing matters to handle. Can the deadline be extended?**

In principle, the specified deadline is strictly applied.

However, if there are reasons such as medical or family circumstances, an application for extension of the departure deadline may be possible, so your case needs to be reviewed.

**Q6. Can I re-enter on F-4 after the entry ban period ends?**

The F-4 status is not automatically restored simply because the entry ban period has ended.

A separate review of overseas Korean status is required, and past disposition records will be fully reflected.
