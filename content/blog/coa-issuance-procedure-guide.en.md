# Complete Guide to Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance (COA) - From Application to Visa Pickup

The Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance (COA) is a pre-screening document that a sponsor in Korea must first apply for at the Immigration and Foreign Affairs Office before inviting a foreign national to Korea.

It applies to foreign nationals seeking long-term residence visas such as E-7, D-8, D-9, F-6, D-2, and E-2, along with their sponsors (corporate or individual).

This article covers why a COA is needed, who applies for it, the required documents and procedures, key review points, and the entire process up to receiving the visa after issuance.

<h2 id="section-1">What is a COA and Why Do You Need It Separately</h2>

### Difference from Regular Visas

Regular visas (short-term visas such as C-3 and C-4) are applied for directly by the foreign national at the Korean diplomatic mission in their home country.

Long-term residence visas, on the other hand, require a sponsor in Korea (company, school, spouse, etc.) to first undergo pre-screening at the Immigration and Foreign Affairs Office, and the document issued as a result is the COA.

When the foreign national presents this COA number at their home country's Korean diplomatic mission, the consul issues the visa without additional review.

The key point is this: **the review is completed at Korean Immigration, not at the home country's diplomatic mission**.

### Why Go Through the Trouble of Getting a COA

Reviews at home country diplomatic missions leave a lot to consular discretion, and reasons for denial are often kept confidential.

Going through pre-screening at Korean Immigration instead allows the sponsor to directly submit supplementary documents, and if denied, the specific reasons can be confirmed in more detail.

Especially for visas requiring extensive company documentation, such as E-7 and D-8, the COA route is effectively the standard path.

> **Practical Tip:** If you have a history of visa denial after applying in your home country, rather than reapplying directly to the same diplomatic mission on the same matter, you have a higher chance of success by restructuring your case through the COA route.

<h2 id="section-2">COA Application Targets and Eligibility</h2>

### Representative Visa Types for COA Applications

| Visa | Sponsor | Main Target |
|------|---------|-------------|
| D-2 | Korean university | Degree-seeking international students |
| D-8 | Foreign-invested company | Investors and executives |
| D-9 | Trade/service corporations | Trade managers |
| E-1~E-7 | Hiring company/institution | Specialized professionals |
| F-6 | Korean spouse | Marriage immigrants |
| D-10 | (Self-application possible) | Job seekers |

### Basic Requirements the Sponsor Must Meet

For corporate sponsors, the basics include business registration, enrollment in the four major insurances, and a history of revenue and corporate tax filings.

For individual sponsors (e.g., F-6 Korean spouse), identity guarantees and proof of income/assets are key.

This is usually where things get stuck. If there are disqualifying issues on the sponsor's side (tax arrears, past violations involving foreign employment, etc.), the COA itself will be blocked no matter how good the foreign applicant's documents are.

> **Caution:** If the sponsor is registered as a workplace with a history of undocumented foreign workers, sponsorship itself is restricted for a certain period. Sometimes sponsors are registered without their knowledge, so advance verification is necessary.

<h2 id="section-3">Required Documents and Preparation Points</h2>

### Documents from the Foreign National (Invitee)

The basic documents are as follows:

- Passport copy
- COA application form
- Standard format photo
- Supporting documents per visa type (academic background, work experience, certifications)
- Criminal record certificate (depending on visa type)
- Health examination certificate (for some visas such as E-2)

### Documents from the Sponsor

These are the basic documents for corporate sponsorship:

- Business registration certificate
- Corporate registry certificate
- Tax payment certificate
- List of enrollees in the four major insurances
- Employment contract or letter of invitation explaining the reason
- Office lease agreement

### What Matters More Than Documents

Even with extensive documentation, a weak **reason for invitation and job description** can immediately derail the process.

In actual reviews, the questions "Why does it have to be this specific foreign national?" and "Why can't a Korean national fill the role instead?" come up first.

For E-7 cases, a single page of job description often carries more weight than ten pages of academic credentials.

For detailed differences in documents by visa type, you can do an initial check at the [HiKorea COA Information page](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

<h2 id="section-4">Step-by-Step Breakdown of the COA Application Process</h2>

### Overall Flow

| Step | Content | Applicant |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Step 1 | Determine visa type and review requirements | Sponsor and invitee |
| Step 2 | Prepare documents, including notarization and translation | Both parties |
| Step 3 | Make advance reservation on HiKorea | Sponsor (or agent) |
| Step 4 | Visit and submit at the jurisdictional Immigration and Foreign Affairs Office | Sponsor (or agent) |
| Step 5 | Review (respond to requests for supplementation) | Immigration |
| Step 6 | COA issuance (notification of number) | Immigration |
| Step 7 | Apply for and receive visa at home country diplomatic mission | Invitee |

### Where to Apply

Applications are submitted to the **Immigration and Foreign Affairs Office with jurisdiction over the sponsor's address**.

In-person submission is the rule, but some offices also accept electronic submissions through [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

Processing times vary by office, so the same documents can take 1-2 weeks longer or shorter depending on which office receives them. The fastest office at any given time depends on the case, so we'll guide you during consultation.

### Review Period

The standard processing period by law varies by visa type, but in practice, it's common to receive 1-2 requests for supplementary documents.

The faster you respond to supplementation requests, the shorter the overall period.

If responses at this stage are delayed or inaccurate, the review may restart from the beginning, sometimes setting things back by a month or more.

<h2 id="section-5">What Reviewers Actually Look At</h2>

### Authenticity of the Invitation

The first thing to verify is **whether the reason for invitation is genuine**.

Paper companies, businesses using borrowed names, and schools without real substance get filtered out at the first stage.

Company size, revenue, the ratio of existing domestic employees, and the physical existence of an office are all reviewed comprehensively.

### The Foreign National's Qualifications

Requirements for education, experience, and certifications differ by visa type.

For E-7, the baseline is a bachelor's degree plus one year of relevant experience, or a master's degree or higher in a related field.

For D-8 (investment), the investment amount and its source are key. Since specific investment amount standards change with each year's operational guidelines, please confirm this year's exact applicable standards during consultation.

### Disqualifying Factors

If the invitee has a past history of overstaying in Korea, deportation, or criminal records, the COA may be denied.

On the sponsor side, a history of illegally employing foreign nationals or reports of unpaid wages can also count against the application.

In many cases, what blocks the process is a past record that the applicant themselves was unaware of.

> **Caution:** Past immigration violations don't automatically expire with the passage of time. You need to verify in advance whether the entry restriction period (5 years, 10 years, etc., depending on visa type) has actually lifted.

<h2 id="section-6">From COA Issuance to Visa Pickup</h2>

### How the COA Number Is Communicated

Once issued, the **COA issuance number** is sent to the sponsor by SMS and email.

You don't need a separate paper certificate—the number alone is enough to apply for the visa at the home country diplomatic mission.

The sponsor then passes this number on to the invitee.

### Applying for the Visa at the Home Country Diplomatic Mission

The invitee goes to the Korean diplomatic mission in their home country and submits the following:

- Passport
- Visa application form
- Photo
- COA number
- Visa fee

The consul confirms the Korean Immigration review result by looking up the COA number, then issues the visa.

This stage usually takes 1-3 business days.

### Validity Period of the COA

The COA must be used to apply for a visa at the home country diplomatic mission **within 3 months from the issuance date**.

If this deadline is missed, the COA expires and the process has to start from the beginning.

If accompanying family members are involved, schedule management is essential to avoid situations where one person gets a visa while the others' applications fall out of sync.

<h2 id="section-7">What to Do When Denied</h2>

### Confirming the Reason for Denial

When a COA is denied, the reason is stated in the disposition notice.

However, it's often briefly written as something like "deemed unqualified upon review," so the actual reason needs to be confirmed separately with the case officer.

If this part is weak, you can be denied two or three times for the same reason.

### Reapplication and Appeal

A simple reapplication after denial tends to come back with the same result.

You need to first identify the exact reason, strengthen the weak points, and then resubmit.

There are also routes for administrative appeals and administrative litigation, but since these take six months to over a year, in practice, reinforced reapplication is the priority.

For strategies on reinforcement by denial case, a joint review of immigration laws on the [Korea Law Information Center](https://www.law.go.kr) along with actual disposition cases is needed.

<h2 id="section-8">FAQ</h2>

**Q1. Can the foreign national apply for the COA themselves?**

In principle, the sponsor in Korea applies. However, for some visas, such as the D-10 job-seeker visa, the applicant can apply directly at a Korean diplomatic mission, so it depends on the visa type.

**Q2. Does COA issuance guarantee that the visa will be issued?**

Almost always, but not 100%. The consul at the home country diplomatic mission can deny the visa if they find separate grounds (passport forgery, additional disqualifying factors, etc.).

**Q3. How much does the COA application cost?**

Costs vary by case, so we'll provide an exact estimate during the free consultation. It consists of government fees plus administrative processing fees.

**Q4. Can family members also receive a COA together?**

The main applicant (e.g., E-7) and family members (F-3 accompanying) are usually processed together as a bundle. Consular confirmation or apostille for family documents (marriage certificates, birth certificates, etc.) is required additionally.

**Q5. Are there ways to shorten the processing time?**

Preparing documents completely in advance, responding immediately to supplementation requests, and comparing processing speeds across offices are key. In some cases, you can change the jurisdictional office by changing your address, so case-by-case review is necessary.

**Q6. After the COA is issued, how many days until entry into Korea?**

It's just visa issuance at the home country diplomatic mission (1-3 days) plus arranging your flight. You only need to enter Korea within the COA's 3-month validity period.
