# E-7 Visa Education Requirements by Occupation: Complete Guide (2026 Edition)

The E-7 visa has separate education and experience requirements set for each occupation code, so if you don't precisely meet the standard for your specific occupation, your application will be blocked at the document stage.

It targets foreign nationals seeking to work in approximately 85–87 professional, semi-professional, and skilled technical occupations designated by the Ministry of Justice, and each occupation has different rules regarding bachelor's degree requirements, master's degree requirements, and whether work experience can substitute for education.

This article covers education standards by occupation group, the conditions under which experience can replace education, and the points where applications most often get stuck in practical review.

<h2 id="section-1">Basic Structure of E-7 Visa Education Requirements</h2>

E-7 is not a single standard but rather separate standards for each occupation group.
It splits into four branches with different education and experience requirements: **professional workers (managers and specialists)**, **semi-professional workers**, **general skilled workers**, and **skilled technical workers (E-7-4)**.
The first thing to check is which occupation code (e.g., E-7-1, E-7-2, E-7-3, E-7-4) your hired position falls under.

### Big Picture by Occupation Code

| Category | Occupation Code | Basic Education Requirement | Experience Substitution |
|------|----------|---------------|----------|
| Professional Workers | E-7-1 (Managers/Specialists, ~67 occupations) | Bachelor's degree + 1 year related experience | Master's or higher may waive experience |
| Semi-Professional Workers | E-7-2 (Office Workers, ~9 occupations) | Associate degree or higher + experience | Varies by occupation |
| General Skilled Workers | E-7-3 (Technicians, ~10 occupations) | Certification or related experience | Education requirement relaxed |
| Skilled Technical Workers | E-7-4 (Points System) | Education is one scoring item | Can be substituted by experience/income points |

> **Note:** Occupation codes are partially added or removed each year based on Ministry of Justice announcements. To verify which code your role falls under this year, check the [Hi Korea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) announcements or consult with a specialist.

### "Job Relevance" Is the Core of Education and Experience

Even if you have a bachelor's degree on paper, if your major is far removed from the hired position, it will be flagged immediately.
In practice, reviewers check the major listed on graduation/transcript documents alongside the job description to assess relevance.
If this connection is weak, even a bachelor's diploma can become grounds for rejection.

<h2 id="section-2">E-7-1 Professional Worker Education and Experience Requirements</h2>

E-7-1 is the occupation group with the highest number of applicants.
It covers managerial and specialist positions and is mainly used for hiring a company's core personnel.

### Standards for Bachelor's Degree Holders

The rule is a bachelor's degree or higher plus at least 1 year of experience in the relevant field.
The connection between major and job duties must be clear, and a diploma alone is not sufficient.
Employment certificates must include job duties, employment period, and position title.

### Standards for Master's Degree Holders or Higher

If you hold a master's degree or higher, the experience requirement may be waived.
However, the relevance between your master's major and the hired position is scrutinized more strictly.
In fact, even doctoral degree holders are frequently rejected when their major doesn't align.

### When Experience Substitutes for Education

Even without a bachelor's degree, 5 or more years of experience in the relevant field can substitute for the education requirement.
In actual review, the documents proving this 5-year experience are scrutinized most strictly.
The employing company's business registration, social insurance enrollment records, and salary transfer records are often required together.

> **Practical Tip:** Overseas experience must be supported by an employment certificate with an apostille or consular confirmation to be recognized. Documents bearing only a company seal typically get blocked at this stage.

<h2 id="section-3">E-7-2 Semi-Professional Worker Education Requirements</h2>

E-7-2 covers office worker occupations.
It includes approximately 9 occupations such as duty-free shop salesperson, airline office clerk, and hotel reception clerk.

### Associate Degree or Higher + Experience Requirement

The baseline is an associate degree (2–3 year college graduate) or higher.
Depending on the occupation, a bachelor's degree may additionally be required.
Experience requirements range from 1 to 5 years depending on the occupation.

### Occupations With Additional Korean Language Requirements

Customer-facing occupations such as duty-free shop salesperson and hotel reception clerk require proof of Korean language ability such as TOPIK.
Korean language score thresholds change by occupation and by year, so the current standard for your specific occupation needs to be verified separately.

<h2 id="section-4">E-7-3 General Skilled Worker Requirements</h2>

E-7-3 covers technician occupations such as animal handler, aquaculture technician, shipbuilding welder, painter, and musical instrument maker.

### Certification or Experience Focused

Rather than a bachelor's degree, the focus is on national technical certifications or practical work experience in the relevant field.
Without a relevant certification, 5 or more years of experience in the same occupation is generally required.
Some occupations such as shipbuilding welder also require passing additional technical examinations.

### Quota Restrictions on Introduction

E-7-3 has annual quotas for the number of workers introduced per occupation.
The number of hires available varies based on company size, ratio of Korean nationals employed, and wage levels.
Once the quota is filled, additional applications are blocked for that year, so the timing of your application itself is critical.

<h2 id="section-5">E-7-4 Skilled Technical Worker Points System</h2>

E-7-4 is a pathway for foreigners who have worked in Korea for a certain period under visas like E-9, H-2, or E-10 to transition to skilled worker status.
The scale of introduction expanded significantly after 2024, and as of 2026, it continues to operate as a major transition track.

### Education Item in the Points System

Education enters as one item within the cumulative score.
There are point differences between doctoral, master's, bachelor's, associate, and high school graduates, but lower education can be compensated by experience, age, income, and Korean language ability.
This is the biggest difference from E-7-1.

### Distinguishing Mandatory Requirements From Bonus Points

It's not enough to just meet the total score — you must first meet the mandatory requirements (domestic work experience, wage level, Korean language ability, etc.).
Falling short on even one mandatory requirement results in immediate rejection regardless of total score.
The specific passing line and bonus point items for this year are adjusted annually, so check the [Ministry of Justice Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr) announcements directly or confirm via consultation.

<h2 id="section-6">Common Documents and Frequent Pitfalls in Practice</h2>

Regardless of occupation, there are common documents required, and these are where applications frequently get blocked.

### Common Required Documents

| Document | Issuing Authority | Notes |
|------|--------|------|
| Visa Issuance Application | Application Form | Download form at [Hi Korea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) |
| Degree Certificate/Transcript | Graduating University | Overseas degrees require apostille |
| Employment Certificate | Previous Employer | Must specify duties, period, position |
| Employment Contract | Korean Hiring Company | Must specify duties, wages, work location |
| Business Registration and Other Company Documents | Korean Hiring Company | Financial statements may be added |
| Proof of Wage Payment Ability | Company Financial Materials | Revenue and Korean employee count also reviewed |

### The 3 Most Frequent Blocking Points

First is major–job mismatch.
If you have a diploma but the major is disconnected from the job duties, requests for additional materials will come in.

Second is failing to meet wage standards.
E-7 requires wages above a certain percentage of the previous year's Gross National Income (GNI), and this standard changes annually.
The basis for wage calculation (hourly/annual salary calculation method) more frequently causes issues than the wage amount itself.

Third is failure to meet company requirements.
It's common for rejections to stem from the hiring company — not the applicant — failing to meet requirements.
Korean employee ratio, revenue scale, and whether foreigners are already hired in the same occupation are reviewed together.

> **Note:** Wage standards and company requirements vary by company size even within the same occupation. Whether your company meets this year's standards requires separate review.

<h2 id="section-7">Common Patterns in Rejection Cases</h2>

Looking at actual rejection cases, "insufficient proof" is more common than the education or experience itself being inadequate.

### When Documents Exist but Explanations Are Weak

Many employment certificates list only a line or two of job duties, leaving job relevance unclear.
Even with many documents, weak job relevance can derail an application immediately.
In fact, a single supplementary job description sheet often decides the outcome.

### When Education Is Not Authenticated

Overseas degrees require authentication appropriate to the occupation — KCUE academic verification, apostille, or consular confirmation.
Submitting only a copy of the degree certificate without verification triggers immediate requests for supplementation.
Recently, some countries have faced strengthened additional verification due to suspicion of credential forgery.

### When the Wrong Code Was Selected

It's also common for applications to be rejected because the wrong code was used when a more suitable code exists for the actual job.
The same person can pass after only changing the code.
This is where the difference shows up.

<h2 id="section-8">FAQ</h2>

**Q1. Can I apply for E-7 with only work experience and no bachelor's degree?**
A. E-7-1 can substitute the education requirement with 5+ years of experience in the relevant field, and E-7-3 and E-7-4 are experience- and certification-focused from the start.
However, the documentation proving experience is required more rigorously.

**Q2. Do the major and job duties need to match exactly?**
A. Not exact matching, but "relevance" must be demonstrated.
A business major taking on a marketing role is acceptable, but applying as an IT developer with a food and nutrition major will almost certainly be rejected.

**Q3. How do I authenticate an overseas degree?**
A. You either obtain an apostille from the country where the degree was issued, or — if the country isn't an apostille signatory — get consular confirmation from a Korean consulate.
Chinese degrees often additionally require separate academic verification from China's Ministry of Education.

**Q4. What happens if wages fall slightly short of the GNI standard due to company circumstances?**
A. This typically gets blocked at this stage.
Falling short of the wage standard is difficult to resolve through supplementation, and the contract itself often needs to be rewritten.

**Q5. Are education requirements stricter when switching from D-10 to E-7?**
A. The education requirements themselves are the same, but the hiring company is scrutinized more strictly.
If company requirements are weak, applicants with identical education and experience can be rejected.

**Q6. If I'm rejected once, can I reapply for the same occupation?**
A. Yes.
However, you must accurately analyze the rejection reason and supplement the documents — reapplying with the same documents produces the same result.
The supplementation strategy for each rejection reason varies case by case and requires separate review.
