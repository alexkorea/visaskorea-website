## Capital Requirements: Approval Relies on Fund Source Explanation

### Official Capital Standards & Real-World Review  
The capital requirements for the D-8 Business Investment Visa are defined by Korea’s Foreign Investment Promotion Act and Immigration Control Act.  
However, the main challenge isn’t just meeting the numbers—it’s clearly explaining how your funds actually enter Korea and proving their legitimate source.  
Capital standards differ by industry and entity type (corporation, partnership, etc.),  
and weak evidence on fund origin can cause immediate delays or rejection.  
Be sure to confirm specifics with the relevant authority (Immigration, investment reporting bodies), as standards may change annually.

### Actual Requirements by Capital Type  
| Type of Capital | Official Requirement | Common Review Issues | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash Investment | Requires proof from a foreign exchange bank | Delays if remittance details or account flow explanation is insufficient | Requirements vary by agency |
| In-Kind Investment | Needs legal appraisal | Missing appraisals or value discrepancies demand additional proof | Frequently occurs in practice |
| Additional Payment | Must document each capital increase phase | Weak board resolution or payment proof delays approval | Watch for issues with multiple payments |

> **Practical Tip:** Having the funds in your bank account is not enough; you must show evidence that you personally transferred the funds and can explain their source, or your application may falter.

### Minimum Capital Requirements  
- Under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act:  
  - Minimum investment differs by industry (e.g., manufacturing and specific fields have separate thresholds)
  - Immigration office standards: minimum amounts and detailed requirements update annually  
- Investment criteria connect to your residency purpose in Korea  
- Fund transfers must be verified through a foreign exchange bank

> **Note:** Even if your capital has been deposited, lacking a clear explanation of the fund's origin often leads to rejection during review.

## Investor Eligibility & Company Formation: First, Check Investment Type & Entity Structure

### Investment Types & Permitted Scope  
The review process requires clarity on how you are participating—incorporating a joint-stock company, sharing equity, partnership, etc.  
Non-profit entities and limited liability companies face certain restrictions, so research by your business type and industry.  
Some applications have been delayed due to poorly explained investment structures in recent similar cases.

### Basic Steps to Set Up a Company  
| Step | Required Documents | Key Review Points | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incorporation | Articles of incorporation, company registration, investor ID | Proof of management control, actual capital contribution | Must clearly state management authority in registration |
| Investment Reporting | Foreign investor report, remittance documentation | Actual fund flow, explanation of source | Reporting requirements vary by agency |
| Immigration Application | Business plan, capital evidence | Quality of plan, justification of capital | Content matters more than plan length |

- Frequent issues occur with capital deposit following company setup  
- Business plans must be concrete and specific to pass review

### Checklist

- Remittance history under the investor’s own name
- Completed company registration
- Confirm the foreign investment reporting institution
- Attach supporting materials for the business plan
- Evidence of management participation

## D-8 Application Process: Commonly Missed Steps are Fund Transfer & Business Plan Explanation

### Main Steps  
The D-8 Business Investment Visa application generally follows these steps:

1. Foreign investment report (to local government or KOTRA)
2. Fund remittance and proof via foreign exchange bank
3. Company formation and official registration (business license)
4. Proof of capital origin and investment funds
5. Submission of business plan (prioritizing content over length)
6. D-8 visa application at Immigration

> **Practical Tip:** Most delays happen at the fund transfer stage, especially if explanations of account flows or realistic business plan content are lacking.

> **Note:** Missing documentation at any stage will result in repeated requests for additional materials, extending the review process.

### Required Documents By Stage  
| Stage | Required Documents | Explanation Needed | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foreign Investment Report | Report form, identification | Explanation of investment purpose | Form differs by agency |
| Company Formation | Articles, registration, proof of capital | Explanation of fund inflow post-incorporation | Common practical issue |
| Visa Application | Business plan, fund proof, management evidence | Concrete plans for business operation | Most critical in actual review |

> Recently, the strength of your business plan evidence—not its length—matters most during evaluation.

## Business Plan Preparation: The Key Is Persuasiveness—Weak Plans Get Rejected

### Practical Business Plan Tips  
Don’t just describe your business: show specific feasibility, plans for using the invested funds, sales projections, and detailed operations.  
Lacking evidence of investor status or business planning will result in repeated requests for more information during review.  
Submitting a business plan without proper review can lead to immediate rejection.

### Content Checklist  
- Clear business objectives and industry explanation  
- Detailed plan for using investment funds  
- Justification for revenue and profit projections  
- Management and operational structure  
- Clarify the investor’s role
