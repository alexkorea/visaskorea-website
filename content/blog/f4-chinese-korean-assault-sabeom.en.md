# Chinese-Korean F-4 Assault & Sexual Harassment Immigration Review and Deportation Standards: A Practical Guide

If you're staying in Korea on an F-4 visa and become involved in an assault or sexual harassment case, even a light criminal penalty often leads to deportation through immigration violation review.

This guide is for F-4 (Overseas Korean) visa holders who have become criminal suspects or defendants, as well as those who have already received fines or suspended sentences.

We'll cover the practical standards and key response points—from the moment an assault or sexual harassment case is referred to immigration review through to a final deportation order.

<h2 id="section-1">When Chinese-Korean F-4 Assault Immigration Review Begins</h2>

F-4 assault cases don't end at the police stage.

Once the prosecutor's disposition is reported to immigration authorities, the immigration violation review formally begins.

### How Criminal Cases Are Reported

When the police file a case, it's forwarded to the prosecutor's office.

Once the prosecutor's disposition (indictment, summary indictment, non-indictment) is finalized, the result is automatically reported to the Immigration and Foreign Residents Office.

A commonly overlooked point: the immigration violation review begins not from the date the fine is finalized, but from the moment the prosecutor's disposition report arrives.

It's common for records to already be in the immigration system without the person even knowing.

### Major Offenses That Refer F-4 Holders to Immigration Review

Assault, bodily injury, assault resulting in injury, and aggravated assault are the most common.

For sexual harassment, the most frequent violations are indecent act by force, illegal photography (camera offenses), and obscene communications—all violations of the Act on Special Cases Concerning the Punishment of Sexual Crimes.

When DUI, theft, or fraud are added to the mix, the severity of the disposition grows significantly heavier.

> **Warning:** Don't assume you're safe just because you paid a fine through a summary order. Even fine sentences are reported to immigration in full and become subject to the violation review.

<h2 id="section-2">What Actually Determines F-4 Deportation Outcomes</h2>

The legal grounds for deportation are specified in Article 46 of the Immigration Control Act (see the [Korean Law Information Center](https://www.law.go.kr)).

Foreigners who receive a sentence of a fine or higher may be included as deportation candidates.

### Case Content Matters More Than the Fine Amount

This is the most commonly misunderstood part in practice.

A 3 million won fine isn't automatically dangerous, nor is a 2 million won fine automatically safe.

In actual review, what's examined first is the offense type, whether there's a victim, whether a settlement was reached, and the likelihood of reoffending.

A 1 million won fine for sexual harassment carries a much higher deportation risk than a 5 million won fine for simple assault.

### Settlement Agreement: The Decisive Variable

Settlement with the victim matters at the criminal punishment stage, but it weighs even more heavily in the immigration review.

Without a settlement agreement, the victim's statement becomes the direct basis for the immigration authorities' judgment.

For sexual harassment cases especially, lack of settlement frequently leads straight to deportation.

| Case Type | With Settlement | Without Settlement | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple Assault (fine) | Departure order or stay permission | Possible deportation | Severity of harm matters |
| Assault Resulting in Injury | Mostly departure orders | Deportation more likely | Medical report has major impact |
| Indecent Act by Force | Departure order possible | Deportation nearly certain | Victim's statement is decisive |
| Illegal Photography | Case-by-case | Deportation | Reoffending risk assessed |

### How F-4 Status Itself Becomes an Aggravating Factor

F-4 is not a simple employment visa—it's granted on the basis of recognized status as an overseas Korean.

Ironically, this often works against you in immigration review.

The fact that someone granted the privilege of settling in Korean society as a fellow Korean committed assault or a sexual offense is treated as an aggravating factor.

<h2 id="section-3">The Immigration Review Process and What You'll Face Directly</h2>

The immigration violation review usually begins when you receive a Notice to Appear.

### What to Do First When the Notice Arrives

The Notice to Appear lists the appearance date, the offense, and the assigned investigator.

A common stumbling block: people just show up without knowing what materials to prepare.

The investigator's questions resemble a criminal interrogation record, but the angle is different.

Criminal investigators ask whether you committed the crime. Immigration review asks whether you're qualified to remain in Korea.

### Where Statements Often Go Wrong

Many people, while admitting fault, try to explain the circumstances and let their statements drag on too long.

Instead of writing lengthy explanations, it's better to clearly organize the facts, the status of settlement efforts, family relationships, and the basis of your life in Korea.

In particular, records of family support, children's schooling, and economic activity in Korea are frequently recognized as grounds for mitigation to a departure order.

### Disposition Outcomes and Notification Timing

A disposition is typically issued between 2 weeks and 2 months after appearance.

The possible outcomes break down as follows:

- Deportation Order: 5-year (or longer) entry ban
- Departure Order: Voluntary departure, with possible re-entry after a set period
- Stay Permission (with fine): Status maintained, continued stay in Korea allowed

Even for the same case, how you organize and present your circumstances can swing the outcome.

<h2 id="section-5">Why F-4 Holders Are Especially Disadvantaged in Sexual Harassment Cases</h2>

Even when sentences for sexual harassment aren't heavier than for assault, they carry the strongest weight in the immigration review.

### The Victim's Statement Is Reflected Almost Verbatim

Sexual harassment cases rely more on testimony than physical evidence.

When the victim's statement is consistent, suspects are often indicted at the prosecutor's stage even when they deny the charges.

Once indicted, the immigration review almost automatically heads toward deportation.

### Illegal Photography Cases Are Evaluated Separately

If this element is present, reoffending risk is judged to be high.

Even a single photographic instance tilts the disposition toward deportation.

When distribution is added on top, the entry ban period extends even further.

### Late Settlement Loses Its Weight

Settlement before the prosecutor's disposition has the greatest effect.

Settlements reached after the verdict are noted in the immigration review but rarely reverse a disposition.

> **Practical Tip:** For sexual harassment cases, securing a statement of no desire for punishment from the victim—in addition to the settlement agreement—is weighed even more heavily in the immigration review.

<h2 id="section-6">Avenues to Contest a Deportation Decision</h2>

A disposition isn't the end of the road.

### Objection and Administrative Appeal

After receiving a deportation order, you can file an objection within a set period from the date of notification.

If the objection is rejected, the next steps are an administrative appeal or administrative litigation.

The commonly overlooked piece here is the deadline.

Miss the deadline once, and the disposition becomes final as is.

### How to Have Deportation Reduced to a Departure Order

Petitioning to have deportation converted to a departure order is also a common practical move.

When children hold Korean nationality or attend school in Korea, when a spouse is a Korean national or permanent resident, or when there's a parental support obligation, these may be recognized as mitigating grounds.

### Application for Temporary Release from Protective Custody

If you're already in protective custody, you can apply for temporary release to secure your release.

It requires a deposit and conditions, but it's a necessary step for working out settlement with family and preparing the administrative appeal.

| Stage | Available Action | Deadline | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Immediately after disposition | Objection | Short period after notification | Filed with Immigration Office |
| After objection denial | Administrative appeal | 90 days after notification | Central Administrative Appeals Commission |
| After appeal dismissal | Administrative litigation | 90 days after ruling delivery | Administrative Court |
| During protective custody | Temporary release | Anytime | Deposit and conditions apply |

Processing times and acceptance rates vary by case, so a strategy tailored to your specific case requires direct review.

<h2 id="section-7">Often-Overlooked Practical Points in F-4 Immigration Review Response</h2>

Submitting a large stack of documents alone doesn't improve outcomes.

### Records of Settlement in Korea: Flow Matters More Than Form

Children's school enrollment certificates, spouse's family relation certificate, your four major insurances enrollment history, lease contract, tax payment records.

These materials are weak when scattered separately.

It's far more persuasive to organize chronologically: when you entered Korea, what work you did, and how you've supported your family.

### Letters of Apology: Specificity Matters More Than Length

A letter of apology that just repeats "I was wrong" has no effect.

You should specifically describe the circumstances of the incident, your apology to the victim, the concrete actions you've taken to prevent recurrence, and the impact on your family.

### Accuracy of Interpreter-Mediated Statements

Even if Chinese-Korean speakers are proficient in Korean, you can request an interpreter for the immigration review to ensure accurate statements.

People who feel comfortable in Korean and proceed without an interpreter sometimes find that subtle differences in expression get recorded against them.

In sexual harassment cases especially, a single word can change the outcome.

<h2 id="section-8">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. I received a 1 million won summary fine—will my F-4 be revoked?**

The decision isn't based on fine amount alone. The offense, whether there's a victim, whether a settlement was reached, and the time elapsed since obtaining F-4 status are all considered together. For a 1 million won simple assault fine, there's room for a departure order or stay permission, but for a 1 million won sexual harassment fine, deportation is highly likely.

**Q2. Does a late settlement still have any effect?**

It still has effect up until the immigration disposition is issued. However, the effect is weaker than settling earlier at the prosecutor's stage. Even after receiving the Notice to Appear, securing and submitting a settlement quickly before the disposition is far better than not.

**Q3. Can I re-enter Korea after being deported?**

Only after the entry ban period passes. The standard is typically 5 years, but it can extend longer depending on the case. In some cases, you'll also need to file a separate application to lift the entry ban. The exact period and prospects for lifting depend on the contents of your specific disposition order, which needs to be checked individually.

**Q4. Can I still be deported even if my family is in Korea?**

Family circumstances are considered as mitigating factors, but they're not absolute. If your spouse is Korean or your child holds Korean nationality, conversion to a departure order is possible—but for sex offenses, family circumstances are less readily accepted.

**Q5. What happens if I delay or skip my immigration review appearance?**

Refusing to appear or failing to appear without notice has the most damaging effect. You may be judged a flight risk and placed in protective custody, and the disposition will be decided without your own statement. If the schedule is difficult, you must request a change in advance.

**Q6. Do I still face immigration review if my assault case ended with no charges?**

If the prosecutor returns a finding of no charges, in principle you're excluded from immigration review. However, dispositions like suspension of indictment or dismissal aren't criminal convictions but still leave a record with immigration and can affect future applications.
