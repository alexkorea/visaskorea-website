# E-7-1 Visa Application Requirements and Process: A Complete Guide from Occupation Codes to Approval

The E-7-1 Specially Designated Activities visa is granted only when your education, work experience, and Korean employment contract align precisely with the designated occupation code.
It targets foreign professionals working in one of 86 specialized occupations designated by the Ministry of Justice, with a bachelor's degree or higher — or at least 5 years of experience in the same field — serving as the baseline requirement.
This guide walks through eligibility, occupation code matching, required documents, key review points, and rejection reasons, all from a practical standpoint.

<h2 id="section-1">What Is the E-7-1 Visa? Core Eligibility Requirements</h2>

Among the E-7 visa categories, E-7-1 is the most common one.
It is issued only for professional occupations officially designated by the Ministry of Justice — basic office jobs and general sales positions are not eligible.
The relevant regulations can be found in Appendix 1 of the [Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act](https://www.law.go.kr) and the [HiKorea visa issuance guide](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

### Education and Experience Requirements

The basic rule is **a bachelor's degree plus at least 1 year of experience**, **a master's degree or higher**, or **at least 5 years of experience in the relevant field**.
In practice, the first thing reviewed is how closely your education aligns with the job duties.
For example, if an applicant with a business degree applies for an IT development position, the case typically gets stopped at this stage.
When the degree and job duties don't match, the experience certificate becomes the deciding factor.

### Employer (Sponsoring Company) Requirements

The hiring company's qualifications are reviewed at the same time.
Reviewers look at the national employment protection assessment (whether the hire would displace Korean workers), revenue scale, foreign-employee ratio, and salary levels.
For newly established entities or those with low revenue, the foreign-employment quota is usually where things get blocked.
Since the industry-specific quota benchmarks are adjusted annually, you'll need a consultation to confirm where your company stands.

<h2 id="section-2">The 86 Specialized Occupation Codes: The First Hurdle Most People Hit</h2>

E-7-1 is tied to occupation codes based on the KSCO (Korean Standard Classification of Occupations).
The education and experience requirements shift depending on which code your job falls under.
What matters even more than the documents themselves is **whether the occupation code matches your actual job duties**.

### Examples of Occupation Code Categories

| Job Category | Representative Codes | Education/Experience Standard | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Managers | 1212–1349 | Bachelor's + experience, or master's | Executive level reviewed separately |
| Professionals and related | 2212–2855 | Bachelor's + 1 year of experience | Job-duty alignment emphasized |
| Selected clerical roles | 3125–3201 | Bachelor's + experience | Strong national employment protection |
| Selected sales/service | 4etc | Bachelor's + experience | Very limited |

> **Caution:** Even for the same role at the same company, the chance of rejection can swing dramatically depending on which occupation code you select. Once chosen, the code is difficult to change.

### Common Code-Matching Pitfalls

Roles like overseas sales, marketing, and office administration face stricter national employment protection screening.
In particular, when a company tries to hire a Korean-fluent foreign national simply for interpretation or translation work, applications filed under clerical codes are frequently rejected.
If this area is weak, no amount of paperwork will get the case through.

<h2 id="section-3">Required Documents: From Both the Applicant and the Company</h2>

E-7-1 requires separate document packages from the applicant and the sponsoring company.
If either side comes up short, a supplementation request follows and the review drags on.

### Applicant Documents

- Visa issuance certificate application form
- Passport copy and standard-format photo
- Degree certificate (apostille or consular authentication)
- Experience certificate (specifying employment period, duties, and salary)
- Certifications, awards, and other supporting evidence

### Sponsoring Company Documents

- Business registration certificate
- Employment contract (specifying duties, salary, and workplace)
- Tax payment certificate and financial statements
- National Pension and Employment Insurance enrollment list
- Statement of reasons for hiring a foreign national

> **Practical tip:** Degree apostilles can only be issued in the country of origin, and processing times vary from 2 weeks to 2 months depending on the country. Trying to obtain one after arriving in Korea usually throws off the entire schedule.

<h2 id="section-4">Application Process: Certificate of Visa Issuance vs. Direct Visa Application</h2>

E-7-1 can be pursued through two routes.
Which path is faster depends on the applicant's current location and country of origin.

### Certificate of Visa Issuance (COA) Route

The Korean sponsoring company first applies for a Certificate of Visa Issuance at the Immigration Office.
Once approved, the applicant receives the visa at the Korean embassy in their home country.
This is the standard approach in most cases.
Processing times vary by Immigration Office, and in some cases you can select the fastest-processing jurisdiction.

### Direct Visa Application Route

In this route, the applicant applies directly at the Korean embassy in their home country.
Required documents and processing times differ by embassy, and some countries require an additional interview.

| Category | COA Route | Direct Application |
|---|---|---|
| Filing party | Korean sponsoring company | Applicant in person |
| Filing location | Immigration Office | Korean embassy abroad |
| Processing time | Typically 2–4 weeks | Varies by embassy |
| Additional interview | Rarely | Depends on country |

Processing times vary widely by jurisdiction and filing season, sometimes stretching 1.5 to 2 times longer during peak periods.
Selecting the optimal jurisdiction for your timeline requires advance consultation.

<h2 id="section-5">What Reviewers Actually Look At</h2>

Even with a perfectly assembled document package, plenty of applications still fail at review.
In practice, reviewers focus less on checking off document items and more on **why this particular role has to be filled by a foreign national**.

### Job Suitability

Reviewers look at how directly your education and experience connect to the hiring role.
A computer science graduate with 5 years of development experience moving into a developer role is straightforward.
By contrast, when an unrelated degree is paired with a different occupation, the experience justification has to do a lot more work.

### Salary Level and National Employment Protection

Salary must meet or exceed the industry average — or a defined percentage of the prior year's Gross National Income (GNI).
This threshold is adjusted annually, so this year's exact figure needs to be verified through [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr) notices or a consultation.
If salary falls short, the case usually gets stopped here regardless of the occupation code.

### Company Credibility

Revenue scale, tax payment history, and prior history of hiring foreign nationals are all reviewed together.
For newly established entities less than a year old, weakness in this area can extend review times or lead to rejection.

<h2 id="section-6">Rejection Reasons and Reapplication Strategy</h2>

The rejection notice lists the stated reason, but the real cause often runs deeper.

### Common Rejection Reasons

- Inappropriate occupation code (the most common)
- Mismatch between degree and job duties
- Salary below the threshold
- Company exceeds revenue or foreign-employment quota limits
- Document formatting issues (missing apostille, etc.)

### Considerations for Reapplication

Resubmitting the same package after a rejection produces the same result.
You need to analyze the rejection reason precisely and decide where to make changes — occupation code, strengthened experience records, or improved company-side documentation.
Reapplication typically requires 1–3 months of preparation, and can stretch longer depending on the case.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)</h2>

**Q1. Can I apply for E-7-1 with only an associate's degree?**
A. Less than a bachelor's degree is difficult in principle, but it remains possible if at least 5 years of relevant field experience can be documented. How the experience certificate is written becomes the critical factor.

**Q2. The Korean company hiring me is quite small — can I still apply?**
A. The application itself is possible even with modest revenue and headcount. However, if you exceed the foreign-employment quota ratio, rejection is the usual outcome. Reviewing quota limits by company size is the first step.

**Q3. Can I switch from D-10 to E-7-1 without leaving Korea?**
A. Yes, through a domestic application for change of status — no departure required. That said, whether you met the job-suitability requirements during your D-10 stay is reviewed separately.

**Q4. My degree is from a foreign university — is an apostille mandatory?**
A. Apostille for Hague Convention countries, consular authentication for non-member countries. The two are frequently confused, so confirming in advance is essential.

**Q5. How long does E-7-1 visa processing take?**
A. Typically 2–4 weeks, but this varies significantly by jurisdiction and timing. We can advise on the fastest-processing jurisdiction during consultation.

**Q6. I've been rejected before — can I reapply?**
A. Yes. Be aware, though, that rejection records influence subsequent reviews, so addressing the specific rejection reason takes priority. Resubmitting the same documents is not recommended.
