# Practical Guide to F-4 Korean-Chinese Assault & Sexual Harassment Violation Review and Deportation Standards

When an F-4 holder receives criminal punishment for assault or sexual harassment, depending on the fine amount and outcome, they become subject to a violation review (사범심사), and if certain thresholds are crossed, this leads to cancellation of residence status and deportation.

These standards partially overlap with H-2 as well, but F-4 (Overseas Korean status) differs in that recovering the status once lost is far more difficult.

We'll walk through the flow from the occurrence of an assault or sexual harassment incident, through violation review notification, opinion statement submission, to the deportation decision — along with the points where things most often get stuck in practice.

<h2 id="section-1">When Does the Violation Review Begin?</h2>

### How Criminal Punishment Results Are Reported to Immigration

Once a sentence is finalized by the prosecution or court, the result is automatically transmitted electronically to the Immigration & Foreign Residents Office.

Even a fine can trigger a violation review depending on the amount and the offense, and the data is transferred without the person needing to report it themselves.

In practice, the most common scenario is someone thinking "I paid the fine, so that's the end of it," only to be caught off guard months later when they receive a violation review notice.

### What to Prepare Before the Notice Arrives

Here's the key point. By the time the notice arrives, the initial internal assessment by Immigration is often already complete.

The first thing to secure is the original court ruling, summary order, or prosecutor's disposition record.

Without these documents, even a well-written opinion statement cannot contest the facts.

For detailed disposition records, you can look up your own case through the [Criminal Justice Portal](https://www.kics.go.kr), but some dispositions require a direct visit to the prosecutor's office.

<h2 id="section-2">Practical Standards for Assault Violation Reviews</h2>

### The Difference Between Simple Assault and Bodily Injury

Simple assault (Criminal Act Article 260) and bodily injury (Criminal Act Article 257) carry different weight in Immigration's judgment.

Simple assault is a crime "not prosecutable against the victim's will," so reaching a settlement often ends the case with a "no prosecution" outcome — but with bodily injury, punishment remains even after settlement.

In actual reviews, the **issuance of a medical injury certificate**, **whether a weapon was used**, and **the number of victims** become the key factors.

| Offense | Punishment Outcome | Review Severity | Notes |
|---------|--------------------|-----------------|-------|
| Simple assault (settled) | No prosecution | Low | Status retention possible |
| Simple assault (fined) | Fine | Medium | Varies by amount |
| Bodily injury (fined) | Fine | High | Amount and context viewed together |
| Bodily injury (suspended sentence) | Suspended prison term | Very high | Deportation review candidate |
| Special assault/injury | Aggravated punishment | Very high | High likelihood of status cancellation |

### What the Fine Amount Actually Means

Generally, fines above a certain amount automatically trigger a violation review.

In practice, however, **prior convictions**, **whether it's a repeat offense**, and **the circumstances of the incident** carry more weight than the single fine amount.

In particular, assaults during drinking, domestic assaults, and cases where the victim is Korean are judged differently even at the same fine level.

This year's violation review standards have been partially adjusted, so check through consultation whether the updated criteria apply to your case.

<h2 id="section-3">Deportation Standards for Sexual Harassment Cases</h2>

### Distinguishing Indecent Act by Force from Indecency in Crowded Places

For sexual harassment, the offense name alone tells you the weight of the disposition.

Indecent act by force (Criminal Act Article 298) has a heavy statutory penalty, making it difficult to pass a violation review even when the outcome is a fine.

Indecency in crowded places (Sexual Violence Punishment Act Article 11) applies to subways, buses, and the like — even a small fine carries significant impact on residence status.

### Whether the Person Is Subject to Sex Offender Registration

The critical question is whether the person becomes subject to sex offender registration.

If so, under Article 42 of the [Act on Special Cases Concerning the Punishment of Sexual Crimes](https://www.law.go.kr/법령/성폭력범죄의처벌등에관한특례법), personal information is registered and managed for a set period.

In this case, Immigration practice almost automatically moves into a deportation review.

> **Warning:** Sex crimes — even with only a fine — are highly likely to qualify as grounds for deportation under Article 46 of the Immigration Control Act. Don't let yourself off the hook just because the matter was settled.

### The Practical Effect of Settlement

In sexual harassment cases, whether a settlement was reached heavily influences sentencing, but at the violation review stage, what matters more than the settlement document itself is **the genuineness of victim recovery**.

A formal settlement document with only money exchanged can actually become a weakness.

The victim's expression of intent, a non-punishment statement, and the offender's conduct after the incident all need to be presented together for it to count as a plus.

<h2 id="section-4">Deportation Decision Standards and Procedures</h2>

### Statutory Basis Under the Immigration Control Act

The direct legal basis for a deportation order is Article 46 of the [Immigration Control Act](https://www.law.go.kr/법령/출입국관리법).

In particular, Article 46(1)(13) (a person released after being sentenced to imprisonment or heavier) and (3) (persons designated by Ministry of Justice ordinance) are frequently applied.

For F-4, this must be considered together with disqualification grounds under Article 5 of the Overseas Koreans Act.

### How Status Cancellation and Deportation Proceed Together

In practice, the sequence runs: violation review → cancellation of residence status (F-4 stripped) → departure order or deportation.

| Step | Authority | Notification Format | Your Response |
|------|-----------|---------------------|---------------|
| Violation review notice | Immigration & Foreign Residents Office | Mail or registered post | Submit opinion statement |
| Investigation appearance | Local Immigration office | Summons | Statement of fact |
| Status cancellation decision | Ministry of Justice | Disposition notice | Appeal possible |
| Departure order/deportation | Immigration | Official order | Administrative litigation possible |

### The Difference Between a Departure Order and Deportation

A departure order is a format in which the person leaves voluntarily, while deportation is a procedure where the person is repatriated after being physically secured.

The big difference between the two dispositions is **when reentry becomes possible**.

If deported, you fall under the grounds for entry ban in Article 11 of the Immigration Control Act, and reentry is blocked for an extended period.

Converting a deportation into a departure order whenever possible is the single most important negotiation point in practice.

<h2 id="section-5">What People Most Often Miss in Opinion Statements</h2>

### Hit the Key Points Rather Than Writing Long

Writing a long opinion statement does not make it more persuasive.

This is usually where people get tripped up. They write at length about their own position, while the information Immigration actually wants to see is missing.

What the reviewing officer wants to see in the opinion statement is clear:

- The circumstances of the incident and the degree to which the person acknowledges responsibility
- Efforts at victim recovery (settlement, apology, medical expense support, etc.)
- Family relationships and livelihood foundation in Korea
- Concrete measures to prevent recurrence
- Settlement circumstances in Korea since acquiring F-4

### Attached Documents Can Matter More Than the Opinion Statement Itself

More important than the documents themselves is the consistency among the attached documents.

Family relationship certificates, resident registration extracts (when family members are Korean), employment certificates, settlement agreements, written apologies, and volunteer activity verifications shouldn't be mere attachments — they need to be organized as **evidence supporting your claims**.

If this part is weak, the opinion statement itself loses persuasive force.

> **Practical tip:** A written apology should be handwritten, and must concretely show acknowledgment of the incident and resolve to prevent recurrence. Repeating only formulaic expressions actually becomes a deduction.

<h2 id="section-6">Possibility of Reentry After Deportation</h2>

### Calculating the Entry Ban Period

A deported person is barred from entry under Article 11(1)(3) and (4) of the Immigration Control Act.

The length of the ban varies by case, considering the content of criminal punishment, attitude, and any record of violations during prior stay.

For those deported for sex crimes, the entry ban period is usually calculated as longer than for general assault.

### Applying for Entry Ban Lifting

Even during the ban period, in some cases you can apply to lift it through the [Ministry of Justice](https://www.moj.go.kr) or [Hi Korea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

That said, for those deported for serious offenses such as sex crimes or bodily injury, the application itself is often rejected.

The point at which lifting becomes possible depends on the type of disposition, so accurate applicability needs to be confirmed by an expert.

<h2 id="section-7">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

**Q1. My fine is under KRW 1 million — could I avoid the violation review?**

The fine amount alone is not the deciding factor. The offense, prior convictions, and circumstances are considered together. At the same fine level, sexual harassment and simple assault are handled very differently.

**Q2. I reached a settlement — why am I still subject to a violation review?**

A settlement has effect at the criminal punishment stage, but the Immigration violation review proceeds separately based on the criminal punishment outcome. Even after a settlement, if a fine is finalized, a notice can still arrive.

**Q3. What happens if I receive a violation review notice but don't submit an opinion statement?**

Failing to submit is treated as abandoning your position. The disposition will be decided solely on Immigration's internal materials, which is highly likely to produce an unfavorable result.

**Q4. If my F-4 is canceled, can I switch to a different status?**

Depending on family relationships, options may exist — F-6 if your spouse is Korean, F-1 if your child holds Korean nationality, and so on. However, the violation review grounds often carry forward as disqualifying grounds, so separate review is needed.

**Q5. If I get it converted to a departure order, can I come back later?**

A departure order has shorter reentry restrictions than deportation. That said, the underlying entry-ban grounds don't simply disappear, so a reentry application is still needed after a certain period.

**Q6. My criminal trial is ongoing in Korea — does the violation review proceed at the same time?**

Before the criminal trial ends, the violation review is usually suspended. However, in cases such as detention, it may be handled separately depending on the specifics.
