# H-2 Working Visit Visa: Allowed Industries and Restricted Occupations — Complete 2026 Guide

H-2 Working Visit visa holders cannot work in every industry — employment is only permitted in industries authorized by law.
Working outside the permitted industries constitutes illegal employment, which is grounds for visa cancellation and deportation.
This guide covers the 2026 list of permitted industries, prohibited occupations, mandatory employment education requirements, employer reporting obligations, and penalties for violations.

## What Is the H-2 Working Visit Visa?

The H-2 Working Visit visa allows ethnic Koreans from China and former Soviet Union countries (overseas Koreans) to engage in employment activities in Korea.
Issued by the [Immigration and Foreign Affairs Office](https://www.immigration.go.kr), the maximum stay is 3 years (extendable to a maximum of 4 years and 10 months with one renewal).
It is designed for overseas Koreans who wish to work rather than simply visit, and operates on a separate system from the E-9 Employment Permit System.

The defining feature of the H-2 visa is its "industry restriction" model.
Unlike E-9 visa holders who are tied to a specific workplace, H-2 holders may change jobs freely within the permitted industries.
However, working in a non-permitted industry immediately constitutes illegal employment.

## List of Permitted Industries (2026)

Based on the [Ministry of Employment and Labor](https://www.moel.go.kr) official notice, H-2 visa holders may work in the following industries.

### Manufacturing

- Food manufacturing
- Textile, clothing, and footwear manufacturing
- Paper and wood manufacturing
- Chemical, rubber, and plastic manufacturing
- Metal, machinery, electrical, and electronics manufacturing
- Other manufacturing (limited to businesses with fewer than 30 employees)

### Agriculture and Livestock

- Crop cultivation (rice farming, field crops, greenhouse horticulture, specialty crops)
- Livestock farming (animal husbandry, slaughtering)
- Agricultural support services

### Fishing Industry

- Coastal and offshore fishing
- Aquaculture
- Seafood processing and handling

### Construction

- Simple manual labor at construction sites (permitted workplaces only)
- Building finishing construction
- Some civil engineering work

### Service Industry (Limited Scope)

- Food service (simple tasks such as kitchen assistance and serving)
- Accommodation (simple support work such as cleaning and laundry)
- Recycling collection
- Building cleaning services
- Domestic services (including care services)
- Parking lot operations
- Security services (limited)

**Important:** The above list is based on major industry categories. Permitted status varies by detailed industry classification codes.
If you are unsure whether your specific occupation falls within the permitted industries, it is safest to check with a professional before starting work.

## Prohibited Occupations

The following occupations are not available to H-2 visa holders.

**Professional Occupations (Requiring License or Qualification)**

- Healthcare professions: doctors, nurses, pharmacists
- Legal professions: lawyers, legal scriveners, administrative agents
- Education professions: teachers, instructors (foreign language conversation instructors require a separate visa)
- Certified public accountants, tax accountants

**Management and Office Occupations**

- Corporate executives
- General office work (administration, accounting, etc.)
- Human resources and labor management

**Other Restricted Occupations**

- Entertainment venue-related work (covered under the E-6 visa)
- Adult entertainment and gambling-related work
- Insurance agents and securities sales
- Manufacturing businesses with 50 or more employees (some exceptions apply)

## Mandatory Employment Education

H-2 visa holders who wish to work in Korea for the first time must complete mandatory employment education before starting work.

**Employment Education Overview**

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Education provider | Designated institutions under the Human Resources Development Service of Korea |
| Duration | 16 hours (2-day course) |
| Content | Korean labor law, workplace safety, employment procedures, etc. |
| When required | Must complete before first employment |
| Cost | Small self-paid fee (varies by case) |

Working without completing the education constitutes illegal employment.
Course schedules are available on the [Human Resources Development Service of Korea](https://www.hrdkorea.or.kr) website.
After completing the course, you will receive an employment education certificate which must be submitted to your employer.

## Employer Hiring and Termination Reporting Obligations

Employers who hire foreign nationals on H-2 visas must fulfill the following reporting obligations.

**Hiring Report**

- Within 14 days of hiring
- Via the [Ministry of Employment and Labor EPS Portal](https://www.eps.go.kr) or in person at an employment center
- Required documents: employment contract, copy of Alien Registration Card, copy of business registration certificate

**Termination/Resignation Report**

- Within 14 days of the employment relationship ending
- Filed with the same agency
- Failure to report: administrative fine of up to KRW 1 million

**Note:** Hiring H-2 foreign nationals without proper reporting can result in illegal employment penalties.
The [Ministry of Employment and Labor](https://www.moel.go.kr) has recently intensified enforcement against illegal employment of foreign nationals.

## Workplace Change Rules

Unlike E-9 visa holders, H-2 visa holders can change jobs relatively freely within the permitted industries.

**Conditions for Free Job Changes**

- The new workplace must fall within the permitted industries
- Employment education certificate must remain valid
- New employer must file a hiring report within 14 days

**Process After Changing Workplaces**

After the job change, the new employer files a new hiring report with the employment center.
There is no requirement for a separate employment permit or change count limit as with E-9 visas.
However, changing to a non-permitted industry is illegal and grounds for visa cancellation.

## Penalty Standards for Violations

**Violations by Foreign Workers**

| Violation | Penalty |
| --- | --- |
| Working outside permitted industries | Visa cancellation, deportation, entry ban |
| Working without completing employment education | Fine, visa cancellation |
| Illegal employment | Deportation, 5-year entry ban |

**Violations by Employers**

| Violation | Penalty |
| --- | --- |
| Illegal employment (outside permitted industries) | Up to 3 years imprisonment or fines up to KRW 20 million |
| Failure to file hiring/termination reports | Administrative fine up to KRW 1 million |
| Hiring workers who have not completed employment education | Fine, employment permit restrictions |

In actual enforcement cases, deportation frequently occurs when workers misunderstand the scope of permitted industries.
Always verify whether your specific occupation is permitted before starting work.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Can I work at a restaurant with an H-2 visa?**

The food service industry is included in the H-2 permitted list, but only for simple tasks such as kitchen assistance and serving.
Formal chef positions at upscale restaurants or management roles may not qualify.
Please confirm the specific industry classification code with an employment center or professional before starting work.

**Q2. Do I need to take employment education only once?**

If you complete the 16-hour education before your first employment on an H-2 visa, you do not need to retake it every time you change jobs.
However, if you renew your visa or return to Korea after a long absence, re-completion may be required.
Check the latest regulations with the [Human Resources Development Service of Korea](https://www.hrdkorea.or.kr).

**Q3. Can I work in an office job with an H-2 visa?**

General office work, accounting, human resources management, and similar office occupations are not included in the H-2 permitted industries.
If you wish to work in an office role, you should explore other visa options such as the E-7 Special Activities visa.
The appropriate visa type for your individual situation can be determined through professional consultation.

**Q4. If my employer fails to fulfill the reporting obligation, does the foreign worker also face penalties?**

The reporting obligation rests in principle with the employer.
However, if the illegal employment status continues, the foreign worker may also be treated as an illegal worker.
If your employer fails to file the report, report the situation directly to the employment center or seek assistance from an administrative agent.

**Q5. Can I change from an H-2 visa to an F-4 or F-5 visa while staying in Korea?**

If you meet the eligibility requirements, a change of status is possible.
The F-4 (Overseas Korean) visa requires nationality and residency registration conditions, while the F-5 (permanent residence) visa requires meeting multiple criteria including length of stay, income, and criminal background.
Eligibility varies by individual circumstances, so professional consultation is strongly recommended.
