# E-1 Professor Visa: Complete Guide to Eligibility and Requirements

The E-1 professor visa is a long-term residency status issued to foreign academics formally appointed under Korea's Higher Education Act.

It is primarily for foreign professors, associate professors, assistant professors, and lecturers who handle teaching, research, and student supervision at universities, junior colleges, and graduate schools. Approval requires three conditions to align simultaneously: academic credentials, appointment type, and institutional eligibility.

This article walks through eligibility standards, differences by appointment type, required documents, common review bottlenecks, and FAQs — all from a practical workflow perspective.

<h2 id="section-1">Legal Basis and Scope of the E-1 Visa</h2>

E-1 is one of the long-term residency statuses listed in Annex 1-2 of the Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act, designed for qualified foreign faculty under Articles 14 and 17 of the Higher Education Act.

The governing statutes can be reviewed at the [Enforcement Decree of the Immigration Act](https://www.law.go.kr) and the [Higher Education Act](https://www.law.go.kr), while procedural guidance is published on [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr).

### Positions Covered

The positions typically grouped under E-1 include:

- Professors, associate professors, and assistant professors
- Full-time lecturers and formally appointed part-time lecturers
- Honorary, visiting, and adjunct professors when an official appointment contract has been signed
- Senior researchers at university-affiliated research institutes who also carry teaching duties

### Statuses Often Confused with E-1

The most frequent point of confusion in consultations is distinguishing between similar statuses.

Pure research roles often fall under E-3, conversation-based foreign language instruction under E-2, and teaching at private academies or lifelong education facilities under E-7.

> **Note:** Simply "working at a university" does not automatically qualify someone for E-1. The appointment format and job content must meet the faculty requirements set out in the Higher Education Act.

<h2 id="section-2">Eligibility — Academic, Career, and Appointment Requirements</h2>

### Academic Requirements

The baseline standard is a doctoral degree.

A master's degree may still qualify when paired with recognized experience in the relevant field, and in fields such as arts and athletics, demonstrated career achievements often weigh more heavily than the degree itself.

The first thing to assess is how your credentials map onto Korean academic standards.

For overseas degrees, applicants not infrequently get stuck at the degree authentication stage handled by bodies like the [National Research Foundation of Korea](https://www.nrf.re.kr).

### Appointment Requirements

For E-1, the appointment format is just as critical as the applicant's personal credentials.

- A formal appointment contract (signed and sealed)
- A clearly specified appointment period
- Concrete teaching hours or research duties stated in writing
- An appointment processed through official channels such as the university personnel committee

In practice, weakness here triggers supplementary document requests almost immediately, no matter how thick the file is.

### Institutional Requirements

The hiring institution must qualify as a school under the Higher Education Act.

| Institution Type | E-1 Eligibility | Notes |
|----------|-------------|------|
| Four-year university | Eligible | Most common case |
| Junior college | Eligible | Verify appointment format |
| Graduate school university | Eligible | When degree programs are offered |
| Cyber university | Case-by-case | Depends on instruction format |
| Lifelong education facility | Limited | Consider E-7 or other statuses |
| Private academy | Not eligible | E-2 or other status applies |

When institutional eligibility is unclear, the school classification standards published by the [Ministry of Education](https://www.moe.go.kr) serve as a primary reference.

<h2 id="section-3">Differences by Appointment Type — Full-time, Visiting, Adjunct</h2>

While the title "professor" looks the same on the surface, review intensity varies considerably depending on the appointment format.

### Full-time Tenure-track Appointment

This is the most clear-cut case.

The appointment period, compensation, and teaching/research duties are spelled out concretely, and enrollment in the four major social insurance programs becomes mandatory.

### Visiting Professor

This format is intended for short-term teaching or research.

The shorter the appointment period, the more closely reviewers scrutinize the substance of the role and the level of compensation.

Compensation thresholds shift year by year, so the exact figure applicable to your case should be verified through consultation.

### Adjunct and Part-time Lecturer

This format involves teaching alongside another primary occupation. For a part-time lecturer to qualify under E-1, the appointment contract must have gone through formal procedures.

Insufficient explanation here can lead to the role being treated as mere "outsourced lecture hours," which puts qualification at risk.

<h2 id="section-4">Required Documents — Where Cases Actually Stall</h2>

### Standard Documents

| Document | Content | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| Visa application form | Completed by applicant | With photo attached |
| Passport copy | Validity of 6+ months | Verify remaining period |
| Appointment contract | Bearing university seal | Original or notarized copy |
| Diploma | Highest degree | Apostille or consular verification |
| Career certificate | Including previous employer | Field relevance matters |
| Employer's business registration | Under the university's name | Recently issued |
| Letter of guarantee | Prepared by hiring institution | Follow prescribed format |

### What Often Gets Left Out

The documents most frequently missed in practice are:

- Degree authentication paperwork (apostille or Korean consular verification)
- A contract stating the appointment period and compensation (summary versions trigger supplementary requests)
- A teaching plan or research proposal (to substantiate the actual role)
- A copy of the university's internal appointment approval documents

Even with a large stack of documents, weak proof on degree authentication or contract substance will stop a case in its tracks.

> **Practical Tip:** If the appointment contract omits "weekly teaching hours" or "required research time," a supplementary request is almost guaranteed. Address this at the contract drafting stage.

<h2 id="section-5">Decisive Points During Review</h2>

### Relevance of Degree and Major to the Role

Even with a doctorate, if the field of study is far removed from the teaching subject, reviewers will ask for additional justification.

The core principle is simple:

Degree → career → assigned duties should form a single connected line.

When this throughline is weak, the outcome can swing regardless of how many documents are submitted.

### Credibility of the Hiring Institution

The university's overall operational standing, its track record of hiring foreign faculty, and any past administrative issues with immigration status all exert quiet influence.

The same qualifications can move through processing at different speeds and face different review intensity depending on the institution, and the most suitable timing and approach are best worked out through consultation.

### Compensation Level and Substance

Review becomes especially stringent for visiting and adjunct formats.

If an appointment is nominal but compensation is unusually low or teaching hours are token in nature, the substance of the qualification comes into question.

Recent reviews of foreign faculty appointments have applied tighter standards on this point, so whether your case is affected should be checked individually.

<h2 id="section-6">Family Accompaniment and Stay Management</h2>

E-1 holders can invite a spouse and minor children on an F-3 dependent visa.

If family members wish to study or work in Korea, separate status considerations apply, and F-3 in principle does not permit employment.

### Extending the Period of Stay

The initial period of stay is granted within the bounds of the appointment period, and stay extensions are processed alongside appointment renewals.

When the extension timing falls close to the appointment expiration, scheduling complications commonly arise.

Applications to extend the period of stay can be filed up to four months before expiration.

### Path to Permanent Residency (F-5) and Naturalization

After maintaining E-1 status for a qualifying period, applicants may become eligible to apply for F-5 permanent residency.

That said, point thresholds, income, and stay duration criteria are revised yearly, so determining how close your current situation is to the permanent residency threshold requires case-by-case review.

<h2 id="section-8">FAQ</h2>

**Q1. Can I get an E-1 without a doctoral degree?**

The default standard is a doctorate, but a master's degree combined with relevant career experience — or a strong record in specialized fields such as arts or athletics — can keep the option open.

Whether your particular combination of credentials falls within the recognized range should be reviewed in advance.

**Q2. Does teaching at a cyber university qualify for E-1?**

The outcome depends on the institution's legal classification and the format of instruction.

Even among cyber universities, results differ based on whether degree programs are offered and the form of the appointment contract.

**Q3. Can a short-term visiting professor appointment qualify for E-1?**

Yes, provided the appointment contract went through formal procedures and the substance of the role and compensation are verifiable.

That said, shorter appointment periods tend to invite more rigorous scrutiny.

**Q4. Can I bring my family?**

A spouse and minor children can be invited on an F-3 dependent visa.

Inviting parents is generally difficult and is considered under different statuses depending on the situation.

**Q5. How do I get my foreign degree authenticated?**

For degrees from Apostille Convention countries, an apostille is required; for non-member countries, Korean consular verification is needed.

If the school name or degree designation differs from Korean conventions, additional justification may be requested.

**Q6. Can I change from E-1 to another status?**

If you move into a research role after your appointment ends, conversion to E-3 may be possible; for a corporate specialist role, E-7 is a candidate.

Each status carries its own requirements, so timing the change and preparing documents in advance prevents bottlenecks.
