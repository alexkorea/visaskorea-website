# E-6 Arts & Entertainment Visa: Eligibility and Review Criteria — Where Cases Actually Split in Practice

The E-6 Arts & Entertainment Visa splits into three subtypes depending on activity: E-6-1 (arts/entertainment), E-6-2 (hotel/entertainment venue performance), and E-6-3 (sports), each with completely different review standards.
The main targets are artistic activities like music, visual arts, and literature; entertainment activities such as singers, musicians, and models; and professional athletes.
This article walks through the eligibility requirements for each of the three types, the points where applications most often get stuck in actual review, sponsoring-entity requirements, required documents, and the patterns that trigger refusals.

<h2 id="section-1">The Three Subtypes of the E-6 Visa and How They Differ</h2>

The E-6 may look like a single visa, but in practice it splits into three streams with very different characters.
If you don't first determine which category you fall under, the paperwork gets tangled from the very beginning.

### E-6-1 Arts and Entertainment Activities

Music, visual arts, literature, theater, film, advertising and fashion modeling, and other arts activities undertaken for profit fall under this category.
Orchestra members, classical performers, ballet dancers and other dancers, advertising models, and actors in films and dramas are typical examples.
In practice, the key is how you prove the "artistic nature" of the activity.

### E-6-2 Performance at Hotels and Entertainment Venues

This covers performances at hotel facilities under the Tourism Promotion Act, entertainment venues, and similar locations.
This subtype receives the strictest review from a human-rights protection standpoint, and a performance recommendation letter is essentially mandatory.
Most cases get blocked at the Korea Media Rating Board recommendation stage.

### E-6-3 Sports Activities

This covers professional athletes, coaches, and trainers.
Formal contracts with clubs and registration with the relevant sport's federation are reviewed together.
e-sports athletes are increasingly applying under this subtype as well.

| Type | Main Targets | Recommendation Requirement | Review Intensity |
|------|----------|------------|----------|
| E-6-1 | Artists, models, film and drama performers | KMRB recommendation (for performances) | Moderate |
| E-6-2 | Hotel and entertainment venue performers | KMRB recommendation required | Very strict |
| E-6-3 | Professional athletes, coaches | Federation/association confirmation | Moderate |

<h2 id="section-2">E-6-1 Arts and Entertainment: Where Cases Actually Get Decided</h2>

A thick stack of documents does not guarantee approval.
On the contrary, if proof of the substance of the activity and the applicant's career is thin, the application gets caught immediately.

### Weak Career Documentation Stops You Right Away

Simply asserting that you engage in "artistic activity" is not enough.
Performance videos, album release records, exhibition catalogs, a list of works appeared in, awards, and press coverage need to be included together.
If this part is weak, the very authenticity of the activity is called into question.

### The Sponsoring Entity's Business Substance Is Reviewed Too

It's not only the applicant's career that's examined.
Whether the inviting agency, production company, performance venue, or modeling agency actually has the capacity to run the activity is reviewed alongside the applicant's profile.
Business registration, revenue records, the state of the office, and the history of past foreign-national invitations are all subject to review.

> **Practical tip:** If the sponsoring entity is a newly established corporation or has thin revenue, requests for supplementary materials will come in no matter how strong the applicant's career is. Review has to start at the stage of choosing the sponsoring entity.

### Reasonableness of Contract Terms

If the performance fee, activity period, activity location, and accommodation arrangements don't line up with industry norms, suspicion follows.
In fact, when a performance fee is unrealistically high or low, there are many cases where additional investigation is triggered on suspicion of a sham invitation.

<h2 id="section-3">E-6-2 Hotel and Entertainment Venue Performance — The Toughest Subtype</h2>

This has the highest refusal rate among E-6 visas.
Separate safeguards are in place because of concerns over human trafficking and prostitution.

### Korea Media Rating Board Performance Recommendation

The [Korea Media Rating Board](https://www.kmrb.or.kr) performance recommendation is in effect the first gate to clear.
The performance content, performers, venue, and contract terms must be reviewed in advance and a recommendation letter obtained before moving on to the visa application stage.
If you're refused at the recommendation stage, the visa application itself becomes difficult.

### Use of the Standard Contract

For E-6-2 hotel and entertainment venue performances, use of the standard contract is mandatory.
Clauses involving additional activities beyond the hours specified in the contract, forced auxiliary duties, or holding the performer's passport translate directly into grounds for refusal.
If contract clauses deviate from the standard, you get blocked right away.

### Past Enforcement History at the Venue

If the same venue has a history of administrative penalties for prostitution or human-rights violations, the invitation itself may be restricted.
It's common for applicants to be refused because of the venue's history, even when they themselves have done nothing wrong.

> **Caution:** For E-6-2, checks continue after entry to protect the applicant themselves. If labor in a form different from the standard contract is discovered, the applicant may also become subject to immigration measures.

<h2 id="section-4">Key Review Points for the E-6-3 Sports Visa</h2>

This covers professional athletes, coaches, and trainers, with confirmation from the sport's federation or association reviewed alongside the application.

### Formal Contracts and Registration

A formal contract with a club or team, the sport-specific federation registration certificate, and a confirmation of registration eligibility are the core documents.
Issuance of E-6-3 is difficult for amateur status, and such cases generally divert to D-2 (study abroad) or other status categories.

### Quotas on Foreign Athletes

Each sport has limits on the number of foreign athletes that can be registered.
If a club has already filled its foreign-player quota, visa issuance can be delayed even when a contract is in place.
This is typically the stage where the schedule gets tangled.

<h2 id="section-5">Commonly Required Documents and How to Prepare Them</h2>

There is a set of basic documents that applies to all three subtypes, with additional documents added per subtype.

| Document | E-6-1 | E-6-2 | E-6-3 |
|------|-------|-------|-------|
| Visa Issuance Certificate application | Required | Required | Required |
| Passport copy and photo | Required | Required | Required |
| Letter of invitation/employment contract | Required | Standard contract required | Club contract |
| Career certificate | Required | Required | Athlete registration certificate |
| Degree/certifications | If applicable | If applicable | If applicable |
| Performance recommendation | If performing | Required | Not applicable |
| Sponsor's business registration | Required | Required | Required |
| Tax/revenue records | Required | Required | Required |

### The Content of the Career Certificate Matters More Than Its Form

A career certificate is far stronger when it comes with a concrete list of works, performances, and competitions rather than a single page stating "active for X years."
Adding performance video URLs, music platform links, and screenshots of press coverage allows the reviewer to verify the substance of the activity at a glance.
Attached, verifiable materials earn trust faster than long-form writing.

### What's Often Missing from the Sponsoring Entity's File

Many submit only the business registration certificate, but actual review also calls for revenue records, the four-major-insurance member roster, and the office lease agreement.
Without these, suspicion of a "paper company" sets in.
Since this section can only be filled in with the sponsor's direct cooperation, prior coordination is essential.

<h2 id="section-6">Common Patterns That Lead to Refusal</h2>

Reasons for E-6 refusal are somewhat distinctive compared to other visas.
Beyond insufficient career documentation, human-rights and labor-related factors also come into play.

### Mismatch Between Actual Activity and Status of Stay

If someone enters on E-6-1 but is found performing at a hotel or entertainment venue, the individual faces immigration measures and the business faces administrative penalties.
Choose the wrong subtype and the visa may be issued, but things will unravel after entry.
You have to apply from the start under the subtype that matches the actual location of the activity.

### History of Activity After Entering on a Short-Term Visa (C-3)

If there's a history of entering on a tourism or short-term visit visa and engaging in performances or modeling, the individual is punished for illegal employment and the business for illegal hiring.
With such a history on record, the likelihood of refusal on a later formal E-6 application is high.
This is the very point where many cases get caught.

### Lack of Proof of Performance Fees and Living Expenses

A performance fee sufficient to maintain the applicant's livelihood during the activity period must be specified in the contract.
Unpaid activities or arrangements offering only room and board are met with suspicion in review.
The relevant criteria follow the notices of the [Ministry of Justice Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr), and since applicable standards can change, you should verify them at the time of application.

> **Caution:** Review has tightened recently as sham invitations have been uncovered more frequently in some performance and modeling sectors. Whether and how the rules apply to your specific case needs to be confirmed by a professional.

<h2 id="section-8">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

### Q1. Can I do work in other fields under an E-6 visa?

In principle, activity is limited to the scope of the subtype you were issued.
An E-6-1 holder performing at an E-6-2 venue constitutes activity outside the granted status and is subject to penalties.
To expand the scope, you need to obtain advance permission for activity outside your status or change subtypes.

### Q2. I only need to do one short performance — do I really need an E-6?

Short-term performances and recordings within 90 days are often handled under C-4 (short-term employment).
For repeated or continuous activities, or stays exceeding 90 days, E-6 is the correct choice.
Since this depends on the actual schedule and frequency of activities, case-by-case confirmation is needed.

### Q3. Can I bring my family with me?

Spouses and minor children can enter together on the F-3 (dependent) visa.
Note in advance that F-3 holders are restricted from working.
The period of stay follows that of the primary stayer (the E-6 holder).

### Q4. Can I apply for an E-6 even if my career is short?

What's looked at first is the substance of your activity and verifiable records of works and performances, not the number of years.
Even newcomers may have a viable application if music releases, video, exhibition records, and awards are clearly documented.
That said, the chances of approval rise only when the sponsoring entity's credibility backs you up as well.

### Q5. Can I change from E-6 to another status of stay?

There are cases of changing from E-6 to E-7 (specific activity) or F-2 (residence).
Eligibility for the change depends on the activity field, income, and period of stay.
Because the criteria are revised somewhat each year, verification at the time of application is needed.

### Q6. What happens if the Korea Media Rating Board denies the recommendation?

If denied at the recommendation stage, you cannot move on to the visa application stage.
Depending on whether the issue was performance content, the cast composition, or the venue's history, you may be able to reapply after supplementation.
If you misjudge which point to address, you'll be denied again for the same reason, so stage-by-stage review is needed.
